# Labour, Ration & Daily Devotion

## Citizenship Confers No Rights {#citizenship}

The previous Chapter described where a trillion people live. This one describes what their
lives consist of, and the sources begin with a single sentence that settles the legal
position of the entire species.

**The population of the Imperium is unimaginably vast. The Imperium's roughly one million
worlds are inhabited by humans or human-descended abhumans. Although they are all citizens
of the Imperium, citizenship confers no rights, only
responsibilities.**[^lex-imperial-citizen]

There is no charter, no guaranteed protection, and nothing a citizen may demand. The word
*citizen* in the Imperium denotes an obligation to the state and describes nothing the state
owes in return. Set that beside [[IV.T1.Ch2]], where the Arbites treat a trial as a privilege
of the clergy and the Inquisition, and the two provisions agree exactly. A person with no
rights cannot be denied any.

One distinction exists above that floor. **Adepts are servants of the Emperor and are legally
above simple citizens, possessing at the very least, the legal benefits of housing and
employment within the Imperium. The difference between adept and common citizen is much more
pronounced on Terra.**[^lex-imperial-citizen]

Housing and employment, guaranteed — to the clerks of the Administratum, and to nobody else.
The Imperium's hereditary bureaucratic caste is also its only legally protected one, and the
gap between them and everyone else is widest at the seat of government.

What the state requires instead is stated as a quantity. The **Imperial Tithe** — also called
the **Grand Harvesting**, the **Great Tithe** or **Terra's Due** — is **the amount of
materials an Imperial world must supply to the Administratum**.[^lex-imperial-tithe] It comes
in two forms. **The first is a military tithe requiring 1/10th of a planet's PDF being
gathered as new recruits for the Imperial Guard. The second, known as Exacta, includes all
other types of resources or wealth.**[^lex-imperial-tithe] The Departmento Munitorum collects
the first, the Departmento Exacta the second — both offices of [[IV.T1.Ch2]] — and the tithe
is taken by **Administratum authorities, successors of the Crusade-era Eaxectors, which
periodically arrive on the subject world**.[^lex-imperial-tithe]

*Periodically* is doing a great deal of work. **The frequency of visits varies world to
world, but in at least one case the Departmento Munitorum visited a planet nine times in a
year, demanding new Imperial Guard conscripts each time.**[^lex-imperial-tithe]

Nine levies in twelve months. A tenth of the planetary defence force, nine times over, from a
world whose Governor has no standing to refuse and whose population has no standing to
object.

What may be demanded is unbounded in kind. **A tithe can be any form of material: food,
weapons, minerals, people, etc.**[^lex-imperial-tithe] *People* appears in that list between
minerals and the abbreviation. And the machinery for tracking it does not work: **despite the
number of agencies tracking tithes, the sheer size of the Imperium and the difficulty of Warp
travel makes the time-consuming process of tithe collection excessively confusing and
inefficient**.[^lex-imperial-tithe]

Then the provision that defines Imperial rule more precisely than any constitutional document
in this Volume. **It is the duty of Planetary Governors to ensure the tithe of their world is
paid, and any world that fails to pay its tithe meets with vicious reprisal from the Adeptus
Terra. At the slightest breach of protocol, Imperial officials will often order the Adeptus
Arbites to immediately execute the Governor. However, as long as the planetary ruler
dutifully pays his world's tithe, he may rule the planet in almost any way he sees
fit.**[^lex-imperial-tithe]

That is the whole arrangement, stated by the Imperium about itself. A Governor may do
anything to his population — anything at all — provided the shipment arrives. Miss it, and he
is executed without process. [[IV.T1.Ch2]] records the Arbites watching a coup succeed
because the new ruler kept paying; this is the same rule from the other end. The Imperium is
not indifferent to how its worlds are governed as an oversight. Indifference is the policy,
and the tithe is the only term in the contract.

## The Ration {#ration}

What a citizen eats depends on altitude, as [[IV.T2.Ch4]] establishes, and at the bottom of
the hive the sources name the food.

**Corpse-Starch** is **a synthetic food appearing as a grey paste that is created from the
recycled dead bodies of the Imperium's citizens**.[^lex-corpse-starch] It is **processed into
a tasteless grey paste**, also into **crackers**, and is **most often seen packaged in
tins**.[^lex-corpse-starch]

The purpose is administrative before it is nutritional. **Corpse Starch serves the dual
purpose of dealing with masses of dead bodies and feeding the down-trodden
masses.**[^lex-corpse-starch] A hive world produces two problems at scale — corpses and
hunger — and the Imperium has arranged for each to solve the other.

It is not a rumour or a local aberration. It **can be found offered on various hive worlds**
and **can be part of the rations available to unluckier Astra Militarum regiments** as
**supplied by the Departmento Munitorum**.[^lex-corpse-starch] The same office that levies a
tenth of a planet's defence force also issues its soldiers tins of processed human being.

The manufacturing is described with a precision that is worse than vagueness would be.
**Corpse Starch production is a highly supervised and intensive rendering process that
thoroughly purees flesh into a unified blandness devoid of flavour and
identity.**[^lex-corpse-starch] It is **often manufactured through the process of protein
reclamation, which happens in large, corpse-blending machines known as protein
reclamators** — **large churns that corpses are dumped into, with a pipe that siphons out
ground solid corpse matter**.[^lex-corpse-starch]

*Devoid of flavour and identity.* The second word is the operative one. The process is
supervised specifically to ensure that what comes out cannot be traced to who went in, which
means somebody considered the alternative.

On Necromunda there is an industry and a guild. The **Mercator Pallidus** **sends Corpse
Harvesting Parties, led by their Pale Consorts and Bone Scrivener attendants, to acquire dead
bodies, which are then sent to the Corpse Grinders for processing into the final
product**.[^lex-corpse-starch]

A chartered guild with officers and clerks, whose licensed trade is collecting the dead. The
Clan Houses of [[IV.T2.Ch4]] hold power by controlling heat and transmission; this one holds
it by controlling the supply of corpses.

And the reaction to eating it is policed. **A meal of pure corpse starch is noted as poor
quality.** But: **in some parts of the Imperium it is considered overtly seditious conduct to
express disgust in response to the weird taste of corpse starch. If a worker's foreman hears
of this, said individual could find themselves on a prison ship within
hours.**[^lex-corpse-starch]

That is the most complete description of Imperial citizenship in this Chapter. The state
feeds you the recycled bodies of your neighbours; noticing the taste is sedition; and the
person who reports you is your foreman, not a policeman. The Arbites of [[IV.T1.Ch2]] monitor
whether **those who foment rebellion are being shut down before they can spread their
message**, and this is what that looks like at the scale of one worker complaining about
lunch.

## Shrine Worlds and the Pilgrim Economy {#shrine-worlds}

The third element of a citizen's life is devotion, and it is organised into worlds.

**Shrine Worlds** — also **Shrineworlds** and **Cathedral Worlds** — are **Imperial worlds
devoted to a Saint of the Imperium and are often named in their honour**. They are **ruled
directly by the Adeptus Ministorum and attract huge amounts of pilgrims from all over the
Imperium**.[^lex-shrine-world]

Note the jurisdiction. These are worlds where the Ecclesiarchy of [[IV.T3.Ch8]] is not a
church operating under a Governor but the government itself. The Ministorum's Cardinals hold
whole planets outright, which is why the seat the Ecclesiarch took in early M32 mattered so
much: the state religion is also a territorial power.

Often the entire surface is given over to the dead. **Often Shrine Worlds are places of
eternal rest for holy men and women of the Imperium, and the entire surface of these sacred
planets are given over to the hallowed crypts of sainted and great monolithic
cathedra.**[^lex-shrine-world]

Most are not purpose-built. **Most shrine worlds usually fall into another category — for
example, Hagia is an agri world while Herodor is a Hive World. They have a strong
relationship with the saint in question, such as being the world of his or her birth or
martyrdom.**[^lex-shrine-world] Sainthood, and the process that produces it, belongs to
[[IV.T3.Ch9]]; what matters here is that a planet's economy can be reclassified by an event
in one person's biography.

The atmosphere is manufactured, and its supply chain is the detail this Section turns on.
**Shrine worlds are often thick with incense and candle smoke. Tallow Worlds are known to
export significant amounts of their candles to nearby shrine worlds.** And then:
**although, these candles made from rendered down human bodies tend to give off an unpleasant
smell.**[^lex-shrine-world]

There are worlds whose export commodity is candles rendered from human corpses, and the
Imperium's holiest planets are lit by them. Set that beside the previous Section and the
system closes: the dead of the Imperium are rendered into food for the living at the bottom
of the hive, and into the light by which the living pray at the shrine. Two industries, two
guilds, one raw material. Neither article mentions the other, and neither treats the practice
as remarkable.

The Imperium also acknowledges the inverse of these worlds. **There are also the dark mirrors
to these places of Imperial devotion — fallen worlds where the inhabitants offer up ceaseless
prayer to the Dark Gods. These places of obscene sacrifices and bloody rites are not suffered
continued existence for long.**[^lex-shrine-world]

The difference between a shrine world and a world marked for destruction is which entity is
being prayed to. Both are planets given over entirely to worship, both organised around
sacrifice — the Chaos worlds of [[V.T1.Ch5]] explicitly, the Imperial ones through the tallow
trade and the crypts. The Imperium draws the line at the object of devotion, and draws it
absolutely.

Take the three Sections together and a citizen's life is fully specified. You are born with
no rights and a set of responsibilities. Your world owes a tenth of its defence force and
whatever else Terra names, as often as the collectors arrive, and your Governor may do
anything he likes to you provided he pays it. What you eat is determined by which level you
live on, and at the lower levels it is made from the people who lived there before you —
saying so aloud is sedition. And the consolation offered is a pilgrimage to a planet owned
outright by the church, lit by candles rendered from the same source as your rations.
