# The Schola Progenium & Imperial Service

## Orphans of the Emperor's Servants {#intake}

The Imperium takes the children of its dead and raises them itself. Presented that way it
sounds like charity, and the sources are unusually direct about the fact that it is not.

The **Schola Progenium** is **an institution of various academies run by Missionaries of the
Ecclesiarchy**, teaching and training **orphans of Imperial officials and other Highborn who
have given their lives in the service of the Emperor of Mankind**. The intake is **primarily
of noble birth**, and **the child stock is indoctrinated into the Imperial Cult from an early
age, and soon learns to regard the Emperor as their spiritual
father**.[^lex-schola-progenium] It **is regarded as providing the best education the
Imperium can offer — utter loyalty being the foremost trait instilled into
students**.[^lex-schola-progenium]

*Child stock.* That is the sources' own phrase, and it recurs. The Schola is run by the
Ecclesiarchy of [[IV.T3.Ch8]] but is **an offshoot of the Departmento Munitorum** — the
Administratum office of [[IV.T1.Ch2]] that levies soldiers.[^lex-schola-progenium] Its
Commandant is **one of the lesser members of the Senatorum
Imperialis**,[^lex-schola-progenium] which places him in the building where the Imperium is
governed, as [[IV.T1.Ch1]] records, without a seat among the Twelve.

The purpose is stated without any pretence at all. **The Schola Progenia does not feed,
clothe, and educate these children out of any sense of altruism or charity. Rather, it is to
raise the best and brightest as officers and elite warriors who see the God-Emperor with
unswerving adoration.**[^lex-schola-progenium] And the method: it **is designed to
homogenise, break and rebuild the children of the Imperium's ruling classes in order to
create iron-fisted authoritarians that will keep the grinding wheels of the Adeptus Terra
functioning**.[^lex-schola-progenium]

*Break and rebuild.* The institution that supplies the Imperium's officer class describes its
own process in those words.

How children arrive is the part that turns the Chapter. Some come as genuine orphans: **newly
made aristocrat-blooded orphans that are brought to the attention of an Officio Prefectus
official** are shipped **to the nearest Schola Progenium**.[^lex-schola-progenium] Others do
not. **Other times, a Schola may steal particularly remarkable children from a backwater
world, having a Commissar murder their parents if
needed.**[^lex-schola-progenium]

The Schola manufactures the orphans it requires. A promising child on an obscure world does
not need to have lost their parents; a Commissar is dispatched, and then they have. Every
sympathetic reading of this institution ends at that sentence.

There is a third route, which is commerce. **Noble families might trade their children over
to the Schola Progenium in return for various political favours.** Those children **may
undergo elaborate "orphaning" rituals where all ties and inheritance to their former families
and ancestry are severed**, and **are told to forget their prior lives and cultures and are
stripped of any belongings they may have had**.[^lex-schola-progenium]

A ceremony that converts a living child into an orphan by decree, so that a house can buy a
political favour. Note the quotation marks around *orphaning* — they are the article's, and
they are the only acknowledgement anywhere in the source that the word is being used
strangely.

**New cadets arrive at various ages, typically from ages 6 to 12, and may arrive in groups
that were all pulled from a single disaster.** And a filter is applied: **those hailing from
a world that has suffered a plague or spiritual rebellion will be removed from the
equation.**[^lex-schola-progenium]

Children from a world that suffered a heresy are not admitted, whatever their aptitude,
because the Imperium regards contamination as heritable. The same logic governs the Black
Ships of [[I.T2.Ch9]] and the mutant provisions of [[IV.T1.Ch2]].

The buildings are chosen to prevent departure. **Schola facilities exist on many worlds
across the Imperium, primarily on planets with strong connections to either Terra or the
Adeptus Ministorum. Many Schola are deliberately built in remote or isolated regions of their
worlds, both to ensure the security of the facilities, and to prevent the progena from
attempting to escape.**[^lex-schola-progenium]

And they are guarded against rescue. **The masters and guards of the scholum won't hesitate
to slaughter their young charges with bullet and chainsword if any outside force attempts to
free or kidnap them.**[^lex-schola-progenium]

The Schola's answer to an attempted rescue is to kill the children. That is a policy about
what the children are: not wards to be protected but assets that must not fall into other
hands.

## Childhood Under Sol-Lamps {#childhood}

What the intake experiences is documented in more detail than almost anything else in this
Volume, and it is worth setting out plainly rather than summarising.

The youngest arrivals **can be any age, from infancy upwards, as many scholae double as
orphanages and nurseries for the children of Imperial
servants**.[^lex-schola-progenium] They are raised without daylight: the facilities **are
rarely built to allow the progena to see the sun or sky. Days are spent under sol-lamps to
condition them to a simulacrum of a sun in its
absence.**[^lex-schola-progenium]

That is the hive of [[IV.T2.Ch4]] reproduced deliberately, for children who were mostly born
elsewhere. And the nights are documented too: **nights are spent listening to the sobbing of
Schola-mates and punishments being given in nearby
antechambers.**[^lex-schola-progenium]

Their parentage is retrievable and forbidden. **While the child-orphans are technically able
to discover their true parentage in the Schola's meticulously curated archives of such
information, it is frowned upon and dissuaded. The truth of forefathers, especially their
deviations and sins, are stored for later use if needed for future
investigations.**[^lex-schola-progenium]

The archive exists, is meticulous, and is maintained not for the children but as evidence
against them later. What they are told instead is that **the Emperor is the sole parent they
require, and that the Schola is a gift few in the Imperium receive** — and that **it should be
sufficient to know that any of the many martyrs of the Imperium could be their
parents**.[^lex-schola-progenium]

The curriculum is basic and religious. Children **are raised in creches where they receive
their basic education**, taught **the basics of reading and prayer by blunt-headed abbots,
narrow-eyed deacons, and equally dim-witted servitor-tutors**, with classes in **rhetorics,
arithmetics, physical exercise and the lives of saints**.[^lex-schola-progenium]

Then a detail that would be comic if it were not what it is. **The lobotomised cyborgs
function as the children's primary companions, acting as friends and entertainers for the
groups of future servants to the Throne being raised. The servitors will sometimes lead
role-plays for these children or play games of childish cruelty, like knock-down-heretics or
find-the-mutant.**[^lex-schola-progenium] Another game they play is
**Inquisitor**.[^lex-schola-progenium]

The primary companions of these children are servitors — the mind-wiped cyborgs of
[[IV.T6.Ch20]], made from convicts and the surplus. A lobotomised human being is the closest
thing to a friend the Imperium's future officer class is given, and the games it leads them
in are rehearsals for the work they will do.

Cruelty is not tolerated; it is curriculum. **When a single student in the class
underperforms, it is normal for the entire class to be punished harshly.** And **unchecked
brutality amongst peers is a tradition that is encouraged and cruel bullying is
rewarded.**[^lex-schola-progenium] The rigours **are designed to be as harsh as possible,
constantly testing their physical and mental fortitude** so that **the Schola's cadets are
conditioned to handle and overcome challenges in a manner that best serves the Imperium's
authoritarian regime**.[^lex-schola-progenium]

Assessment is annual and total. **Every Emperor's Day, all wards are assessed for aptitude,
piety and dedication. Those who excel in a specific area are split off into special classes or
transferred to the respective specialised facilities. Those who are found to have no special
aptitude for a role in the wider Imperium form a staff of menials and guards who look after
the needs of their founding orphanage. Others are swept away to more secretive service to the
Imperium.**[^lex-schola-progenium]

Three outcomes: promotion, permanent confinement as staff in the building you grew up in, or
removal to something unnamed. Sorting happens **typically around the age of
10**.[^lex-schola-progenium]

And the removals are visible to the children. **Eventually, children's Schola-mates disappear
one-by-one without any explanation from the adult overseers or servitors. Other children
meanwhile are taken with black bags placed over their heads from Schola grounds and
interrogated about anomalous activity or hidden secrets regarding their classmates (cheating,
copying, slacking, etc.) in secluded chambers. Underachievers and malingerers are made into
cruel spectacles for other progena. The children may even hear rumour of even crueller fates
and crueller institutions that draw from the
Scholas.**[^lex-schola-progenium]

The rumour is true, and this codex has already recorded it. [[IV.T1.Ch3]] establishes that the
Officio Assassinorum recruits from the Schola Progenium, tests candidates against each other
on the voyage to Terra, and expects nine in ten to die on the way. The children who vanish
without explanation are, some of them, going there.

One category is removed for a different reason. **Any potential psykers will be rooted out and
sent about the Black Ships.**[^lex-schola-progenium]

By adolescence a specialism should have emerged, and the youths **are shipped off to new
locations**, **sometimes being placed aboard interstellar munitions shipments en route to
their new academies**.[^lex-schola-progenium] There, alongside the specialised training, they
meet **the unregulated cruelty of their peers**, which is **allowed and encouraged, justified
as an adherence to custom and values, upheld as vaunted Schola traditions across the
galaxy**.[^lex-schola-progenium] Graduation has no fixed point: it ranges from **16** or
**17** to well into **an individual's early twenties**, and **there is no set age for
graduation**.[^lex-schola-progenium]

## Progena, Truants and the Dead {#outcomes}

The sources sort every child who enters a Schola into exactly three categories, and the third
is listed as plainly as the first two.

**The students of the Schola Progenium fall into three categories: those who have completed
their education are progenii, those few who escaped their schola facility are truants, and
lastly are the dead who have either failed their studies or their attempt at
freedom.**[^lex-schola-progenium]

Death is one of the three recorded outcomes of an Imperial education, and the article does not
present it as a scandal: **death is commonplace in the
scholum**.[^lex-schola-progenium]

**Truants** are **those former students who manage to escape a schola facility**. Their
reasons are given without editorial: **a student may wish to escape for any number of reasons,
whether it be to simply escape the torturous rigours and abusive masters, or to find the
freedom to decide their own futures.**[^lex-schola-progenium] Where they end up closes a loop
with [[IV.T2.Ch4]]: **the children that do manage to escape may end up living a life of
freedom amongst the local Hive Scum**.[^lex-schola-progenium]

The word the sources choose is *freedom*, and they apply it to the underhive. A child who
runs from the best education the Imperium can offer joins the masterless people at the bottom
of the tower, and the article calls that liberty.

The **Progena** who complete the course are **frequently sent for further training and service
within other Imperial organisations**, and are **often earmarked for roles that call for the
most rigorous dedication to the Imperium**.[^lex-schola-progenium] The destinations are a
roll-call of this Volume: **the Ordo Tempestus, Adeptus Arbites, high offices in the Adeptus
Administratum, Adeptus Terra, Officio Medicae, Commissars of the Imperial Guard, officers of
the Imperial Navy, Imperial Assassins, and even budding
Inquisitors**.[^lex-schola-progenium] **Female Progena might end up joining one of the Orders
of the Adepta Sororitas**, and **boys and girls who prove exceptional combatants may join the
ranks of the Tempestus Scions**.[^lex-schola-progenium]

Stop on that list. The Arbites who police the tithe, the Administratum's senior clerks, the
Commissars, the Navy's officers, the assassins, the Inquisition, the Sisters of Battle. Every
institution in Volume IV that requires absolute reliability is staffed from the same source:
children taken between six and twelve, some of them orphaned to order, raised without daylight
by lobotomised servitors, sorted at ten, and told that any martyr might have been their
parent.

One further note is recorded, and it matters for [[I.T2.Ch6]]: **Progenii will almost never
have psychic powers, unless they emerge later in life, as any potential psykers are keenly
rooted out from amongst their peers and sent to the Black
Ships.**[^lex-schola-progenium]

## Commissars and Scions {#commissars}

Two of those destinations belong here, because both are the Schola's output rather than the
Astra Militarum's.

**Commissars** are **special Imperial officers assigned to Imperial Guard regiments and
Imperial Navy ships, whose purpose is to enforce discipline and devotion to the Emperor of
Mankind**. They are **independent of the conventional Imperial Guard Hierarchy**, attached
instead to **the Departmento Munitorum's Commissariat**, and are **formally part of the
Militarum Tempestus**.[^lex-commissar]

Their recruitment is the Schola's: **cadet Commissars are drawn from the Schola Progenium —
Ecclesiarchy-run schools where the pupils have already received a strict Imperial Cult
education.**[^lex-commissar] The institution that murders parents to acquire children also
supplies the officers who will, if required, do the murdering — the previous Section records a
Commissar performing exactly that errand for a Schola.

Their powers are absolute within the regiment. **Every regiment has one or more attached
Commissars who remain with the regiment throughout its duties**, and they have **authority
over both the soldiers and regimental officers**. Critically, **Commissars have both the right
and duty to immediately execute any Imperial guardsman or officer who shows cowardice or
incompetence in battle**. They must also **ensure the regiment does not harbour the likes of
Genestealer Cultists, Chaos heretics, spies, or
mutants**.[^lex-commissar]

*Right and duty.* Not a discretion. An officer who fails to shoot a man for incompetence has
himself been incompetent.

They are also diplomats. In regiments **like the Death Korps of Krieg**, another duty is **to
act as diplomats and mediators between Planetary Governors, civilians, the Adeptus Mechanicus,
or other authorities that may not agree with the actions of their regiment in a military
campaign**.[^lex-commissar] The Krieg of [[IV.T5.Ch17]] are the extreme case, and the fact
that a regiment needs a permanent envoy to explain it to the Imperium's own officials says
what it is.

The relationship this produces has a recorded failure mode. **It is rare, but possible, for
Commissars to be "fragged" in a staged friendly fire accident by their own
troops.**[^lex-commissar]

The **Tempestus Scions** are the other product. They are **the elite fighting force of the
Ordo Tempestus, a quasi-independent agency within the Administratum that also includes
Commissars**.[^lex-tempestus-scions] Taken from the Schola Progenium, **Progena are then taken
by the Ordo Tempestus to be schooled in its harsh and miserable training
camps**,[^lex-tempestus-scions] and the Ordo looks for **Schola Progenium recruits that
display self-sufficiency, intuition, raw aggression, free thought, and
improvisation**.[^lex-tempestus-scions]

*Free thought* appears in that list, which is startling in an institution designed to
homogenise, break and rebuild. The Imperium requires a small number of people capable of
independent judgement and has decided the safest way to obtain them is to grow them in the
most controlled environment it possesses, then select for the trait it spent twelve years
suppressing.

The result is what the conditioning is for: **Tempestus Scions are indoctrinated with a sense
of obedience and duty that overrides any instinct of self
preservation**.[^lex-tempestus-scions] A distinction is recorded and worth keeping straight:
**Tempestus Scions are storm troopers, but not all storm troopers are Tempestus
Scions.**[^lex-tempestus-scions]

The Schola's history includes one period where the arrangement collapsed into what it always
resembled. **During the Age of Apostasy, the Scholas were rife with child slavery and
depravity. The Ecclesiarchy further fuelled their exorbitant wealth with child slaves working
in mines and factories.**[^lex-schola-progenium]

[[III.T4.Ch19]] records Goge Vandire holding the Administratum and the Ecclesiarchy at once.
The Schola sits at the junction of exactly those two bodies — a Munitorum offshoot staffed by
the Ministorum — which is why it was the first thing to be converted into a slaving operation
when one man held both. The institution did not need to be altered to become that. It needed
only to stop being supervised.
