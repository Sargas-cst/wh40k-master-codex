# Hive Worlds & the Underhive

## A Trillion People on a Planet the Size of Terra {#hive-worlds}

Most human beings alive in the 41st Millennium live inside a building, and have never been
outside one.

A **Hive** is **a huge urban accretion, built-up over many thousands of years, and home to
countless numbers of people**.[^lex-hive] There are **approximately 32,380 Hive Worlds in
the Imperium** — though **the exact number is difficult to establish due to the bureaucratic
features of the Imperium itself**.[^lex-hive-world] The Administratum of [[IV.T1.Ch2]] has
lost track of its own departments; it has also lost count of its most populous worlds to the
nearest few thousand.

The scale is the thing to fix first. Hive Worlds **have a population far too numerous to be
fed or supported on their own, often exceeding a trillion inhabitants on a planet the size
of Terra**. These planets **often have no more natural ground surface left, having become a
giant urban agglomeration, with new construction happening on top of old
ones**.[^lex-hive-world] **Billions of people can live crowded together in a single Hive
city**,[^lex-hive-world] and **there are often several thousand hives on a single Hive
world**.[^lex-hive]

A trillion people on one planet, in several thousand towers, with no ground left anywhere.
And the pattern is not exceptional — **around the entire Imperium, over 85% of all planets
have at least one hive city cluster**.[^lex-hive]

The consequence of that density is a permanent dependency, and it is the most important
strategic fact in this Chapter. **The population of every Hive World is enormous, and almost
all food needs to be imported.**[^lex-hive-world] What follows from that is stated without
euphemism:

**A Hive World rendered temporarily inaccessible through Warp space will suffer a
devastating famine within a very short space of time. It will become a vast catacomb of
lunatics driven to excesses of anarchic, urban savagery by starvation and
claustrophobia.**[^lex-hive-world]

That is the Age of Strife of [[III.T2.Ch9]] as a standing condition rather than a historical
period. Terra starved when the shipping stopped in M25; thirty-two thousand worlds are
arranged so that the same thing happens to them within weeks. The Warp storms of
[[I.T1.Ch4]] do not need to destroy a hive world. They only need to isolate it.

The Imperium's assessment of its own citizens follows. **Hive Worlds are dangerous, being
too large to monitor safely, and their citizens are typically unbalanced, if not utterly
crazed. It has been known for the Adeptus Arbites to cull these planets in order to bring
their populations down to manageable levels.**[^lex-hive-world]

*Cull.* The police force of [[IV.T1.Ch2]] — the one that ignores murder unless it affects
production quotas — periodically reduces the population of a world because the population
has become inconveniently large. It is listed as a known practice, not an atrocity.

What the Imperium gets in return is soldiers. **Each of these Hive Worlders is a potential
soldier for Imperial forces, including the Imperial Guard and the Space Marines. Hive Worlds
contribute the vast bulk of the recruits for the Imperial Guard.** The reason is
explicit: **the violent gangland lifestyle which most residents are forced to live means
they are already hardened and experienced in warfare. Almost every recruit will already know
how to handle a gun.**[^lex-hive-world]

Read that as the Imperium's recruitment policy and it becomes something else. The Astra
Militarum of [[IV.T5.Ch16]] draws most of its strength from worlds whose inhabitants are
pre-trained by conditions the Imperium created and maintains. The gang wars in the lower
levels are not a policing failure. They are the basic-training programme, run at no cost,
and the Arbites cull the surplus.

They are also a source of colonists. **Hive Worlds serve to populate newly discovered
planets. Imperial citizens are gathered from various Hive Worlds (willingly or unwillingly)
and shipped off to distant colonies.**[^lex-hive-world] The parenthesis is the sources'
own.

Economically the arrangement is simple and one-directional. Hive Worlds **have huge
import/export ratios, exporting a vast range of materials, and relying on imports of food
and water**, and **in common with most other Imperial worlds, Hive World society has
distinct classes, with a ruling class and a working class. Invariably the lower classes
inhabit the lower and more decayed and polluted levels of the
Hive.**[^lex-hive-world]

Class is altitude. That is not a metaphor in a hive; it is an address.

Not every hive is a tower. **Exotic planetary environments give rise to unusual hives**, such
as those of **Landunder**, which **cling to the floating surface crust and extend downwards
into the world's oceans**. **Even gas giants can host hive cities suspended in their upper
atmospheres, like Scitalyss or Vesmir II, which are sometimes known as
skyhives** — connected to the planet below **via elevator mineshafts providing a steady flow
of raw materials**.[^lex-hive]

A city hanging in the atmosphere of a gas giant, mining downwards on cables. The Imperium
cannot build a machine it lacks a template for, per [[III.T2.Ch7]], and it can do this.

## The Vertical City, Read from the Top Down {#anatomy}

The anatomy that follows is recorded in detail for **Necromunda**, and this Chapter keeps
that attribution rather than assuming every hive is identical. What it describes is a city
where social position is a physical elevation, and the sources present it as a cross-section.

**Hives consist of great housing and factory blocks, forming ranges of man-made
mountains.** Their **individual peaks or towers are called city spires**, and **a close
group of hives is known as a hive cluster**. A hive **consists of many layers; the lowest
layers form the underhive**.[^lex-hive]

And then the line that defines the experience of most of the species. **Hives are worlds in
themselves, where the populace has never seen the outer world, and concepts such as the
ground and sky are completely alien.**[^lex-hive]

Not *have not* seen. *Are alien to.* A trillion people per world, across thirty-two thousand
worlds, for whom the sky is not a thing they are missing but a thing they have no category
for.

**The Spire** is the top. **The upper hive is the region in which the administrators of the
hive and various important people live. The homes are better, space is a premium and power
is free for all. Life is easy here.** The spires are **the furthest from the horrors of the
lower levels and as such are the best fitted**, and **some hive spires pierce through the
cloud barriers**.[^lex-hive]

*Power is free for all.* Four words, at the top of the structure. Hold them for the
manufactory levels below.

**The Shell** is the skin. It is **punctuated by deep vertical and angled shafts that allow
light and air into the hive's core**, covered by **movable, massive shields**, and it houses
**travel tunnels, gateway fortresses, and garrison blocks**, plus **giant defence lasers
mounted on the shell** that **target orbiting threats, whether human or alien
spacecraft**.[^lex-hive]

The shell's other job is weather. It defends **against fierce ash storms**, which **can strip
away outer layers, including laser defences and essential facilities, threatening the
stability of the hive**, so **regular maintenance by work gangs is necessary to reinforce and
repair the shell**.[^lex-hive] Somewhere on the outside of the city, permanently, there are
crews patching the wall against the weather so the tower does not open.

**The Heat Sink** is the engine, and it explains the whole economy. At the core of every
spire is **a vertical shaft known as the heat sink, extending from the topmost levels to deep
below the planet's crust** — **a vast, hollow plasteel tube, several kilometres wide**, which
**harnesses heat from the planet's core to generate power for the spire**. Generator stations
along it **convert raw heat into energy, which is distributed to manufactories and hab layers
around the core**.[^lex-hive]

Then the sentence that pays off "power is free for all." **In the lower levels, the heat sink
provides warmth but no direct power.**[^lex-hive]

The same shaft runs the length of the city. At the top the electricity is free and unmetered;
at the bottom the identical structure delivers heat and nothing else. The hive is not a place
where the poor happen to have less. It is a machine that carries power past them.

Who owns that machine is the local politics. **Control of power generation systems is a major
marker of a powerful clan in Necromunda's inner core, as clans in whose territory these
systems fall earn substantial income. Other clans may control territories through which power
is transmitted, extracting tolls from factories and power producers. This creates a feudal
economy where clans act as producers, suppliers, and consumers.** In the upper habs, **power
services are regulated and controlled by the government, specifically by the troops of Lord
Helmawr**.[^lex-hive]

Feudalism, rebuilt from scratch inside a building, around a heat pipe. The tolls are levied
on transmission rather than land, which is the only difference.

**The Hab Zones** are where people live, and the sources grade them by light. **The majority
of a hive's population belongs to the indentured worker class, living in overcrowded and
squalid hab zones**, and **social standing within the spire is reflected by residential
levels**. At the top **the elite live luxuriously in the uppermost levels, enjoying natural
light, fresh air, and real food imported from agri-worlds**. Below them are **the twilight
levels, where conditions are harsher with dim natural light, no fresh air, and heavily
recycled food**. And at the bottom is **the undercity, shrouded in artificial light and
characterized by extreme recycling, including air and even
people**.[^lex-hive]

*Including air and even people.* That clause is the reason [[IV.T2.Ch5]] exists, and this
codex will not soften it: at the bottom of the hive the recycling loop includes the
inhabitants. Sunlight, real food, recycled food, no light and no exemption — four rungs, and
your position on them is set by what floor you were born on.

**The Manufactories** are the point of the whole structure. The industrial complexes **produce
various goods traded for food to sustain the planet's vast population**, extending **from
below the hab zones to the surface of the Ash Wastes**, with **their waste solidifying around
the hive bases and adding to the planet's layers**. As ash rises, **lower factories are buried
but remain operational as long as waste can be pumped to the
surface**.[^lex-hive]

A factory continues working after it has been buried by its own by-products. That is the
Imperium in one image, and it is also literally how the ground under these cities came to
exist.

Production is organised by the same feudal logic. It is **controlled by numerous clans, each
fitting into a feudal system of supply and demand**, with **the six Clan Houses** as
intermediaries; alliances and feuds between them **often result in gang
warfare**.[^lex-hive] And the workers are inventory: they **live near the manufactories, are
essential resources, and may undergo expensive surgical enhancements to improve their
utility, making them highly valuable to their employers**.[^lex-hive]

Surgery to make a worker more useful, paid for by the employer, which makes the worker a more
valuable asset. The servitors of [[IV.T6.Ch20]] are the end of that road; this is the
beginning of it, applied to people who are still legally citizens.

## Below the Law {#underhive}

Beneath the manufactories the Imperium's authority simply stops, and what fills the gap is
the subject of this Section.

The **Underhive**, also known as the **Badzones**, is **the area below the Hive cities, out of
reach of the laws of the Clan Houses, the Planetary Governor, and the Merchants
Guilds**.[^lex-underhive] Its origin is archaeological rather than social: these areas
**predate the hive cities by centuries or even millennia, originating from Necromunda's
earliest settlements before the planet's ecology was devastated**, and the underhive, **the
remnants of these old cities, may still contain relics from the original colony
ships**.[^lex-underhive]

The bottom of the hive is the top of the first colony. People are living in the ruins of the
settlement their ancestors arrived in, underneath a city built on top of it, and the wreckage
of the ships that brought humanity to that world is still down there somewhere. The lost
technology the Mechanicus of [[IV.T6.Ch19]] hunts across the galaxy is, on these worlds,
directly below the factory floor.

Physically it is a honeycomb. A hive **is made of several hab-domes stitched together with
tunnels and shafts over a long period of time**, and the underhive interior is **a vast,
industrial-scaled cathedral of metal and decay, where machines the size of battleships have
been abandoned long ago** — with the detail that makes the place: **it is said that some gangs
worship those ancient machines as hungry gods, to which they sacrifice
captives**.[^lex-underhive]

Machine-worship at the bottom of the hive, with human sacrifice, in a civilisation whose
official religion is *also* machine-worship. The Cult Mechanicus of [[IV.T6.Ch19]] venerates
the machine spirit and reveres technology it cannot explain. The difference between the Cult
and the gang is the altitude, and the sacrifices.

The domes themselves **are filled with rubble, toxic dust, and filth, creating a hazardous
environment**, each providing **a wide open space, divided into zones of factories, houses,
commercial buildings, and other structures**. **Between each dome, the maze of tunnels and
shafts is the scene of bitter gang wars and bloody raids**, which is why the underhive
**highly values scouts who can navigate them**.[^lex-underhive]

People live there anyway, and the sources are careful to record that they build rather than
merely survive. **Despite the harsh living conditions, habitable pockets exist and are
fiercely contested by gangs for their valuable remnants of machinery and minerals. Many areas
remain unexplored due to blocked and flooded tunnels.**[^lex-underhive]

**Underhive communities are usually small, consisting of simple holes and dens, although
larger settlements like Dust Falls and Dead End Pass exist. These are protected by stockades
made from debris, with shelters and buildings repurposed from ruins. Survival involves
cultivating edible fungi and algae or raising native animals such as rats and mutant
dogs.**[^lex-underhive]

Fungi, algae, rats and mutant dogs — a functioning food economy assembled with no assistance
from the empire directly overhead, by people the Lex Imperialis of [[IV.T1.Ch2]] would
classify as illegal the moment their mutations were noticed.

And the larger settlements have institutions. They have **their own order with trading places,
small workshops for equipment repair, and services for travellers**, where **goods and
services can be bought or bartered**.[^lex-underhive]

That is the finding of this Section. Below the reach of the Governor, the Clan Houses and the
guilds, people did not descend into anarchy; they built markets, repair shops and inns. The
Imperium's absence produced a functioning if brutal society. Nothing in the sources suggests
the Imperium has noticed.

## Scum {#scum}

One class of person moves between the world above and the world below, and the Imperium has a
word for them.

**Hive Scum**, or **Scummers**, are **masterless or itinerant Imperial hivers who will fight
for anyone who offers them coin**.[^lex-hive-scum] The term is deliberately broad: **Scum is a
catch-all term for any and all manner of criminals and unsavoury characters. Whether they're
petty thieves, Imperial Guard deserters, escaped penal convicts, scam artists, fallen nobles,
gangers, desperados, murderers, smugglers, hab-rats and more.**[^lex-hive-scum]

Look at what that list actually contains. Alongside the murderers and smugglers sit **Imperial
Guard deserters** and **fallen nobles** — people who were inside the system and left it or were
dropped by it. The Imperium has one word for a soldier who ran, an aristocrat who lost
everything, and a thief, and it is the same word.

**Some are mercenaries who travel from zone to zone, earning whatever easy money is around
before moving on. Many though are drunkards and down-and-outs who, despite their appearances,
are more than capable of holding their own in a fight.** And they cannot be organised:
**regardless of their background, hive scum are too wild and independent to submit to the
leadership of anyone for very long and will only work when they feel like it — as their
services are always in demand.**[^lex-hive-scum]

They can afford to be unreliable because there is always work. In the underhive **most hive
scum end up working for the Merchant Guilds, though a few are always willing to aid one of the
Hive World's numerous gangs**, and they are **especially valuable to newly-created
gangs**.[^lex-hive-scum]

Put the four Sections together and the Imperium's most populous environment resolves into a
single machine. A tower holding a billion people, most of whom have no concept of sky, sorted
by altitude into those with free power and real food and those whose air and bodies are
recycled. A heat sink running the full height of it, delivering electricity at the top and
warmth at the bottom. Factories that keep operating after they have been buried in their own
waste. Beneath all of it, the ruins of the first colony, where people who left the Imperium's
jurisdiction built markets and workshops out of debris. And moving between the two, a class of
masterless people the Imperium files under one word, whose fighting experience — acquired in
gang wars the Arbites permit — makes them the raw material for the largest army in human
history. None of it was designed. All of it is load-bearing.
