# Abhumans & Sanctioned Strains

## Seventy-Three Strains, Forty-Six Extinct {#strains}

The Imperium's doctrine holds that the human form is sacred and any deviation from it is
spiritual corruption. It also fields regiments of eight-foot giants and keeps a department to
count them.

**Abhumans are human subspecies — descendants of humans who have physically evolved after
long periods of isolation on worlds with various extreme environmental
conditions.**[^lex-abhuman] Their origin is the history of [[III.T2.Ch9]] rendered as
biology: abhuman strains **almost invariably arose in isolation on various human worlds
settled during the Dark Age of Technology and evolved into the current physiology during the
Age of Strife**.[^lex-abhuman]

They are what five thousand years of enforced separation did to a species. When the Warp
storms closed and travel stopped, isolated colonies adapted to the worlds they were stranded
on. The Imperium later reconquered those worlds and found the results.

The bookkeeping is exact in a way that says a great deal about who is doing it. **The Imperium
recognises 73 stable abhuman strains, of which 46 types are now listed as extinct, with a
further 12 suggesting they too have died out or been assimilated into the greater population.
The status of the remaining 15 strains is an issue of constant debate within the
Administratum's Tithes Chamber Notaries, sub. Planetary Census (Abhumans), the administrative
body that deals with abhuman affairs.**[^lex-abhuman]

Seventy-three recognised; forty-six confirmed gone; twelve probably gone; fifteen still
arguable. A named sub-department of the Administratum of [[IV.T1.Ch2]] exists to argue about
those fifteen — filed, note, under *Tithes Chamber Notaries*, which places the classification
of human subspecies inside the office that works out what worlds owe. Whether a population is
abhuman is, administratively, a taxation question.

And the crucial finding is stated flatly: these strains **are recognised as no more prone to
mutation than the standard human**.[^lex-abhuman]

That single clause makes everything in the next three Sections a policy rather than a
precaution. The Imperium's own record establishes that an Ogryn is no more likely to produce
a deviant child than a hive worker. The persecution that follows is not built on the evidence
the Imperium itself holds.

Because the persecution is real and its source is named. **Although abhumans have fought and
died for the Imperium alongside baseline humanity since its inception, they face significant
persecution throughout Imperial society to this day. A significant factor to this is the
Ministorum, whom preaches that the form of man is sacred and any perceived mutation is a
blasphemy and a reflection of internal spiritual corruption. Acceptance of abhumans is often
hard to reconcile with the teachings of the Adeptus
Ministorum.**[^lex-abhuman]

The Ecclesiarchy of [[IV.T3.Ch8]] is identified by name as the reason. Not the Inquisition,
not the Arbites, not popular prejudice — the state religion, whose doctrine on the sanctity of
the human form cannot be reconciled with the existence of people the Imperium simultaneously
conscripts. Two of the nine permanent seats on the Senatorum are in permanent tension over
this: the Ecclesiarch preaches the sanctity of the form, and the Master of the Administratum
counts the strains for the tithe.

Their military use is old and organised. Abhumans serve **in the Militarum Auxilla as their
own regiments, platoons, or squads**,[^lex-abhuman] which is to say the Astra Militarum of
[[IV.T5.Ch16]] fields them in segregated units rather than integrating them. And the worlds
they came from were not abandoned: **during the Age of the Imperium, some of these worlds
brought under Imperial rule would again have sizable standard human populations, alongside
that of their respective abhuman populations. Other worlds remained solely inhabited by their
respective abhuman populations to this day.**[^lex-abhuman]

## Ogryns and Ratlings {#ogryn-ratling}

Two strains supply most of the Imperium's abhuman soldiery, and each is defined in the
sources by an insult before it is defined by a capability.

**Ogryns** — *Homo sapiens gigantus* — are **the largest and most physically powerful type of
abhuman** and **the most commonly seen type of abhuman in the
Imperium**.[^lex-ogryn] They **evolved on worlds with harsh and barren environments and high
gravity**, and the sources add the detail that explains the rest: **most of these worlds,
having no other use to humanity, were originally used as prison
planets**.[^lex-ogryn]

The Imperium's most numerous abhuman strain is descended from convicts, dumped on high-gravity
worlds nobody wanted, who adapted. They are **large and bulky, standing between 2½ and 3
metres tall**, and their **appearance varies according to their world of origin, but all are
tough and powerful**.[^lex-ogryn]

The register the sources use for them is worth reproducing exactly, because it is the
Imperium's own voice. Ogryns are **stupid and repulsively unhygienic** and **have earned a
variety of names (derogatory or otherwise)** — **Fats**, **Flabs**, **Slobs**, **Planks**,
**Slabs**, **Big Men**, **Brutes**, **Behemoths**, **Lumps** and
**Stenches**.[^lex-ogryn] Ten recorded slurs for a population the same article confirms
produces **mutant individuals to the same extent as humans**.[^lex-ogryn]

What they are valued for is stated without qualification: they **make ideal warriors and are
often recruited into Imperial Guard regiments to be used as close assault shock
troops**,[^lex-ogryn] and they are **loyal, making up for their lack of
intelligence**.[^lex-ogryn] Some are promoted — **Bone 'eads are sometimes assigned to high
ranking Astra Militarum personnel**.[^lex-ogryn]

**Ratlings** — *Homo Sapiens Minimus* — are the inverse in every dimension. Known as
**Runtlings**, **Stunties** and **Maggots**, but **among themselves as Halflings**, they are
**small and hedonistically jovial abhumans who commonly work aboard spacecraft as cabin crew
and runarounds, benefiting from their small stature**.[^lex-ratling]

Note that the sources record what they call themselves separately from what the Imperium calls
them. It is a small courtesy and the Ogryn entry does not extend it.

Their military employment is grudging and specific. They **rarely find employment in the
Imperial Guard due to their physical weakness and less than ideal temperament**, but **their
expertise in marksmanship, low weight, and short height (which lends them a stealthy quality)
make ratlings well suited to the role of sniper** — and within the Guard they are **often
employed as snipers specialists in the Militarum Auxilla**.[^lex-ratling]

Their origin story is recorded with an attribution that changes it completely. They **hail
from the very few Agri Worlds in the Imperium that lack horrific dangers**; and then —
**according to the propagandists that spread anti-abhuman rhetoric** — **the Ratling form
adapted to these conditions**, their ancestors **becoming stunted by thousands of years of
inbreeding on these worlds**.[^lex-ratling]

*According to the propagandists.* [[III.T2.Ch9]] carries the same inbreeding claim about
Ratlings from the Age of Strife article, stated as fact. Here the identical claim is
attributed to people running an anti-abhuman campaign. The codex records the disagreement in
framing without resolving it: one Imperial source presents it as natural history, another
identifies it as propaganda.

## Beastmen, Felinids and the Kin {#beastmen-kin}

Three further strains show the Imperium's classification system under strain, and the last of
them breaks it.

**Beastmen** — *Homo sapiens variatus* — are **bestial abhumans combining the forms of animal
and human**, and are **considered to be fairly genetically stable compared to those afflicted
by more random, unpredictable mutations**.[^lex-beastman] They **first appeared during the Age
of Strife** and are **most common among the mutant and abhuman strains that arose during those
times**, with **stable beastlike mutations**, and are **prized for their perceived prowess at
war**.[^lex-beastman]

Physically they are described precisely: **cloven hooves, digitigrade legs, goat-like horns,
and bestial faces that resemble animals**, with **arms and hands usually similar to a standard
human's, allowing them to use human weapons and tools**, and bodies giving **brutish strength
and speed**, agile enough to **traverse terrain impassable to ordinary
humans**.[^lex-beastman]

And the sources deliver a verdict that is rare in Imperial material. **Many consider such
creatures subhuman, but often these warriors have proven themselves the equal of any
unafflicted human in both intelligence and loyalty during the horrors of the Horus Heresy
onward.**[^lex-beastman]

The word *unafflicted* is still doing prejudicial work in a sentence that is otherwise a
correction. Even when the record defends abhumans, it defends them in the Ecclesiarchy's
vocabulary.

**Felinids** — *Homo sapiens hirsutus* — are **a recognised strain of abhuman within the
Imperium**, **primarily known as being native to the world of Carlos McConnell** and
**considered a rarity outside their world**. The detail that fixes them is commercial:
**there are known con men who sell supposed felinid remains which turn out to be human
carcasses stitched with animal skins**.[^lex-felinid]

A market exists for abhuman corpses, and it is large enough to sustain forgery. Somebody is
buying.

Their combat reputation is recorded through an unusual witness. The Rogue Trader and
xenologist **Janus Draik** recorded a discussion with the Kroot mercenary **Dahyak Grekh**, in
which the Kroot described felinids as **cool in the face of the most brutal opposition and as
some of the most efficient killers in an Astra Militarum regiment** — but **ultimately found
it difficult to distinguish one human from another, and as such, felinids, to Kroot eyes,
looked no different from the rest of humanity**.[^lex-felinid]

An alien mercenary cannot tell the difference. The Imperium maintains a census sub-department
to adjudicate the same question, and its answer determines whether a person is persecuted.
[[IV.T1.Ch2]] establishes that a Rogue Trader's warrant is what makes a conversation with a
Kroot legal; this is what such a conversation produced.

The **Kin** are the case that will not fit. **Kin** — spelled **Kyn** in their own language,
and **known by the Imperial pejorative term of Squats** or *Homo Sapien Rotundus* — are **an
ancient human descendant species predominantly found in the galactic
core**.[^lex-kin] They **exist in a variety of civilisations, most notably the Leagues of
Votann**, but also **the Ironhead Prospectors of Necromunda**.[^lex-kin]

[[III.T2.Ch7]] records the Leagues of Votann as the branch of humanity that kept its Standard
Template Constructs and went on improving them, while the Imperium copied templates it could
not read. The Kin are that branch, and the Imperium cannot decide what they are.

!!! disputed "Recorded conflict — kin-abhuman-or-xenos"

    The Kin's **Imperial classification varies from abhuman to
    xenos**.[^lex-kin] The article adds its own note: **it is possible that Kin are both the
    most ancient and derived form of abhuman strain in existence. Being so derived from
    baseline humanity, and by extension the Imperium, that in practicality their culture,
    religion, and technology are classified as xenos by many in the Imperium, most notably
    the Inquisition.**[^lex-kin]

    Both classifications are attested and the codex takes neither. What hangs on the answer
    is not academic: an abhuman strain may be conscripted into the Militarum Auxilla, while
    a xenos species falls under the Ordo Xenos of [[IV.T7.Ch24]] and the extermination
    doctrine that follows from it.

## Where the Line Is Drawn {#mutant-line}

Everything above depends on a boundary the Imperium draws between the abhuman, who may be
tolerated and armed, and the mutant, who may not. This Section is about where that line sits
and what it is actually made of.

**Mutants are creatures bearing some form of severe genetic deviance from the normal members
of their species. Some are born mutants, while others have suffered more spontaneous mutation
through the influence of the Warp, whether by worshipping it or being exposed to its
energies. Persecuted and hunted by Imperial society, Mutants often make up the forces of
Chaos.**[^lex-mutant]

That definition contains two entirely different phenomena welded together. One is genetic
deviance with a physical cause. The other is Warp corruption. The Imperium treats them as the
same category, which is why the Ecclesiarchy can preach that a physical deformity is **a
reflection of internal spiritual corruption**[^lex-abhuman] and be doctrinally consistent.

The physical cause is recorded, and it is human. **Humanity has suffered greatly since the
20th Century from the effects of mutation due to exposure to chemical and nuclear
contamination. Nuclear war was widespread during both the Dark Age of Technology and the
following Age of Strife, which has left many worlds badly exposed to the radioactive
aftermath and has led to a significantly increased rate of
mutation.**[^lex-mutant]

The rates are given: **genetically healthy parents may have in some cases a 5% chance of
rearing mutant offspring, while mutant parents may give birth to mutant children around 90%
of the time.**[^lex-mutant]

One birth in twenty, to healthy parents, on contaminated worlds. That is the scale of the
population the Lex Imperialis of [[IV.T1.Ch2]] declares illegal in its entirety — which is
why the same statute concedes that **miscellaneous mutants amongst scavvy populations and
beyond remain rife throughout the vast and squalid Imperium**. The law cannot be enforced
because the law describes five per cent of births on a large fraction of Imperial worlds.

And the boundary itself turns out to be paperwork. The sources record populations holding a
**Sanctioned-Mutation Work Pass**, which makes the holder a **sanctioned strain of mutant and
hence not legally abhuman**.[^lex-abhuman]

Read that carefully, because it inverts the whole doctrine. A mutant with the right document
is not reclassified as abhuman; they remain a mutant, sanctioned. The categories are not a
biological hierarchy with a permit system bolted on. They are two separate registers, and
which one a person appears in is an administrative outcome.

So the line between the tolerated and the hunted is drawn as follows. An abhuman is a strain
the Administratum's census sub-department has recognised — one of seventy-three, of which
fifteen are still being argued about. A sanctioned mutant is an individual holding a work
pass. Everyone else with a deviation is illegal, in a population where one birth in twenty
produces one. Nothing in that structure refers to the Warp, to Chaos, or to spiritual
corruption, and the Ecclesiarchy's doctrine that a deviant body indicates a deviant soul is
applied across all three categories regardless.

The Imperium's own record supplies the refutation, in the article that opens this Chapter:
abhuman strains **are recognised as no more prone to mutation than the standard
human**.[^lex-abhuman] The Administratum knows. The Ministorum preaches otherwise. Both hold
permanent seats on the Senatorum of [[IV.T1.Ch1]], and neither has ever had to reconcile with
the other, because in an empire where citizenship confers no rights, nobody affected by the
disagreement has standing to raise it.
