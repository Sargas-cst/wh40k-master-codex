# Famous Imperial Guard Regiments

## Cadians and Catachans {#cadia-catachan}

[[IV.T5.Ch16]] records that Guard regiments are deliberately not standardised, because every
world that raises them wages war in its own way. This Chapter is seven of those worlds, and
what each one's answer to the same tithe looks like.

The **Cadian Shock Troopers** are **Regiments of the Astra Militarum originally from the
fortress world of Cadia before its destruction**. **Located at the edge of the Eye of Terror,
Cadia formed the first line of defence against Chaos incursions, such as the Black
Crusades.**[^lex-cadian-shock-troopers]

*Before its destruction.* [[II.T3.Ch8]] describes the world in detail — 71.75% of the population
under arms, a birth rate set equal to the recruitment rate, children taught to strip a lasgun
before they could read — and [[III.T5.Ch22]] records its end. The regiments continue. A world
whose entire society existed to produce soldiers now produces them from nowhere at all, and
what remains of Cadia is the regiments themselves.

Their standing in the Guard is the reason they matter beyond their own story. **Their
reputation is so great that many other worlds base their own Imperial Guard regiments'
equipment, force organisation, and methods of war upon those used by the
Cadians.**[^lex-cadian-shock-troopers]

In an army with no standard, one world became the standard by example. [[IV.T5.Ch16]] records
Cadian Shock Troopers **often forming infantry "cores"** around which other regiments'
armour and artillery are arranged — so the Imperium's default answer to "what does a regiment
look like" is a description of a planet that no longer exists.

The **Catachan Jungle Fighters**, **sometimes referred to as "baby ogryns" (although not to
their faces) due to their thick slabs of muscle**, are **the fearsome Imperial Guard Regiments
from Catachan, the most notorious Death World in the
Imperium**.[^lex-catachan-jungle-fighters]

[[II.T3.Ch9]] carries Catachan itself. The point for this Chapter is the contrast with Cadia:
both produce superb infantry, and neither did it on purpose in the same way. Cadia militarised
a population deliberately, by policy, with a measured birth rate. Catachan simply kills
everyone who cannot fight, and the Imperium collects the survivors. Two of the Guard's most
celebrated formations are the outputs of a training programme and of an ecosystem
respectively, and the Imperium values them equally.

## Krieg, and the Regiment With No Home {#krieg-tanith}

Two regiments define themselves by a loss, and they are opposite in every other respect.

The **Death Korps of Krieg** are **Imperial Guard Regiments raised from the world of Krieg**.
**Notoriously grim and fatalistic warriors, Korpsmen seek to atone for their planet's past
rebellion by dying glorious deaths in service to the
Imperium.**[^lex-death-korps-of-krieg]

The rebellion is recorded, and its ending is the most extreme act of self-punishment in this
Volume. **For days Krieg was engulfed in a sea of nuclear fire, resulting in a total collapse
of its ecosystem and subsequent nuclear winter, but the civil war dragged
on.**[^lex-death-korps-of-krieg]

A world that atomised itself to end its own heresy — and the war continued anyway, through the
nuclear winter. [[IV.T3.Ch10]] records the Redemptionists concluding that mankind must be
purged of sin by fire; Krieg is a world that reached the same conclusion about itself and had
the means.

What that produced is a doctrine. **Death Korps regiments routinely request to fight in the
galaxy's deadliest warzones, grinding their enemy down through long battles of attrition and
siege warfare.**[^lex-death-korps-of-krieg]

They ask for the worst assignments. [[IV.T2.Ch6]] notes that Krieg regiments require a
Commissar acting as **diplomat and mediator between Planetary Governors, civilians, the
Adeptus Mechanicus, or other authorities that may not agree with the actions of their regiment
in a military campaign** — which is the Imperium conceding that the Death Korps' way of
fighting alarms its own allies enough to need a permanent envoy.

The **Tanith First and Only**, **simply the Tanith First**, **was an Imperial Guard Regiment
raised on the planet Tanith during the Sabbat Worlds Crusade**, **colloquially known as
"Gaunt's Ghosts", after their commanding officer**.[^lex-tanith-first-and-only]

The name is the whole story. *First and Only* — Tanith was destroyed while the regiment was
being raised, so there was never a second. [[IV.T5.Ch16]] records that each world's regiments
belong to a Militarum Regimentum managing that world's contribution; Tanith's contains one
regiment and will never contain another. Every other formation in this Chapter can be
replaced from its homeworld. This one is a closed set, walking.

They fight in the Sabbat Worlds Crusade — the sector named for the saint of [[IV.T3.Ch9]], who
was linked to the order that became the Sororitas, and which fell to Chaos anyway. The Tanith
lost their world in a campaign to reclaim worlds named after a girl who died reclaiming them.

## Mordians, Tallarns, Valhallans {#mordian-tallarn-valhalla}

Three more worlds, three more answers to the same levy.

The **Mordian Iron Guard** are **Regiments of the Imperial Guard hailing from the planet
Mordian**, **famed across the Imperium for their discipline, infantry quality, and brightly
coloured uniforms**.[^lex-mordian-iron-guard]

The reputation invites the obvious dismissal, and the sources record a soldier's answer to it:
**"They may spend every off-duty minute polishing their shiny boots and marching up and down
the parade ground in perfect formation, but don't let that fool
you."**[^lex-mordian-iron-guard]

Parade discipline as a combat asset. In an army where the Commissars of [[IV.T2.Ch6]] hold the right
and duty to execute anyone showing cowardice, a regiment that will not break under fire is a
regiment that needs its Commissar least — and Mordian's export is precisely that.

The **Tallarn Desert Raiders** are **the Imperial Guard Regiments raised from the bleak desert
world of Tallarn**.[^lex-tallarn-desert-raiders] The world's economy is stated in one line: **as
a barren desert planet with so little to offer the Imperium besides manpower, Tallarn has made
exporting soldiers its primary contribution to
mankind.**[^lex-tallarn-desert-raiders]

That is the tithe of [[IV.T2.Ch5]] reduced to its simplest form. A world that cannot pay in
minerals or food pays in people, and the arrangement is described as a *contribution to
mankind*.

Why it is a desert at all is not natural history. **Following the desecration of their homeworld
during the Horus Heresy by the Iron Warriors that saw their once fertile Agri-World reduced to
waste, the Tallarn have been forced to make use of Mechanised Armour and APCs in order to
safely move their infantry across the barren
wastes.**[^lex-tallarn-desert-raiders]

Tallarn was an agri-world. A traitor Legion of [[III.T3.Ch14]] destroyed it during the Heresy,
and ten thousand years later the survivors' descendants pay their tithe in soldiers because
there is nothing else left to grow. The regiment's doctrine — mechanised infantry, because the
ground cannot be crossed on foot — is a tactical consequence of an atrocity committed in M31.

The **Valhallan Ice Warriors** are **the Imperial Guard Regiments from the Ice world of
Valhalla**, specialising in **Winter, Mountain, Attrition & Anti-Ork
Warfare**.[^lex-valhallan-ice-warriors] They are **famous for their tenaciousness in holding
their ground against even the most hopeless odds, and their ability to suffer the most
appalling casualties without breaking**.[^lex-valhallan-ice-warriors]

*Without breaking.* That is a formal military virtue in this army, and it is worth being precise
about what it means. [[IV.T5.Ch16]] establishes that the Imperium treats its manpower as a
functional infinity, with casualty lists running to millions a day. A regiment celebrated for
absorbing appalling losses is celebrated for making that doctrine work.

Take the seven together and every one is defined by damage. Cadia's world is gone and its
regiments are the Guard's template. Catachan kills its own population as a matter of ecology.
Krieg burned itself and fights to atone. Tanith has no homeworld and never will. Tallarn was an
agri-world until a traitor Legion made it a desert. Valhalla is admired for how many of its
people can die without the line giving way. Only Mordian is famous for something it chose.

That is not a coincidence in the sources; it is the Imperium's recruitment doctrine visible in
its own honour roll. [[IV.T2.Ch4]] records that hive gang warfare is the Guard's unpaid basic
training and that the Arbites cull the surplus. The Imperium does not produce good soldiers by
training them. It finds populations that catastrophe has already trained, and levies a tenth.
