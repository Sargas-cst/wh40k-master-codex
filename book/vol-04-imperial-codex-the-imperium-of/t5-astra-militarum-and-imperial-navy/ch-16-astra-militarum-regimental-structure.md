# Astra Militarum Regimental Structure

## A Force Nobody Has Counted {#scale}

The Imperium's largest institution after the Administratum is its army, and like the
Administratum it cannot measure itself.

The **Astra Militarum**, **also known as the Imperial Guard**, is **the primary fighting force
of the Imperium of Man, so numerous in size that even the Departmento Munitorum cannot place a
figure on the number of Guardsmen under arms at any one time; the lists of new recruits and
toll of casualties can run into the millions in a single day.**[^lex-astra-militarum]

Millions of names a day, in both columns. The office responsible for counting them is the same
Administratum subdivision of [[IV.T1.Ch2]] that has mislaid whole departments of itself, and
here the failure is not administrative sloppiness but arithmetic: the intake and the losses
both exceed what anyone can tally.

The supply is described as effectively unlimited. **Every single year, trillions of new
recruits are tithed into the Guard, giving their commanders a functional infinite source of
manpower**, and **it is said that, if the entirety of the Imperial Guard would form up in a
column, it would reach from Terra to Armageddon**.[^lex-astra-militarum]

**It would be unfeasible trying to put any exact number on the strength of the Guard; however,
it is believed that there must be many billions of Guardsmen, divided into
millions**[^lex-astra-militarum] of regiments.

*Functional infinite source of manpower.* That phrase is the Imperium's actual military
doctrine, and it explains almost everything else about how it fights. [[IV.T2.Ch5]] records
where the trillions come from — a tenth of every world's defence force, levied as often as the
collectors arrive, with one recorded case of nine levies in a single year. [[IV.T2.Ch4]]
records why hive worlds supply most of them: gang warfare in the lower levels produces
recruits who already know how to handle a gun, at no cost to the Imperium.

Set this against [[IV.T4.Ch13]] and the contrast is the whole of Imperial military policy.
The Astartes are capped at a thousand per Chapter, aiming at a thousand Chapters — roughly a
million soldiers, deliberately limited so that no single force could threaten Terra. The Guard
is uncounted, uncountable, and numbers in the billions. The Imperium restricts the army it
fears and lets the other one grow without limit.

## The Regiment, and Why It Is Never Complete {#regiment}

The Guard's basic unit is the regiment, and the striking thing about it is that the Imperium
refuses to standardise it.

**A regiment is the primary organisational unit of the Imperial Guard, and of the Imperial
Army ground forces before that. The size and composition of Imperial Guard regiments is not
standardised across the Imperium; the number of individual Guardsmen alone can vary enormously
between regiments, with some only a few hundred strong at founding-strength, whilst others
possess tens of thousands of fighting troops.**[^lex-astra-militarum-regiment]

Anywhere from a few hundred to tens of thousands, both called a regiment. Each belongs to a
**Militarum Regimentum**, **the organisation that manages the collection of Regiments drawn
from a single world**.[^lex-astra-militarum-regiment]

The recruitment rule is the tithe of [[IV.T2.Ch5]] stated from the army's side. **Every
Imperial world has to provide one-tenth of its Planetary Defence Force for recruitment into
the Imperial Guard. This happens in regular intervals and when required by circumstances.
These recruits have to be of the highest quality and are usually recruited from the best PDF
units.**[^lex-astra-militarum-regiment]

The Imperium takes a tenth of each world's defenders, and takes the best tenth.

Why regiments differ so much is not an accident of administration. **The root of this
non-standardisation is the fact that every world which produces Imperial Guard regiments has
its own martial traditions, practices and experiences, all combining to dictate the way that
world's inhabitants choose to wage war.**[^lex-astra-militarum-regiment]

Then the provision that this Chapter exists to state, because nothing else in Imperial military
organisation is as revealing.

**Except in rare circumstances or within certain isolated sectors, true combined arms regiments
are not normally raised within the Imperial Guard. Infantry Regiments are unlikely to contain
much or any heavy artillery, while tank regiments contain little to no infantry. This was
intentionally designed, as it prevents Traitor Regiments from having full arsenals should they
turn against the Emperor.**[^lex-astra-militarum-regiment]

Read that carefully. The Imperium's regiments are deliberately built incomplete — infantry
without guns, tanks without infantry — so that a regiment which rebels cannot fight a proper
war on its own. Every tactical inefficiency in the Astra Militarum follows from a decision
taken about loyalty rather than about warfare.

It is the same instinct that produced everything in [[IV.T4.Ch13]]: the thousand-Marine cap,
the Astartes exclusion from the Senatorum. The Imperium's answer to the Heresy was to make
every armed force structurally incapable of acting alone. In the Astartes it is a headcount;
in the Guard it is an equipment list.

The consequence is admitted immediately. **Thus, success requires that many Regiments work
together, and battlefield realities often necessitate interweaving numerous tactical
operations and structures. In these scenarios, training of officers and personnel capable of
operating outside of their specific Regiment's role is required, and there are several Imperial
worlds that specialize in producing such soldiers. These types of personnel are keenly sought
by campaign commanders.**[^lex-astra-militarum-regiment]

An empire that hobbled its own regiments then had to create a specialist trade in officers who
can compensate for it, and those officers are scarce enough to be competed over.

In practice the shortfall is patched by combination. **"Combined Arms" forces can be organised
by comprising several separate Regiments together during campaign should circumstances require
it. Regiments perform together with each being a certain core role, with Cadian Shock Troopers
for instance often forming infantry "cores" alongside support squadrons of tanks and
artillery.** Such forces **frequently also see the deployment of Abhuman auxiliaries such as
Ogryns and Ratlings.**[^lex-astra-militarum-regiment]

The abhumans of [[IV.T2.Ch7]] appear here as auxiliaries attached to human formations — which
is the segregation that Chapter records, seen from the battlefield.

Below the regiment the structure is loose and honest about being so. Regiments **can also be
sub-divided, most often into a number of Companies. These number between five and twenty-five,
depending on the number of troopers in the Company itself and the quality of their
equipment.** **Battalions are another common subdivision**, often **called Brigades or other
terms, depending on point of regimental origin**, and they are **strictly administrative
groupings, not used in combat engagements**.[^lex-astra-militarum-regiment]

Fighting is done by ad-hoc formations. **To accomplish specific combat tasks, Regimental Battle
Groups, Task Groups or Assault Groups may be formed, working alongside groups from other
Regiments.** They are **commanded by a senior officer, often a colonel or captain**, and **vary
in size from around a hundred troops and five or six vehicles to a force nearly rivalling a
regiment in size.**[^lex-astra-militarum-regiment]

## The Departmento Munitorum {#munitorum}

Everything above is supplied, moved and paid for by one office, and the Guard's own opinion of
it is on the record.

The **Departmento Munitorum**, or **Munitorium**, is **a department of the Administratum
devoted to the general administration, supply, and command of the Imperial Guard, but despite
its necessity it is often viewed by the Guard as
ineffective.**[^lex-departmento-munitorum]

Its remit is total. The Munitorum **has ultimate responsibility for the raising of new
regiments, training of troops, provision of equipment and supplies, and transportation of
troops and equipment to and from theatres of war**. **It is primarily a logistical organisation
like the Administratum, but while the Administratum deals with civilian logistics, the
Munitorum deals with the logistics of war.** It uses **military as opposed to civilian ranks**,
and **dates back to the Great Crusade**.[^lex-departmento-munitorum]

So the largest army in human history is administered by the tax office wearing military rank
badges. [[IV.T1.Ch2]] establishes what that means in practice: an institution that keeps its
records on vellum, has lost departments of itself, and knows its own methods are inefficient
without changing them. The soldiers' verdict — *ineffective* — is recorded in the Imperium's own
sources, and the Imperium has done nothing about it.

The Munitorum's other entanglement is political. It became **enmeshed in the mire of politics
surrounding the great fleets and their military
leaders**.[^lex-departmento-munitorum] The Navy of [[IV.T5.Ch18]] moves everything the Guard
owns, and the office that arranges the transport sits between the two.

Two further pieces of this Volume belong to the same department. [[IV.T2.Ch6]] records that the
Commissariat — the Commissars with the right and duty to shoot any soldier or officer showing
cowardice — is a Munitorum department, and that the Schola Progenium is a Munitorum offshoot
staffed by the Ecclesiarchy. [[IV.T2.Ch5]] records that corpse-starch is **part of the rations
available to unluckier Astra Militarum regiments as supplied by the Departmento
Munitorum**.

Assembled, the picture is complete and unlovely. One Administratum office levies a tenth of
every world's defenders, forms them into regiments deliberately built without full arsenals so
they cannot rebel effectively, feeds some of them processed human remains, attaches to each an
officer from a different department with standing authority to execute them, and cannot say how
many of them there are. The Guard's own assessment of that office is that it is ineffective,
and the Imperium's assessment of the Guard is that it is a functionally infinite source of
manpower.
