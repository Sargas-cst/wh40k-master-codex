# Foundings & Renowned Successors

## What a Founding Is, and Why the Records Are Gone {#foundings}

**A Founding is an occasion when several new Space Marine Chapters are
created.**[^lex-founding]

The mechanism has no schedule. **A Founding does not occur at any set interval, but is ordered
when it is deemed necessary. As Chapters are occasionally completely destroyed, Foundings are
often meant to ensure that a thousand Chapters exist at a
time.**[^lex-founding]

A thousand Chapters of a thousand Marines. [[IV.T4.Ch13]] records the per-Chapter cap that
Guilliman wrote to keep any one force from threatening the Imperium; this is the aggregate the
Imperium tries to maintain, and it means roughly a million Space Marines exist to garrison a
million worlds. One Chapter per world would leave nothing spare.

Then the fact that shapes everything after it. **This also means that many Chapters are created
during unstable times of strife and rebellions that have caused many Chapters to lose the
knowledge of their Founding or even the millennia of that
Founding.**[^lex-founding]

Chapters that do not know when they were made. And the reason is named: **this is especially
common to Chapters created before or during the Age of Apostasy, as during the Reign of Blood
many Imperial records were shamelessly destroyed
forever.**[^lex-founding]

[[III.T4.Ch19]] carries Goge Vandire's rule and [[IV.T3.Ch11]] carries how it ended. This is
its longest-lasting damage: the Imperium's most powerful military units cannot establish their
own origins, because a man who held the Administratum and the Ecclesiarchy at once destroyed the
paperwork. The Administratum of [[IV.T1.Ch2]] keeps its records on vellum in departments it has
sometimes forgotten it has; when those burn, they are gone.

Some Chapters exist outside the system altogether. **Exceptions to Foundings include the
creation of the Grey Knights, who were created before the Second Founding by order of the
Emperor Himself during the Horus Heresy**; **the Sons of Medusa, who were ratified by
unprecedented edict in 011.M37**; and **the Red Seraphs as well as Angels Numinous, which
represent splinter factions of the Blood Eagles**.[^lex-founding]

The Grey Knights of [[IV.T7.Ch25]] predate the entire Chapter system, ordered personally by the
Emperor while the Heresy was still being fought.

## The Second Founding, the Dark Founding, the Cursed {#named-foundings}

Three Foundings have names, and the names are the Imperium's own assessment.

**The Second Founding of Space Marine Chapters occurred seven years after the Horus Heresy. The
remaining loyal Legions after the Horus Heresy (sometimes referred to as the "Progenitor") were
divided into smaller Chapters that became known as the Second Founding
Chapters.**[^lex-founding]

Seven years. [[III.T4.Ch18]] records the breaking of the Legions; this is how quickly it
happened after the Emperor was interred. What followed was genetic divergence by design: **each
Second Founding Chapter initially shared their parent Legion's gene-seed, but subsequently each
Chapter's gene-seed was isolated, forming new genetic lines and forging their own histories and
traditions.**[^lex-founding]

Isolating the lines is what turns nine bloodlines into hundreds. It also guarantees the drift
[[IV.T4.Ch12]] records — each line accumulating its own flaws, with no way back to a common
stock except the Ultramarines' unusually stable material.

The **13th Founding**, in **late M35 / early M36**, is called **the "Dark Founding", the only
one for which the Adeptus Terra does not have a gene-seed
sample.**[^lex-founding]

Read that twice. The Imperium created a set of Space Marine Chapters and does not hold the
genetic material they were made from. Every other Founding is on file at Terra. This one is
not, and nothing in the sources explains why.

The **21st Founding**, in **991.M35**, is **the "Cursed Founding", which created Chapters which
fell victim to mutation, heresy, rebellion, or simple bad
luck**[^lex-founding] — so named **because of the appalling bad luck that seems to dog the
Chapters created in it**.[^lex-founding]

*Mutation, heresy, rebellion, or simple bad luck.* The first is a genetic outcome, the second
and third are the Imperium's words for a Chapter turning, and the fourth is an admission that
it cannot tell the difference. Given [[IV.T4.Ch12]] — gene-seed degrades, Chapters differ in
stability, and the Ultramarine line is preferred because it is the most stable — a Founding
that produced mutation at scale is most plausibly a Founding that used bad material. The
Imperium calls it a curse.

The list of Foundings preserved in the sources reads like an index of Imperial crises. An
**8th** Founding in mid-M34 whose creation of the Mantis Warriors is **unconfirmed**; a **10th**
in mid-M35 whose founding of the Astral Claws is likewise unconfirmed; an unnumbered Founding
**during the time of the Nova Terra Interregnum**; one **soon after the culmination of the
Reign of Blood**; one **right before the Great Cull in 020.M37**; and the **23rd**, the
**"Sentinel" Founding**, in late M37 / early M38.[^lex-founding]

Two of those are marked *unconfirmed* — the Imperium is unsure whether it created particular
Chapters. [[III.T4.Ch19]] holds the Interregnum and the Reign of Blood, and the Foundings
cluster around them, because Chapters are made when Chapters have just been lost.

## Templars and Fists {#templars-fists}

Two successors of the same Legion show how differently a bloodline can develop once its
gene-seed is isolated.

The **Black Templars** **are a Second Founding chapter derived from the Imperial Fists. Since
their founding, the Black Templars have campaigned on a never-ending
crusade.**[^lex-black-templars] Their first Champion and founder was **Sigismund**, and the
Chapter's origin lies in **Horus's attack on the Imperial
Palace**.[^lex-black-templars]

They are one of the two Chapters [[IV.T4.Ch13]] names as paying **mere lip service to the
Codex** — and their permanent crusade is the reason. A Chapter that never returns to a
homeworld cannot maintain ten companies in garrison, so the structure Guilliman wrote does not
fit them. [[IV.T3.Ch8]] also lists them among the Chapters that worship the Emperor as a god
outright, which makes the Black Templars a Chapter that rejects the Codex and accepts the
Creed — the reverse of the Imperium's usual arrangement with the Astartes.

The **Crimson Fists** are the other side of the same descent: a **Second Founding** Chapter
whose **Founding Chapter** was the **Imperial Fists**, with their homeworld at **Rynn's World**,
their fortress-monastery **Arx Tyrannus**, and their Chapter Master **Pedro
Kantor**.[^lex-crimson-fists]

One Legion, two successors: one that never stops moving and one with a named fortress on a
named world. [[II.T3.Ch7]] establishes that a fortress-monastery is the most heavily defended
structure a Chapter possesses, which is precisely what the Templars decline to have.

## Tearers, Sharks and a Chapter With No Past {#tearers-sharks-ravens}

Three more successors, each illustrating a different way a Chapter can be an outlier.

The **Flesh Tearers** **are a Second Founding chapter, created when the Blood Angels legion
split up after the Horus Heresy in accordance with the Codex Astartes.** And then the reason
they are known: **they suffer from a mutation in their gene-seed, making them far more prone to
suffer the Black Rage than other chapters with Blood Angels
gene-seed.**[^lex-flesh-tearers]

[[IV.T4.Ch14]] records the Black Rage as the Blood Angels' inherited wound — the death of
Sanguinius, still being experienced ten thousand years later — and records that it is getting
worse as the gene-seed degrades. The Flesh Tearers are what that degradation looks like when it
happens to one isolated line: the same injury, concentrated. Isolating gene-seed per Chapter was
meant to create distinct traditions. It also created a Chapter that inherits the worst of its
parent.

The **Carcharodons**, **also known as Space Sharks or Carcharodon Astra**, are **a loyalist
Space Marine Chapter with a reputation for ruthlessness and
brutality**.[^lex-carcharodons] The name is High Gothic; **Carcharodon Astra** translated into
Low Gothic is what the rest of the Imperium calls them.[^lex-carcharodons] [[I.T1.Ch2]] records
that High Gothic is the hierarchic language of ancient institutions and Low Gothic the common
tongue that varies world to world — and here a Chapter's formal name and its nickname are the
same words in the two registers.

The **Blood Ravens** are **a mysterious Chapter of Space Marines dedicated to the acquisition of
knowledge as well as service to the Imperium of Man**, possessing **a cadre of powerful
Librarians** and noted for **their ability to predict enemy
movements**.[^lex-blood-ravens]

And the fact that defines them: **their origins are unknown and a cause of much speculation to
themselves and to others.**[^lex-blood-ravens]

The first Section of this Chapter explains how that is possible without any conspiracy —
records burned during the Reign of Blood, Foundings whose millennium is lost. But the Blood
Ravens are the case where the gap is a subject of study by the Chapter itself. A thousand Space
Marines who do not know which Legion they descend from, in an institution where descent
determines your gene-seed, your flaws and your duties. The Dark Angels of [[IV.T4.Ch14]] keep a
secret about their own past deliberately; the Blood Ravens do not have one to keep.

They are also being brought into the present arrangement. **The Custodes brought with them teams
of Tech-Priests, Thralls, and Servitors as well as the new gene-seed needed to begin Primaris
Space Marine production within the Blood Ravens.**[^lex-blood-ravens]

The Custodes of [[IV.T8.Ch27]] delivering Cawl's gene-seed of [[III.T5.Ch24]] to a Chapter that
cannot identify its own. Whatever the Blood Ravens were, what they will be from now on is
whatever the Primaris material makes them.

Take the Foundings whole and the Adeptus Astartes are not the fixed institution they appear to
be. The Imperium aims at a thousand Chapters and does not know how many it has; it has created
Chapters it cannot confirm creating, from a Founding whose genetic material it does not hold,
and one Founding in twenty-three produced so much mutation and rebellion that the Imperium
named it cursed rather than investigate it. Underneath all of it is the mechanism of
[[IV.T4.Ch12]]: gene-seed that degrades, isolated per Chapter by design, with no route back to
a clean sample except one Legion's unusually stable line — and the man who has now improved on
that material, per [[IV.T4.Ch13]], did it without permission over ten thousand years.
