# The 9 Loyalist First Founding Legions

## Nine of Twenty {#nine}

**The twenty Space Marine Legions, also known as the Legiones Astartes, were created by the
Emperor.**[^lex-space-marine-legion] Nine of them were still loyal when the Heresy ended. This
Chapter is those nine, and the first thing to say about them is that they no longer exist in
the form the name implies.

**The loyalist Legions were broken up by Roboute Guilliman as per the new Codex
Astartes**, and **later reorganised by the Codex Astartes and broken up into several 1,000
Marine-strong Chapters during the Second Founding**.[^lex-space-marine-legion]

[[IV.T4.Ch13]] gives the reasoning: a book written by a frightened government, capping any
Astartes force at a thousand men. So "the nine loyalist Legions" names nine bloodlines rather
than nine armies. Each survives as a First Founding Chapter keeping the original name, plus
successors carrying the same gene-seed — and [[IV.T4.Ch15]] follows what became of them.

Two things recur across all nine, and both are worth having before the individual entries.

Every Legion has a Roman numeral, and the numerals are not consecutive among the loyal. I, V,
VI, VII, IX, X, XIII, XVIII, XIX survived; the gaps are the traitors of [[III.T3.Ch14]] and the
two Legions the Imperium does not discuss. And every Legion had an earlier name, from before
its Primarch was found — the Emperor raised these forces on Terra and named them again as the
scattered sons of [[III.T3.Ch12]] were recovered.

## The Ultramarines {#ultramarines}

Of the nine, one Legion requires more room than the others, and the reason is not favouritism.
Its Primarch wrote the document that governs every Chapter in the Imperium; its gene-seed is
the material two-thirds of all Space Marines are grown from; and it is the only Chapter in the
Adeptus Astartes that governs a sub-sector rather than a world. The Ultramarines are not simply
one of nine. Most of what [[IV.T4.Ch13]] and [[IV.T4.Ch15]] describe is downstream of them.

### A child found in a forest

Like every Primarch, Guilliman arrived by accident. **Like the other Primarchs, Roboute
Guilliman was taken as an infant from the Emperor's labs by the powers of Chaos, and ended up
on a far-flung world. The infant Guilliman's capsule fell on Macragge, where it was discovered
by a group of noblemen hunting in the forest. Inside the capsule they found a child, surrounded
by a glowing aura.**[^lex-ultramarines]

[[III.T3.Ch12]] records the scattering — twenty engineered sons taken from the laboratory and
flung across the galaxy before they could mature. What distinguishes this landing is who picked
the capsule up. The child **was taken back to Konor, one of the two Consuls who governed
Macragge, who adopted the infant as his son and named him
Roboute**.[^lex-ultramarines]

Not a feral world, not a prison moon, not a tribal kingdom. A functioning republic with two
consuls and a civil government — and the infant went to one of the two men running it. Set that
beside the childhoods recorded elsewhere in this codex: Angron in a gladiator pit, Curze alone
in the dark under a hive, Corax on a penal moon. Guilliman was raised by the head of state of a
prosperous world, and it shows in everything he later built.

He developed as the design intended. **Roboute was a prodigy, growing fast in both body and
intellect. By age ten, he had mastered every subject the wisest men of Macragge could teach
him, and his insights into matters of history, philosophy, and science often stunned his
elders. However, his greatest talents were as a military
leader.**[^lex-ultramarines]

His first command was a counter-insurgency. His father **gave him command of an expeditionary
force to Illyrium, a mountainous region in the far north of Macragge, whose wild inhabitants
had terrorised the civilised regions for years and successfully resisted every previous
military campaign**.[^lex-ultramarines]

Every previous campaign had failed. His did not, and the pattern of what he did afterwards is
the pattern he would apply to a galaxy.

### The five years before the Emperor arrived

The Emperor's recovery of Guilliman went wrong, and the accident is the most consequential
delay in this Chapter. **While Roboute was prosecuting his war against the Illyrian rebels, the
Emperor of Mankind and his armies had reached the neighbouring planet of Espandor. It was there
that the Emperor heard stories of the extraordinary son of Consul Konor, and realised that he
had found one of the lost Primarchs.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Then the Warp intervened. **However, due to an unexpected warp storm, his ship was thrown far
off course and by the time it reached Macragge, Roboute had been ruling for almost five
years.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Five years of a Primarch governing a world without supervision. The Warp storms of
[[I.T1.Ch4]] are usually recorded in this codex as a destroyer — they severed the Imperium
during the Age of Strife, they opened the Great Rift. Here one of them delayed a ship, and the
delay produced the only Primarch who arrived at the Great Crusade with a completed political
philosophy and the evidence that it worked.

What the Emperor found is recorded as an inspection. **When the Emperor reached Macragge, he
found a world that was self sufficient, prosperous, with a strong and well-equipped military,
and engaging in trade with nearby systems. Impressed, the Emperor assigned command of the
Ultramarines Legion to Guilliman, and relocated the Legion's forward base to
Macragge.**[^lex-ultramarines]

The Legion existed before he did — **originally known as the War-Born**, the **XIII Legion of
the original twenty**[^lex-ultramarines] — but the Emperor moved its base to the world
Guilliman had built, which is a judgement about which of the two mattered more.

### The Legion that built as it conquered

The Ultramarines' Crusade record is stated in comparative terms, and the comparison is the
important part. **With the exception of the Luna Wolves, no Legion conquered as many worlds, or
conquered worlds as fast, or left conquered worlds in such good state during the Great Crusade,
as the Ultramarines.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Second only to Horus, and the third clause is the one no other Legion earns. What "in such good
state" means is set out precisely:

**Whenever Guilliman liberated a world, he would not move on until he had set up a
self-sufficient defence system, and left advisors behind to create industry, set up trade
routes with the rest of the Imperium, and form a government whose first concern would always be
the well-being of the people.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Read that against [[III.T3.Ch13]], where the Great Crusade is four thousand three hundred
fleets moving at speed, and against [[IV.T2.Ch5]], where the Imperium the Crusade built permits
a Governor to rule his world in almost any way he likes provided the tithe arrives. Guilliman's
compliance procedure included a government whose first concern was the population. The Imperium
did not keep that clause. It kept the tithe.

The Legion's suitability for this is attributed to its biology. **The success of the
Ultramarines may be thanks to their diverse but stable gene-seed. In them was found a mixture
of aggression and restraint, discipline and determination which rendered them supremely suited
for joint taskforce operations and cross-theatre warfare.**[^lex-ultramarines]

*Restraint* appears in a list of Space Marine attributes exactly once in this codex, and it is
here.

### Calth: sixty per cent of a Legion, in one battle

The Heresy reached the Ultramarines as an ambush, and the mechanism was communications.

**As Guilliman and his fleets arrived at Calth, they knew something was wrong, because none of
their Astropaths could get messages through the Warp and the storms were interfering with the
navigation of their ships; they were blocked from the rest of the
Imperium.**[^lex-ultramarines]

The Astropaths of [[IV.T9.Ch29]] and the Navigators of [[IV.T9.Ch30]] are what make an Imperial
fleet more than ships. Cut both and a Legion is deaf and lost, which is the condition Calth
began in.

**It was at this moment that the traitor forces of the Word Bearers Legion attacked, rendering
the Ultramarines unable to participate in much of the Battle of Terra. Simultaneously, the Word
Bearers under Lorgar as well as the World Eaters attempted to cut off the rest of Ultramar from
the greater Imperium in the Shadow Crusade.**[^lex-ultramarines]

The cost is the largest single figure in this Chapter. **The battle at Calth, though ultimately
a victory for Guilliman, was devastating for the Ultramarines. Nearly 120,000 of the Legion's
Space Marines were lost and another 28,000 rendered combat-incapable. This represented almost
60% of the Legion's Pre-Heresy strength of 250,000.**[^lex-ultramarines]

A quarter of a million Space Marines in one Legion — set that against the thousand-Marine
Chapter cap of [[IV.T4.Ch13]] and the scale of what Guilliman later dismantled becomes clear.
He commanded two hundred and fifty Chapters' worth of Astartes, lost a hundred and forty-eight
thousand of them at Calth, and then wrote the document that made a force that size illegal.

And the strategic result mattered more than the casualties. The Word Bearers' object was not to
destroy the Ultramarines but to *delay* them, and it worked: the Legion best placed to
reinforce Terra spent the Siege of [[III.T3.Ch16]] fighting its way out of its own realm.

### Imperium Secundus: a replacement Imperium

What Guilliman did next has no parallel among the loyalists, and this codex will state it
plainly rather than soften it.

**After the formation of the Ruinstorm, Guilliman feared Terra lost and used the Ultramarines
and the realm of Macragge to create a new contingency empire known as Imperium
Secundus.**[^lex-ultramarines]

A loyalist Primarch concluded that the Emperor was dead and founded a second Imperium with
himself at its centre. It was a contingency, and it was also — on any reading — a Primarch
raising a rival state while the first one was still fighting. **Imperium Secundus faced a major
test in the Battle of Sotha, where the Ultramarines and their fellow Astartes allies that had
gathered fought off a force of Night Lords.**[^lex-ultramarines]

It ended by revelation rather than by defeat. **Ultimately after a number of disasters, clashes
between Guilliman, Sanguinius, and Lion El'Jonson, and a vision by Sanguinius that the Emperor
was alive, Imperium Secundus was abolished.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Three Primarchs then forced the passage home. **The three Primarchs led their legions in an
attempt to breach the Ruinstorm and reach Terra. Through an arduous journey, they eventually
reached Davin, the nexus of the Ruinstorm, and engaged a vast Daemonic host. After the battle
and the destruction of Davin, a way to Terra through the Ruinstorm was
clear.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Davin is [[III.T3.Ch14]]'s subject — the world where Horus was corrupted, the Serpent Lodge,
the Anathame. The Ruinstorm's nexus turned out to be the same planet where the Heresy started,
and clearing the road to Terra meant destroying it.

Guilliman arrived too late. **Guilliman was ultimately able to set course for Terra, destroying
Chaos reinforcements along the way, but arrived after Horus had already been defeated, and the
Traitor Legions in full retreat.**[^lex-ultramarines]

### The Codex, and what it cost him

Arriving late made the Ultramarines the Imperium's inheritors, and the sources say so in one
sentence. **Because of this, the Ultramarines were one of the few Legions at full strength in
the aftermath of the Heresy; thus, Guilliman and his Ultramarines bore much of the burden of
holding the Imperium together against aliens and Chaos
forces.**[^lex-ultramarines]

He organised the succession. **As he secured Terra, Guilliman gathered the surviving loyalist
Primarchs and High Lords and organised the Grand Council of Reconstruction as a transitionary
ruling body.**[^lex-ultramarines] [[IV.T1.Ch1]] records that council becoming the Senatorum
Imperialis, and records the non-Astartes High Lords using it to make themselves a permanent
counterweight to the Primarchs.

The first argument was about revenge. **A key debate arose as Rogal Dorn and others such as
Leman Russ and Lion El'Jonson wished to immediately pursue the fleeing traitors in a Crusade of
Vengeance, while Guilliman and his powerbase wanted to take a more careful strategy to
consolidate their holdings and retake key assets such as Luna and Mars. With the support of the
High Lords, Guilliman was able to prevail.**[^lex-ultramarines]

He won by counting votes, not by argument among brothers. The pattern of [[IV.T1.Ch1]] — a
civilian council deciding what the Astartes may do — begins here, with a Primarch using it
against three other Primarchs.

Then the Codex. **After the Horus Heresy, Roboute Guilliman dedicated himself to creating the
Codex Astartes, which would define the tactics and organisation of all Space Marines, from
battlefield strategies to squad markings. Most important of these changes was the division of
the Legions into 1,000-strong Chapters. One of the Chapters would retain the heraldry of the
original Legion, but the others would be given a new name and symbol. In doing so, Guilliman
hoped to divide the power of the Space Marines to ensure that a civil war on the scale of the
Heresy could never occur again.**[^lex-ultramarines]

His brothers did not accept it. **This, however, caused dissention among the remaining
Primarchs. Rogal Dorn, Vulkan and Leman Russ opposed the splitting of their legions while
Guilliman himself was supported by Jaghatai Khan and Corax. Neither side was willing to
relent and the controversy grew more intense, with Rogal Dorn calling Guilliman a coward for
not having participated in the Siege of Terra, and Guilliman accusing Dorn of being a rebel for
his refusal.**[^lex-ultramarines]

That exchange is worth holding still. The man who fortified the Imperial Palace called the
author of the Codex Astartes a coward to his face, on the grounds that he had not been at the
Siege — and Guilliman, who had spent the Siege cutting his way out of a trap laid specifically
to keep him from it, called Dorn a rebel. Two loyalist Primarchs, immediately after the
Heresy, accusing each other of cowardice and rebellion over how to prevent the next one.

[[IV.T4.Ch13]] records the Codex as the doctrine of most Chapters and notes that some give it
only lip service. This is where that began. The Space Wolves and Black Templars who ignore it
descend from Legions whose Primarchs opposed it at the time.

### Ultramar, and the blood

The Ultramarines' territory is unique in the Adeptus Astartes and the sources open with the
distinction. **The Ultramarines are unique in that, unlike their fellow chapters, they control
an entire sub-sector of space, rather than a single planet. Collectively, these systems are
known as the Five Hundred Worlds of Ultramar, with Macragge as its
capital.**[^lex-ultramarines]

It is governed as a state, not a fief. **The realm itself is divided into smaller sectors which
are each in turn ruled by Tetrarchs that are appointed by Roboute Guilliman
himself.**[^lex-ultramarines] Its named worlds are a functioning economy rather than a
garrison: **Calth**, a cavern world and **main shipyard of Ultramar**; **Konor**, an Adeptus
Mechanicus **forge world**; **Parmenio**, a **training world**; **Talasa Prime**, an
**Inquisition fortress**; **Ardium**, a **hive world**; and the **"Three Planets"** —
**Masali**, **Quintarn** and **Tarentus** — all **agri-worlds**.[^lex-ultramarines]

[[II.T2.Ch6]] carries Ultramar itself, including the fact that it pays no tithe. What this
Section adds is why the arrangement is stable: the realm feeds itself from three agri-worlds,
builds its own ships at Calth, and makes its own machines at Konor. A Chapter that owns its own
food, shipyards and forge is not dependent on the Administratum of [[IV.T1.Ch2]] in the way
every other Imperial institution is.

Recruitment follows from owning the population. **The Ultramarines continue the tradition, begun
by Guilliman, of recruiting from the worlds of Ultramar. There is rarely a shortage of
volunteer Aspirants, since the Ultramarines are revered throughout the realm, and many families
can boast of having sent several generations of their sons to serve the
Chapter.**[^lex-ultramarines]

*Volunteer.* Set that against [[IV.T4.Ch12]], where the Astartes prefer hive gangs and **whole
gangs of hive scum are sometimes hunted down and captured for recruitment**. The Ultramarines
do not hunt their recruits. They have five hundred worlds that consider it an honour, and they
draw first from **Macragge's Ultramar Auxilia Juventia**, **due to the level of their
training**.[^lex-ultramarines]

The trials themselves are recorded in general terms. In the **Exposure Trial**, **the Aspirant
must simply go out into the wilderness and survive for a set period of time** — **extremely
difficult as the trial is often set on Death Worlds, Desert Worlds, or Ice Worlds**, and
**other times a Feral World savage might be inserted into the middle of a Hive city**. It **is
often impossible to complete, with a subject succumbing but being recovered and healed by
Apothecaries should he be deemed worthy**.[^lex-ultramarines]

A test designed to be failed, where the assessment is how you fail it.

The gene-seed is the reason this Chapter's reach extends past its own borders. **The gene-seed
of the Ultramarines is highly regarded as one of the most stable, with only a ten percent
chance of mutation. Ultramarines gene-seed perfectly utilizes every organ and implant with low
risk of mutation or defect. Moreover, there is little observed degeneration of gene-seed over
time, making Ultramarines gene-seed as perfect today as it was in the time of the Horus
Heresy.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Ten thousand years without measurable degradation. [[IV.T4.Ch12]] establishes that gene-seed
degrades, that Chapters differ in stability, and that the Ultramarine line is the preferred
source; [[IV.T4.Ch15]] records a whole Founding cursed by mutation. This is the material none
of that happened to.

It is not neutral material, though, and the sources name what it carries. The gene-seed is
**noted for having a predisposed increase in aggression and a tendency towards cohesion and
adaptation of hierarchy**.[^lex-ultramarines]

*A tendency towards cohesion and adaptation of hierarchy* — written into the genome. The Legion
whose Primarch wrote the Imperium's rulebook is built from material that inclines its carriers
towards accepting rank. Whether that is why the Codex took, or whether the Codex was written by
a man whose own biology inclined that way, the sources do not say and neither will this codex.

The consequence is the largest single fact about the modern Adeptus Astartes. **As the largest
Space Marine Legion, the Ultramarines gene-seed was subsequently used to create most Second
Founding Chapters and today are still the most over-represented origin of gene-seed among the
Adeptus Astartes Chapters.**[^lex-ultramarines] **Roughly two thirds of all Space Marines can
trace their origins to the Ultramarines, and many still revere Roboute Guilliman as their
creator.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Two Space Marines in three. Their successors — **known as the Firstborn or Primogenitors** —
**maintain a far closer bond with the Ultramarines than many other chapters**, and **even the
most distant Chapters send representatives to the Fortress of Hera, to confer with the Lord
Macragge to coordinate the war across the entire
Imperium**.[^lex-ultramarines] **The Primogenitors all venerate Roboute Guilliman as their
founder and patron.**[^lex-ultramarines]

So a Chapter forbidden a seat on the Senatorum, per [[IV.T1.Ch1]], holds a standing conference
at which two-thirds of the Adeptus Astartes coordinate the Imperium's wars. The Codex divided
the Legions' power. It did not divide the Ultramarines' influence, because that runs through
descent rather than command.

### The discipline, and its price

The culture that results is the strictest in the Adeptus Astartes and it is unembarrassed about
it. **The Ultramarines pride themselves on their strict adherence to the Codex Astartes, and
view themselves as an exemplar for all other Space Marine Chapters. They believe — not without
cause — that their Primarch's teachings have moulded the Adeptus Astartes, and the Imperium as
a whole, in the aftermath of the Horus Heresy, and that it is their responsibility to continue
to serve as this paragon.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Other Chapters find them insufferable, and the sources say which ones. **For this reason they
are viewed as strait-laced and somewhat elitist by their brother chapters, from the more
anarchic Space Wolves to even their own successor chapters, such as the
Mortifactors.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Their own successors think so too. That is the sharper half of the observation.

And the enforcement is severe. **The chapter treats deviation from the Codex as a crime
tantamount to heresy, punishable by a Death Oath — exile from the chapter on a suicidal mission
to combat the forces of Chaos inside the Eye of Terror.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Departing from a tactical manual is punished as heresy, by exile into the Eye of Terror. There
is no proportionality here and the sources supply the reasoning: **to the Ultramarines, the
Codex is more than a set of rules, it represents discipline and respect for order, the key
virtues that separate the Ultramarines and their brethren from the Traitor
Legions.**[^lex-ultramarines]

They deviate exactly once, and the exception proves the rule. Their **rare deviations** include
**the recent formation of the Tyrannic War Veterans, comprised entirely of Veterans of battles
with the Tyranids**.[^lex-ultramarines] The Chapter that treats departure from the Codex as
heresy created a formation the Codex does not describe, because of one campaign: **the Battle
for Macragge**, **the concluding battle of the First Tyrannic War that stopped Hive Fleet
Behemoth on the Ultramarines' Homeworld of Macragge**.[^lex-ultramarines]

[[III.T4.Ch21]] carries that war. Behemoth was stopped on this Chapter's homeworld, at a cost
[[II.T2.Ch6]] records, and the Ultramarines' response was to break their own rule for the first
time in ten thousand years — to create a unit for men who had fought the thing that nearly ate
their world.

Which is the fairest summary of the Ultramarines available. They are not the Imperium's best
soldiers by any measure the sources offer; the Legion record puts them second to Horus. What
they are is the Imperium's most complete answer to the question the Heresy asked. They govern
five hundred worlds as a state rather than a fief, they recruit volunteers instead of hunting
gangs, their blood is the only Astartes material that has not decayed in ten thousand years,
two-thirds of all Space Marines descend from them, and they punish a departure from the
rulebook as heresy — because their Primarch wrote it, watched three of his brothers call him a
coward for it, and imposed it anyway.

## Imperial Fists and Blood Angels {#fists-angels}

The **Imperial Fists** were **the VII Legion of the twenty original Space Marine Legions, the
sons of the Primarch Rogal Dorn**. They were **one of the nine legions which remained loyal
during the Horus Heresy, after which it was reorganised according to the Codex
Astartes**.[^lex-imperial-fists] Their homeworld is **Terra, formerly Inwit**, their battle cry
**"Primarch-Progenitor, to your glory and the glory of Him on
Earth"**.[^lex-imperial-fists]

A Chapter whose homeworld is Terra itself. Dorn fortified the Imperial Palace for the Siege of
[[III.T3.Ch16]], and his sons never left. [[IV.T1.Ch1]] records the one occasion the Astartes
exclusion from the Senatorum was broken — during the War of the Beast, when two successive
Imperial Fists Chapter Masters, Koorland and Maximus Thane, held the chairmanship of the
Imperium because the civilian government had stopped working.

The **Blood Angels**, **originally known as the Revenant Legion**, were **the IX Legion under
their Primarch Sanguinius**. They are **particularly well known for their bloodthirsty nature
in battle**.[^lex-blood-angels] Their homeworld is **Baal** and their Chapter Master is
**Dante** — the Marine [[IV.T4.Ch12]] records as **over 1,100 years old**, the oldest active
Astartes known.

Their defining trait is a wound rather than a temperament. **The death of Sanguinius caused the
Black Rage to suddenly fall upon the Blood Angels, both on the *Vengeful Spirit* and on
Terra.**[^lex-blood-angels] Their Primarch died at the Siege, and ten thousand years later his
sons still experience his death.

And it is getting worse. **In the countless generations since the Heresy, genetic
deteriorations in the Blood Angels gene-seed have seen an increase in this
flaw.**[^lex-blood-angels] [[IV.T4.Ch12]] records that gene-seed degrades and that Chapters
differ in stability; this is what that looks like from inside one of them. There is a
recorded hope attached to the newest arrivals: **Brother Corbulo becomes fascinated with the
Primaris Marines as a potential source for a cure to the Blood Angels
flaw.**[^lex-blood-angels]

Cawl's work of [[III.T5.Ch24]] may or may not fix a ten-thousand-year-old genetic injury. A
Chapter apothecary is following Primaris squads into battle to find out.

## Dark Angels, Space Wolves, White Scars {#angels-wolves-scars}

The **Dark Angels**, **known in High Gothic as Angelis Tenebraium, and originally known as the
Uncrowned Princes and the Angels of Death**, were **the I Legion of the twenty
original**.[^lex-dark-angels] The First. Their Primarch is **Lion El'Jonson**, their Chapter
Master **Azrael**, their homeworld **Caliban — destroyed** — leaving them
**fleet-based**.[^lex-dark-angels] Their battle cry is **"Repent! For tomorrow you
die!"**[^lex-dark-angels]

Their organisation carries a permanent secret duty. Their **Deathwing** are **Terminator
veterans and hunting the Fallen**, and **all of the Dark Angels descendants continue to work
together to hunt The Fallen as the Unforgiven**.[^lex-dark-angels]

The oldest Legion lost its homeworld and has spent ten millennia hunting its own. Every
successor Chapter of this bloodline is inducted into the same search, which makes it the only
bloodline in this Chapter organised around a fact about itself that the Imperium at large does
not know.

The **Space Wolves** — **known before the Horus Heresy as the Sky Warriors of Russ, the Rout or
Vlka Fenryka, Wolves of Fenris in the Fenrisian language** — were **the VI
Legion**.[^lex-space-wolves] Their Primarch is **Leman Russ**, their homeworld **Fenris**, their
Chapter Master **Logan Grimnar**, and their battle cry **"For Russ and the
Allfather"**.[^lex-space-wolves]

*The Allfather*, not the Emperor. [[II.T3.Ch7]] records that the Missionaria did not have to
convert Fenris — it only had to supply an Imperial name for a god the Fenrisians already had —
and [[IV.T3.Ch10]] records that as standard practice. The battle cry is the compromise,
preserved.

They are one of the two Chapters [[IV.T4.Ch13]] names as paying **mere lip service to the
Codex**, and their gene-seed is one of the two [[IV.T4.Ch12]] names as carrying drastic flaws.
One episode connects them to the present: **the 13th Great Company, led by Jorin Bloodhowl,
pursued the traitors and elements of the Wulfenkind would not be seen for the next 10,000
years.**[^lex-space-wolves]

A company of Space Marines went into the Eye of Terror after the Heresy and came back in the
41st Millennium.

The **White Scars**, **known to themselves as the Ordu of Jaghatai, or the Talskar, meaning
Sons of Chogoris, and originally known as the Star Hunters**, were **the V Legion of the
original twenty of the First Founding**.[^lex-white-scars] Their Primarch is **Jaghatai Khan**,
their homeworld **Chogoris**, their Chapter Master **Jubal Khan**, and their battle cry **"For
the Khan and the Emperor!"**[^lex-white-scars]

Three names for themselves and one imposed. The Imperium calls them White Scars; they call
themselves the Ordu, or the sons of their own world. [[I.T1.Ch2]] describes Low Gothic varying
world to world; this is a Chapter that kept its own language for its own identity and answers
to the Imperial name in Imperial company.

## Raven Guard, Salamanders, Iron Hands {#raven-salamanders-iron}

The last three are the Legions that were nearly destroyed, and each survived differently.

The **Raven Guard** were **the XIX Legion**. Their Primarch is **Corvus Corax**, and their
homeworld is **Deliverance, originally named Lycaeus, a moon which orbits the Hive World
Kiavahr**.[^lex-raven-guard] Their battle cry is **Victorus Aut Mortis** — **Victory or
Death**.[^lex-raven-guard]

A Chapter recruited from a prison moon above a hive world. [[IV.T4.Ch12]] establishes that the
Astartes prefer the lower levels of hives for their recruits; the Raven Guard's homeworld is
the penal colony that served one.

**The Legion was devastated at the outbreak of the Horus Heresy and survived only by the most
desperate of measures.**[^lex-raven-guard] Those measures were genetic, and they half-worked:
**within a few weeks more aspirants had been successfully implanted with stable gene-seed than
ever before**, the new material also meaning that **warriors matured into their new forms
rapidly compared to standard Astartes**.[^lex-raven-guard] And then the sabotage — traitors
**managed to covertly spike the gene tech with a daemon blood poison (while managing to keep
the pure technology for themselves), using the Raven Guard gene-seed to produce deformed
monsters**.[^lex-raven-guard]

A Legion facing extinction accelerated its own gene-seed to rebuild, and the acceleration was
poisoned. That is the same substance and the same logic as everything in [[IV.T4.Ch12]]: a
Chapter *is* its gene-seed, so the way to destroy one is to corrupt the material rather than
kill the men.

The **Salamanders**, **originally known as the Dragon Warriors**, were **the XVIII Legion**.
Their Primarch was **Vulkan**, their homeworld **Nocturne**, their Chapter Master **Tu'Shan**,
and their battle cry **"Into the fires of battle, unto the anvil of
war"**.[^lex-salamanders] **The Legion remained loyal during the Horus Heresy, but were
severely mauled**[^lex-salamanders] — and their recovery is recorded in a single concrete
image: **when Vulkan arrived he did not do so alone, he brought with him 3,000 new initiates
and equipment from Nocturne.**[^lex-salamanders]

The **Iron Hands** were **the X Legion, sometimes referred to as the "Iron
Tenth"**.[^lex-iron-hands] Their Primarch is **Ferrus Manus**, their homeworld **Medusa**, and
their battle cry is three words: **The Flesh Is Weak!**[^lex-iron-hands]

**The Iron Hands are notable for their heavy use of bionics and their reverence for all things
mechanical, and thus their close ties to the Adeptus Mechanicus.**[^lex-iron-hands] Their
Chapter Master, **Kardan Stronos**, is **elected by the Iron
Council**[^lex-iron-hands] — which makes them the only Chapter in this Chapter whose commander
is chosen by a vote rather than by succession or appointment.

A Legion that lost its Primarch at Isstvan and concluded that flesh was the problem. The
Mechanicus of [[IV.T6.Ch19]] holds that the machine is holier than the body, and one Space
Marine Chapter agrees with them doctrinally — which is why the Iron Hands sit closer to Mars
than to Terra, and why their battle cry is a theological statement rather than a boast.

Take the nine together and the pattern is one the Imperium never states. Two lost their
homeworlds. Two carry gene-seed flaws severe enough to define them. One was poisoned at the
root. One keeps a secret from the rest of the Imperium and inducts every descendant into
keeping it. Two pay lip service to the rulebook the Imperium regards as sacred, and one wrote
it. The nine loyal Legions are not nine versions of the same thing. They are nine survivors of
the same catastrophe, each damaged in a different place, and the Codex Astartes of
[[IV.T4.Ch13]] was written to make sure the damage could never again be pointed at Terra.
