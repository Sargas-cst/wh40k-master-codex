# The 9 Loyalist First Founding Legions

## Nine of Twenty {#nine}

**The twenty Space Marine Legions, also known as the Legiones Astartes, were created by the
Emperor.**[^lex-space-marine-legion] Nine of them were still loyal when the Heresy ended. This
Chapter is those nine, and the first thing to say about them is that they no longer exist in
the form the name implies.

**The loyalist Legions were broken up by Roboute Guilliman as per the new Codex
Astartes**, and **later reorganised by the Codex Astartes and broken up into several 1,000
Marine-strong Chapters during the Second Founding**.[^lex-space-marine-legion]

[[IV.T4.Ch13]] gives the reasoning: a book written by a frightened government, capping any
Astartes force at a thousand men. So "the nine loyalist Legions" names nine bloodlines rather
than nine armies. Each survives as a First Founding Chapter keeping the original name, plus
successors carrying the same gene-seed — and [[IV.T4.Ch15]] follows what became of them.

Two things recur across all nine, and both are worth having before the individual entries.

Every Legion has a Roman numeral, and the numerals are not consecutive among the loyal. I, V,
VI, VII, IX, X, XIII, XVIII, XIX survived; the gaps are the traitors of [[III.T3.Ch14]] and the
two Legions the Imperium does not discuss. And every Legion had an earlier name, from before
its Primarch was found — the Emperor raised these forces on Terra and named them again as the
scattered sons of [[III.T3.Ch12]] were recovered.

## The Ultramarines {#ultramarines}

The **Ultramarines** are the **XIII Legion** of the original twenty, **originally known as the
War-Born**, and the only Space Marine Chapter that governs a realm rather than a
world.[^lex-ultramarines] Their Primarch is **Roboute Guilliman**, who wrote the Codex Astartes
that defines every Chapter in the Imperium; their homeworld is **Macragge**, capital of the
five hundred worlds of Ultramar; their gene-seed is the most stable in existence and the source
from which roughly **two-thirds of all Space Marines** descend. They follow the Codex more
strictly than anyone, and punish departing from it as heresy.

Everything else in this Section explains how a Legion came to be all of those things at once.

### What they are

Their standing is stated without hedging. **The Ultramarines are possibly the most important
Imperial organisation on the Eastern Fringe. Roughly two thirds of all Space Marines can trace
their origins to the Ultramarines, and many still revere Roboute Guilliman as their
creator.**[^lex-ultramarines]

They are a First Founding Chapter, and the naming convention of [[IV.T4.Ch13]] applies to them
as to everyone: **this loyalist Legion was later re-organised and divided into Chapters
according to the Codex Astartes, with only the foremost Chapter retaining the original
title**.[^lex-ultramarines] So "the Ultramarines" today means one thousand Marines, and also
means a bloodline that most of the Adeptus Astartes belongs to.

Their doctrine follows from their parentage. **Because their Primarch wrote the text that
defines a Space Marine Chapter, the Ultramarines follow this Codex
strictly**[^lex-ultramarines] — and the culture that produces is the strictest in the Astartes.
**The Ultramarines pride themselves on their strict adherence to the Codex Astartes, and view
themselves as an exemplar for all other Space Marine Chapters. They believe — not without
cause — that their Primarch's teachings have moulded the Adeptus Astartes, and the Imperium as
a whole, in the aftermath of the Horus Heresy, and that it is their responsibility to continue
to serve as this paragon.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Other Chapters find this insufferable, and the sources name which. **For this reason they are
viewed as strait-laced and somewhat elitist by their brother chapters, from the more anarchic
Space Wolves to even their own successor chapters, such as the
Mortifactors.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Their own descendants think so too. That is the sharper half of the observation.

And the discipline is enforced at a severity that has no proportion to the offence. **The
chapter treats deviation from the Codex as a crime tantamount to heresy, punishable by a Death
Oath — exile from the chapter on a suicidal mission to combat the forces of Chaos inside the Eye
of Terror.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Departing from a tactical manual, punished by exile into the Eye. The reasoning is supplied:
**to the Ultramarines, the Codex is more than a set of rules, it represents discipline and
respect for order, the key virtues that separate the Ultramarines and their brethren from the
Traitor Legions.**[^lex-ultramarines]

### Ultramar

**The Ultramarines are unique in that, unlike their fellow chapters, they control an entire
sub-sector of space, rather than a single planet. Collectively, these systems are known as the
Five Hundred Worlds of Ultramar, with Macragge as its
capital.**[^lex-ultramarines]

It is governed as a state. **The realm itself is divided into smaller sectors which are each in
turn ruled by Tetrarchs that are appointed by Roboute Guilliman
himself.**[^lex-ultramarines] Its named worlds form a working economy rather than a garrison:
**Calth**, a cavern world and **main shipyard of Ultramar**; **Konor**, an Adeptus Mechanicus
**forge world**; **Parmenio**, a **training world**; **Talasa Prime**, an **Inquisition
fortress**; **Ardium**, a **hive world**; and the **"Three Planets"** — **Masali**,
**Quintarn** and **Tarentus** — all **agri-worlds**.[^lex-ultramarines]

[[II.T2.Ch6]] carries Ultramar in full, including the fact that it pays no tithe. What matters
here is why that is sustainable: the realm grows its own food on three worlds, builds its own
ships at Calth and makes its own machines at Konor. A Chapter that owns its food, its shipyards
and its forge is not dependent on the Administratum of [[IV.T1.Ch2]] the way every other
Imperial institution is — and that independence, not the Codex, is the real measure of what
Guilliman built.

Recruitment follows from owning the population. **The Ultramarines continue the tradition,
begun by Guilliman, of recruiting from the worlds of Ultramar. There is rarely a shortage of
volunteer Aspirants, since the Ultramarines are revered throughout the realm, and many families
can boast of having sent several generations of their sons to serve the
Chapter.**[^lex-ultramarines] They draw first from **Macragge's Ultramar Auxilia Juventia**,
**due to the level of their training**.[^lex-ultramarines]

*Volunteer.* [[IV.T4.Ch12]] records the Astartes preferring hive gangs, and that **whole gangs
of hive scum are sometimes hunted down and captured for recruitment**. The Ultramarines do not
hunt. Five hundred worlds consider it an honour to be asked.

The trials are recorded in general terms. In the **Exposure Trial**, **the Aspirant must simply
go out into the wilderness and survive for a set period of time** — **extremely difficult as the
trial is often set on Death Worlds, Desert Worlds, or Ice Worlds**, and **other times a Feral
World savage might be inserted into the middle of a Hive city**. It **is often impossible to
complete, with a subject succumbing but being recovered and healed by Apothecaries should he be
deemed worthy**.[^lex-ultramarines]

A test designed to be failed, where what is assessed is how you fail.

### The blood

**The gene-seed of the Ultramarines is highly regarded as one of the most stable, with only a
ten percent chance of mutation. Ultramarines gene-seed perfectly utilizes every organ and
implant with low risk of mutation or defect. Moreover, there is little observed degeneration of
gene-seed over time, making Ultramarines gene-seed as perfect today as it was in the time of
the Horus Heresy.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Ten thousand years without measurable decay. [[IV.T4.Ch12]] establishes that gene-seed degrades,
that Chapters differ in stability, and that this line is the preferred source; [[IV.T4.Ch15]]
records an entire Founding the Imperium named *cursed* because of mutation. This is the material
none of that happened to.

It is not neutral material. The gene-seed is **noted for having a predisposed increase in
aggression and a tendency towards cohesion and adaptation of
hierarchy**.[^lex-ultramarines]

*A tendency towards cohesion and adaptation of hierarchy*, written into the genome. The Legion
whose Primarch wrote the Imperium's rulebook is grown from material that inclines its carriers
to accept rank. Whether that is why the Codex took among them, or whether the Codex was written
by a man whose own biology ran that way, the sources do not say and neither will this codex.

The consequence is the largest single fact about the modern Adeptus Astartes. **As the largest
Space Marine Legion, the Ultramarines gene-seed was subsequently used to create most Second
Founding Chapters and today are still the most over-represented origin of gene-seed among the
Adeptus Astartes Chapters.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Those successors are **known as the Firstborn or Primogenitors**, and they **maintain a far
closer bond with the Ultramarines than many other chapters**. **Even the most distant Chapters
send representatives to the Fortress of Hera, to confer with the Lord Macragge to coordinate the
war across the entire Imperium**, and **the Primogenitors all venerate Roboute Guilliman as
their founder and patron**.[^lex-ultramarines]

So a Chapter barred from the Senatorum, per [[IV.T1.Ch1]], holds a standing conference at which
two-thirds of the Adeptus Astartes coordinate the Imperium's wars. The Codex divided the
Legions' power. It did not divide the Ultramarines' influence, because that runs through descent
rather than command.

---

That is what they are. The rest is how it happened, and almost none of it was planned.

### A child found in a forest

Like every Primarch, Guilliman arrived by accident. **Like the other Primarchs, Roboute
Guilliman was taken as an infant from the Emperor's labs by the powers of Chaos, and ended up on
a far-flung world. The infant Guilliman's capsule fell on Macragge, where it was discovered by a
group of noblemen hunting in the forest. Inside the capsule they found a child, surrounded by a
glowing aura.**[^lex-ultramarines]

[[III.T3.Ch12]] records the scattering. What distinguishes this landing is who picked the
capsule up: the child **was taken back to Konor, one of the two Consuls who governed Macragge,
who adopted the infant as his son and named him Roboute**.[^lex-ultramarines]

Not a feral world, not a prison moon, not a tribal kingdom. A functioning republic with two
consuls and a civil government — and the infant went to one of the two men running it. Set that
beside the childhoods recorded elsewhere in this codex: Angron in a gladiator pit, Curze alone
in the dark beneath a hive, Corax on a penal moon. Guilliman was raised by a head of state, and
every institution he later built shows it.

He developed as the design intended. **Roboute was a prodigy, growing fast in both body and
intellect. By age ten, he had mastered every subject the wisest men of Macragge could teach him,
and his insights into matters of history, philosophy, and science often stunned his elders.
However, his greatest talents were as a military leader.**[^lex-ultramarines]

His first command was a counter-insurgency his world had already failed at. His father **gave
him command of an expeditionary force to Illyrium, a mountainous region in the far north of
Macragge, whose wild inhabitants had terrorised the civilised regions for years and successfully
resisted every previous military campaign**.[^lex-ultramarines]

### The five years nobody intended

The Emperor's recovery of Guilliman went wrong, and the accident produced everything above.

**While Roboute was prosecuting his war against the Illyrian rebels, the Emperor of Mankind and
his armies had reached the neighbouring planet of Espandor. It was there that the Emperor heard
stories of the extraordinary son of Consul Konor, and realised that he had found one of the lost
Primarchs.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Then the Warp intervened. **However, due to an unexpected warp storm, his ship was thrown far
off course and by the time it reached Macragge, Roboute had been ruling for almost five
years.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Five years of a Primarch governing a world unsupervised. The Warp storms of [[I.T1.Ch4]] appear
everywhere else in this codex as destroyers — they severed the Imperium during the Age of
Strife, they opened the Great Rift. Here one delayed a ship, and the delay produced the only
Primarch who reached the Great Crusade with a finished political philosophy and five years of
evidence that it worked.

What the Emperor found reads as an inspection report. **When the Emperor reached Macragge, he
found a world that was self sufficient, prosperous, with a strong and well-equipped military,
and engaging in trade with nearby systems. Impressed, the Emperor assigned command of the
Ultramarines Legion to Guilliman, and relocated the Legion's forward base to
Macragge.**[^lex-ultramarines]

The Legion existed before he did. The Emperor moved its base to the world Guilliman had built,
which is a judgement about which of the two mattered more.

### The Legion that built as it conquered

The Crusade record is stated comparatively, and the comparison is the point. **With the
exception of the Luna Wolves, no Legion conquered as many worlds, or conquered worlds as fast,
or left conquered worlds in such good state during the Great Crusade, as the
Ultramarines.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Second only to Horus — and the third clause is one no other Legion earns. What it means is
specified:

**Whenever Guilliman liberated a world, he would not move on until he had set up a
self-sufficient defence system, and left advisors behind to create industry, set up trade routes
with the rest of the Imperium, and form a government whose first concern would always be the
well-being of the people.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Read that against [[III.T3.Ch13]], where the Great Crusade is four thousand three hundred fleets
moving at speed, and against [[IV.T2.Ch5]], where the Imperium those fleets built lets a
Governor treat his world however he likes provided the tithe arrives. Guilliman's compliance
procedure required a government whose first concern was the population. The Imperium did not
keep that clause. It kept the tithe.

The Legion's fitness for the work is attributed to its biology. **The success of the
Ultramarines may be thanks to their diverse but stable gene-seed. In them was found a mixture of
aggression and restraint, discipline and determination which rendered them supremely suited for
joint taskforce operations and cross-theatre warfare.**[^lex-ultramarines]

*Restraint* appears in a list of Space Marine attributes exactly once in this codex. It is here.

### Calth

The Heresy reached the Ultramarines as an ambush, and it began with communications.

**As Guilliman and his fleets arrived at Calth, they knew something was wrong, because none of
their Astropaths could get messages through the Warp and the storms were interfering with the
navigation of their ships; they were blocked from the rest of the
Imperium.**[^lex-ultramarines]

The Astropaths of [[IV.T9.Ch29]] and the Navigators of [[IV.T9.Ch30]] are what make a fleet more
than ships. Cut both and a Legion is deaf and lost, which is the condition Calth started in.

**It was at this moment that the traitor forces of the Word Bearers Legion attacked, rendering
the Ultramarines unable to participate in much of the Battle of Terra. Simultaneously, the Word
Bearers under Lorgar as well as the World Eaters attempted to cut off the rest of Ultramar from
the greater Imperium in the Shadow Crusade.**[^lex-ultramarines]

The cost is the largest figure in this Chapter. **The battle at Calth, though ultimately a
victory for Guilliman, was devastating for the Ultramarines. Nearly 120,000 of the Legion's
Space Marines were lost and another 28,000 rendered combat-incapable. This represented almost
60% of the Legion's Pre-Heresy strength of 250,000.**[^lex-ultramarines]

A quarter of a million Space Marines in one Legion. Set that against the thousand-Marine cap of
[[IV.T4.Ch13]] and the scale of what Guilliman later dismantled is legible: he commanded two
hundred and fifty Chapters' worth of Astartes, lost a hundred and forty-eight thousand of them
in a single campaign, and then wrote the document that made a force that size illegal.

And the strategic result mattered more than the casualties. The object was not to destroy the
Ultramarines but to *delay* them — and it worked. The Legion best placed to reinforce Terra
spent the Siege of [[III.T3.Ch16]] fighting its way out of its own realm.

### The empire he founded to replace Terra

What Guilliman did next has no parallel among the loyalists.

**After the formation of the Ruinstorm, Guilliman feared Terra lost and used the Ultramarines
and the realm of Macragge to create a new contingency empire known as Imperium
Secundus.**[^lex-ultramarines]

A loyalist Primarch concluded the Emperor was dead and founded a second Imperium with himself at
its centre. It was a contingency, and it was also — on any reading — a Primarch raising a rival
state while the first was still fighting. It was tested: **Imperium Secundus faced a major test
in the Battle of Sotha, where the Ultramarines and their fellow Astartes allies that had gathered
fought off a force of Night Lords.**[^lex-ultramarines]

It ended by revelation rather than defeat. **Ultimately after a number of disasters, clashes
between Guilliman, Sanguinius, and Lion El'Jonson, and a vision by Sanguinius that the Emperor
was alive, Imperium Secundus was abolished.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Three Primarchs then forced the road home. **The three Primarchs led their legions in an attempt
to breach the Ruinstorm and reach Terra. Through an arduous journey, they eventually reached
Davin, the nexus of the Ruinstorm, and engaged a vast Daemonic host. After the battle and the
destruction of Davin, a way to Terra through the Ruinstorm was
clear.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Davin is [[III.T3.Ch14]]'s subject — the world where Horus was corrupted, the Serpent Lodge, the
Anathame. The Ruinstorm's nexus turned out to be the planet where the Heresy began, and clearing
the road to Terra meant destroying it.

He still arrived too late. **Guilliman was ultimately able to set course for Terra, destroying
Chaos reinforcements along the way, but arrived after Horus had already been defeated, and the
Traitor Legions in full retreat.**[^lex-ultramarines]

### The Codex, and what it cost him

Arriving late made the Ultramarines the Imperium's inheritors. **Because of this, the
Ultramarines were one of the few Legions at full strength in the aftermath of the Heresy; thus,
Guilliman and his Ultramarines bore much of the burden of holding the Imperium together against
aliens and Chaos forces.**[^lex-ultramarines]

He organised the succession. **As he secured Terra, Guilliman gathered the surviving loyalist
Primarchs and High Lords and organised the Grand Council of Reconstruction as a transitionary
ruling body.**[^lex-ultramarines] [[IV.T1.Ch1]] records that council becoming the Senatorum
Imperialis, and records the non-Astartes High Lords using it to make themselves a permanent
counterweight to the Primarchs.

The first argument was about revenge. **A key debate arose as Rogal Dorn and others such as
Leman Russ and Lion El'Jonson wished to immediately pursue the fleeing traitors in a Crusade of
Vengeance, while Guilliman and his powerbase wanted to take a more careful strategy to
consolidate their holdings and retake key assets such as Luna and Mars. With the support of the
High Lords, Guilliman was able to prevail.**[^lex-ultramarines]

He won by counting votes rather than by persuading his brothers. The pattern of
[[IV.T1.Ch1]] — a civilian council deciding what the Astartes may do — starts here, with a
Primarch using it against three other Primarchs.

Then the Codex itself. **After the Horus Heresy, Roboute Guilliman dedicated himself to creating
the Codex Astartes, which would define the tactics and organisation of all Space Marines, from
battlefield strategies to squad markings. Most important of these changes was the division of
the Legions into 1,000-strong Chapters. One of the Chapters would retain the heraldry of the
original Legion, but the others would be given a new name and symbol. In doing so, Guilliman
hoped to divide the power of the Space Marines to ensure that a civil war on the scale of the
Heresy could never occur again.**[^lex-ultramarines]

His brothers refused it. **This, however, caused dissention among the remaining Primarchs. Rogal
Dorn, Vulkan and Leman Russ opposed the splitting of their legions while Guilliman himself was
supported by Jaghatai Khan and Corax. Neither side was willing to relent and the controversy
grew more intense, with Rogal Dorn calling Guilliman a coward for not having participated in the
Siege of Terra, and Guilliman accusing Dorn of being a rebel for his
refusal.**[^lex-ultramarines]

Hold that exchange still. The man who fortified the Imperial Palace called the author of the
Codex Astartes a coward to his face for not being at the Siege — and Guilliman, who had spent
the Siege cutting his way out of a trap laid specifically to keep him from it, called Dorn a
rebel. Two loyalist Primarchs, weeks after the Heresy ended, accusing each other of cowardice
and rebellion over how to prevent the next one.

[[IV.T4.Ch13]] records the Codex as the doctrine of most Chapters and notes that some give it
only lip service. This is where that began: the Space Wolves and Black Templars who ignore it
descend from Legions whose Primarchs opposed it in the room.

### The one exception

The Ultramarines have broken their own rule once, and what it took is the measure of the rule.

Their **rare deviations** include **the recent formation of the Tyrannic War Veterans, comprised
entirely of Veterans of battles with the Tyranids**.[^lex-ultramarines] The occasion was **the
Battle for Macragge**, **the concluding battle of the First Tyrannic War that stopped Hive Fleet
Behemoth on the Ultramarines' Homeworld of Macragge**.[^lex-ultramarines]

[[III.T4.Ch21]] carries that war. Behemoth was stopped on this Chapter's own homeworld, at the
cost [[II.T2.Ch6]] records — and the Chapter that punishes deviation from the Codex with exile
into the Eye of Terror responded by creating a formation the Codex does not describe. Ten
thousand years of strict compliance, and the exception is a unit for the men who fought the
thing that nearly ate Macragge.

Which is the fairest summary available. The Ultramarines are not the Imperium's best soldiers by
any measure the sources offer — the Crusade record puts them second to Horus. What they are is
the Imperium's most complete answer to the question the Heresy asked. They govern five hundred
worlds as a state rather than a fief, recruit volunteers instead of hunting gangs, carry the
only Astartes blood that has not decayed in ten thousand years, supply two-thirds of all Space
Marines, and treat departure from a rulebook as heresy — because their Primarch wrote it,
watched three of his brothers call him a coward for it, and imposed it anyway.

## Imperial Fists and Blood Angels {#fists-angels}

The **Imperial Fists** were **the VII Legion of the twenty original Space Marine Legions, the
sons of the Primarch Rogal Dorn**. They were **one of the nine legions which remained loyal
during the Horus Heresy, after which it was reorganised according to the Codex
Astartes**.[^lex-imperial-fists] Their homeworld is **Terra, formerly Inwit**, their battle cry
**"Primarch-Progenitor, to your glory and the glory of Him on
Earth"**.[^lex-imperial-fists]

A Chapter whose homeworld is Terra itself. Dorn fortified the Imperial Palace for the Siege of
[[III.T3.Ch16]], and his sons never left. [[IV.T1.Ch1]] records the one occasion the Astartes
exclusion from the Senatorum was broken — during the War of the Beast, when two successive
Imperial Fists Chapter Masters, Koorland and Maximus Thane, held the chairmanship of the
Imperium because the civilian government had stopped working.

The **Blood Angels**, **originally known as the Revenant Legion**, were **the IX Legion under
their Primarch Sanguinius**. They are **particularly well known for their bloodthirsty nature
in battle**.[^lex-blood-angels] Their homeworld is **Baal** and their Chapter Master is
**Dante** — the Marine [[IV.T4.Ch12]] records as **over 1,100 years old**, the oldest active
Astartes known.

Their defining trait is a wound rather than a temperament. **The death of Sanguinius caused the
Black Rage to suddenly fall upon the Blood Angels, both on the *Vengeful Spirit* and on
Terra.**[^lex-blood-angels] Their Primarch died at the Siege, and ten thousand years later his
sons still experience his death.

And it is getting worse. **In the countless generations since the Heresy, genetic
deteriorations in the Blood Angels gene-seed have seen an increase in this
flaw.**[^lex-blood-angels] [[IV.T4.Ch12]] records that gene-seed degrades and that Chapters
differ in stability; this is what that looks like from inside one of them. There is a
recorded hope attached to the newest arrivals: **Brother Corbulo becomes fascinated with the
Primaris Marines as a potential source for a cure to the Blood Angels
flaw.**[^lex-blood-angels]

Cawl's work of [[III.T5.Ch24]] may or may not fix a ten-thousand-year-old genetic injury. A
Chapter apothecary is following Primaris squads into battle to find out.

## Dark Angels, Space Wolves, White Scars {#angels-wolves-scars}

The **Dark Angels**, **known in High Gothic as Angelis Tenebraium, and originally known as the
Uncrowned Princes and the Angels of Death**, were **the I Legion of the twenty
original**.[^lex-dark-angels] The First. Their Primarch is **Lion El'Jonson**, their Chapter
Master **Azrael**, their homeworld **Caliban — destroyed** — leaving them
**fleet-based**.[^lex-dark-angels] Their battle cry is **"Repent! For tomorrow you
die!"**[^lex-dark-angels]

Their organisation carries a permanent secret duty. Their **Deathwing** are **Terminator
veterans and hunting the Fallen**, and **all of the Dark Angels descendants continue to work
together to hunt The Fallen as the Unforgiven**.[^lex-dark-angels]

The oldest Legion lost its homeworld and has spent ten millennia hunting its own. Every
successor Chapter of this bloodline is inducted into the same search, which makes it the only
bloodline in this Chapter organised around a fact about itself that the Imperium at large does
not know.

The **Space Wolves** — **known before the Horus Heresy as the Sky Warriors of Russ, the Rout or
Vlka Fenryka, Wolves of Fenris in the Fenrisian language** — were **the VI
Legion**.[^lex-space-wolves] Their Primarch is **Leman Russ**, their homeworld **Fenris**, their
Chapter Master **Logan Grimnar**, and their battle cry **"For Russ and the
Allfather"**.[^lex-space-wolves]

*The Allfather*, not the Emperor. [[II.T3.Ch7]] records that the Missionaria did not have to
convert Fenris — it only had to supply an Imperial name for a god the Fenrisians already had —
and [[IV.T3.Ch10]] records that as standard practice. The battle cry is the compromise,
preserved.

They are one of the two Chapters [[IV.T4.Ch13]] names as paying **mere lip service to the
Codex**, and their gene-seed is one of the two [[IV.T4.Ch12]] names as carrying drastic flaws.
One episode connects them to the present: **the 13th Great Company, led by Jorin Bloodhowl,
pursued the traitors and elements of the Wulfenkind would not be seen for the next 10,000
years.**[^lex-space-wolves]

A company of Space Marines went into the Eye of Terror after the Heresy and came back in the
41st Millennium.

The **White Scars**, **known to themselves as the Ordu of Jaghatai, or the Talskar, meaning
Sons of Chogoris, and originally known as the Star Hunters**, were **the V Legion of the
original twenty of the First Founding**.[^lex-white-scars] Their Primarch is **Jaghatai Khan**,
their homeworld **Chogoris**, their Chapter Master **Jubal Khan**, and their battle cry **"For
the Khan and the Emperor!"**[^lex-white-scars]

Three names for themselves and one imposed. The Imperium calls them White Scars; they call
themselves the Ordu, or the sons of their own world. [[I.T1.Ch2]] describes Low Gothic varying
world to world; this is a Chapter that kept its own language for its own identity and answers
to the Imperial name in Imperial company.

## Raven Guard, Salamanders, Iron Hands {#raven-salamanders-iron}

The last three are the Legions that were nearly destroyed, and each survived differently.

The **Raven Guard** were **the XIX Legion**. Their Primarch is **Corvus Corax**, and their
homeworld is **Deliverance, originally named Lycaeus, a moon which orbits the Hive World
Kiavahr**.[^lex-raven-guard] Their battle cry is **Victorus Aut Mortis** — **Victory or
Death**.[^lex-raven-guard]

A Chapter recruited from a prison moon above a hive world. [[IV.T4.Ch12]] establishes that the
Astartes prefer the lower levels of hives for their recruits; the Raven Guard's homeworld is
the penal colony that served one.

**The Legion was devastated at the outbreak of the Horus Heresy and survived only by the most
desperate of measures.**[^lex-raven-guard] Those measures were genetic, and they half-worked:
**within a few weeks more aspirants had been successfully implanted with stable gene-seed than
ever before**, the new material also meaning that **warriors matured into their new forms
rapidly compared to standard Astartes**.[^lex-raven-guard] And then the sabotage — traitors
**managed to covertly spike the gene tech with a daemon blood poison (while managing to keep
the pure technology for themselves), using the Raven Guard gene-seed to produce deformed
monsters**.[^lex-raven-guard]

A Legion facing extinction accelerated its own gene-seed to rebuild, and the acceleration was
poisoned. That is the same substance and the same logic as everything in [[IV.T4.Ch12]]: a
Chapter *is* its gene-seed, so the way to destroy one is to corrupt the material rather than
kill the men.

The **Salamanders**, **originally known as the Dragon Warriors**, were **the XVIII Legion**.
Their Primarch was **Vulkan**, their homeworld **Nocturne**, their Chapter Master **Tu'Shan**,
and their battle cry **"Into the fires of battle, unto the anvil of
war"**.[^lex-salamanders] **The Legion remained loyal during the Horus Heresy, but were
severely mauled**[^lex-salamanders] — and their recovery is recorded in a single concrete
image: **when Vulkan arrived he did not do so alone, he brought with him 3,000 new initiates
and equipment from Nocturne.**[^lex-salamanders]

The **Iron Hands** were **the X Legion, sometimes referred to as the "Iron
Tenth"**.[^lex-iron-hands] Their Primarch is **Ferrus Manus**, their homeworld **Medusa**, and
their battle cry is three words: **The Flesh Is Weak!**[^lex-iron-hands]

**The Iron Hands are notable for their heavy use of bionics and their reverence for all things
mechanical, and thus their close ties to the Adeptus Mechanicus.**[^lex-iron-hands] Their
Chapter Master, **Kardan Stronos**, is **elected by the Iron
Council**[^lex-iron-hands] — which makes them the only Chapter in this Chapter whose commander
is chosen by a vote rather than by succession or appointment.

A Legion that lost its Primarch at Isstvan and concluded that flesh was the problem. The
Mechanicus of [[IV.T6.Ch19]] holds that the machine is holier than the body, and one Space
Marine Chapter agrees with them doctrinally — which is why the Iron Hands sit closer to Mars
than to Terra, and why their battle cry is a theological statement rather than a boast.

Take the nine together and the pattern is one the Imperium never states. Two lost their
homeworlds. Two carry gene-seed flaws severe enough to define them. One was poisoned at the
root. One keeps a secret from the rest of the Imperium and inducts every descendant into
keeping it. Two pay lip service to the rulebook the Imperium regards as sacred, and one wrote
it. The nine loyal Legions are not nine versions of the same thing. They are nine survivors of
the same catastrophe, each damaged in a different place, and the Codex Astartes of
[[IV.T4.Ch13]] was written to make sure the damage could never again be pointed at Terra.
