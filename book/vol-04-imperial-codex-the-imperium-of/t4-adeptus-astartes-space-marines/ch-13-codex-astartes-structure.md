# Codex Astartes Structure

## A Book Written by a Frightened Government {#origins}

The document that governs the Imperium's finest soldiers was written to make sure they could
never do again what they had just done.

The **Codex Astartes** is **the doctrine of most Space Marine Chapters, governing all aspects
of Chapter organisation and battlefield tactics**. **The wisdom of thousands of Imperial
warriors has contributed to the Codex, offering detailed insight on topics ranging from unit
markings, to launching a full-scale planetary assault, to religious instruction and strategic
supply.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

Its origin is the aftermath of [[III.T3.Ch17]]. **In the aftermath of the Horus Heresy, the
Emperor had been incapacitated, and the Space Marines' numbers had been decimated by
defections to Chaos and battle losses. With the Emperor confined to the Golden Throne the High
Lords of Terra found themselves the new leaders of the Emperor's
domain.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

And then the motive, stated outright. **Both the High Lords and surviving Primarchs became
paranoid of the possibility of such events repeating themselves.** **Concerned that once again
there could be a threat from such a terrible force, each Legion was to be divided into smaller
Chapters and a new set of regulations would be created to govern
them.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

The Codex Astartes is not a manual of best practice that happens to include a size limit. It
is a size limit with a manual of best practice attached. [[III.T4.Ch18]] carries the Legions'
breaking in full; this Chapter is the rulebook that came with it.

Who wrote it, and why he was the one to do it, is recorded in a single line. **The Ultramarines
were left as the largest loyal legion, and Guilliman assumed the title of Lord Commander of the
Imperium.**[^lex-codex-astartes] **The first edition of the Codex was written and formalised by
the Primarch of the Ultramarines, Roboute Guilliman, following the Horus Heresy. Guilliman
created the Codex to provide structure to the Chapters of the newly formed Adeptus Astartes,
created by splitting up the original loyalist Legions into smaller autonomous
organisations.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

The commander of the largest surviving Legion wrote the rule that broke all the Legions,
including his own. It is worth holding that against [[IV.T1.Ch1]], where the same man is
recorded as keeping the Astartes off the Senatorum on the grounds that Space Marines should
protect mankind rather than rule it. Both measures are his, both were taken in the same
period, and together they describe a man who had concluded that the force he commanded was too
dangerous to leave as he found it.

The book had two halves. **The original Codex was largely a work of two parts. In the first,
Guilliman laid out the organisational structure of the Chapters, their insignia, officers and
role. The second was a collection of strategic and tactical theses intended to provide guidance
to future leaders of the Space Marines.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

And the first edition may not survive. **The earliest known version of the Codex, which may or
may not be the first, was named the Apocrypha of Skaros.**[^lex-codex-astartes] The Imperium's
most closely followed text cannot be certainly identified with its original.

## One Thousand, in Ten Companies {#structure}

The core provision is one sentence, and everything else in Astartes organisation follows from
it.

**The Codex states that a Space Marine Chapter should be split into 10 companies of 100 Marines
each and led by a Captain.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

A thousand men. Set that against the Astra Militarum of [[IV.T5.Ch16]], which fields regiments
by the million, and against the Legions of [[III.T3.Ch13]], which numbered in the tens of
thousands each. The cap is not a judgement about the ideal size of a fighting force. It is the
number at which a Chapter cannot take a sector on its own.

Command runs from a single office. **Every Chapter should be led by the Chapter Master, an
accomplished warrior and strategist who coordinates the Chapter's strength as a whole, assisted
in his tasks by various headquarters staff including an Honour Guard as well as the Chapter's
servitors and serfs.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

Five organs sit outside the company structure, and they are where a Chapter's real
distinctiveness lives. **Each Chapter has an Armoury consisting of the Chapter's Techmarines,
tanks, gunships and vehicle staff as it can muster, a Reclusiam hosting the Chaplains, a
Librarius hosting the Librarians, an Apothecarion containing Apothecaries and guarding a
Chapter's gene-seed, and a combat-capable Chapter Fleet.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

*Guarding a Chapter's gene-seed.* [[IV.T4.Ch12]] establishes why that phrasing is right: the
Apothecarion is not a hospital, it is the vault in which a Chapter's ability to replace itself
is stored, and its staff recover progenoid glands from the dead.

The companies are not interchangeable. The **1st Company** is **the most powerful company,
consisting entirely of Veteran Space Marines**. It has **access to the Chapter's rarest and
most advanced technology** and is **the only one trained in and equipped with Terminator
Armour** — and it does not fight as a block: **the veterans of the 1st are attached to other
Space Marine strike forces rather than the entire formation fighting as
one**.[^lex-codex-astartes]

The **2nd, 3rd, 4th, and 5th Companies are known as Battle Companies, as they generally form
the main battle force deployed for engagements**. Each is **led by a Captain with two
supporting Lieutenants**, with a **Company Champion**, a **Company Ancient** and **Company
Veterans**.[^lex-codex-astartes] Each Battle Company has ten squads: **six Battleline Squads,
two Close Support Squads, two Fire Support Squads**.[^lex-codex-astartes]

Captains carry duties beyond their companies, and the titles are a small map of what a Chapter
must attend to. **The 2nd Company Captain holds the title of the Master of the Watch, the 4th
as the Master of the Fleet, and the 5th as the Master of the
Marches.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

A Company Captain is **supported by 2 Lieutenants, each commanding a demi-company, and a
Command Squad**, which **generally includes an Apothecary, a Chaplain, a Company Ancient, a
Company Champion, and multiple (usually 2) Company Veterans**.[^lex-codex-astartes]

## Who Obeys It {#compliance}

The Codex is universally acknowledged and unevenly followed, and the sources sort the Chapters
into three groups.

**Some Chapters, such as the Ultramarines and their successors, regard the Codex as a holy
text, and follow it without question. Many other Chapters also follow the Codex, though not
always as strictly as the Ultramarines; these two types of Chapters are collectively known as
Codex Chapters.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

Then the third group. **A few Chapters, such as the Space Wolves and Black Templars, pay mere
lip service to the Codex and instead prefer their own traditions of
combat.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

A text written specifically to prevent another Horus Heresy is treated as a *holy text* by
some and given *lip service* by others, and the Imperium tolerates both. [[IV.T3.Ch8]] records
the same latitude in religion — some Chapters worship the Emperor as a god and others keep the
Imperial Truth that denied his divinity — and the two facts are the same fact. The Adeptus
Astartes are the one Imperial institution permitted to disagree with the Imperium's founding
documents, and [[IV.T1.Ch1]] records the price: no seat on the Council.

Its use has spread past the Astartes. **The Codex has also seen use in some non-Adeptus Astartes
organisations, such as the Ultramar Defence Auxilia.**[^lex-codex-astartes] The realm of
[[II.T2.Ch6]] applies the Astartes rulebook to its own human troops — which is what one would
expect of the place its author governed.

## The Author Came Back and Rewrote It {#reformation}

Ten thousand years after the Codex was written, the man who wrote it returned and read it
again.

**After the resurrection of Guilliman in late M41, he began to see many of the flaws that came
from overly-strict adherence to the Codex Astartes. As a result, he has reformed much of the
Codex and eliminated much of its restrictions on Space
Marines.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

That is a remarkable sentence to find in an Imperial source. The Imperium of [[IV.T6.Ch19]]
treats innovation as heresy and cannot build what it lacks a template for. Its most sacred
military text was revised because its author objected to how literally it had been obeyed —
and the objection is recorded not as heresy but as reform, because the man making it was the
one who wrote the text.

The changes are specific. **New additions include the addition of Primaris Space Marine
formations such as Intercessor, Aggressor, Inceptor, and Reiver Squads, as well as the
inclusion of two Lieutenants below the Company Captain, each commanding a
demi-Company.**[^lex-codex-astartes] The vocabulary changed too: **squads in the Codex Astartes
are now defined as "battleline" rather than tactical, "close support" rather than assault, or
"fire support" rather than devastator**.[^lex-codex-astartes] And the **10th company of a
Chapter began to include 10 "vanguard" squads alongside scout squads, acting as a permanent
infiltration and reconnaissance force that could be attached to other
companies**.[^lex-codex-astartes]

The Primaris formations are the point. [[IV.T4.Ch12]] records the three implants Belisarius
Cawl added to the Emperor's design across ten thousand years; this is the organisational half
of the same delivery. Cawl improved the soldier and Guilliman rewrote the rulebook to
accommodate him, and both men were doing the thing the Imperium's own doctrine forbids.

What the reform did *not* do is worth noting as clearly as what it did. Nothing in the sources
suggests Guilliman raised the thousand-Marine ceiling. The formations changed, the ranks
changed, the words changed. The number that exists to keep any one Chapter from being able to
threaten the Imperium stayed exactly where he put it after the Heresy — set by a man who has
now seen, twice, what the Astartes are capable of when they turn.
