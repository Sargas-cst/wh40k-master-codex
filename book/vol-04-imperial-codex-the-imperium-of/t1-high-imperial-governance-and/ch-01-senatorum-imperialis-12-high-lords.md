# Senatorum Imperialis (12 High Lords)

## The Nine That Cannot Be Moved {#permanent-seats}

The Imperium is governed by twelve people, and the interesting thing about them is that
nobody elected any of them and nobody can remove most of them.

The **Senatorum Imperialis** — officially the **Lords Temporal, Martial and
Ecclesiarchical of the Most Divine and Righteous Imperium of Mankind** — collectively form
the **Council of the High Lords of Terra**.[^lex-senatorum-imperialis] It is an Imperial
governing body led by twelve leaders of the most powerful organisations of the Imperium,
and it rules in the Emperor's name.[^lex-senatorum-imperialis]

The full body is much larger than twelve. The Senatorum **is composed of tens of thousands
of Imperial dignitaries, noblemen, lords, and other officials, but these rarely meet and
instead the "High Twelve" conduct the vast majority of
affairs**.[^lex-senatorum-imperialis]

Tens of thousands of members who do not convene. That is the first fact about Imperial
government and it recurs at every level below: a deliberative body exists, is constituted,
is entitled to meet, and does not, so its authority collects in a committee small enough to
fit in a room. The Great Chamber of the Senatorum on Terra is where the Imperium is
actually run, and the other tens of thousands are a constitutional formality.

Their function is stated in one line, and the wording repays attention: **the task of the
High Lords is to interpret and enact the will of the
Emperor**.[^lex-senatorum-imperialis] *Interpret.* The being they serve has been silent on
the Golden Throne since the Siege — [[III.T3.Ch17]] records what is left of him — so the
twelve most powerful officials in human history hold their power by claiming to know what
a man who cannot speak would want. There is no appeal from an interpretation, because
there is nobody to appeal to.

Nine of the twelve seats are effectively permanent. Determining who holds a High Lordship
**has resulted in millennia of political intrigue between the various bureaucracies
seeking to increase their power** — but **some organisations are so powerful, so
fundamentally important to the Imperium, that their position on the Council is considered
sacrosanct**.[^lex-senatorum-imperialis] Those nine offices are the Master of the
Administratum; the **Inquisitorial Representative**; the **Ecclesiarch** of the Adeptus
Ministorum; the **Fabricator-General** of the Adeptus Mechanicus; the **Grand Provost
Marshal** of the Adeptus Arbites; the **Paternoval Envoy** of the Navis Nobilite; the
Master of the Astronomican; the **Grand Master of Assassins** of the Officio Assassinorum;
and the Master of the Adeptus Astra Telepathica.[^lex-senatorum-imperialis]

Read that list as a description of what the Imperium believes it cannot function without,
because that is what it is. Taxation and record-keeping. Internal security against heresy.
The state religion. Manufacturing. Law enforcement. Warp navigation. The beacon that makes
navigation possible. State murder. And interstellar communication. Four of the nine —
Navigators, the Astronomican, the Astra Telepathica, and to a degree the Mechanicus — exist
solely to hold a galaxy-spanning empire together across distances that would otherwise
dissolve it. They are treated in [[IV.T9.Ch30]], [[IV.T9.Ch31]] and [[IV.T9.Ch29]]. The
Imperium's supreme council is, by composition, a logistics board with a chaplain and an
assassin.

And one absence defines the rest. **Despite their vital importance to the Imperium, the
Adeptus Astartes are not represented among the High Lords.** The article records the
reason as a deliberate one: **this may have been set up intentionally by Roboute Guilliman
himself as the Primarch fervently believed that it was the duty of the Space Marines to
protect mankind, not rule it.**[^lex-senatorum-imperialis]

The most effective military force the species possesses has no vote, by the design of the
man who wrote its rulebook. [[IV.T4.Ch13]] sets out the rest of that design — a thousand
Marines per Chapter, no more — and the two provisions are the same provision. Guilliman
capped the Astartes at a size that could not conquer the Imperium and then kept them out of
the room where the Imperium is governed. Both measures were taken by someone who had just
watched half the Legions defect.

## The Three That Are Fought Over {#rotating-seats}

If nine seats are sacrosanct, three are not, and the contest for them is the whole of
Imperial high politics.

The remaining three positions are **most likely to be filled from among** eight named
candidates: the Lord Commander of Segmentum Solar; the **Lord Commander Militant** of the
Imperial Guard; **Cardinals** of the Holy Synod of Terra; the **Abbess** of the Adepta
Sororitas; the **Captain-General** of the Adeptus Custodes; the **Chancellor of the Estate
Imperium**; the **Speaker for the Chartist Captains**; and the **Lord High Admiral** of the
Imperial Navy.[^lex-senatorum-imperialis]

Eight claimants, three chairs, permanently. That is not an oversight in the constitution;
it is the mechanism by which the Imperium's institutions are kept competing rather than
combining. Note who is on the wrong side of the line. The Astra Militarum of
[[IV.T5.Ch16]] — the largest fighting force in human history — has no guaranteed seat. Nor
does the Imperial Navy that moves it. Nor do the Custodes, who guard the Emperor's body
personally. Nor do the Sisters of Battle. Every one of them can be excluded in favour of a
Cardinal or a merchant captain, and periodically is.

The **Speaker for the Chartist Captains** deserves singling out, because a merchant
shipping interest sitting among the twelve rulers of humanity is the kind of detail that
tells you how the Imperium actually works. The Chartist fleets move the tithe. An empire
that cannot feed Terra without them — [[III.T2.Ch9]] records what happened the last time
the freight stopped — gives their spokesman a chance at the top table.

Below the High Twelve sits a second tier that the sources are careful to distinguish. The
institution of the Senatorum **is overseen by a Chancellor of the Imperial Council**;
**while not among the High Twelve, it is nonetheless a powerful and influential
position**.[^lex-senatorum-imperialis] Other **lesser lords** are full members of the
Senatorum without being of the Twelve — among them the **Commandant of the Schola
Progenium**, the **Lord Constable of the Synopticon**, the **Mistress Plenary of the
Catacombs**, the **Consul Pre-Eminus** of the Navis Nobilite, the **Chirurgeon-General** of
the Order Hospitaller, and the **High Lord of the Imperial
Chancellery**.[^lex-senatorum-imperialis]

The Commandant of the Schola Progenium is on that list, which is worth holding onto. The
institution that raises the orphaned children of dead Imperial servants and turns them into
commissars, storm troopers and adepts — [[IV.T2.Ch6]] — has a permanent voice in the
Senatorum without a vote among the Twelve. The Imperium does not consider the manufacture
of its own officer class important enough for the top table, but does consider it important
enough to have someone in the building.

## What the Council Is For, and What It Does Instead {#failures}

The Senatorum's history is not a record of governance. It is a record of four occasions on
which the government of the Imperium was seized, and of what the Council did while that
happened, which was nothing.

Its origin is administrative rather than noble. The original High Lords were **bureaucrats
of the Emperor who followed in his armies' wake during the Unification Wars to build the
foundations of his new empire** — the **Magisters Temporal**, later reorganised into the
**Lords Civilian**, of whom the four greatest became the **High Lords
Civilian**.[^lex-senatorum-imperialis] Those four original offices were the Master of the
Administratum, the Chancellor of the Estate Imperium, the Grand Provost Marshal, and the
Lord Commander Militant of the Imperial Armies.[^lex-senatorum-imperialis]

Tax, treasury, police and army. The Imperium's founding executive contained no priest, and
[[III.T3.Ch11]] explains why: the Emperor's own project was explicitly atheistic, and the
Ecclesiarchy that now holds a sacrosanct seat is a later growth on a state that was
designed to have no religion at all. **In early M32, the rise of the Imperial Creed and
the Cult of the Saviour Emperor meant that the Ecclesiarch of the Adeptus Ministorum was
granted a seat, which soon became permanent.**[^lex-senatorum-imperialis] The creed that
did that is treated in [[IV.T3.Ch8]]; the point here is constitutional. It took roughly two
thousand years for the Emperor's state to be captured by a religion about the Emperor.

Then the failures, in order.

**The War of the Beast**, 544.M32. During the conflict the **Senatorum was paralysed by
inaction as the Orks devastated humanity, and multiple High Lords were removed, arrested,
and even killed**. The Imperial Fist **Koorland** rose to become Lord Commander of the
Imperium and removed the Ecclesiarchy from the High
Twelve.[^lex-senatorum-imperialis] That is the exception to the Astartes exclusion
described above, and it took an existential Ork invasion to produce it: a Chapter Master
took the chairmanship because the civilian government had stopped functioning under
pressure.

**The Beheading, 546.M32.** Two years later, **the High Lords were killed to a man in the
mad Grand Master of Assassins Drakan Vangorich's coup, and he ruled for the next hundred
years at the head of a puppet cabinet**. After his death at the hands of **Maximus Thane**,
**anarchy reigned for a period before the Space Marines were able to restore
peace**.[^lex-senatorum-imperialis]

Every word of that is worth slowing down for. One of the nine permanent seats belongs to
the head of the Imperium's assassins. He used it. He killed the other eleven, installed a
cabinet that did as it was told, and ran the human species for a century before another
Space Marine killed him — and the aftermath was not restoration but anarchy. The
Officio Assassinorum of [[IV.T1.Ch3]] is a standing member of the body it is capable of
destroying in an afternoon, and it has demonstrated this once.

**The Reign of Blood, M36.** **Goge Vandire**, Master of the Administratum, **led a coup
d'état and became the Ecclesiarch of the Adeptus Ministorum, holding a dual role**. During
his Reign of Blood **the High Lords were powerless to act**. When Fabricator-General
**Gastaph Hediatrix** demanded that the High Lords account for themselves and execute
Vandire, **Goge dissolved the High Lords and declared the Space Marines and Mechanicus as
traitors**. Before long Vandire was killed and the High Lords returned to
power.[^lex-senatorum-imperialis]

A single man held two of the nine sacrosanct seats at once — the tax office and the church
— and that combination was sufficient to rule the Imperium and to declare its army and its
industry traitorous. The Council's response to being asked to act was to be dissolved by
the man they were asked to act against. [[III.T4.Ch19]] carries the Age of Apostasy in
full; what belongs here is the constitutional finding, which is that nothing in the
Senatorum's design prevents one office-holder from holding two seats, and nothing in it
compels the other ten to intervene.

Between those crises the Council does govern, and the surviving examples are instructive
about what it governs. It **declared the founding of a new Space Marine Chapter known as
the Astral Claws** in 598.M35, charged with guarding the space lanes around the
Maelstrom.[^lex-senatorum-imperialis] It **issued an edict to ratify the creation of the
Sons of Medusa** in 011.M37.[^lex-senatorum-imperialis] And in 0266999.M41 it **issued the
Orphean Decree, dissolving the Orpheus Sector and charging the Minotaurs with the
Exterminatus of its worlds**.[^lex-senatorum-imperialis]

Three recorded acts of government: two Chapter foundings and the extermination of a
sector. The Senatorum creates armies and it deletes populations, and the sources preserve
no example of it doing anything else.

## The Regent, the Purge and the Hexarchy {#hexarchy}

In the closing days of M41 the arrangement described above met the one thing it had no
procedure for: a Primarch came back.

**The reborn Primarch Roboute Guilliman returned to Terra and again declared himself Lord
Commander of the Imperium.** He **pledged to appropriately deal with the High Lords and
promised to reorganise and rearm the Imperium to face the coming threats.** Soon after
taking power, **five of the High Lords were removed and replaced by Guilliman**, and he
oversaw a general purge of Imperial bureaucracy known as the **Primarch's
Scourge**.[^lex-senatorum-imperialis]

Five of twelve, replaced by one man, without any process the sources describe. Set that
against the previous Section and the symmetry is uncomfortable: Vangorich removed eleven
High Lords and is recorded as mad; Vandire dissolved the Council and is recorded as a
tyrant; Guilliman removed five and is recorded as a reformer. The distinction the sources
draw is about the man, not the mechanism, because there is no mechanism. The office of
High Lord has no defence against whoever is strong enough to take it.

What the removed men did next is the story of the **Hexarchy**, and it is the sharpest
illustration in this Chapter of how Imperial power actually changes hands.

The Hexarchy **was a group composed of both current and deposed High Lords who refused to
accept the reforms Lord Commander Roboute Guilliman imposed upon the
Imperium**.[^lex-hexarchy] They were led by **Irthu Haemotalion**, the former Master of the
Administratum whom Guilliman had dismissed, and included the deposed **Baldo Slyst** along
with the sitting High Lords **Aveliza Drachmar**, **Mar Av Ashariel**, **Merelda Pereth**
and **Fadix**.[^lex-hexarchy]

**Using their vast resources, the Hexarchy attempted to forcibly take control of Terra
while Guilliman was away conducting the Indomitus Crusade.** Once Terra was theirs they
would have command of the Imperium and could undo Guilliman's changes, **ensuring Imperium
Eterna was restored**. They **even dared to use the Minotaurs Space Marine Chapter, which
arrived on Terra in its full might to bolster the Hexarchy**.[^lex-hexarchy]

The Indomitus Crusade of [[III.T5.Ch25]] took the Imperium's saviour off Terra for decades.
The Hexarchy's plan was to take the capital while he was busy, and it very nearly
worked — they had a Space Marine Chapter on the ground, the same Minotaurs the Council had
previously used to exterminate a sector.

It failed for one reason. **Their plot ended in failure after it was revealed that
Fadix's loyalty to the Hexarchy's cause had been a ruse from the start. He was the Grand
Master of Assassins, and after a signal was given, each of the Hexarchy's members were
killed by Fadix's well-placed assassins. The Minotaurs had been used to bring the coup
members into the open.**[^lex-hexarchy]

The Grand Master of Assassins saved the Imperium's government by joining a conspiracy
against it and then killing everyone in the room. Four hundred years and eight thousand
years earlier, the holder of the same office had destroyed that government by exactly the
same method. The seat has now been used once to decapitate the Senatorum and once to
preserve it, and nothing in the Imperium's constitution distinguishes the two occasions
except the outcome. [[IV.T1.Ch3]] describes the instrument; this is what it is for.

!!! disputed "Recorded conflict — who-rules-the-imperium"

    The position of High Lord **was the most powerful in the Imperium** — until the
    reemergence of Roboute Guilliman, **who declared himself Imperial Regent, or the
    Emperor's Living Voice**.[^lex-senatorum-imperialis] Yet **even subsequent to his
    return, the High Lords retain their position and task of ruling the Imperium in the
    Emperor's name**.[^lex-senatorum-imperialis]

    The article flags its own account here. Whether the Senatorum now advises a Regent who
    governs, or governs under a Regent who commands the armies, is not settled by the
    sources: the claim that Guilliman supersedes them and the claim that they continue to
    rule are separately attested and stated side by side. The codex records both.

That ambiguity is not a defect in the record so much as a description of the situation.
Guilliman holds two titles — Lord Commander of the Imperium and Imperial Regent — and is
almost never on Terra, having spent the Era Indomitus at the front. The twelve are on
Terra, permanently, running the machine. Whichever reading is correct on paper, the
practical answer is that the Imperium is governed by whoever is in the Great Chamber when a
decision is needed, and for most of the last century that has not been the Regent.
