# Administratum, Arbites & Rogue Traders

## Tithes, Scribes and Vellum {#administratum}

The Imperium's largest institution is a filing system, and understanding it explains more
about how a galactic empire actually functions than any account of its armies.

The **Adeptus Administratum**, also known as the **Scriptists** and the **Enumerators**, is
the administrative and bureaucratic division of the **Adeptus Terra**, **consisting of
untold billions of clerks, scribes and administrative staff constantly working to manage
the Imperium at every level, from assembling war fleets to levying
taxes**.[^lex-adeptus-administratum] It is the largest of the departments comprising the
Adeptus Terra — **the mere administration of the Imperial Palace is estimated to involve
over ten billion adepts**.[^lex-adeptus-administratum]

Ten billion people to run one building. That figure is not a description of a palace; it is
a description of what it costs to administer a million worlds from a single address, and
the Palace is merely where the paperwork lands. The Administratum **is so synonymous with
the Adeptus Terra that the two are referred to interchangeably in some places of the
Imperium**.[^lex-adeptus-administratum] For most Imperial citizens, the state and its
clerks are the same thing, because the clerks are the only part of the state they will ever
meet.

What those billions do is enumerated plainly. They are **constantly carrying out
population censuses, working out tithes, recording and archiving information, and the
million other things that are necessary for the running of the
Imperium**.[^lex-adeptus-administratum]

And then the sources say something that no functioning bureaucracy would admit about
itself. **Such is its immense size and complex bureaucracy that whole Administratum
departments have been lost and forgotten. So dogmatically bureaucratic, whole departments
of adepts continue to diligently but purposelessly carry out the role their department was
founded for, even after the department has become long
obsolete.**[^lex-adeptus-administratum]

Departments that the Administratum has mislaid, still staffed, still working. Nobody
remembers commissioning them and nobody has told them to stop, so they file. That is the
single most characteristic fact about the Imperium in this Volume: an institution large
enough to lose parts of itself, and rigid enough that the lost parts keep running. It is
the same failure [[III.T5.Ch26]] records at Segmentum scale, where whole systems went
silent and nobody noticed because the wheels of Imperial bureaucracy turn slowly.

The organisation's standing is stated with equal candour. **Of all the Imperium's
divisions, the Administratum is regarded as the least prestigious, but its high ranking
members are among the most powerful men in the Imperium. Despite its massive inefficiency,
the Administratum is so integral to the running of the Imperium that without it the whole
Imperium would grind to a halt. As a result, the Master of the Administratum is the most
powerful member of the High Lords of Terra.**[^lex-adeptus-administratum]

The least respected department in the Imperium supplies its single most powerful official.
That is why [[IV.T1.Ch1]] records two of the four attested coups being launched from this
office: Goge Vandire was Master of the Administratum before he took the Ecclesiarchy as
well, and Irthu Haemotalion was a dismissed Master of the Administratum when he assembled
the Hexarchy. Whoever controls the tithe records controls what every world owes and what
every army receives.

Careers are inherited. **Many Administratum positions are hereditary; parents pass on their
ranks and positions to their children, who continue the work that had occupied the lives of
their ancestors.** Typical adepts **labour within gargantuan vaults, sitting at
logic-engines, and can be considered learned by Imperial
standards**.[^lex-adeptus-administratum] Rank is legible at a glance: like other
non-combatant servants of the Adeptus Terra they wear **a monastic style of dress, their
habits varying in colour according to rank and area of responsibility** — **the basic
colour for the lowliest scribe is black; higher-ranking members have increasingly lighter
shades of grey, and eventually pure white**.[^lex-adeptus-administratum]

A hereditary clerical caste in monastic habits, graded from black to white. The Imperium
did not design a civil service and then make it religious; the two were never distinct.

The medium those clerks work in is the detail worth keeping. **Despite holding sway over
untold trillions and managing a galactic bureaucracy, the Administratum's primary method of
record keeping and distribution of information is vellum.** Parchment **appeals to the
dogmatic values and traditionalism of the Administratum**, and is **considered a far more
secure and a cheaper method of data collection than electronic counterparts such as
data-slates or cogitators while being far more durable than standard
paper**.[^lex-adeptus-administratum]

The consequences are industrial. **Enormous amounts of vellum are produced from the hides
of porcine bred on agri-worlds or artificially grown in bio-vats. Vellum is a prized
resource in the Imperium, so much so that inter-agency wars have been fought over it and
wealthy familial dynasties solely dedicated to the creation of parchment exist.** It is
said to be **the 7th largest import to Terra in sheer weight** because of the demands of
the bureaucracy — and **the Administratum itself seems aware of the inefficiency of written
record over more modern methods, but nonetheless does not
change**.[^lex-adeptus-administratum]

Departments of the Imperium have gone to war with each other over animal skin. Agricultural
worlds are given over to breeding livestock so that Terra can be sent their hides, and
those hides are the seventh heaviest thing shipped to the homeworld of the species. Set
that against [[III.T2.Ch7]], where the Mechanicus cannot build a machine it has no template
for: this is the same civilisation refusing a technology it *does* have, on grounds of
tradition, and knowing it is doing so. The Administratum's inefficiency is not ignorance.
It is policy.

The divisions are innumerable and mostly, in the sources' own words, **petty and of little
interest**.[^lex-adeptus-administratum] Six are worth naming. The **Departmento Munitorum**
oversees the logistics and management of the Imperial Guard — the machinery of
[[IV.T5.Ch16]]. The **Departmento Exacta** collects the Imperial tithe. The **Estate
Imperium** keeps records. The **Departmento Colonia** handles colonisation. The **Logis
Strategos** is an intelligence and threat-analysis agency. And the **Historical Revision
Unit** **revises historical records that may be 'subversive' to Imperial
rule**.[^lex-adeptus-administratum]

A standing department for editing the past, listed among the filing offices without comment
and without any indication that anyone considers it remarkable.

One of those divisions is not petty at all. The Administratum's list includes **the Officio
Assassinorum**,[^lex-adeptus-administratum] and the Officio's own account confirms it: it
**is a subdivision of the Administratum responsible for the recruitment, training, and
deployment of elite assassins**, whose head **is always a member of the Senatorum
Imperialis**.[^lex-officio-assassinorum]

Both things are true at once, and the combination is the most striking line in the
Imperium's organisation chart. The agency that kills on the state's behalf is filed as a
department of the tax office, and its director sits among the twelve who rule. [[IV.T1.Ch1]]
records what happened the one time a holder of that seat decided to use it.

## The Lex Imperialis and the Book of Judgement {#lex}

Above every planetary legal code sits one body of law, and its defining property is that
no living person has read it.

The **Lex Imperialis**, also called the **Dictates Imperialis** or simply the **book of
law**, is the legal code of the Imperium. **Claimed to be the word of the Emperor himself,
it is a vast body of laws and codes compiled over ten thousand years that Judges of the
Adeptus Arbites spend their entire lives studying and compiling in the Book of
Judgement.**[^lex-lex-imperialis] Though **each world of the Imperium has its own unique
set of rules, the Lex Imperialis is the definitive and absolute word, ruling above all
others**, and it is **ruthlessly enforced by the Adeptus
Arbites**.[^lex-lex-imperialis]

Its core is memorised. **The principal laws of the Lex Imperialis are known as the
Injuncta Cardinal. All Arbitrators can recite it by heart and it is often found emblazoned
across their Precinct Fortresses.**[^lex-lex-imperialis]

The rest is not memorable, and the sources are exact about why. **While the Dictates
Imperialis are extensive, the huge volume of prior cases and sometimes contradictory
rulings can make it difficult to determine the correct decision. In especially complex
cases, it may take centuries to reach an outcome. A Judge may spend his entire life
deliberating, scrutinising, and trying to fathom out the issues, only to pass his work on,
unfinished, for others to continue. Millennia later, though the accused are long dead, a
ruling is finally made and justice must be meted out upon the distant descendants and those
obscurely associated with the original transgressor.**[^lex-lex-imperialis]

That is the Imperium's justice system described by its own record. A case outlives the
accused, the verdict outlives the judge, and the sentence falls on people who were not born
when the offence occurred — not as an abuse but as the intended operation of the law. The
Administratum's lost departments in the previous Section are the same pathology in a
different office: a machine too large to finish a task, still executing it.

An excerpt survives, and its citation line is more revealing than its text. Article
**MMMDCCCLXX** provides that **any and all criminal acts of violence, detention, or
depredation committed for private ends by the officers, crew, or passengers of a private
ship that are directed against another ship, or against persons or property on board a
ship, shall be punishable by death** — attributed to the *Lex Imperialis*, **30,987th
printing, Segmentum Obscuras Volume, Calixis Edition**.[^lex-lex-imperialis]

Nearly thirty-one thousand printings, split by Segmentum and then by sector. The absolute
and definitive word of the Emperor exists in regional editions, and article numbers run to
four Roman digits. Nobody is reading this book. They are consulting the volume they were
issued.

Two provisions matter enough to state plainly, because both are enforced everywhere else in
this codex. **All mutants are considered illegal by the Lex Imperialis** — though
**miscellaneous mutants amongst scavvy populations and beyond remain rife throughout the
vast and squalid Imperium**.[^lex-lex-imperialis] And **it is required by Imperial law that
all psykers must undergo the Adeptus Astra Telepathica's testing and sanctioning process**;
those who **do not submit themselves to be collected by authorities and retrieved by the
League of Black Ships, or otherwise evade this process, are labelled rogue psykers and
considered illegal fugitives**.[^lex-lex-imperialis]

The Black Ships of [[I.T2.Ch9]] are therefore not an extra-legal apparatus. They are the
enforcement arm of a statute, and evading them is a named offence. The abhumans of
[[IV.T2.Ch7]] exist in the gap between the first provision and its second clause: illegal
by law, numerous in fact, and tolerated where they are useful.

## What the Arbites Actually Police {#arbites}

The Adeptus Arbites are usually described as the Imperium's police. The sources describe
something narrower and considerably stranger.

The **Adeptus Arbites** are **the police force of the Adeptus Terra, devoted to enforcing
Imperial law throughout the entire Imperium**. **Utterly dedicated and without mercy, the
Arbites are feared throughout the galaxy, for they are the agents of a harsh law, where
failure and incompetence are crimes, and the only punishment is death.** They are
**empowered to act as judge, jury and executioner** — **citizens have no rights, and only
members of the Priesthood of Terra or the Inquisition could claim anything so elaborate as
a trial**.[^lex-adeptus-arbites]

Failure is a crime and the sentence is death. A trial is a privilege of the clergy and the
Inquisition. Everything else in this Section follows from those two sentences.

Their jurisdiction is deliberately limited. **Each of the Imperium's worlds has its own
government, laws, and its own local police forces to enforce those laws. The Arbites
concern themselves only with the enforcement of the broader laws to which the entire
Imperium is subject; the common duty of the Arbites is overseeing that the Governor of each
world is regularly paying his planet's tithe.**[^lex-adeptus-arbites]

The galaxy's most feared law enforcement agency is, at bottom, a tax inspectorate. The
harsh law it agents is the law that the tithe must arrive.

What it does about non-payment is spelled out. **In the event of a rogue Governor or a
major revolt which threatens Imperial rule, the Arbites will brutally intervene to restore
order**, **killing or incarcerating entire noble bloodlines that were responsible for the
events as well, if necessary**. Afterwards they **hold the power to place a specific tithe
sanction upon a planet as an extra punitive
measure**.[^lex-adeptus-arbites]

The punishment for a world whose rulers rebelled is a higher tax on the survivors.

And then the provision that says most about the Imperium's actual values. **In other
circumstances, a planetary coup headed up by someone who will fulfil their duties to the
Imperium will merely be observed, as a successful revolt by such a one just means that a
weak ruler has been replaced.**[^lex-adeptus-arbites]

Overthrowing an Imperial Governor is not a crime if the tithe keeps coming. The Arbites
will watch it happen and file a report. Legitimacy in the Imperium is not conferred by law
or by descent; it is conferred by remittance, and the police of the Adeptus Terra are the
auditors who confirm it.

The list of what they ignore is the most quotable passage in the article, and it should be
read in full. **The Arbites do not trouble themselves with murder, theft, abuse, or slavery
unless those things are majorly affecting production quotas, system or subsector economies,
or involve non-violent interactions with xenos.** Instead they monitor **the number of
people being sent to serve in the Astra Militarum; how efficiently unsanctioned psykers and
mutants are being rounded up; whether those who foment rebellion are being shut down before
they can spread their message; and if dangerous narcotics are taking hold of the population
of a world**.[^lex-adeptus-arbites]

Murder, theft and slavery are not offences the Imperium's police force investigates.
Conscription shortfalls, psyker concealment, sedition and narcotics are. Each of the second
four is a threat to output or to control; none of the first four is. That is a police force
built to protect the state from its population rather than the population from anything,
and the sources present it without editorial.

Their reach extends past the surface. **Void ships belonging to merchants, and even Rogue
Traders, can be boarded by Arbites with no notice — sometimes just to instil fear into
their hearts, but most times to genuinely search for forbidden
cargo.**[^lex-adeptus-arbites] Their presence is resented exactly as one would expect: **as
a result of their constant oversight, the presence of Arbites is often a source of
discontent for Governors, but something they must nonetheless
tolerate**.[^lex-adeptus-arbites]

The Arbites' own history follows the pattern of every other institution in this Title. They
**date from at least the Great Crusade**, when compliant worlds had **Lord Marshal's Offices
installed to deal with 'non-local' matters**, and during the Horus Heresy the Grand Provost
Marshal sat on the **War Council**.[^lex-adeptus-arbites] During **the Age of Apostasy the
Adeptus Arbites fell under the control of Goge Vandire**.[^lex-adeptus-arbites] After **the
Blackness**, with Chaos cults activating across Terra and **billions more of starving and
maddened citizens** rioting, **most of the Arbites stations were overrun as only the
Imperial Palace complex remained secure**; order returned only with the Battle of Lion's
Gate and Guilliman's arrival, and the Arbites then **aided Guilliman in his purge of
Imperial bureaucracy that became known as the Primarch's
Scourge**.[^lex-adeptus-arbites]

The force that polices the Imperium has been captured once by a tyrant, overrun once on
Terra itself, and used once as the instrument of a Primarch's purge of the government it
belongs to. [[IV.T1.Ch1]] records that purge from the Senatorum's side. This is the same
event seen from the hands that carried it out.

## Warrants of Trade {#rogue-traders}

The last institution in this Title is the exception the Imperium built into itself: a
private individual licensed to ignore most of what the previous three Sections describe.

Rogue Traders date **back to the very birth of the Imperium during the time of the Great
Crusade**.[^lex-rogue-trader] Their defining possession is a document. **The most valuable
possession of a Rogue Trader family is a Warrant of Trade, an ancient legal document that
describes the accepted limits of its operations. These charters are hereditary, thus
creating a Rogue Trader dynasty**, and the dynasties **are granted a personal coat of arms
identifying them amongst the Imperial elite**.[^lex-rogue-trader]

What the warrant buys is the thing the Imperium otherwise forbids absolutely. Rogue Traders
**are empowered with the authority to travel freely within the Imperium and beyond**, which
**allows them to interact with cultures for which contact is normally forbidden, be they
non-Imperial human worlds or xenos-controlled planets** — and they **are granted the
permission and freedom to deal with these cultures as they see fit, so long as it is in the
interests of the Imperium**.[^lex-rogue-trader]

An empire whose law makes contact with aliens a capital matter issues hereditary licences to
trade with them. The qualifier — *so long as it is in the interests of the Imperium* — is
doing enormous work, and nobody is identified as judging it. [[VI.T7.Ch21]] describes xenos
the Imperium deals with in practice while denouncing them in doctrine; the Warrant of Trade
is the legal instrument that makes the contradiction survivable.

The claiming formula is preserved, and it is worth reading as the piece of theatre it is.
Upon stepping onto a non-Imperial world a Rogue Trader may claim it by saying: **"I claim
this world for the Emperor of Humanity and his Imperium. I bring justice and truth for the
loyal. Punishment and death for the guilty."**[^lex-rogue-trader]

The people so addressed have generally never heard of the Emperor. They are, from the moment
the sentence ends, subject to a law of which one clause is that failure is a capital crime.

The holders are not a class so much as a licensing category. Rogue Traders are **highly
exceptional individuals who are driven to success even though these exceptional people
often have extreme character quirks themselves; some destroy entire worlds for the
slightest reason, or include alien warriors and mutants among their
entourage**.[^lex-rogue-trader] **Some are highly pious while others are no more than
legitimised pirates** — one, **Jan van Yastobaal**, **became little more than a desperado
plundering whatever world he came upon**.[^lex-rogue-trader]

Aliens and mutants in the entourage. Both are illegal under the Lex Imperialis of the
Section above; both are tolerated aboard a ship carrying the right piece of parchment.

Their means vary as widely as their characters. New Rogue Traders come from **the Imperial
Guard, the Imperial Navy, the Merchant Fleets, the Administratum or even the
Inquisition**. **Some are relatively poor, possessing a single ancient and dilapidated
ship. Others are incredibly wealthy and powerful and have whole fleets and entire private
armies at their disposal. Certain favoured individuals may even call upon detachments of
Space Marines, having entered pacts with individual Chapters.** Some **operate as
mercenaries, renting the service of their ship(s)**.[^lex-rogue-trader]

A private citizen who can call on Space Marines by private arrangement. The Chapters of
[[IV.T4.Ch15]] answer to nobody below the High Lords, and a warrant-holder with the right
relationship can borrow them.

Take the four institutions of this Title together and the shape of Imperial governance is
visible. Twelve unelected office-holders interpret the will of a silent god. Billions of
hereditary clerks record what is owed, on parchment, in departments some of which have been
forgotten. A police force enforces one law — that the tithe arrives — and ignores murder
unless it affects output. And a handful of licensed private operators are exempted from all
of it, provided they say they are acting in the Imperium's interest. Nothing in that
arrangement is designed to govern well. All of it is designed to keep the tithe moving, and
it has done so for ten thousand years.
