# Officio Assassinorum (7 Clades)

## A Department That Requires a Two-Thirds Majority {#authority}

The Imperium maintains a bureau for killing people, and the surprising thing about it is
how carefully its use is rationed.

The **Officio Assassinorum** — the Office of Assassins — **is a subdivision of the
Administratum responsible for the recruitment, training, and deployment of elite
assassins**.[^lex-officio-assassinorum] Its head is the **Grand Master of Assassins**, and
**due to the power of his organisation, and by tradition, he is always a member of the
Senatorum Imperialis**.[^lex-officio-assassinorum]

That single sentence settles the organisational question [[IV.T1.Ch2]] raises: the Officio
is filed under the tax office *and* its director sits among the twelve. Both, at once.

The constraint on him is the most interesting provision in this Title. **Although the Grand
Master oversees the organisation, they may only deploy assassins with the authority of the
Senatorum, requiring a two-thirds majority of the High Lords to authorise the deployment of
a single assassin.**[^lex-officio-assassinorum]

Eight votes out of twelve, for one assassin. Weigh that against everything else this codex
has recorded the Senatorum doing. [[IV.T1.Ch1]] shows the same body founding Space Marine
Chapters and ordering the Exterminatus of an entire sector — the killing of every human
being on every world of the Orpheus Sector — and nothing in the sources suggests either
required a special majority. The Imperium exterminates populations by ordinary
resolution and kills individuals by supermajority.

The reason is visible in the next provision. **The location of the Officio's headquarters
on Terra is secret, and the Master of Assassins is never seen by the Adeptus Terra, though
he is rumoured to have access to the Emperor himself. Typically, the Grand Master's loyalty
to the Emperor must be beyond doubt, as he holds power that could feasibly topple the
Imperium itself.**[^lex-officio-assassinorum]

*Power that could feasibly topple the Imperium.* The two-thirds rule is not a safeguard for
the targets. It is a safeguard against the Grand Master, written by people who had watched
one of them use the office to kill every other High Lord and rule for a century. The
Beheading of [[IV.T1.Ch1]] is the reason this clause exists, and the Hexarchy is the
occasion on which the same office saved the government instead. Nothing in the rule
distinguishes those two uses; it only requires eight signatures before an assassin leaves
Terra.

Misuse is capital. **Under Imperial and Mechanicus law, the improper activation of a member
of the Officio Assassinorum is considered treason of the highest
order.**[^lex-officio-assassinorum]

Secrecy is maintained at two levels, and the sources are precise about which parts of the
Imperium know. **The agency is almost as secretive as the Inquisition itself, and it is not
surprising to see that the two organisations work closely with one
another.**[^lex-officio-assassinorum] Its existence **is known by some facets of the
Imperium** — **veteran mercenaries, certain soldiers of the Imperial Guard, members of the
Adeptus Mechanicus, and Planetary Governors or
nobility**.[^lex-officio-assassinorum]

Everyone else is left with a story. **To the average Imperial citizen and Imperial Guard
grunt, the Officio Assassinorum is little more than a dark fairytale, a bedtime story used
to scare wayward children into complying. Most common folk or guardsmen grunts who hear
whispers of an "Assassin" tend to simply write it off as a fanciful rumour about
boogeymen.**[^lex-officio-assassinorum]

A real institution that most of the species files as folklore, and which uses that filing as
cover. The bedtime story and the operational security are the same thing: a citizen who
believes assassins are imaginary will not recognise one, and will not be believed if they
do.

## Recruitment, and the One in Ten {#training}

Where the assassins come from is the most quietly appalling passage in this Title, and it
connects the Officio to an institution that appears benign.

**Assassins are recruited at a very young age, from the orphans of Imperial servants raised
in the Schola Progenium and the populations of feral
worlds.**[^lex-officio-assassinorum]

The Schola Progenium of [[IV.T2.Ch6]] takes in the orphaned children of Imperial servants
killed in service and raises them for the state. It produces commissars, storm troopers and
adepts, and it is generally presented as the Imperium keeping faith with its dead. It also
supplies the assassin temples. The children in question have no parents, no world of their
own, and no capacity to decline.

Selection begins before arrival. **Prospective candidates are tested mercilessly even as
they are shipped to Terra, pitted against hazardous environments as well as each other,
with perhaps one in ten surviving the journey.**[^lex-officio-assassinorum]

Nine in ten die on the way, and the deaths are the test. That is not an accident of harsh
transport; the candidates are set against *each other* deliberately, and the ship is part of
the assessment. Whatever the Officio is measuring, it is willing to spend ninety per cent of
its intake to measure it.

The survivors are then divided. **The survivors are divided among the six (or potentially
seven; the current status of the Adamus temple is unknown) temples, and then undergo ten
years of intensive training under the Lord Assassins.** From then on **they live in and
operate out of one of the Assassin temples said to be located somewhere on Terra**, where
they are **harshly tested and trained, their senses and resolve heightened with implants**.
**Even after the initial training, the life of an agent of the Officio remains hard and
unforgiving.**[^lex-officio-assassinorum]

The organisation is built on temples, which the sources also call **clades**. **The primary
division of the Officio is into temples (also called clades), each of which is led by a
small group of Masters — often termed Lord Assassins or Grand Masters — and specialises in
a unique brand of murder.** Those Masters **rarely work directly but instead are
responsible for planning, organising, research and watching over the younger assassins**,
while **the greater number of the assassins are unranked, though some can be more
experienced than others**.[^lex-officio-assassinorum]

*A unique brand of murder.* The five Sections of this Chapter are the six extant brands and
the ones that no longer exist. The training is also exportable: **some inquisitors are
known to organise Officio Assassinorum training for Death Cultists that serve in their
retinue, which transforms these already highly efficient killers into much more deadly and
honed executioners.**[^lex-officio-assassinorum] The Inquisition of [[IV.T7.Ch23]] cannot
requisition an assassin without eight votes, so it has its own people trained instead.

## Vindicare and Eversor — Patience and Its Opposite {#vindicare-eversor}

Two clades define the range. One waits; one detonates.

The **Vindicare Temple** deploys elite snipers, specialising in **vengeance and revenge
killings** with a long-range weapon called the **Exitus Rifle**.[^lex-vindicare-temple]
**Many rebellions and cults have been ended with one, perfect shot from a Vindicare's
rifle** — and the figure that conveys the method is this: **Vindicare Assassins have been
known to wait in a position for as long as six years before taking a
shot.**[^lex-vindicare-temple]

Six years in one place, for one round. The Imperium's answer to an insurrection is
sometimes a single person lying still for longer than the rebellion has existed.

The conditioning is designed to make that possible. The temple **teaches the assassin the
art of patience and the perfection of marksmanship**, and these **stoic assassins are
conditioned by their temple to be unflinchingly loyal to the Imperium, often being trained
to limit their vocabulary only to words that are useful for their profession, cutting off
all emotional attachments to other humans so that they will never hesitate to eliminate any
target**.[^lex-vindicare-temple]

Their vocabulary is deliberately reduced. The Imperium removes words from a person's mind so
that the concepts those words carry cannot interfere with a shot.

The surgery is extensive. **Their brains are augmented to regulate their breathing, they are
taught automated mantra-meditation that can loop through their minds to achieve focus,
neural augmetics are inserted into their occipital lobe to allow them to rule out obstacles
while searching for their target, and connection nodes are inserted beneath the skin of
their palms so that they can neurally bond with their Exitus
Rifle**.[^lex-vindicare-temple] In the field they wear a **Spy Mask** that scans for heat
and energy at long range, monitors enemy communications, and carries **concentrated food and
water cartridges for extended operations**, over a **Stealth Suit** combining **normal
synskin with chameleon chemicals**.[^lex-vindicare-temple]

Food and water built into the mask, because the assassin will not be moving for years.

Their careers are short and the temple's accounting of them is cold. **Many Vindicare
operatives die on their first mission, their names remembered and passed to a new initiate
if they completed a kill — forgotten if they have failed.**[^lex-vindicare-temple] A name is
a reward for success, recycled to the next candidate; failure erases it. Green operatives
are sent to battlefields on **journeyman** work against **mega-armoured Orks or bedraggled
cult leaders**, reporting their missions by subvocal journal, and **after five successful
missions** graduate to **Planetary Governors, Chaos Lords, T'au sept leaders, rogue
Mechanicus Archmagi, or other operational
lynchpins** — and stop having to file the journal.[^lex-vindicare-temple]

The **Eversor Temple** is the opposite instrument. Where other temples produce **assassins
who specialise in stealth and subtlety, Eversor assassins are berserk killing machines,
trained to wipe out entire command hierarchies or heretic families, in spectacular
fashion**, and the temple sits **in one of the magnetic poles of
Terra**.[^lex-eversor-temple]

*Or heretic families.* The Vindicare removes one person. The Eversor is dispatched against
households.

They are **drug crazed, mind-wiped, killing monsters, enhanced by bio-engineering and
experimental surgical procedures**, whose **state of mind is amplified to turn a mere
dislike for the enemy into raging hatred, or the wish to serve the Imperium into suicidal
determination**.[^lex-eversor-temple] The implants include **adrenal ducts, which speed up
the brain's higher functions**, let the assassin **make complex calculations and tactical
decisions in seconds**, and can **feed the brain, allowing the Eversor to work briefly in a
hard vacuum**; a **secondary heart** is added as a fail-safe.[^lex-eversor-temple]

The chemistry has a terminal property the temple has turned into a weapon. The assassins
**possess a hyperactive immune system to counteract the toxins in the drugs they use** —
but **should an Eversor's brain or hearts cease to function, his advanced immune system will
no longer be able to keep them in check. The chemical concoctions inside him will rapidly
accelerate, causing him to violently explode.** They are **well aware of this fact** and
**often use it as a last-ditch effort to eliminate their target**, the **bio-meltdown**
detonating **with the force of a plasma bomb**.[^lex-eversor-temple]

And then the detail that makes the Eversor the bleakest thing in this Chapter. **The large
amount of drugs and modification used to make the Eversor into an unstoppable killing
machine are highly addictive and without them the assassin will surely die. This means that
the assassins are completely addicted to death itself and the only way they can survive is
by being placed in cryo-suspension**.[^lex-eversor-temple]

An Eversor cannot be alive and idle. Between missions he is frozen, because consciousness
without a target kills him.

So **every Eversor is stored within cryo-crypts on secret ships scattered across the
Imperium**, vessels that **remain at a constant state of alert and await the orders from the
High Lords of Terra**.[^lex-eversor-temple] When deployed, the assassin is **transferred
from the cryo-crypt into a special drop pod** where **neuro-links feed the details of the
mission to the assassin while he remains in cryo-suspension**; the pod is launched at the
planet, **remote links activate and begin to reanimate the Eversor while fine-tuning his
body for the task at hand**, and **by the time the drop pod lands the assassin is fully
awake and ready**.[^lex-eversor-temple]

He is briefed while frozen, thawed in flight, and lands awake. **Due to the nature of
cryo-suspension the life of the Eversor is prolonged**, and **the assassin may be completely
unaware of how much time has passed, which can be years or even centuries. To the assassin,
it is as if only moments have passed between the last mission and the current
one.**[^lex-eversor-temple]

A man who has been alive for centuries and conscious for perhaps a few weeks of them.

There is an off switch, and a named case. Should it be warranted, the Officio **can have
them placed in permastasis, if they are no longer deemed suitable to carry out missions** —
done to the Eversor **Tormentius** for **displaying erratic extremes of psychopathic
behavior**. It can be revoked: Tormentius **was later revived and sent to ensure the
Imperium's efforts to reclaim Uphrateus did not fail**.[^lex-eversor-temple]

An assassin whose psychopathy was excessive *by the standards of the Eversor Temple* was
shelved indefinitely, and then taken off the shelf when a campaign was going badly.

## Culexus and Callidus — The Soulless and the Shapeless {#culexus-callidus}

The next two clades are defined not by a weapon but by what their operatives are.

**Culexus Assassins are the most sinister, feared, and hated of all Imperial
Assassins.**[^lex-culexus-temple] Psykers are their exclusive targets. They **are
null-entities in the Warp, and their unnatural lack of a presence inspires a sense of unease
even in non-psykers** — to the point that **some cultures developed folklore around the
Culexus agents as skull-faced assassins that can phase through solid walls**. **To psykers
their mere presence is terrifying, invoking panic**, and **to purely psychic entities like
daemons, they are invisible**.[^lex-culexus-temple]

They are the blanks of [[I.T2.Ch6]], weaponised. Culexus assassins **possess the Pariah
Gene, making them essentially "soulless," accounting for their lack of Warp-presence. This
gene manifests itself very rarely, in a single individual among a billion, or
more.**[^lex-culexus-temple] That rarity, **coupled with the inevitable losses during
training, makes the Culexus assassins the rarest of all Imperial
assassins**.[^lex-culexus-temple]

One in a billion, minus the nine in ten lost getting to Terra. And where the population is
insufficient, the temple manufactures it: it **is known to hunt down and recruit these
Pariahs but also to vat-grow them**. The consequence is recorded plainly. **Due to the
nature of being parentless, combined with the pariah gene making them seem soulless, these
Culexus struggle with social interaction. Whilst able to mimic predictive human behaviour of
a character they are playing as part of a mission, they may not actually feel
emotions.**[^lex-culexus-temple]

The Imperium grows people in vats to kill psykers, and the people it grows cannot form
attachments — partly from the gene, partly from having no parents. The record does not
distinguish the two causes and neither can anyone else.

Their temple's location is the strangest in the Officio: **said to be located on a planet
which orbits no sun and which is beyond the reach of the
Astronomican**.[^lex-culexus-temple] The beacon of [[IV.T9.Ch31]] is what makes Imperial
navigation possible; the Culexus keep their temple outside it, on a rogue world, where the
Imperium's own light does not reach.

The weapon is not fully understood by the people using it. The Culexus **slays his foe by
using a deadly psychic blast directed through an ancient device known as an Animus
Speculum**, and **it is not fully known how this device functions**, though **it is
speculated that the Warp energy separated from its targets by the null aura of the Culexus
is held in this device and then unleashed**. It **becomes more and more potent in the
vicinity of psykers**.[^lex-culexus-temple] He also carries an **Etherium**, inducing
**enormous distortion and confusion in all nearby enemies, psychic or not**, and
**psyk-out grenades**, devastating to psykers and inert against everyone else — using **a
substance that is said to be a by-product of the constant workings of the Golden Throne
itself**.[^lex-culexus-temple]

The Golden Throne of [[III.T3.Ch17]] produces a waste product, and the Imperium packs it into
grenades.

One episode records what the temple is worth to the Imperium's enemies. **The Eldar, whose
leaders are usually psychics, fear the Culexus greatly.** Farseer **Alladrios Kulcassian**
of **Alaitoc** ran **a centuries-long effort to locate and destroy this temple**, somehow
found it, and dispatched a cruiser — then **discovered to his amazement that the temple's
destruction would lead to the annihilation of Alaitoc. Choosing the lesser of two evils, he
recalled the cruiser home.**[^lex-culexus-temple]

A craftworld spent centuries finding the one place that could not be allowed to exist, and
then let it exist, because the Aeldari foresight of [[VI.T2.Ch4]] showed the alternative was
worse. The Culexus Temple survives because its most determined enemy calculated that killing
it would cost more.

The **Callidus Temple** specialises in **trickery, deception, deceit, infiltration and
impersonation**.[^lex-callidus-temple] **Their greatest asset is their ability to use the
shape-changing drug polymorphine**, which with training **allows Callidus agents to change
their very shape and appearance, allowing them to impersonate other persons, members of the
opposite sex, and even humanoid xenos like Orks and
Aeldari**.[^lex-callidus-temple] One agent, **Meh'Lindi**, **managed to successfully
impersonate a Genestealer Hybrid at least twice**, though **she required additional surgery
to complete the illusion**.[^lex-callidus-temple]

This makes them **experts at getting close to their targets, be they rebellious Imperial
Governors, corrupt Imperial high officials, heretic prophets, or alien leaders**, allowing
them to **manipulate the enemy's plans and eventually assassinate the main target at the
best possible moment** — and **once the target is eliminated, the Assassin may even replace
the victim by assuming his appearance**.[^lex-callidus-temple]

The Imperium does not merely kill officials. It sometimes continues them.

The drug's use is physical and unpleasant, and the sources dwell on it. Where a subject is
very different in build, **the Callidus must achieve a transitional state where they turn
genderless and featureless, more like a sculptor's model than a human, as their nose
retreats until it is two holes in their face while their cheeks flatten, hair goes away,
lips thin out and veins retreat before transformation is fully
complete**.[^lex-callidus-temple] Smaller doses make **certain body parts as soft and
pliable as gelatine to escape from a tight grasp**, and larger ones let the operative **worm
their way through pipes or tunnels like a reptile as their skulls deform and brains
flatten**.[^lex-callidus-temple] **Polymorphine works better with women, and therefore the
vast majority of the temple operatives are female.**[^lex-callidus-temple]

The mental cost is the part worth keeping. Callidus operatives **undergo
psycho-indoctrination, as during longer operations their assumed identities take over their
conscious minds and their true self merges temporarily into the black waters of their
subconscious. It is only when the time for a kill is close that a Callidus starts to
mentally identify themselves as a woman again, reverting slowly to their real identity
within their minds.**[^lex-callidus-temple]

The assassin forgets who she is for the duration, by design, and comes back to herself only
in order to kill. Both clades in this Section make a person into an instrument by removing
something — the Culexus a soul, the Callidus an identity — and neither removal is presented
as a cost.

One kill from this temple belongs to the Scouring recorded in [[III.T4.Ch18]]. The traitor Primarch
**Konrad Curze** **was assassinated by M'Shen, a Callidus Assassin. However, he allowed her
intrusion as he wanted to die, to show that his rebellion was
justified.**[^lex-callidus-temple] The Officio's most famous success was permitted by its
target.

## Vanus, Venenum and the Temples That Are Gone {#vanus-venenum}

Two clades remain, and they are the ones the Officio's own organisation chart marks
**classified**.[^lex-officio-assassinorum]

The **Vanus Temple** kills nobody directly. Its agents are **infocytes**, who use
**predictive engines in order to base their calculations on all available data as well as
prognostic simulations in order to determine the success rate of the
operations**.[^lex-vanus-temple] They are **essentially human computing engines that are far
different from mindless automata such as servitors**, their **skills unparalleled in matters
of strategy and tactics**, and **it has been said that they have never been known to make an
error in judgement — though some considered this
disinformation**.[^lex-vanus-temple]

A claim of infallibility, issued by the clade responsible for propaganda, about itself. The
sources note the obvious problem without resolving it, and the clade **is well known for the
creation and dissemination of propaganda, which is noted as being its greatest
strength**.[^lex-vanus-temple]

Their method is stated as a maxim by the infocyte **Fon Tariel**: **'the cleanest kill is
one that another performs in your stead with no knowledge of your
incitement.'**[^lex-vanus-temple] They **often assassinate their targets indirectly, using
their intelligence and knowledge to bring down targets**,[^lex-officio-assassinorum] and
**take part in operations through telepresent means conducted from their sanctum or from an
Officio Assassinorum sponsored starship, with them physically deployed in the field a rare
occurrence**.[^lex-vanus-temple] Their tools are **cogitator gauntlets that project
hololithic panes**, **swarms of organic-metal netfly automata that can tap into opti-cables**
and build a picture of a district, and **data phages and blackouts designed to subvert the
enemy**.[^lex-vanus-temple]

Then the line that explains why the chart marks them classified. **Ultimately, the Vanus
Clade watched everyone, with their data-stacks being filled with information on all the
other Clades, which was the means they maintained their position by way of
blackmail.**[^lex-vanus-temple]

The intelligence arm of the Imperium's assassins holds files on the Imperium's assassins,
and uses them. Some in the Officio believe **the operatives of this Clade were incapable of
following assassin instincts in the field, and this is the reason why the Officio
Assassinorum kept them at their scrying stations**[^lex-vanus-temple] — a comfortable
explanation for why the clade that blackmails the others is never sent anywhere dangerous.

The **Venenum Temple** is the smallest entry in the record and the most precisely defined.
It **trains and deploys its assassins on behalf of the Officio Assassinorum upon receiving
orders by the High Lords of Terra**, and is **presumably located, like most other assassin
temples, in a secret location on Terra**.[^lex-venenum-temple] Its members are **experts in
subtlety and poison**, wielding **toxins potent enough to quickly dispatch even the toughest
of prey**; the assassins were **selected from the finest alchemists that were capable of
creating exotic concoctions that cause death without leaving a metabolic
trace**.[^lex-venenum-temple] They carry **Toxin Injectors, Poison Globes, and poisoned
Hookfang blades**, and **were said to have had the greatest level of success during the
Horus Heresy**.[^lex-venenum-temple]

*Death without leaving a metabolic trace.* The other five clades produce corpses that are
recognisably the work of an assassin. The Venenum produce deaths that read as natural, and
the Imperium's best period for that was its civil war.

That leaves the seventh, and the ones that are gone. The **Adamus Temple** is **the oldest
of the formal Assassin orders, specialising in the decapitation
strike**[^lex-officio-assassinorum] — and its status is the reason this Chapter's count is
uncertain. The sources describe recruits divided among **the six (or potentially seven; the
current status of the Adamus temple is unknown)
temples**.[^lex-officio-assassinorum] The Imperium's own record cannot say whether one of
its assassin temples still exists.

Others definitely do not. **Over the millennia, there have been numerous
temples.**[^lex-officio-assassinorum] One is named. The **Maerorus Temple** **attempted to
create an assassin using illegal human/mutant/xenos hybrids capable of absorbing the
biomass of victims to rapidly evolve new bio-weapons. The temple is now
extinct.**[^lex-officio-assassinorum]

Human, mutant and xenos genetic material, combined, in an organisation belonging to a state
whose law makes both mutants and xenos contact capital matters — and the word the sources
use is *illegal*, meaning the Officio knew. [[VI.T4.Ch13]] describes the Tyranid capacity to
absorb biomass and evolve counters to what it eats; the Maerorus were attempting the same
trick on a human frame. The entry does not say whether the temple was destroyed from outside
or consumed itself.

Take the clades together and the design is clear enough. The Imperium cannot rely on any one
method, so it maintains six or seven parallel institutions that share nothing but a
recruitment pipeline: a marksman who waits six years, a berserker kept frozen because
consciousness would kill him, a vat-grown null who cannot feel, a shapeshifter who forgets
her own name, an analyst who kills by rumour, and a poisoner whose work is never identified
as murder. Every one of them was an orphan of the Emperor's servants, taken from
[[IV.T2.Ch6]] at an age too young to refuse, and nine in ten of their cohort died on the
journey to Terra. Deploying any one of them requires eight votes out of twelve — a
precaution the Imperium adopted not to protect the targets, but because the man who holds
the files once used them to take the throne.
