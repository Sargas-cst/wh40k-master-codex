# The Imperial Creed & the Cult Imperialis

## A Religion the Emperor Outlawed {#origins}

The Imperium worships a man who forbade worship. That is not an irony the sources leave to
be inferred — they state it outright, and it is the correct place to begin.

The **Imperial Cult**, called the **Cult Imperialis** in High Gothic, is **the cult based on
the worship of the Emperor of Mankind as Master, Defender, and Father of Mankind, developed
following his internment in the Golden Throne**. In the 41st Millennium it has **almost
unrivalled power and influence within the Imperium**; **heresy against it is punished
severely**; and it **is administered by the Ecclesiarchy**.[^lex-imperial-cult]

Then the correction. **By M41, the ordinary Imperial citizen believes that the Emperor has
always been venerated as an immortal and omnipotent god, but this is
inaccurate.**[^lex-imperial-cult]

At the beginning of the Great Crusade **there was no Ecclesiarchy; on the contrary, the
Emperor had deliberately outlawed organised religion in any form, declaring it the source of
much of the strife and ignorance that had prevented humanity from achieving its
potential**. **The idea of worship was anathema to the Emperor's vision of a secular empire
of man, ruled by reason and science.**[^lex-imperial-cult]

The official position had a name and a precise content. **The official Imperial doctrine was
that the Emperor was an extremely powerful being, the rightful ruler of all mankind, and the
perfect image of humanity, but no matter how supreme, still a human being. This doctrine —
which also denied the existence of Chaos — was called the "Imperial
Truth".**[^lex-imperial-cult]

Two clauses, and the second is the one that killed him. [[III.T3.Ch14]] records Horus falling
to a power the state had officially declared nonexistent, and [[III.T3.Ch15]] records Magnus
breaking the Webway project because sorcery was forbidden by the same doctrine. The Imperial
Truth was not merely wrong about Chaos. It was a policy of denying Chaos, held by an empire
that Chaos was actively recruiting, and the Heresy is what that policy cost.

The cult grew anyway, and it grew from below. **During the Great Crusade many ordinary
Imperial citizens found that the light of reason and truth brought by the Emperor was not
enough, and they took to worshipping him as a deity. The Emperor himself did not wish to be
considered a deity, but his deeds during the Great Crusade and the very fact of his
existence — an immortal man, and the most powerful psyker in the history of the galaxy — gave
rise to a cult.**[^lex-imperial-cult]

That is a fair summary of the problem the Emperor could not solve. He was, by the account of
[[III.T3.Ch11]], immortal and the most powerful psyker who has ever lived, and he asked to be
regarded as a man. The evidence was against him.

The founding text came from the worst possible author. The cult was **known as the *Lectitio
Divinitatus*, based on a tome written by the Primarch Lorgar, postulating that the Emperor
was in fact a divine being**.[^lex-imperial-cult] The Word Bearers' worship of the Emperor as
a god is recorded as the culmination of those early cults.[^lex-ecclesiarchy]

The Imperium's state religion was founded on a book by the Primarch who would go on to
convert his Legion to Chaos and open the Heresy. [[III.T3.Ch14]] carries that turn. The
Ecclesiarchy of the 41st Millennium rests on the theology of a traitor, and does not say so.

Its early figures are named. **The early central figure of the Imperial Creed was the
Remembrancer and later first Saint, Euphrati Keeler**, who with **the former Iterator Kyril
Sindermann** **oversaw the spread of the Cult as the Heresy
unfolded**.[^lex-imperial-cult] The first saint of the Imperium was a photographer attached
to the Crusade to document it, and the man beside her had been employed to teach the
Imperial Truth she was contradicting.

The suppression was real while it lasted. Despite the ban, the cults **continued on in
secret**,[^lex-ecclesiarchy] and their growth **did not go unopposed, and occasionally
resulted in open war between cultists and supporters of the Imperial Truth even as the Horus
Heresy raged**.[^lex-imperial-cult]

Imperial citizens fought each other over whether the Emperor was a god, during the civil war,
while the Emperor was still alive to be asked.

## The Five Tenets, and Everything Else {#tenets}

What the Imperium actually requires its citizens to believe is remarkably little, and the
looseness is deliberate.

**The Imperial Creed is highly flexible and is tailored by Missionaries to fit the native
culture, religion, and practices of whatever world it exists upon. As such, practices adhered
to on one world may be held as abhorrent on another. The Ministorum tolerates this vast range
of practices and beliefs, as it would be impossible to maintain a complete standardization of
the faith across the Imperium.**[^lex-imperial-cult]

An empire that executes people for heresy concedes that it cannot say precisely what heresy
is, because it cannot reach far enough to check. The Missionaria Galaxia of [[IV.T3.Ch10]] is
the machinery of that tailoring, and [[II.T3.Ch7]] records what it looks like in practice on
Fenris, where the missionaries did not have to convert anyone — they only had to supply a
name for a god the Fenrisians already had.

What is not negotiable is five lines. **The Ecclesiarchy does enforce basic key
tenets:**[^lex-imperial-cult]

- **The Emperor once walked among men, but He is, and always has been, a god.**
- **The Emperor is the one true god, regardless of what past faiths any human may have
  worshipped.**
- **To purge the heretic, beware the psyker and mutant, and abhor the alien.**
- **Every human being has a place within the Emperor's divine order.**
- **To unquestionably obey the authority of the Imperial government and one's
  superiors.**[^lex-imperial-cult]

Read them in order and the shape is clear. The first is a falsehood by the Imperium's own
record — the previous Section establishes that he did not claim divinity and outlawed the
claim. The second forecloses every prior faith. The third is the operative one for anybody
living: it converts three categories of person into targets, and [[IV.T2.Ch7]] records the
consequence for abhumans, whom the Administratum's own census holds to be no more prone to
mutation than anyone else. The fourth tells a hive worker in the undercity of [[IV.T2.Ch4]]
that their position is divinely intended. The fifth requires obedience without question to
the Governor who, per [[IV.T2.Ch5]], may treat them in any way he likes provided the tithe
arrives.

Those five are the whole of compulsory Imperial belief. Everything else — the rites, the
festivals, the local gods absorbed under Imperial names — is left to the world.

The Creed's stated principal tenets are summarised elsewhere in the article more bluntly
still: **the persecution of mutants, the abhorrence of xenos, and the worship of both the
Emperor and Imperial ideals**.[^lex-imperial-cult]

Two of the three are hatreds. The devout are also expected to travel: **the more devout
followers of the Creed will undertake The Grand Pilgrimage to Terra at least once in their
lives**.[^lex-imperial-cult]

Even the Adeptus Astartes are not uniform on the question. **While many Space Marine Chapters
follow their own cults, or continue to observe a form of the older Imperial Truth, some do
abide by the Imperial Cult and worship the Emperor as a god** — the **Black Templars**, **Fire
Angels**, **Fire Hawks**, **Red Hunters**, **Red Scorpions**, **Star Phantoms** and **White
Consuls** among them.[^lex-imperial-cult]

Ten thousand years after the ban, some of the Emperor's own Legions are still keeping the
Imperial Truth, and the Imperium tolerates it. The Chapters of [[IV.T4.Ch15]] have no seat on
the Senatorum, as [[IV.T1.Ch1]] records, and it may be that a warrior order permitted to
disbelieve the state religion is one the state prefers to keep out of the room.

## How a Cult Became the State {#ascent}

The Imperial Cult did not win a theological argument. It won a merger, and then a war.

After the Heresy, **the Emperor became an object of general veneration following his
internment within the Golden Throne**, and **over the following decades many individual
Imperial cults sprang up throughout the Imperium, with their central theme being the
redemption of humanity through the Emperor's self-sacrifice**. Then consolidation: **after a
few hundred years, a single cult known as the Temple of the Saviour Emperor was formed from
the unification of a number of smaller cults, which gradually absorbed the main body of
believers.**[^lex-imperial-cult]

Why that one won is recorded, and it has nothing to do with doctrine. The Temple **had the
advantages that it was based on Terra, and that its leader had been a successful and
respected officer of the Imperial Army who had fought at the Battle of Terra, defending the
Imperial Palace**. He **re-named himself as Fatidicus** and preached **to anyone who would
listen**; the faith **spread among the members of the Imperial Guard and Navy, but also to
lowly scribes and minor adepts of the Adeptus Terra**, who carried it to other planets. **When
Fatidicus died at the age of 120, the Temple had more than a billion followers on Terra and
untold numbers of the faithful throughout the Segmentum
Solar.**[^lex-ecclesiarchy]

A veteran of the Siege with the right address and access to the clerical class. The Imperium's
religion was distributed along the Administratum's own internal mail, by its own staff.

**In M32, this cult became the official religion of the Imperium, gaining the title of Adeptus
Ministorum. Remaining cults were persecuted and mostly destroyed**, while **the meaning of
"Imperial Truth" itself was also twisted, becoming simply another way to refer to the
burgeoning Imperial Cult as a whole.**[^lex-imperial-cult]

The name of the doctrine that denied the Emperor's divinity now denotes the doctrine that
asserts it. That is the single most complete act of historical revision in this codex, and
[[IV.T1.Ch2]] identifies the standing department that does this kind of work: the
Administratum's Historical Revision Unit, whose job is revising records that may be
subversive to Imperial rule.

The Ecclesiarch's seat followed the same century. [[IV.T1.Ch1]] records that in early M32 the
rise of the Creed meant the Ecclesiarch was granted a place among the High Lords, which soon
became permanent — so within a few hundred years of the Emperor's death, the religion he
banned held one of the nine sacrosanct seats of his government.

Then it overreached, and the correction is the whole of [[III.T4.Ch19]]. **The dominance of
the Temple of the Saviour Emperor was broken during the Age of Apostasy in M36, when Sebastian
Thor and his "Confederation of Light" reformed the Imperial Cult. After this point, the
Ecclesiarchy took the form it would keep until M41. The Temple was subsequently declared
heretical, and its remaining followers were hunted just like other unorthodox
cults.**[^lex-imperial-cult]

The church that had been the official religion for four thousand years was declared heretical
and its congregation hunted. Nothing in the sources suggests the doctrine changed
substantially; what changed was who held the office, after Goge Vandire held both the
Ecclesiarchy and the Administratum at once and used them to kill on a scale that produced a
reformer. And the settlement Thor imposed is the one that still governs: the **Decree
Passive**, **which banned the Ministorum from maintaining men under
arms**.[^lex-ecclesiarchy]

That single clause produced the Adepta Sororitas of [[IV.T3.Ch11]], and the reason is a
loophole in its wording that this codex will come to.

**Despite the continued dominance of Thor's version of the Imperial Cult over the next
millennia, minor Imperial cults would continue to rise and fall across the
Imperium.**[^lex-imperial-cult]

## Cardinals, Dioceses and the Creed Temporal {#structure}

The organisation that administers all of this is a territorial power, and its position
relative to the rest of the Imperium is unusual enough to state plainly.

**The Ecclesiarchy is not part of the Adeptus Terra, but a wholly separate
organisation.**[^lex-ecclesiarchy] Every other body in this Volume — the Administratum, the
Arbites, the Assassins, the Astra Telepathica — sits under the Adeptus Terra of
[[IV.T1.Ch2]]. The church does not. It answers to its own head, and that head sits on the
Council: **at its head is the Ecclesiarch, who, by tradition, is always one of the High Lords
of Terra**.[^lex-ecclesiarchy]

Beneath him the hierarchy is short and wide. **Below him are the Cardinals, of whom there are
several thousand. Below the Cardinals are the Ministorum Priests: Bishops, Deacons, Abbots,
Pontifices, Confessors, Missionaries and Preachers**, aided by **a host of subservient
functionaries: Logistoras, Quire Masters, Reliquindus**.[^lex-ecclesiarchy] The bulk of the
organisation is **the Frateris clergy, Preachers, Confessors and Cardinals, who see to the
spiritual needs of humanity**, and **few amongst the Ecclesiarchal priesthood are trained
warriors**.[^lex-ecclesiarchy]

Its ruling body is collegiate. **The Ministorum's ruling body is the Holy Synod, comprised of
the organisation's Cardinals.**[^lex-ecclesiarchy]

Territorially it divides the galaxy on its own lines, not the Administratum's. **The
Ministorum divides the Imperium into thousands of dioceses, each generally encompassing an
entire Imperial world — an exception being Terra, which has several dioceses. A diocese is
further divided into parishes centred around a shrine. Each diocese is headed by a Cardinal,
while each parish is headed by a Preacher. Ranking between these individuals are Pontifices
whose authority extends over several parishes and preachers within a single
diocese.**[^lex-ecclesiarchy]

One Cardinal per world, several thousand of them. Set that against the Segmenta and sectors
of [[II.T1.Ch1]] and there are now two complete administrative maps of the same galaxy,
drawn by two organisations, which do not coincide. Terra alone has several dioceses, which is
the church admitting that the Throneworld is too large to be one parish.

And it has its own civil service. **The Ministorum also includes an administrative
bureaucracy, the Creed Temporal, headed by Arch-Deacons, who control all secular business.
Arch-Deacons are the administrative counterparts to Cardinals, responsible for the temporal
affairs of an entire diocese. Their servants deal with the money entering and leaving a
specific diocese or parish, regulate the construction of new shrines and temples and deal
with the other physical requirements of the organisation.**[^lex-ecclesiarchy]

A parallel Administratum, inside the church, with its own accountants and its own building
programme. Every structural feature of the Imperial state — the hereditary clerks, the
territorial divisions, the seat on the Council — is reproduced inside an organisation that is
formally outside the state.

It also rules worlds directly. **The Ecclesiarchy directly rules several types of worlds of
the Imperium, most notably Shrine Worlds and Cardinal Worlds.**[^lex-ecclesiarchy] The shrine
worlds of [[IV.T2.Ch5]] are those planets, and what they run on is recorded there: pilgrims,
crypts, and candles rendered from human bodies.

Recruitment comes from the institution of [[IV.T2.Ch6]]. **Much of the Ecclesiarchy's
personnel is recruited from the Schola Progenium, with many entering the clergy as a preacher
or dean**, while **a few select girls may enter the Adepta
Sororitas**.[^lex-ecclesiarchy]

Which closes the circle on this Title's opening. The children the Schola takes — some of them
orphaned to order — are raised by Ecclesiarchy missionaries, and the ones who show the
aptitude are returned to the Ecclesiarchy as priests. The church supplies the teachers,
takes the graduates, and holds a permanent seat in the government that funds it.

One new senior rank is recorded, and it dates from the present. **A new senior rank, the
Militant-Apostolic, was recently created by Roboute Guilliman.**[^lex-ecclesiarchy] The
Primarch who returned in [[III.T5.Ch24]] to a galaxy that worships his father as a god — a
proposition his father outlawed and he himself grew up denying — has not abolished the
church. He has added a rank to it.
