# Missionaria Galaxia & the Frateris Militia

## Converting a World {#missionaries}

The Imperial Creed of [[IV.T3.Ch8]] is deliberately flexible, tailored world by world. This
Chapter is about the people who do the tailoring, and their method is more patient and more
calculating than the Imperium's reputation would suggest.

The **Missionaria Galaxia** — also the **Missionarus Galaxia** and the **Ministorum
Galaxia** — is **an Imperial organisation within the Adeptus Ministorum tasked with spreading
the word of the Emperor's divinity to the farthest reaches of the
galaxy**.[^lex-missionarus-galaxia]

Its reach is built into Imperial exploration itself. **At least one Missionary is often
attached to every exploratory vessel in case human life is
encountered.**[^lex-missionarus-galaxia] Every ship that goes looking for lost colonies
carries a priest, because the Imperium expects to find people and expects to convert them.
The Rogue Traders of [[IV.T1.Ch2]] claim worlds with a formula spoken to people who have never
heard of the Emperor; the Missionaria is what arrives afterwards.

The method itself is anthropology before it is preaching. **Should they make contact with a
lost community, the Missionary works to learn as much about their customs, culture and forms
of worship whilst teaching the natives doctrines of the Imperial
Creed.**[^lex-missionarus-galaxia]

Learn first, then substitute. **This can be a long process as it involves the replacing of the
local primary deities with worship of the Emperor and the introduction of Ministorum practices
as well as religious ceremonies.**[^lex-missionarus-galaxia] And the timescale is stated
without embarrassment: **in some cases, this process can take generations before it can see
fruition and the Missionarus Galaxia is satisfied with the state of natives'
religion**.[^lex-missionarus-galaxia]

Generations. An institution that will spend eighty years replacing a god is not the crude
thing it is usually taken for — it is the same patience [[IV.T3.Ch9]] records in canonising a
world's own heroes to redirect devotion that already exists.

Some local practice is removed. **The barbaric and less desirable aspects of the native
culture such as blood sacrifices are also removed.**[^lex-missionarus-galaxia] Set that
against [[IV.T2.Ch5]], where the Imperium's own shrine worlds are lit by candles rendered from
human bodies, and against [[IV.T2.Ch4]], where underhive gangs sacrifice captives to machines
they worship. The Missionaria's standard is not the practice but the practitioner.

The missionary is also an inspector. These agents **are charged with watching for any other
undesirable traits such as genetic mutation, Chaos worshippers or xeno
influence**.[^lex-missionarus-galaxia] A priest sent to a rediscovered world is simultaneously
the Imperium's first survey of whether that world's population is legal under the provisions
of [[IV.T1.Ch2]] — and [[IV.T2.Ch7]] establishes that on a contaminated world one birth in
twenty will fail that test.

When persuasion does not work there is an alternative. **If these methods fail, the
Missionarius Galaxia is not above using more brutal and forceful methods to change the
religion of a world.**[^lex-missionarus-galaxia] They travel with the means: **missionaries
often accompany Imperial Guard, Imperial Navy, Black Templar, and Sisters of Battle forces on
their expeditions across the galaxy**.[^lex-missionarus-galaxia]

And then the detail that shows the organisation thinking in centuries. **The missionaries also
make use of coded prophecies and prepared omens that can be used by their successors to better
create a foothold for themselves.**[^lex-missionarus-galaxia]

A missionary plants a prophecy in a culture, and a missionary arriving generations later
fulfils it. The Imperium's church manufactures its own foretold arrival, deliberately, across
lifetimes, and leaves the instructions for the next man. Nothing else in this Volume shows the
Ecclesiarchy planning on that horizon.

## Confessors, and the Crowd That Betrays Itself {#confessors}

Below the missionaries are the preachers who work on populations already converted, and the
sources describe their function in terms that leave nothing to interpretation.

**Confessors are evangelical zealots of the Ecclesiarchy. They were once Preachers but have
since been promoted for their zeal.**[^lex-confessor]

Promotion for zeal, not for learning or administration. What that produces is a specialist in
crowds. **Their oratory skills can stir the emotions of entire crowds, leading them to confess
personal heresies and mutations, and to betray their neighbours as psykers or other
deviants.**[^lex-confessor]

That is the Imperium's actual internal security apparatus, and it is worth stating plainly
against the alternatives. The Adeptus Arbites of [[IV.T1.Ch2]] monitor whether unsanctioned
psykers and mutants are being rounded up — but they do not do the rounding up. The Black Ships
of [[I.T2.Ch9]] collect psykers once identified — but they do not identify them. What produces
the names is a man in a public square working a crowd until it informs on itself. The
corpse-starch provision of [[IV.T2.Ch5]], where complaining about the taste is sedition
reported by your foreman, is the same mechanism at the scale of one workplace.

Their jurisdiction is broad and mobile. They **are free to wander within an entire diocese and
preach among the population**, and **often work on Imperial colonies with planetary
governors**.[^lex-confessor]

A Confessor answers to no local authority and may go anywhere within a Cardinal's world. The
Governor of [[IV.T2.Ch5]] may rule his planet in almost any way he likes provided the tithe
arrives — but he cannot exclude the church's travelling zealot, and the sources record the two
working together rather than in tension.

## The Mob the Ecclesiarchy Does Not Own {#militia}

The Decree Passive of [[IV.T3.Ch8]] bans the Ministorum from maintaining men under arms. This
Section is about the first of the two devices by which that ban is survived, and it works by
a technicality.

The **Frateris Militia** are **masses of the faithful gathered by the Ecclesiarchy to fight
Wars of Faith against enemies of the Emperor and mankind**.[^lex-frateris-militia] They are
**the unofficial successors of the Frateris Templars, which were forcibly disbanded by order
of the Decree Passive following the Reign of Blood**.[^lex-frateris-militia]

So the church's standing army was abolished, and the church acquired an unofficial one. The
legal reasoning is stated exactly:

**The existence of the Frateris Militia is tolerated due to the fact that they have no formal
connection to the Ecclesiarchy: they are no more than faithful Imperial citizens eager to
prove their dedication to the Imperial Cult.**[^lex-frateris-militia]

The Ministorum does not maintain them, so the Decree is satisfied. They assemble when the
church calls a war, they fight the church's wars, and they are legally strangers to it. The
Ecclesiarch orders a War of Faith under [[IV.T3.Ch9]] and citizens arrive of their own
volition — which is the difference between an army and a crowd, on paper.

The occasions on which one forms are various, and each shows a different point of contact
between clergy and population. **The raising of a Frateris Militia force is often tied to the
announcement of a War of Faith, with the faithful assembling of their own volition alongside
the ranks of the Imperial Guard and Adepta Sororitas** — though they **can come from any
number of sources: a passionate Preacher or Confessor may rouse the local citizenry to dispose
of a recently-discovered heretical cult; a Missionary may acquire a following of recent
converts to protect and help them spread the good word; a temple may come under attack,
requiring the opening of secret vaults to arm the local clergy and lay members in the shrine's
defence.**[^lex-frateris-militia] Their squads **are often led by
Confessor-Sergeants**.[^lex-frateris-militia]

*Secret vaults.* The organisation forbidden to maintain men under arms keeps weapons in its
temples, and the rank *Confessor-Sergeant* is the previous Section's crowd-worker with a
military title attached. The formal disconnection is a fiction maintained on both sides.

The Ecclesiarchy's own assessment of them is the most honest passage in this Chapter. **In
general though the clergy is wary of raising a force of Frateris Militia. Mobs of ill-trained
zealous fanatics are not only hard to control but can be as much a danger to themselves as
they can the enemy; they are equally liable to kill innocent civilians in their attempt to get
at the foes of mankind.**[^lex-frateris-militia]

The church knows what it is summoning. It calls them anyway, and the reason is structural: the
Decree Passive left it nothing else, apart from the order treated in [[IV.T3.Ch11]].

## The Redemption {#redemption}

What happens when nobody calls the mob off is a named sect, and it began in the place
[[IV.T2.Ch4]] describes.

The **Cult of the Redemption**, or **Crusade of the Red Redemption**, is **an extreme religious
sect that worships the God-Emperor of Mankind**. It **originated on the Hive World of
Necromunda, but similar cults can be found throughout the
Imperium**.[^lex-redemption-cult]

Its origin is not a doctrinal dispute. It is a reading of the conditions. **The Cult
originated in the violent Underhives of Necromunda, where the original Redemptionists saw the
lives of toil of the hive inhabitants, the unreachable luxury of the nobles and the violent
anarchy of the Underhive and concluded that they were in a living
hell.**[^lex-redemption-cult]

They were describing accurately. [[IV.T2.Ch4]] records the same three things from the
Imperium's own sources: indentured workers in overcrowded habs, an elite at the spire with
free power and real food, and an underhive outside all law. The Redemptionists' factual
premise is the Imperium's own account of a hive world. Only the conclusion is theirs.

That conclusion is total. **Redemptionist beliefs are based on the Imperial Creed, which holds
that mankind is ruled and guided by the Emperor. While the Imperial Creed has definite militant
overtones, the Redemption has taken it one stage further.**[^lex-redemption-cult] **The
Redemption believes that the only way to achieve redemption for themselves, Necromunda and
ultimately mankind, is to cleanse it of its sinfulness. Redemptionist prophets foretell of the
destruction of not just Necromunda but of mankind. For Man to be saved it must be purged of
sin. Their methods of redemption are highly violent, its members sworn to expunge all sin with
fire and blade.**[^lex-redemption-cult]

And the distinction from ordinary Imperial fanaticism is the one that matters. **Unlike most
extreme Imperial sects, Redemptionists do not limit their hatred and violent purges to just
mutants, psykers or aliens** — **they see sin in every creature, which along with the sinner,
must be destroyed through bloodshed and fire.**[^lex-redemption-cult]

The third tenet of the Imperial Creed instructs the faithful to purge the heretic, beware the
psyker and mutant, and abhor the alien. The Redemption accepts that instruction and deletes
the list. Every creature qualifies, including the faithful. There is no doctrinal error to
point at — they have taken the Creed's own logic one step past where the Ecclesiarchy stops,
and the Ecclesiarchy's objection is therefore about degree rather than principle.

Which is where this Chapter ends. The Ministorum cannot keep an army, so it raises crowds it
admits are liable to kill innocents. Those crowds are assembled by Confessors promoted for
zeal, whose professional skill is working a population until it denounces itself. On one hive
world the process reached its logical end and produced a sect that intends to burn everyone.
Nothing in the Imperium's doctrine explains why the Redemptionists are wrong; the objection
recorded against them is that they cannot be controlled.
