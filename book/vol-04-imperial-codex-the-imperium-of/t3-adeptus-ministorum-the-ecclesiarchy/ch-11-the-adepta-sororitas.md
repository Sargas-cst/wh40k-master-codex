# The Adepta Sororitas

## San Leor, and a Man Who Arranged to Be Shot {#origins}

The Imperium's only legal church army exists because of a confidence trick, and the sources
record the trick in detail.

The **Adepta Sororitas** — also **the Sisterhood** or the **Daughters of the Emperor** — are
**an all-female subdivision of the religious organisation known as the Ecclesiarchy or
Ministorum**. Their **Orders Militant serve as the Ecclesiarchy's fighting arm, mercilessly
rooting out corruption and heresy within humanity and every organisation of the Adeptus
Terra**.[^lex-adepta-sororitas]

Note the scope of that mandate. Not heresy among citizens — heresy within **every organisation
of the Adeptus Terra**, which is to say within the Administratum, the Arbites and the Astra
Telepathica of [[IV.T1.Ch2]] and [[IV.T9.Ch29]]. The church's soldiers are licensed to
investigate the state.

A naming distinction is worth fixing early. **The terms "Adepta Sororitas" and "Sisters of
Battle" are commonly assumed to mean the same thing, but the latter title technically refers
only to the Orders Militant — the military arm of the organisation, which is the largest and
the best-known part of the Sororitas.**[^lex-adepta-sororitas]

They were **officially founded in the 36th Millennium, and more specifically during the later
Age of Apostasy**, but trace their origins to **an older, all-female warrior cult known as the
"Daughters of the Emperor"**. Based on **the world of San Leor**, that cult **was dedicated to
worship of the Emperor and the study of martial arts** and **was fairly isolated from the wider
Imperium**.[^lex-adepta-sororitas] There is a possible link to the other saint named in
[[IV.T3.Ch9]]: **Imperial scholars believe that Saint Sabbat was linked to the Daughters of the Emperor
in some way**, and **it is possible that the Daughters fought in her war for the Sabbat
Worlds**.[^lex-adepta-sororitas]

They came to the Imperium's attention by being noticed. The Daughters **remained a small sect
and mostly unknown cult until they were discovered by the spies of Goge Vandire, the High Lord
of the Administratum (and also Ecclesiarch of the Adeptus
Ministorum)**.[^lex-adepta-sororitas]

That parenthesis is the whole of the Age of Apostasy in one line, and [[IV.T1.Ch1]] records why
it was possible: nothing in the Senatorum's design prevents one office-holder from holding two
of the nine sacrosanct seats. Vandire held the tax office and the church, and used the pair to
rule.

What he did on San Leor is the passage this Chapter turns on. **Realising their potential
usefulness, he decided to visit San Leor and recruit the Daughters into his own private army.
The Daughters at first refused to accept his authority. Vandire decided to show them that he
was blessed by the Emperor — he instructed a soldier in his entourage to shoot him, which,
after a brief hesitation, he did. However, thanks to the protective field generated by the
Rosarius of the Ecclesiarch (which no one but Vandire himself was aware of), Vandire was not
harmed. The Daughters took this to be a sign and swore allegiance to Vandire virtually on the
spot. They were renamed the Brides of the Emperor and became Vandire's most loyal
followers.**[^lex-adepta-sororitas]

A force field concealed under a robe, a soldier ordered to fire, and a warrior order acquired
in an afternoon. The Imperium's most devout military body was founded on a staged miracle, and
the man who staged it is the one [[III.T4.Ch19]] records ruling through the Reign of Blood.

Their release from him came from the only body in the Imperium that could reach past him.
**During the Siege of the Ecclesiarchal Palace, the Adeptus Custodes, the praetorians of the
Emperor himself, tried to approach the Brides and convince them of Vandire's treachery. In a
last-ditch effort to convince them, the Custodes took Alicia Dominica, leader of the Brides,
and her chosen bodyguards deep into the Imperial Palace where they stood before the Emperor
himself.**[^lex-adepta-sororitas]

And then the gap the record keeps. **What happened there remains unknown — Dominica and her
companions were sworn to secrecy — but it became clear that the Brides, who reverted to the
title of Daughters of the Emperor, had been awakened to the evil that Vandire
represented.**[^lex-adepta-sororitas]

Nobody knows what the Emperor showed her. [[III.T3.Ch17]] describes what is on the Golden
Throne — a thing maintained by a Mechanicum priesthood, consuming psykers daily — and whatever
Dominica saw, she never said, and the order she founded has kept the silence for five thousand
years.

**Marching into his audience chamber, Dominica paused only to condemn Vandire for his crimes
before she beheaded the power-crazed dictator.** His reported last words were **"I don't have
time to die — I'm too busy!"**[^lex-adepta-sororitas]

The Custodes of [[IV.T8.Ch27]] could not remove Vandire themselves. They arranged for his own
bodyguard to do it, by taking her to see the man she thought she served.

## The Loophole {#decree}

The reform that followed is the reason this order still exists, and the sources do not
pretend otherwise.

**After this episode, the newly appointed Ecclesiarch Sebastian Thor proclaimed the Decree
Passive, which forbade the Ecclesiarchy from having "men under
arms".**[^lex-adepta-sororitas]

Then the sentence:

**Under the literal interpretation of the decree, the Adepta Sororitas, being female, were not
obligated to disband. Thor, recognising the need for the Ecclesiarchy to have some kind of
force and internal regulator, allowed them to remain even if the spirit of the decree was
rather blatantly disregarded.**[^lex-adepta-sororitas]

*Rather blatantly disregarded.* The wiki says it, and this codex will not soften it. The
Imperium disarmed its church after a churchman seized power, wrote the prohibition in a way
that covered only men, and the reformer who wrote it left the women in place on purpose.

The Sisterhood therefore **serves as the Ministorum's only official military force because the
Decree Passive rules that the Ecclesiarchy cannot maintain any "men under arms". This was
supposed to limit the power of the Ecclesiarchy. However, the Ministorum was able to
circumvent this decree by using the all-female force of the
Sisterhood.**[^lex-adepta-sororitas]

Two devices survive the Decree, and [[IV.T3.Ch10]] holds the other: the Frateris Militia,
tolerated because it has no formal connection to the church at all. One army is legal because
of its sex and the other because of its paperwork. The constraint that followed the Age of
Apostasy constrains nothing.

There is a third body with an interest, and the relationship is stated carefully. **There is
naturally some overlap between the duties of the Sisterhood and the Inquisition; for this
reason, although the Inquisition and the Sisterhood remain entirely separate organisations, the
Orders Militant of the Sisterhood are the go-to favoured option when an Ordo Hereticus
Inquisitor needs soldiers.**[^lex-adepta-sororitas] Formally, the Orders Militant **form the
Chamber Militant of the Ordo Hereticus, often fighting alongside the Ordo's
Inquisitors**.[^lex-adepta-sororitas]

*Entirely separate organisations*, and also the standing military arm of one of them. The Ordo
Hereticus of [[IV.T7.Ch23]] hunts heresy within the Imperium's own institutions and has no
army; the church has an army it is not supposed to have. The arrangement suits both, and it
means the soldiers who investigate the Adeptus Terra answer, in practice, to two masters
neither of whom formally commands them.

## The Orders Militant {#militant}

**Commonly known as the Sisters of Battle, the Orders Militant are the most numerous and
well-known of the Adepta Sororitas. They are the only official fighting force of the
Ecclesiarchy and often prosecute Wars of Faith.**[^lex-adepta-sororitas]

The Wars of Faith are treated in [[IV.T3.Ch9]], and the two facts lock together: the Ecclesiarch
alone may order such a war, and this is the force that fights it. One man's signature commits
these women.

Their organisation dates from a division made a few centuries after the founding. **In 378.M36,
Ecclesiarch Alexis XXII, Sebastian Thor's successor, divides the Orders Militant of the Convent
Sanctorum and Convent Prioris into four Orders**, among them the **Order of the Ebon Chalice**
and the **Order of the Valorous Heart**.[^lex-adepta-sororitas] The two convents are on
**Terra** and **Ophelia VII**[^lex-adepta-sororitas] — the Sisterhood was split across two
worlds from the beginning, which is what one does with a force one has just watched change
hands in an afternoon.

Command runs through a rank the sources define precisely. **A Canoness is a command position
within the Adepta Sororitas, above a Palatine and below a
Prioress.**[^lex-canoness] Within the Orders Militant she is **a veteran Battle Sister who has
risen through the ranks to eventually command an entire order through their qualities of
command, leadership, and sound tactical and strategic knowledge, as well as no small amount of
faith** — **generally the most experienced warrior in the Order, as well as a great spiritual
leader**.[^lex-canoness]

Promotion by combat record and by faith, in the same person. Compare the Astra Militarum of
[[IV.T5.Ch16]], where a Commissar supplied by [[IV.T2.Ch6]] is attached to a regiment
specifically because the officers cannot be trusted to supply the zeal themselves. The
Sororitas need no commissar. Their commander is the commissar.

And [[IV.T3.Ch9]] records where that leads. Living Saints come overwhelmingly from this order,
sainthood follows martyrdom, and **many Sororitas vie to become Living Saints and undertake
arduous tasks to achieve this**. An order whose senior rank combines military command with
spiritual leadership, in a church that canonises the dead, produces women competing to die
well.

## The Orders That Do Not Fight {#non-militant}

The Sisters of Battle are the best-known part of the Sisterhood and not the largest part of its
reach. The non-militant orders are where the Ecclesiarchy touches the rest of the Imperium, and
one of them is quietly the most powerful thing in this Chapter.

The **Orders Hospitaller** are **surgeons, physicians and nurses**. They **aid the poor, and
heal the sick and the wounded in many hospitals and clinics of the Sisterhood and serve in all
departments of the Imperium's armed services except the Adeptus
Astartes**.[^lex-adepta-sororitas]

Every armed service of the Imperium except the Space Marines is treated by nurses belonging to
the church. [[IV.T1.Ch1]] records that the Chirurgeon-General of the Order Hospitaller sits
among the lesser lords of the Senatorum — so the Ecclesiarchy holds both a permanent seat among
the Twelve and a second voice in the chamber through its medical order.

The **Orders Dialogus** **help to translate the innumerable dialects and slangs used throughout
the Imperium**.[^lex-adepta-sororitas] Given the High and Low Gothic of [[I.T1.Ch2]] — where the
common tongue varies world to world and the hierarchic language is the preserve of ancient
institutions — translation is not a clerical convenience. Whoever renders one world's speech
into another's decides what was said.

The **Orders Famulous** are the ones to stop on. They **organise and maintain households of
certain Imperial governors and Imperial nobles, serving as advisors and by their very presence
reminding them of their higher loyalties**.[^lex-adepta-sororitas]

Read that against [[IV.T2.Ch5]]. An Imperial Governor may rule his world in almost any way he
sees fit, provided the tithe arrives; the Adeptus Arbites will watch a coup succeed if the
remittances continue. Into that arrangement the church places a Sister inside the household —
running it, advising its head, and *reminding him of his higher loyalties by her very
presence*. The Ecclesiarchy has a resident in the palace of an unknown number of planetary
rulers, and the sources describe her function as a reminder.

That is a more complete instrument of control than anything the Administratum fields, and it is
staffed by an order that exists only because a five-thousand-year-old decree was written to say
*men*.

The Sisterhood's shape, taken whole, is the Ecclesiarchy's answer to being disarmed. It has
soldiers who are not men, a militia it does not own, nurses in every service, translators
between every tongue, and advisors in the households of Governors. The Decree Passive removed
the church's army and left everything else, and what the church built in the space is harder to
see and considerably harder to remove.
