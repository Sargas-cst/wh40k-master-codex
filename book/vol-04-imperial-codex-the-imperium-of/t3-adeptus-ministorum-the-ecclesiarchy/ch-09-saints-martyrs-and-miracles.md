# Saints, Martyrs & Miracles

## How the Imperium Makes a Saint {#canonisation}

The Ecclesiarchy of [[IV.T3.Ch8]] enforces five tenets and leaves the rest to local
practice. Sainthood is the mechanism by which local practice is absorbed, and the sources
are unusually candid about what it is for.

**Imperial Sainthood is an honour bestowed upon an individual by the Ecclesiarchy for acts of
great faith and piousness towards the Imperial Cult. It is usually bestowed posthumously, but
sometimes bestowed upon a living person.**[^lex-list-of-imperial-saints]

Then the sentence that explains the institution. **The Ministorum sometimes petitions to make
the popular local heroes on a world into minor saints in the hopes that elevating a familiar
figure will assist in conversion efforts on the world.**[^lex-list-of-imperial-saints]

Sainthood is a conversion tool. When the Missionaria Galaxia of [[IV.T3.Ch10]] arrives on a
world with its own heroes and its own dead, the Ecclesiarchy does not need to replace them —
it canonises them, and the world's existing devotion is redirected without the world noticing
it has changed religion. [[II.T3.Ch7]] records the same manoeuvre on Fenris, where the
missionaries had only to supply an Imperial name for a god the Fenrisians already had. This
is that policy given a formal procedure.

Two categories are admitted without individual process. **Besides this list, the loyalist
Primarchs are considered Imperial Saints.** And **Macharius's generals during the Macharian
Crusade are considered "apostles" or Imperial
Saints.**[^lex-list-of-imperial-saints]

The Primarchs of [[III.T3.Ch12]] were engineered beings, built in a laboratory by a man who
denied his own divinity and forbade worship. Nine of them are now saints of the church that
worships him. Guilliman, who returned in [[III.T5.Ch24]] and who created a new Ecclesiarchy
rank per [[IV.T3.Ch8]], is by this classification a saint of a religion he grew up being
taught was false.

The living case has a formal test, and the composition of the panel is the detail worth
holding. **For an individual to be declared a Living Saint, a conclave of the Ecclesiarchy and
the Inquisition must agree that the subject is pure and the reason
just.**[^lex-living-saint]

The Inquisition of [[IV.T7.Ch23]] sits on the panel — which is to say the body that hunts
heresy has a veto over who the church may call holy. That is the same institutional
suspicion that put an Inquisitorial Representative among the nine permanent High Lords in
[[IV.T1.Ch1]]: the Imperium does not trust its own church to certify a miracle unaudited.

*Pure* and *just* are the two criteria. Neither is defined.

## The Living Saints {#living-saints}

A **Living Saint** is **a mighty and pious warrior of the Imperial Creed who displays
miraculous abilities within his or her lifetime and is subsequently resurrected, supposedly
by the Emperor himself**.[^lex-living-saint]

*Supposedly.* The article keeps the qualifier, and this codex keeps it too. What is recorded
is that people die and are seen alive afterwards; the attribution to the Emperor is the
Ecclesiarchy's, not the record's.

They come overwhelmingly from one place. **Living Saints are most often members of the Adepta
Sororitas but some have come from the Imperial Guard.**[^lex-living-saint] The reason given is
doctrinal: **due to their strong faith in the Imperial Creed, many Living Saints hail from the
Adepta Sororitas whilst others have been heroic Imperial
Guardsmen**.[^lex-living-saint] The Sororitas are treated in [[IV.T3.Ch11]], and the two facts
belong together — the church's only armed order is also the source of nearly every miracle
the church certifies.

What resurrection produces is described physically. **Their piety and faith to the Emperor has
often led to their martyrdom, which is rewarded upon death with resurrection. However, instead
of mere revival, Living Saints become infused with a holy and terrible aura that is a wonder
to behold. A Living Saint becomes the manifestation of the Emperor's might on the battlefield,
serving to boost morale in addition to their formidable combat
prowess.**[^lex-living-saint]

And the phenomenon does not last. **Living Saints are a notoriously brief phenomena, their
holy power burning out as quickly as it appeared. As such, they usually appear in times of
great need for the Imperium, often revealing themselves at the forefront of a War of Faith or
Crusade.**[^lex-living-saint] **Only a few Living Saints are confirmed in a millennium, and
they are all revered throughout the Imperium.**[^lex-living-saint]

A handful per thousand years, each burning out quickly, each appearing at a moment of
maximum need. Stated neutrally, that is either divine intervention on a schedule or an
institution that certifies miracles when it most requires them — and the conclave that
decides sits half-staffed by the Inquisition.

One line records what this does to the people below it. **Many Sororitas vie to become Living
Saints and undertake arduous tasks to achieve this.**[^lex-living-saint]

Sainthood is achieved through martyrdom, martyrdom requires dying, and an order of warrior
women competes for it. The Ecclesiarchy has built a promotion structure whose entry
requirement is death.

**Saint Celestine** is the standing example, and her record is stranger than the general
description. She is **a legendary member of the Adepta Sororitas**, now a Living Saint,
**described as a grand design of the Emperor unfulfilled**. **She has lived multiple lives in
the Emperor's service, and each time she is slain is reborn once
more.**[^lex-saint-celestine]

Not resurrected once. Repeatedly, across millennia. **Little is known of her life and battles
before she was proclaimed a Living Saint — presumably they were
suppressed.**[^lex-saint-celestine]

That *presumably* is the article reasoning about a gap in its own sources, and it is worth
keeping rather than smoothing away: the Ecclesiarchy's most famous saint has no verifiable
early biography, and the wiki's own guess is that the church removed it.

What survives of her earlier lives places her at the founding catastrophe. **In her earliest
known form she was a citizen of Terra during the Horus Heresy. She was killed during the
orbital bombardment of Terra during the Siege of the Emperor's
Palace.**[^lex-saint-celestine] In a later life she was **a Sister Repentia of the Order of
Our Martyred Lady**, one warrior **against thousands of heretics** at the **Palatine
Schism**, and was **thought to have been killed in battle before the capital city of the
planet Eurytion**.[^lex-saint-celestine] In the present crisis she **reappeared on Sangua
Terra to rally the defenders and attempt to hold back the Chaos hordes under Haarken
Worldclaimer**.[^lex-saint-celestine]

A woman who died at the Siege of Terra in [[III.T3.Ch16]] is fighting in the Era Indomitus of
[[III.T5.Ch23]]. Whatever else is true, the Imperium's own record has her present at both
ends of its history.

**Saint Sabbat** is the other named case and the more instructive one about what a saint does
to a map. She is described as **a small but beautiful green-eyed girl with short black
hair**.[^lex-saint-sabbat] The sector **that would go on to bear her name was already
considered fully functioning Imperial territory at the time of her
death**[^lex-saint-sabbat] — and later **the Sabbat Worlds were retaken by the forces of
Chaos**.[^lex-saint-sabbat]

An entire sector of the Imperium is named after a teenage girl, was already Imperial when she
died, and subsequently fell to Chaos anyway. The Crusade fought to retake it is [[VII.T1.Ch1]]'s
subject. Sainthood does not protect a world; it names it.

## Wars of Faith {#wars-of-faith}

The Ecclesiarchy cannot maintain men under arms — the Decree Passive of [[IV.T3.Ch8]] forbids
it. It can, however, start a war.

**A War of Faith is a religiously inspired conflict launched by the Imperium at the order of
the Adeptus Ministorum.**[^lex-war-of-faith]

The distinction from a Crusade is precise and constitutional. **Often confused with a Crusade,
a War of Faith is different in that a Crusade is ordered by the authority of the High Lords of
Terra and generally involves the different military organisations of the Imperium including
the Imperial Guard, the Imperial Navy, the Space Marines, and administrative staff from the
Adeptus Terra and Ecclesiarchy. A War of Faith however is by the command of the Ecclesiarch
alone and primarily concerns only members of the Adeptus Ministorum such as the Adepta
Sororitas and Frateris Militia.**[^lex-war-of-faith]

One man can order a war. The Ecclesiarch does not need the twelve of [[IV.T1.Ch1]] to
authorise it, and does not need the Astra Militarum to fight it — he has the Sororitas of
[[IV.T3.Ch11]] and the mobs of [[IV.T3.Ch10]]. Set that beside the two-thirds majority the
same Council requires before the Officio Assassinorum of [[IV.T1.Ch3]] may deploy a single
assassin, and the Imperium's priorities are legible: killing one named person needs eight
votes; launching a religious war needs one signature.

The objectives are the same as a Crusade's. Both **seek the conquest of new territory, purging
of heretics of the Imperial Cult, and the eradication of Xenos and
Mutants**.[^lex-war-of-faith] So is the funding, when the Ecclesiarchy acts alone: such wars
are **funded and conducted solely by the resources of the
Ecclesiarchy**.[^lex-war-of-faith] The shrine worlds and pilgrim economy of [[IV.T2.Ch5]] are
what pays for them.

One restriction survives, and it is the residue of the Age of Apostasy. **Though it is
financed and ordered by the Ecclesiarch, he does not have total control over a War of Faith
and it must be approved by the High Lords of Terra.**[^lex-war-of-faith]

The Council retains a veto it did not have in M36, when Goge Vandire held both the
Ecclesiarchy and the Administratum and the High Lords were, per [[IV.T1.Ch1]], powerless to
act. Every constraint on the church in this Chapter dates from the reforms that followed him.

And the last provision closes the circle on what the church is willing to do to itself.
**Wars of Faith can sometimes even be conducted against radical or heretical factions within
the Ecclesiarchy itself.**[^lex-war-of-faith]

The Ministorum has a standing power to make war on its own clergy, exercised at the
Ecclesiarch's order. [[IV.T3.Ch8]] records the precedent: the Temple of the Saviour Emperor
was the official religion of the Imperium for four thousand years, and was then declared
heretical and its followers hunted. The mechanism by which that was done is the one described
here, and it remains available.
