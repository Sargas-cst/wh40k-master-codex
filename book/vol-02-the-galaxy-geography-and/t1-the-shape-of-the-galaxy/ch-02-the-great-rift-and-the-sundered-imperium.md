# The Great Rift & the Sundered Imperium

## The Cicatrix Maledictum {#cicatrix}

The map described in [[II.T1.Ch1]] no longer holds. At the end of the 41st Millennium a tear
opened across the galaxy and cut the Imperium in two, and every subsequent Chapter of this
Volume describes worlds on one side of it or the other.

The Great Rift — the Cicatrix Maledictum to the Imperium — is a chain of catastrophic warp
rifts that has divided much of the galaxy.[^lex-great-rift] It formed during the Thirteenth
Black Crusade, following the destruction of Cadia.[^lex-great-rift] Its origins remain a
mystery, and [[I.T1.Ch4]] records the five competing theories and Guilliman's own
speculation. The event belongs to [[III.T5.Ch23]]; what belongs here is the shape it left.

Its physical effects go beyond a wound in one place. The laws of physics have begun to fray,
and the temporal inconsistencies once largely confined to large storms such as the Eye of
Terror have spread across the entire known galaxy — some worlds experiencing centuries in an
instant, others all but frozen in time, others suffering constant temporal
shifts.[^lex-great-rift] Entire star systems have vanished; whole civilisations descended
into chaos overnight.[^lex-great-rift]

What followed immediately was the **Noctis Aeterna** — the Blackness, or the Days of
Blindness. The Rift blocked the light of the Astronomican and rendered astropathic
communication impossible, and the result across the Imperium was total chaos as worlds were
cut off.[^lex-noctis-aeterna] Massive warp storms erupted, making travel extremely
hazardous, and riding that wave vast daemonic legions emerged into the Materium alongside
Chaos Space Marines and their cultists.[^lex-noctis-aeterna] The Dark Gods glutted
themselves on the fear and souls of mortals across the galaxy.[^lex-noctis-aeterna]

The duration varied enormously and the variation is the point. On Terra the Astronomican was
disrupted for thirty-three days. On other worlds the blindness lasted days, or months — and it
is considered possible that some regions remain in the grip of the Noctis Aeterna
still.[^lex-noctis-aeterna] There is no galactic "after". The event ended at different times
in different places, and in some places may not have ended.

The recorded cost is stated in a form worth quoting for its precision: slightly less than
half of the thousand Space Marine Chapters remained unaccounted for, and no fewer than twelve
Chapter planets were reported destroyed during the Noctis Aeterna and the campaigns that
followed.[^lex-noctis-aeterna] Roughly five hundred Chapters unaccounted for is not a defeat;
it is a bureaucratic void where an army used to be.

## Imperium Sanctus {#sanctus}

The Rift did not divide the Imperium evenly, and the halves are distinguished by a single
criterion: whether the beacon still reaches you.

**Imperium Sanctus** is the half that fared better — though the sources are careful to add
that this is only comparative.[^lex-imperium-sanctus] It comprises Segmenta Solar,
Pacificus and Tempestus, together with parts of Ultima and Obscurus, and the defining fact
about it is that **it lies within the light of the
Astronomican**.[^lex-imperium-sanctus] The strategic situation there is more
stable.[^lex-imperium-sanctus]

Note what that composition means against [[II.T1.Ch1]]. Sanctus keeps Terra and Mars, and
therefore the Throne, the Golden Throne's occupant, and the beacon itself. It keeps three
whole segmenta and pieces of the other two. The half that retained the Imperium's
institutions is the half those institutions were built around — which is less a strategic
outcome than a statement of where the Imperium's centre always was.

It also means the division is not geographical in any ordinary sense. Sanctus and Nihilus are
not two regions with a border; they are the lit and unlit portions of a single volume, and
parts of the same segmentum fall on either side. An Imperial world's allegiance to one half
or the other is determined by whether a psychic signal from Terra can still be sensed
there — which makes the Astronomican, described in [[IV.T9.Ch31]], not merely a navigation aid
but the instrument that defines Imperial territory.

Stability is relative in another sense too. Sanctus suffers a myriad of problems and threats
of its own; the sources say only that they pale beside what is happening on the other
side.[^lex-imperium-nihilus]

## Imperium Nihilus {#nihilus}

The other half has a name that tells you what was done to it. **Imperium Nihilus** — the Dark
Imperium — encompasses much of Segmentum Obscurus and Ultima Segmentum, and became isolated
from the rest when the Rift formed at the end of the Thirteenth Black
Crusade.[^lex-imperium-nihilus]

The name is earned. There the light of the Astronomican is obscured, travel is extremely
dangerous, astropathic communication is difficult, and a great many enemies of humanity
besiege its remaining isolated worlds.[^lex-imperium-nihilus] Every one of those four
conditions follows from the first: without the beacon there is no navigation, without
navigation no reinforcement, without astropathy no orders, and a world that cannot be
reinforced or instructed is besieged by whatever is nearest.

Crossing to it is described as difficult to nigh-impossible.[^lex-imperium-nihilus] A stable
route was eventually found — the **Nachmund Gauntlet**, now one of the most strategically vital
regions in the galaxy.[^lex-imperium-nihilus] It lies near the remains of Cadia and the Eye of
Terror, focused around the Nachmund Sub-Sector in Segmentum Obscurus, and for a time it was the
*only* stable route through the Cicatrix Maledictum — which made it a source of huge numbers of
Imperial refugees as well as a strategic prize.[^lex-nachmund-gauntlet] Later in the Indomitus
Crusade a second passage, the **Attilan Gate**, was
discovered.[^lex-nachmund-gauntlet] Its own world, Vigilus, is [[II.T3.Ch8]]'s subject.

The Gauntlet has not stayed safe. Both its northern and part of its southern end are under
siege by the forces of Chaos, and during the Nachmund Rift War almost the entire northern
region was lost — the Imperium now seeking to erect a defensive network, the **Sanctus Wall**,
against the Black Legion under Haarken Worldclaimer. Many worlds north of that wall remain
under Imperial control and are, in the sources' phrase, essentially on their own to weather the
coming storm.[^lex-nachmund-gauntlet]

The Imperium found one door through the Rift and is now building a wall inside the corridor
that leads to it, leaving the worlds on the far side of the wall to their own devices. That is
what reunification looks like in practice: not a restored empire but a defended passage, with
everything past the last defensible point written off.

The detail that best conveys the situation is epistemic rather than military. Until the
Indomitus Crusade, it was not even clear to the Imperium **whether anything remained alive on
the other side of the Rift**.[^lex-imperium-nihilus] Half of the largest human polity in the
galaxy went silent, and its government could not determine whether it still existed.

Two responses followed. Guilliman declared the liberation of the stranded worlds of Imperium
Nihilus one of the Indomitus Crusade's aims,[^lex-imperium-nihilus] treated at
[[III.T5.Ch25]]. And a separate authority was established for the far side: the Regent of
Imperium Nihilus, named by Guilliman, is the Blood Angels Chapter Master
Dante,[^lex-imperium-nihilus] who is seeking to make Baal the acting capital of the Dark
Imperium.[^lex-imperium-nihilus]

That last is a structural change worth noting, because it is unusual for this setting. Baal,
described in [[II.T3.Ch7]], is a Space Marine homeworld — a fortress-monastery's planet, not an
administrative centre. Cut off from Terra, the Imperium's response was to appoint a warrior
lord as regent and elevate a Chapter's homeworld to a capital. Dante himself is
[[VIII.T2.Ch6]]'s subject.
