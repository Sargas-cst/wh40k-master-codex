# Segmenta, Sectors & Sub-Sectors

## The Five Segmenta {#segmenta}

Before any of the worlds in this Volume can be placed, the frame they sit in has to be
described — and the Imperium's frame is naval rather than political.

Imperial space is divided into five fleet zones, the **Segmentae Majoris**. The galaxy is
described as so vast that it would be unmanageable without the
system.[^lex-segmentum] Each Segmentum has its own Imperial Navy and merchant fleets, and a
Segmentum Fortress or Naval Base that forms the base of fleet operations within
it.[^lex-segmentum] Fleet commanders hold the rank of Lord High Admiral, and the Fortress
itself is controlled directly by a high-ranking Administratum official titled the Master of
the Segmentum.[^lex-segmentum]

| Segmentum | Fortress | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Solar** | Mars[^lex-segmentum-solar] | Contains Terra; the core of Imperial space |
| **Obscurus** | Cypra Mundi[^lex-segmentum-obscurus] | Contains the Eye of Terror |
| **Pacificus** | Hydraphur[^lex-segmentum-pacificus] | Contains the Halo Zone |
| **Tempestus** | Bakka[^lex-segmentum-tempestus] | Galactic south |
| **Ultima** | Kar Duniash[^lex-ultima-segmentum] | The largest by far |

Two features of that table are worth drawing out.

The first is that **Segmentum Solar's fortress is Mars, not Terra**. The administrative and
naval centre of the segmentum containing humanity's homeworld is the neighbouring planet,
which belongs to the Adeptus Mechanicus. Terra is the spiritual capital;
[[II.T2.Ch5]] holds the machinery. Segmentum Solar is described as the core of Imperial
space and the foundation of mankind, the most densely settled human region of the galaxy
since the Dark Age of Technology.[^lex-segmentum-solar]

The second is that these are **fleet zones**, not provinces. The division exists so that
navies can be based and supplied, which is why each one is defined by a fortress rather
than by a border. Imperial astrography is a logistics scheme that acquired the appearance
of geography, and reading it as a map of governance will mislead.

Note also the name: *Ultima Segmentum* inverts the usual order, and the Imperium's largest
division is the one whose name breaks the pattern of the other four.

## Sectors, Subsectors and the Space Between {#sectors}

Below the segmenta the scheme becomes more specific, and then abruptly stops being a scheme
at all.

A **sector** is a division of a Segmentum. Its size varies with local demand and stellar
density, but it is usually equivalent to a cube two hundred light years on a side, and a
naval battlefleet defends each one.[^lex-sector] Sectors are overseen by Sector
Lords.[^lex-sector]

A **subsector** divides a sector further, running ten to twenty light years across — and here
the logic changes in a way that matters.[^lex-sector] Sectors are volumes of space.
Subsectors are *not*: they are blocks of systems and worlds that can practically be
patrolled by ships, centred on densely populated star clusters, important worlds, or the
meeting points of warp trade routes.[^lex-sector][^lex-subsector]

That shift from volume to cluster is the whole character of Imperial administration in one
detail. At the top the Imperium draws boxes on a chart. At the level where ships actually
go, it abandons boxes and organises around the places worth visiting. The second scheme is
an admission that the first is a fiction.

And then there is what the scheme leaves out, which is most of the galaxy. The areas between
sectors and subsectors are described as vast — in fact making up more of the galaxy than the
Imperium controls.[^lex-sector] Enormous numbers of star systems within any given sector
fall inside no subsector at all.[^lex-subsector] These regions are unexplored, uninhabited,
held by alien empires, or simply inaccessible by warp travel, and they carry a scatter of
names: **wilderness space**, wilderness zones, inter sectors, forbidden zones, empty space,
frontier space.[^lex-sector][^lex-subsector]

Six names for the same thing is itself informative. The Imperium has no single word for the
majority of the galaxy, because it has no single relationship to it. Set this against
[[I.T1.Ch1]]: an empire that cannot count its own worlds also cannot name the space between
them.

## Warp Routes and Stable Passage {#warp-routes}

The reason subsectors cluster around trade routes rather than filling space evenly is that
distance in the Imperium is not measured in light years. It is measured in whether you can
get there.

Subsectors form where warp trade routes meet.[^lex-subsector] That is the operative fact of
Imperial geography, and it follows directly from [[I.T1.Ch3]]: warp transit is unpredictable
in principle, so the routes that prove reliable become infrastructure. A world's importance
is a function of its position on a passage that works, not of its resources or population.
Two systems a few light years apart may belong to different subsectors because no usable
route joins them.

The consequence is that Imperial space is better understood as a network than a territory.
The boxes drawn in the Section above describe a volume; the subsectors describe the parts of
that volume that are actually connected. Everything in between is inside the borders and
outside the state.

The limit case is the **Halo Zone**, in Segmentum Pacificus: the region beyond the light of
the Astronomican, where Navigators can no longer sense the beacon and must navigate
blind.[^lex-halo-zone] Doing that in the Warp is described as extremely hazardous, and most
Navigators — if not all — will simply refuse to go past the beacon's
edge.[^lex-halo-zone] Journey times across the region lengthen accordingly.

That refusal is the hard edge of the Imperium, and it is a human one rather than a physical
one. The border of the Imperium is not where its authority ends but where its pilots stop
agreeing to sail. Lord Commander Solar Macharius found this out directly: he attempted to
continue his crusade into the Halo Zone and his troops refused to go on, possibly believing
they would no longer be under the Emperor's protection outside the beacon's
light.[^lex-halo-zone] His crusade is [[VII.T1.Ch1]].

The beacon that draws that border is treated in [[IV.T9.Ch31]], and what happened when it
failed in [[II.T1.Ch2]].
