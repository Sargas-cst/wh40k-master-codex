# Regions of Terror & Ruin

## The Eye of Terror and the Cadian Gate {#eye-of-terror}

Some regions of the galaxy are not places with bad reputations. They are places where the
distinction between realspace and the Warp has stopped applying.

The **Eye of Terror** is the largest and best known warp rift in the galaxy: an area where
the Warp and realspace overlap, lying at the galactic edge to the north and west of Terra, in
Segmentum Obscurus.[^lex-eye-of-terror] The Imperium also calls it Ocularis Terribus and
Ocularis Malifica, and the Ordo Malleus knows it as *Anathema Nonplus
Ultra*.[^lex-eye-of-terror]

One consequence is worth stating before anything else, because it defines what kind of place
this is: **a ship can enter the Warp without a warp drive, simply by crossing its
border**.[^lex-eye-of-terror] Everywhere else in the setting, entering the Immaterium requires
the machinery described in [[I.T1.Ch3]]. Inside the Eye it requires only arriving.

The region was once the domain of the old Aeldari Empire. When Slaanesh was born, the energy
of that birth permanently breached the boundary between realspace and the Warp, and every
planet and star in the area is now *within* the Warp — their worlds having become daemon
worlds.[^lex-eye-of-terror] At the very centre lies the *byssos*, a hole in the fabric of
reality through which raw Chaos energy pours.[^lex-eye-of-terror]

So the Eye is not a natural feature. It is the scar of a single event, and the Aeldari Fall
that made it is the same event that produced the craftworlds of [[I.T3.Ch11]] and shattered
Khaine in [[I.T4.Ch16]]. One catastrophe, three of this codex's subjects.

Getting out of it mattered enormously to the Imperium, and for ten thousand years there was
one dependable way. The **Cadian Gate** was an area of calm space leading in and out of the
Eye, located to its galactic south-east, and it was the only *predictably* stable
route — which made it a site of frequent Chaos raids.[^lex-cadian-gate] It was not the only
exit; the Arx gap was another, though less stable.[^lex-cadian-gate]

The Gate existed for a reason, and the reason was buried on a planet. It was held open by the
blackstone pylons on the surface of Cadia, which had the effect of strengthening the barrier
between realspace and the Warp.[^lex-cadian-gate] Those structures are of Necron design,
found on numerous worlds, and they employ negatively charged noctilith to dampen the Warp's
effects.[^lex-pylon] The stabilised corridor described in [[I.T1.Ch4]] was, in practice, this
one.

There is a final property of the material that explains everything in [[II.T1.Ch2]].
Noctilith can ward against Warp energies *or* act as a locus for them, and the forces of
Chaos have learned to subvert the pylons so as to thin the boundary rather than
thicken it.[^lex-pylon] The device that held the Eye of Terror in check was reversible, and
was reversed. Cadia is [[II.T3.Ch8]]; its destruction [[III.T5.Ch22]].

## The Maelstrom {#maelstrom}

The Eye has a smaller sibling near the galaxy's heart, and its distinction is that it has
been useful to somebody.

The **Maelstrom** is a *majoris grade* interference between the Warp and real
space[^lex-the-maelstrom] — a massive warp storm and spatial vortex lying near the centre of
the galaxy, second only to the Eye of Terror in size, scope and
intensity.[^lex-the-maelstrom] It carries a long list of names: the Shadow Mere, the Twisted
Reach, the Well of Lies, the Maw, and to the Aeldari *Guair Dromlaith*, the Nightmare
Gulf.[^lex-the-maelstrom]

The grading language is worth pausing on. *Majoris grade* implies a scale with other
grades on it — the Imperium classifying warp phenomena by severity the way it classifies
psykers in [[I.T2.Ch6]] and dating reliability in [[I.T1.Ch2]]. Faced with a hole in reality,
the Imperium's first instinct is to assign it a category.

Its position, unlike the Eye's, is central rather than peripheral, and that has made it a
refuge rather than merely a hazard. Regions of the Maelstrom were the site of the Badab War in
late M41,[^lex-the-maelstrom] treated at [[VII.T1.Ch4]]. After his defeat there, Lugft Huron,
master of the Astral Claws, escaped into the Maelstrom.[^lex-the-maelstrom] It is now the home
of the Red Corsairs, on the world of New Badab.[^lex-the-maelstrom]

That sequence is the Maelstrom's real significance for this Volume. The Eye of Terror is where
Chaos comes from; the Maelstrom is where renegades go. A region no Imperial fleet can
practically police, sitting near the galactic centre rather than at its rim, functions as a
sanctuary — and the Imperium's response to a traitor escaping into it has been, in effect, to
lose him. Huron and the warband he founded are [[V.T3.Ch10]].

## The Ghoul Stars and the Halo Zone {#ghoul-stars}

The last two regions in this Chapter are defined by absence rather than by breach, and they
are absences of two different kinds.

The **Ghoul Stars**, also called the Ghost Stars, are a desolate region in the extreme
galactic north-east — worlds lit by the cold rays of dying suns, within the northern part of
Ultima Segmentum.[^lex-ghoul-stars] What makes them feared is the population. Among the
xenos found there are the Cythor Fiends and creatures out of primal nightmares such as
Togoran Bloodreeks, and among the natives are beings so alien that they seem
supernatural.[^lex-ghoul-stars]

The region was once home to a number of human-inhabited worlds. An ancient threat is believed
responsible for destroying them, and for the large number of formerly human dead worlds
there.[^lex-ghoul-stars] "Believed responsible" is doing the work in that sentence: the
Imperium knows its worlds there are gone and does not know what killed them. The lesser xenos
of the galaxy's dark corners are treated in [[VI.T7.Ch23]].

The **Halo Zone** is an absence of a different order. Introduced in [[II.T1.Ch1]] as the edge
of practical Imperial space, it is the region of Segmentum Pacificus lying beyond the light of
the Astronomican, where Navigators can no longer sense the beacon and must travel
blind.[^lex-halo-zone] Most, if not all, refuse.[^lex-halo-zone]

The contrast between the two is the point of pairing them. The Ghoul Stars are dangerous
because of what is in them. The Halo Zone is dangerous because of what is *not* — no beacon,
therefore no navigation, therefore no return. One is a region the Imperium fears; the other is
a region the Imperium simply cannot enter, and the difference is not military but
infrastructural.

Both mark the same lesson for the Volume that follows. Imperial space is not bounded by
enemies or by distance. It is bounded by the reach of one psychic signal from Terra, and
everything past that — the Halo Zone, the far side of the Rift, the dead worlds of the Ghoul
Stars — is outside the Imperium whatever the charts claim.
