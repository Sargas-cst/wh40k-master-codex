# Xenos Domains

## Craftworlds — Territory Without Ground {#craftworlds}

Everything in this Volume so far has assumed that territory means planets. The galaxy's other
powers do not all share that assumption, and the differences are not cosmetic — they determine
what can be conquered, garrisoned, or even found.

**Craftworlds** are vast craft populated by Eldar, home to the Asuryani, the greater remnant of
the race after the Fall. Each is a continent-sized, self-sufficient, independent realm with its
own culture, capable of housing tens of millions.[^lex-craftworld] They were not built to be
homes. During the Eldar Empire they were **trading vessels**: typical missions took centuries and
carried them thousands of light years beyond Eldar space before turning back, and in their heyday
they were self-contained communities housing hundreds of families, constructed in titanic star
docks crafted by the legendary Bonesingers of Ceith-Cailli.[^lex-craftworld]

The most useful description of one comes from an Imperial Inquisitor: a craftworld is a sentient
being with a hundred thousand minds.[^lex-craftworld]

Note what has happened here. A species whose worlds were destroyed did not lose its territory; its
territory now moves. Their populations were named for the planets the vessels served, and the
planets are gone while the names remain. Compare every Imperial world in this Volume: Terra
cannot relocate, Cadia could not be evacuated, Catachan's settlements retreat across a surface
they cannot leave. A craftworld has no surface to defend and no coordinates to besiege. The Fall
that produced them is [[I.T3.Ch11]]'s and [[I.T4.Ch16]]'s; the Asuryani themselves,
[[VI.T2.Ch4]].

## Commorragh — A City in the Webway {#commorragh}

**Commorragh**, the Dark City, is the capital of Dark Eldar society, located deep within the
labyrinth of the Webway — the space between realspace and the Warp.[^lex-commorragh] It is the
most complete example in this codex of a domain that has no location in the ordinary sense.

Within the Webway, Commorragh appears as a composite entity of impossible scale, acting less like
a city than **a vast collection of satellite realms and cities linked to one another by portals
and hidden pathways** — a loose collection of far-flung nodes spread across the Webway's
passageways. Its most clustered concentrations sit in a reality scattered across the galaxy,
thousands of light years apart in places, but shortcuts in the form of dimensional gateways
allow those immense distances to be crossed **with a single step**. Runic wards protect it from
being breached by outsiders.[^lex-commorragh]

A capital city whose districts are thousands of light years apart and adjacent at the same time.
There is no perimeter to hold and no march to the gates, which is why the Imperium's entire
military doctrine — fleets, sieges, orbital bombardment — has almost no purchase on it. The Webway
itself is [[I.T3.Ch10]].

Its origins explain its character precisely. Commorragh traces to the ancient Eldar empire, when
it became one of the greatest Webway port-cities; **Port Commorragh was established in M18** as
the primary nodal port in the Webway, able to transport a fleet to any of the Eldar's most vital
planets.[^lex-commorragh] Its ability to reach the far-flung corners of realspace made it the
most important location in the entire Webway — and therefore too valuable to belong to any one
part of the Eldar empire, so it was **granted autonomy**. That put it outside the jurisdiction of
the great Eldar councils, and it attracted those who wished to hide their
deeds.[^lex-commorragh]

The most depraved society in the setting is the product of a jurisdictional decision. Commorragh
became what it is because a transport hub was made too important to regulate, and the people who
needed no oversight moved in. Set that against Ultramar's autonomy in [[II.T2.Ch6]]: exemption
from central authority produced the Imperium's best-governed realm in one case and the Dark City
in the other, and the mechanism was identical.

Its power supply is worth recording in full, because it is the clearest single statement of what
the Dark Eldar are. The city is powered by the **Illmaea**, or "stolen suns": before the Fall, the
Eldar brought stars out of the Materium into Webway sub-realms above Commorragh, giving the city a
near-endless supply of energy.[^lex-commorragh] **The theft froze and killed the solar systems the
suns were taken from, and the Eldar did not care.**[^lex-commorragh]

Whole star systems extinguished to light a city, recorded without a note of regret. The
Illmaea predate the Fall, which makes them evidence about the Eldar empire rather than about its
survivors — the decadence of [[I.T4.Ch16]] was not a late symptom.

Its history is a list of things that could not be permanently done to it. The Imperium raided
Commorragh and virtually wiped out the old aristocracy, which by M35 left Asdrubael Vect in total
dominance.[^lex-commorragh] In 227.M38 Orks under the Warboss and Weirdboy Zoggit came through a
Webway rift into the Commorrite port of Blackblood; hundreds of thousands of them rampaged until
the local Kabals isolated and destroyed each mob piece by piece. The city took heavy damage and
several districts were destroyed — and the Dark Eldar still profited, using ten thousand captured
greenskins for a grand gladiatorial spectacle in the Wych arenas.[^lex-commorragh]

An invasion that destroyed districts of the city ended as entertainment. That is Commorragh's
relationship to violence in one episode, and the Kabals and Wych Cults are [[VI.T2.Ch6]].

Its present crisis is internal, and it is the only kind that has worked. In the closing years of
the 41st Millennium the partial awakening of Ynnead via Yvraine created a catastrophic
**Dysjunction**, destabilising much of the Dark City and opening **Khaine's Gate**. Vect retreated
to his outposts while the city fell into anarchy; daemonic incursions became commonplace; and
Kheradruakh opened a portal to the realm of the Mandrakes, turning a large part of the city into
his lair, while Wych Cults, Kabals and Haemonculus Covens fought for
supremacy.[^lex-commorragh]

Vect's counterattack is the detail that matters, because of what it cost. He organised a massive
army led by the Incubi and struck at Khaine's Gate, corralling the daemons into the sub-realm
containing the gate before severing it from the rest of Commorragh — it is now the **Chasm of
Woe**. But the daemons kept coming, rising from abscesses throughout the city, which forced Vect
to **plunge more districts into sub-dimensions, taking entire armies and masses of slaves with
them**.[^lex-commorragh] Commorragh has since been sundered by civil war as Vect's forces seek to
quell Ynnari influence, and in desperation some have turned to the Black Throne
project.[^lex-commorragh]

A ruler amputating parts of his own capital into other dimensions, populations included, to stop a
daemonic infection. It is the same defensive logic as Terra's Arx Doctrine in [[II.T2.Ch4]] — cede
ground, hold the core — executed by a power that can literally remove the ground. Ynnead and the
Ynnari are [[VI.T2.Ch9]].

One caution the sources raise themselves: there are many rumours about Commorragh, and it is
difficult to know what is true and what has been fabricated by the shattered psyches of those
unfortunate enough to have found themselves there.[^lex-commorragh] Vect claims to be the city's
founder and the discoverer of soul-extraction, and he is the only being nearly ancient enough to
know whether that is true, so the claim cannot be tested.[^lex-commorragh]

## Tomb Worlds — Territory Nobody Knows Is Occupied {#tomb-worlds}

**Tomb Worlds** are worlds inhabited by dormant Necrons, and have been for millions of
years.[^lex-tomb-world] They exist in unknown numbers throughout many parts of the galaxy. A Tomb
World seems long abandoned, and many are thought to be ancient Necrontyr worlds, since Necrontyr
architecture appears on so many of them; they tend to be barren, lifeless desert
worlds.[^lex-tomb-world]

The Imperium's contact with them follows one pattern. **Many Adeptus Mechanicus personnel sent to
these planets for exploration are never heard from again** — the Necron inhabitants never having
truly left across the millions of years they spent in stasis.[^lex-tomb-world]

In the absence of an active population, most Tomb Worlds are managed by a master control
programme called a **Deep Spirit**, overseeing the slumbering denizens and the swarms of Canoptek
constructs that serve as labourers, caretakers and defenders.[^lex-tomb-world] Some are obviously
alien and home to great Necron cities; others are hidden, their structures and inhabitants
entombed deep beneath the surface **or isolated in pocket dimensions** that prevent discovery by
subsequent civilisations.[^lex-tomb-world] That concealment was in part enacted to protect them
from marauding Eldar sixty million years ago.[^lex-tomb-world]

Then the sentence that reframes the whole galaxy: **only a tiny portion of Tomb Worlds have been
discovered or reawakened, and the number is far greater than any save the Necrons themselves
realise.**[^lex-tomb-world]

Every map in [[II.T1.Ch1]] is therefore wrong in a way the Imperium cannot correct. Worlds the
Administratum lists as barren, unclaimed, or its own are occupied — the occupants asleep, their
caretaker programmes running, their defences automatic. The Imperium is not sharing the galaxy
with the Necrons in the sense of holding a border against them; it is living on top of them
without knowing the extent. Its own explorators have been disappearing into the evidence for
millennia.

Their internal hierarchy is worth recording because it is a political map rather than a
geographical one. **Crownworlds** were once the hubs of galactic Necrontyr power; their Necrons
tend to have survived the Great Sleep in excellent condition and their worlds are untouched by
disaster or other races, so they serve as the hubs of Necron Dynasties, from which the Overlords
rule.[^lex-tomb-world] **Coreworlds** are next in importance, forming the heart of a
Dynasty.[^lex-tomb-world] **Fringeworlds** are of tertiary importance, not high enough in status
to be counted among a Dynasty's coreworlds — poor and distant colonies, used for manual
labour.[^lex-tomb-world]

A dormant empire that still maintains a distinction between its capitals, its heartlands and its
labour colonies, sixty million years into a sleep. The Dynasties are [[VI.T1.Ch1]]; the awakening,
[[III.T4.Ch21]].

## The T'au Empire — Territory That Is Growing {#tau-empire}

The T'au Empire — sometimes called a Commonwealth by its own citizens — is a **rapidly expanding**
xenos empire inside Ultima Segmentum near the Eastern Fringe, founded by the Ethereals who lead it
in the name of the Greater Good, with several alien races including the Kroot and Vespid allied to
it.[^lex-t-au-empire] It borders the Imperium and, notably, **lies within reach of the
Astronomican**.[^lex-t-au-empire] It has suffered many Ork raids and appears to lie in the path of
several splinter fleets of Hive Fleet Kraken.[^lex-t-au-empire]

That second clause is the one this codex flags. Everything in [[II.T1.Ch1]] and [[II.T1.Ch3]]
treats the beacon's reach as the practical limit of Imperial space — the border where Navigators
refuse to sail. The T'au Empire is inside it, and it is growing. Ultramar, from [[II.T2.Ch6]], lies
just south of it.

Its founding is a story about a siege, and it is worth telling because it explains the Empire's
method. T'au technological advance proceeded at an unusually rapid rate for a newly emergent
race — a Mechanicus Explorator fleet had found them at Stone Age level in
M35[^lex-t-au-empire] — and escalating inter-tribal conflict produced the **Mont'au**, the darkest
period in their history, at the end of M37. Wars lasted years; thousands died each year, and
squalid conditions and lack of fresh food and water bred a plague that killed more T'au than the
fighting. **They were on the verge of wiping themselves out.**[^lex-t-au-empire]

Then, at the fortress city of Fio'taun — under siege for five years by an alliance of plains and air
T'au who would settle for nothing less than annihilation, its defenders beginning to starve — two
mysterious T'au appeared. One entered the besiegers' camp, exuding a quiet authority no T'au could
resist, and persuaded their leader to parlay. The other made his way into the fortress, and within
a few hours the gates stood open. The Ethereals spoke of peace and of a **Greater Good** each T'au
must strive towards; besiegers and besieged agreed, a truce was reached, and across the homeworld
more Ethereals emerged with the same authority and the same message. With the T'au united, the
Empire was founded.[^lex-t-au-empire]

A species days from extinction, unified by two individuals whose authority nobody could refuse.
The sources give this as T'au legend and do not explain the Ethereals' hold; [[VI.T5.Ch16]] holds
the caste system it produced. What matters here is the shape it left: an empire founded by ending
a siege rather than winning one.

Expansion follows a documented procedure, and it is the opposite of the Imperium's. Growth happens
in phases the T'au call **Spheres**, during which nearby worlds are colonised, conquered, **or
sometimes peacefully persuaded** to join the Greater Good.[^lex-t-au-empire] When a new species is
encountered, the Ethereals assign each caste a role: the Air Caste scans the world; those
classified as desirable are investigated further; if a sentient race is found, the Water Caste
sends ambassadors to make trade deals and defence pacts and to offer advanced technologies built
for them by the Earth Caste. **The empire is patient, and this process of negotiation may last
months, years or even decades.** Only if all of it fails does the Fire Caste forcibly absorb the
world.[^lex-t-au-empire]

Diplomacy first, for decades, with conquest as the fallback. No other power in this codex operates
that way. The Imperium's equivalent procedure is the Missionaria Galaxia of
[[IV.T3.Ch10]] followed by compliance or extermination. Only the Orks have been denied the offer
outright, the T'au having abandoned attempts to absorb them.[^lex-t-au-empire]

The results are visible in what the Empire builds. Through the Earth Caste's expertise, **lifeless
and toxic rocks have been transformed into thriving colonies and even artificial planets**, while
orbital docks and relay stations light the empty space between the
stars.[^lex-t-au-empire] Against Terra's boiled seas and Mars's rust in [[II.T2.Ch4]] and
[[II.T2.Ch5]], and Krieg's radioactive ruin in [[II.T3.Ch9]], the contrast is total: one power
industrialises habitable worlds into poison, the other terraforms poison into habitation.

First contact with humanity came shortly after the Second Sphere, in the form of free captains,
pirates and lost colonies so regressed they knew nothing of the wider galaxy. These were
peacefully assimilated or forcibly subdued, and it took the Water Caste time to realise they were
the outer fringes of an unimaginably vast interstellar empire. Calls for war from the Fire Caste
were averted by Aun'va's wisdom, and the T'au embarked instead on a **subtle campaign of trade and
diplomacy with planetary governors to absorb further human worlds**. The Imperium's response was
slow, but when it came it was characteristically brutal.[^lex-t-au-empire] The Damocles Gulf
Crusade is [[VII.T1.Ch3]].

The codex records the Empire's own claims about its human subjects as claims. **It is claimed by
the Empire** that the humans within it — the Gue'vesa, ranging from ideological defectors to
captured guardsmen and their children — are content and treated well, even permitted to worship
the Emperor.[^lex-t-au-empire] Two other provisions sit beside that. Client species on T'au worlds
**cannot access the Empire's public services unless they apply as client-residents**, which
requires mandatory biometric registration.[^lex-t-au-empire] And those rare T'au who do not adhere
to the tenets of the Greater Good are seen as deviants and **can be condemned to a Re-Education
Facility**.[^lex-t-au-empire]

Set the three together and the picture is neither the Imperium's propaganda nor the Empire's. A
polity that negotiates for decades before it fights, terraforms dead worlds, and lets conquered
humans keep their god — which also requires biometric registration for access to services and
maintains facilities for citizens who think incorrectly. The Greater Good and the castes that
serve it are [[VI.T5.Ch16]]. The external image of the T'au is altruistic and idealistic,
believing in the unification of all;[^lex-t-au-empire] the sources' phrasing is *external
image*, and this codex keeps the qualifier.

## Ork Empires — Territory as a Weather System {#ork-empires}

The last kind of xenos domain is the one that behaves least like territory at all.

The **Ork Empire of Charadon** is a major Ork empire centred on the Charadon System in Ultima
Segmentum, ruled by a Warboss styled the **Arch-Arsonist of
Charadon**.[^lex-ork-empire-of-charadon] It is **the largest and most long-lasting Ork empire in
existence**, and has been a bane of Ultima Segmentum for countless
centuries.[^lex-ork-empire-of-charadon]

What kept it in check is the detail that matters, because it is not a border. Until recently
Charadon's expansion was limited by the Ultramarines, with forces led by the Librarian Tigurius
making **surgical strikes** on the Arch-Arsonist's forces to prevent them organising a full-scale
Waaagh! into Ultramar. Tigurius's efforts began to wane as Tyranid encroachment into Ultima
Segmentum meant the Ultramarines could no longer spare troops — and the Ork Empire seized its
chance and invaded the easternmost parts of Ultramar.[^lex-ork-empire-of-charadon]

An empire held back for centuries not by fortifications but by one Chapter's raids on its
leadership, which resumed expanding the moment a different enemy drew that Chapter away. Ork
territory is not defended ground; it is pressure, contained only for as long as something actively
pushes back. Ultramar's borders in [[II.T2.Ch6]] were among the things it pushed into.

Its most notorious act shows what the pressure does when it is not contained. Under the
Arch-Arsonist **Snagrod**, the Ork Empire of Charadon invaded Rynn's World, homeworld of the
Crimson Fists, in 988.M41 — and the ensuing battle saw **the near-total destruction of the Crimson
Fists and the destruction of their fortress-monastery**.[^lex-ork-empire-of-charadon]

A Space Marine Chapter reduced almost to nothing, and its fortress-monastery destroyed, by an Ork
empire the Imperium had been managing with raids. Set that beside [[II.T3.Ch7]]: the
fortress-monastery is the most heavily defended structure a Chapter possesses, and Charadon
removed one. The Orks themselves are [[VI.T3.Ch10]]; Charadon's later campaigns,
[[VII.T4.Ch15]].

Take the five domains in this Chapter together and the Imperium's position looks different from
the way [[II.T1.Ch1]] describes it. Its charts divide space into fleet zones with fortresses and
borders. But one rival's territory travels, another's capital is a set of non-adjacent rooms, a
third's is asleep beneath worlds the Imperium thinks are empty and more numerous than anyone but
its owners knows, a fourth is expanding by treaty inside the Astronomican's light, and a fifth is
weather. Only the T'au Empire can be mapped the way the Imperium maps itself — which is one reason
the Imperium finds it the most legible of its enemies, and not the reason it should worry least.
