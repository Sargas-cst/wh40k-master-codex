# Space Marine Homeworlds

## What a Fortress-Monastery Is {#fortress-monastery}

Space Marine Chapters are highly monastic as well as military organisations. Their members are
warrior-monks, and their fortress-monasteries are devoted both to battle and
worship.[^lex-fortress-monastery] That is the definition, and everything else about these worlds
follows from taking it literally.

Their siting is a matter of arrangement rather than right. Fortress-monasteries are usually
based on Imperial worlds, or on deserted moons or asteroids; for fleet-based Chapters the
flagship serves as a mobile fortress-monastery. The locations of some Chapter planets are
hidden, others well known. The preferred location is an Imperial world, of which the Chapter
Master is sometimes also the planetary governor — but in other cases he strikes a deal with the
existing governor, **paying a tithe in return for the land they occupy**. Space Marines are
usually welcomed, since their presence deters invasion.[^lex-fortress-monastery]

That detail is easy to skip and worth stopping on. A Chapter with the firepower to sterilise a
continent rents its ground from a planetary governor. The Imperium's most autonomous military
force holds its home by agreement rather than by conquest, and pays for it. Compare Ultramar in
[[II.T2.Ch6]], where the Chapter Master *is* the ruler of five hundred worlds and the
tithe runs the other way. The two arrangements are the extremes of the same relationship.

The internal plan is documented in unusual detail, using The Fang as the worked example, and it
reads as a self-sufficient city rather than a barracks. Living space is organised in **cells**
arranged in blocks of ten — one block per Company, each block mirroring a squad's ten
members.[^lex-fortress-monastery] Alongside them: the **Assimularum**, the largest room besides
the Great Hall, vast enough for the entire Chapter to assemble for mass meetings, ceremonies and
feasts; the **Reclusiam**, partitioned off it, used only for cult activity and holding the
Chapter's relics and battle trophies under a Reclusiarch; the **Refectory**, where a strict diet
augmented by complex bio-chem is eaten in silence after prayers by the senior brother
present.[^lex-fortress-monastery]

The architecture is the doctrine made physical. A Chapter's living quarters are laid out in the
shape of its squads, so a Marine's address is his position in the battle order. Its largest room
exists so that all thousand can stand in one place at once, and the room next to it is a church.
Meals are silent. The Codex Astartes of [[IV.T4.Ch13]] is not just an organisational document;
it is a floor plan.

Other chambers record what the institution needs and what it fears. The **Apothecarion** is an
advanced medical facility used as hospital, research site and bio-lab — and it is where warriors
are surgically transformed into superhuman Marines.[^lex-fortress-monastery] The **Librarium** is
more than a book collection: it is the central repository of records and the main seat of
communications, where the Chapter's astropaths spend most of their time, and from an armoured
room within it the base's defences are controlled.[^lex-fortress-monastery] The **Penitorium**
holds battle-brothers guilty of transgression as remedial punishment, while the **Dungeon**,
deep beneath the Apothecarion and lined with reinforced diamantine, holds common prisoners — who
do not stay long before being taken to the Apothecarion for
interrogation.[^lex-fortress-monastery] And the **Solitorium** sits *outside* the fortress: a
remote place for contemplation, occupied for anything from days to years, where Marines seeking
promotion are expected to search their souls and endure self-deprivation for weeks to prove
their worth.[^lex-fortress-monastery]

Two prisons, graded by whether the prisoner is a brother. A hospital that doubles as an
interrogation room. And a hermitage outside the walls that functions as a promotion board. The
implants themselves are [[IV.T4.Ch12]].

The defensive figures are given with the precision of an inventory: 32 defence lasers
permanently active with a further 88 that can be brought to bear within four days; 317 separate
weapons turrets around the perimeter, each mounting four lascannons and a missile launcher; over
400 missile silos, all controlled from the armoured room in the
Librarium.[^lex-fortress-monastery]

!!! disputed "Recorded conflict — space-wolves-fortress-world"

    The published description of a typical fortress-monastery, transcribed from the earliest
    Space Wolves material, repeatedly locates the fortress **on the world of Lucan** — its
    generatorum, its missile silos and its Great Hall are all described as being
    there.[^lex-fortress-monastery] The Space Wolves' fortress-monastery is otherwise the
    Fang, on Fenris.[^lex-fenris]

    The sources record the origin of the discrepancy: when the Space Wolves were introduced,
    their fortress-monastery was said to be sited on Lucan, whose atmosphere is described as
    airless and whose fortress is silent and without mirth. Lucan is separately identified as
    a VI Legion fief world of the Great Crusade era.[^lex-fenris]

    This codex uses the chamber-by-chamber description as what a fortress-monastery contains,
    which is how it is presented, and does not treat Lucan as the Space Wolves' home.

## Fenris {#fenris}

Fenris is the homeworld of the Space Wolves and of their fortress-monastery, **the Fang** — the
Aett in the Fenrisians' own tongue — and it lies relatively close to the Eye of
Terror.[^lex-fenris] Its classifications are Adeptus Astartes Homeworld, Feral World and Death
World at once,[^lex-fenris] and its population is recorded at roughly **3,400,000** before the
Siege of the Fenris System.[^lex-fenris]

Its defining feature is an orbit. Fenris travels an elliptical path around a pale star its
inhabitants call the **Wolf's Eye**, spending most of the year in winter, far from the dim
sun.[^lex-fenris] In summer the volcanoes erupt, burning great areas with lava, churning the
seas and spreading floods and tidal waves; as the long winter comes the temperature drops so far
that most of the planet ices over, giving it the appearance of a snowball from
orbit.[^lex-fenris]

Imperial astronomers recorded how narrow the margin is: **if Fenris's solar apsis were a few
hundred thousand kilometres closer, or the close orbital pass a few days longer, the planet
would be uninhabitable.**[^lex-fenris] At the peak of summer the sub-oceanic crust breaks and
twists, the seas boil, and the skies fill with ash from new islands rising and old ones being
subsumed. So violent is the pull of the star that the planet teeters on its axis, and day and
night are not of regular lengths — a summer night might last a week, the next only a few
hours.[^lex-fenris]

!!! disputed "Recorded conflict — fenrisian-year-length"

    Fenris orbits its sun **once every three to four Terran years**,[^lex-fenris] or one
    Fenrisian year is **roughly two Terran years**.[^lex-fenris]

    The article carries both, the second introduced with "however some say". Both trace to
    published sources. Since a Fenrisian's age, a Space Wolf's seniority and the interval
    between summers all depend on the figure, the discrepancy is not cosmetic.

The geography follows from the orbit, and it explains the society completely. Apart from a single
permanently land-locked area, Fenris is a world of shifting geography and mostly
ocean.[^lex-fenris] Asteroid strikes, mutable coastlines and island masses that rise or sink
swiftly have left the population as **largely nomadic barbarian tribes**, constantly seeking
secure territory, with skirmishes and feuds over land common between
them.[^lex-fenris] Land is the most precious commodity on the planet.[^lex-fenris]

That is the whole of Fenrisian culture derived from a physical fact. The Space Wolves are not
barbarians by temperament or by choice; they recruit from a population that cannot build cities
because the ground it stands on sinks. Compare Macragge in [[II.T2.Ch6]], where a stable planet
produced an administrative culture and a Primarch who wrote a legal code. The setting's two most
opposed Chapters are opposed because of orbital mechanics.

The one exception proves it. **Asaheim**, the only continent that remains constant, stands at the
northern pole on the crest of the world, surrounded by cliffs thousands of feet high that
separate it from everything else — and the tribesmen consider it the land of the
Gods.[^lex-fenris] On it stand the Seven Great Peaks, and in their centre the
Fang.[^lex-fenris] Among them is **Krakgard**, the Space Wolves' funeral mount, said to be
possessed by the whispering voices of brooding spirits.[^lex-fenris]

The Chapter lives on the only piece of Fenris that does not move, above cliffs no tribesman can
climb, and the tribes below have concluded that gods live there. They are not wrong about the
inaccessibility. The Space Wolves carefully watch their tribal brethren but never appear openly
among them.[^lex-fenris]

Recruitment works by a mechanism the sources state plainly: candidates are drawn mainly from
young warriors who **fall in battle with grievous wounds**, or who perform prodigious feats of
arms. They are taken from their tribes, their wounds treated if necessary, and brought to the
Fang to train as Aspirants in camps around Asaheim.[^lex-fenris] Apart from Aspirants, the only
permanent human inhabitants of Asaheim are the **Kaerls**, the Chapter's serfs, who also serve
as the planet's Defence Force.[^lex-fenris]

Note the selection criterion. The Space Wolves take the dying. A Fenrisian's route to the
Chapter runs through a mortal wound, which means the Chapter's recruiting ground is a battlefield
and its selection process is triage. The Fenrisians consider themselves blessed for the
chance.[^lex-fenris]

The religion is what a population under those conditions would be expected to produce. Fenrisians
worship the planet itself as **Mother Fenris**, giving offerings to the soil in the belief that
it wards off evil spirits, and their shamans carve runes to harness the natural energy of the
world.[^lex-fenris] Their pantheon is the **Aesir**, their supreme deity the
**Allfather** — who is the Emperor of Mankind.[^lex-fenris] Those who die of old age are held to
be cowards, condemned to the court of the Erlking.[^lex-fenris] The Great Wolf of the Space
Wolves bears the title **High King of Fenris** and is the planet's de facto
governor.[^lex-fenris]

A world that thinks dying in bed is a moral failure, whose god is the Emperor under a local name,
governed in fact by the Chapter Master. The Imperial Creed of [[IV.T3.Ch8]] did not have to
convert Fenris; it only had to supply a name for a god the Fenrisians already had. Whether that
counts as conversion is [[IV.T3.Ch10]]'s question.

Two features record older and worse things. The first colonists built great **cavern cities**
under the northern pole out of the remains of their colony ships; they became shelters during
the Age of Strife and were then abandoned for reasons that are not known, with worship of Chaos,
mutation and the unleashing of ancient weapons all proposed. The settlers who remained inside
mutated into monsters called **Nightgangers**, and deep within the caverns is an ancient temple
of Tzeentch that the Nightgangers built. Leman Russ forbade anyone from
entering.[^lex-fenris] The second is **Syrtyr's Door**, a volcanic cavern with mystical
properties that the people of Fenris consider the door to the Underverse[^lex-fenris] — and
during the summer solstice, the sources record, that door opens, and Runepriests may ascend
Krakgard to commune with the spirits of the dead and the world spirit of
Fenris.[^lex-fenris]

The Chapter that recruits from a feral population has a sealed Chaos temple under its own
planet's pole and a Warp aperture that opens on schedule. The Space Wolves' answer to both was a
prohibition. Their Runepriests, meanwhile, use the second one deliberately — a practice
[[I.T2.Ch7]] would classify and the Inquisition of [[IV.T7.Ch23]] would want to hear about.

!!! disputed "Recorded conflict — fenris-segmentum"

    Fenris lies in **Segmentum Solar**, or in **Segmentum Obscurus**.[^lex-fenris]

    Two editions of Codex: Space Marines place it in Obscurus; most other references give
    Solar, and the article flags the disagreement itself. Since the Great Rift left Segmentum
    Solar in Imperium Sanctus and much of Obscurus in Imperium Nihilus — see
    [[II.T1.Ch2]] — the two placements put the Space Wolves' homeworld on opposite sides of the
    galaxy's dividing line.

## Baal and Its Moons {#baal}

Baal is the homeworld of the Blood Angels and the site of their fortress-monastery, the **Arx
Angelicum**.[^lex-baal] It is classified as both an Adeptus Astartes Homeworld and a Death
World, and its recorded population before the Devastation was
**122,000**.[^lex-baal] Recruits are taken from all three worlds of the system: Baal itself and
its two moons, **Baal Primus** and **Baal Secundus**.[^lex-baal]

The system's condition is the result of a war, and the sequence is exact. In ancient days Baal
and its moons had earth-like atmospheres — Baal itself always a world of red rust deserts, but its
moons close to paradises. As the Dark Age of Technology ended in the Age of Strife, **civil war
erupted between the world and its colonised moons**, leading to the nuclear devastation of all
three.[^lex-baal] Ancient viral and nuclear weapons turned the once idyllic worlds into toxic
wastelands; the survivors became scavengers, constantly moving and warring to preserve what they
gathered.[^lex-baal]

A parent world and its own colonies destroying each other with weapons of the golden age is the
Age of Strife in miniature, and it is worth setting beside Mars in [[II.T2.Ch5]], where the same
era produced a religion, and Macragge in [[II.T2.Ch6]], where it produced nothing worse than
isolation. The same catastrophe, three outcomes, decided entirely by what each system did to
itself.

By the time Sanguinius arrived — falling upon Baal Secundus after the Primarchs were scattered by
the Gods of Chaos[^lex-baal] — Baal and its moons were a wasteland dominated by a tribe called
**The Blood** and by mutant hordes. Sanguinius defeated the mutants and the people of The Blood
came to ascendancy.[^lex-baal] Baal Secundus has since been a nuclear-blasted desert with deadly
radiation, forcing its human tribes to build cumbersome radiation suits to
survive.[^lex-baal] Sanguinius is [[VIII.T2.Ch5]].

The planet's own history has an older layer, and the sources present it as fact rather than
legend. Before humanity, Baal was inhabited by an unknown xenos race who — like the future Blood
Angels — were influenced by the powers of the Warp, and who worshipped a **golden angel** and an
**angel of darkness** locked in eternal struggle within the Immaterium. The humans of Baal came
to replace that race and to be influenced by the same two gods. **Every time a son of Baal
protects the innocent the Golden Angel grows stronger; every time one succumbs to the Black Rage
the Angel of Darkness grows stronger.** And when Mankind vanishes from the galaxy, another race
will take their place for these Warp entities.[^lex-baal]

That is one of the setting's most complete statements of how Warp gods actually work, and it is
recorded on a planetary article rather than a theological one. The entities are older than the
species that worships them and will outlast it; what they feed on is the behaviour of whoever
happens to be there. The Blood Angels' gene-flaw is not a curse laid on them — it is a diet. Set
it against the Ork gods of [[I.T4.Ch16]], where the precedence question is left open: here the
answer is given, and the answer is that the gods came first and the worshippers are
interchangeable. The Black Rage is [[IV.T4.Ch15]]'s subject.

Baal's transformation into a fortress came by invitation. After unification with the Imperium
during the Great Crusade, the empty rad-scoured world was transformed by the Mechanicum of
Anvillus at Sanguinius's own request; where no unmodified person could tread, the tech-adepts
built a manufacturing site the envy of many smaller Forge Worlds, supplying much of the munitions
and technical needs of the IXth Legion.[^lex-baal] Baal also warranted a large standing guard,
mainly the Crimson Paladins.[^lex-baal] During the Heresy it became a central loyalist hub under
Warden Arkhad, sheltered many of the Shattered Legions, and endured an extended
siege.[^lex-baal]

The Arx Angelicum is now **the only settlement on the world**.[^lex-baal] Around it: the
**Chalice Mountains**, a vast stretch of calderas; the **Golden Sarcophagus**, tomb of
Sanguinius; the **Sepulchre of Heroes**, a memorial running the length of the fortress; and
**Ruberica**, the Heart of Baal — a deep underground cavern sacred to the people of the planet,
lined with tall hexagonal pillars of pure bloodstone, a place of great psychic resonance which
seems to have a mind of its own and is known to repair itself when
damaged.[^lex-baal]

A cavern that heals itself, on the planet whose Chapter cannot heal its own gene-seed. The
sources draw no connection and neither does this codex; it notes only that the Blood Angels'
homeworld contains a psychically resonant structure that regenerates, and that nobody has
explained it.

The recent history is one long defeat. Baal was threatened by the Ork Warlord Big Skorcha in
798.M41 and held.[^lex-baal] In 999.M41 it was threatened again, by the Tyranids of Hive Fleet
Leviathan.[^lex-baal] After the Thirteenth Black Crusade and the formation of the Great Rift,
Baal became stranded from the greater Imperium as one of the key worlds of Imperium Nihilus — and
the Tyranids continued to advance on it.[^lex-baal] The assault culminated in a battle that
nearly saw the planet and both moons consumed; the Blood Angels were saved only by the arriving
Indomitus Crusade, and Guilliman immediately set to rebuilding both Baal and the
Chapter.[^lex-baal]

The cost is recorded in the geography. Baal Secundus was badly damaged and Baal Primus rendered
**completely barren**, and after the battle the skulls of the dead Tyranids were found arranged
on its surface into the symbol of Ka'Bandha. Once the Chapter had been restored with Primaris
reinforcements, Blood Angels ships bombarded the moon with macrocannons, lances and torpedoes,
erasing the daemon's name from its surface.[^lex-baal]

A Chapter shelling its own dead moon to scrub a signature off it. The Devastation is
[[VII.T4.Ch13]]; that Baal is now the acting capital of the Dark Imperium under Dante's regency
is [[II.T1.Ch2]]'s — and the world holding that office is a rad-poisoned rock with one building on
it and a moon its owners have bombed.

## Nocturne {#nocturne}

Nocturne is the homeworld of the Salamanders, and its situation is mechanical rather than
historical: it is a binary planetary system whose over-sized moon, **Prometheus**, circles it
erratically, causing massive tectonic stress. There are vast chains of volcanoes and frequent
earthquakes, destroying what little the people build.[^lex-nocturne] The enormous levels of
stellar radiation have given most of the world's life a dark, obsidian-like
appearance — including its people.[^lex-nocturne] Nocturne is classed both an Astartes Homeworld
and a Death World.[^lex-nocturne]

Once every fifteen Terran years — one Nocturnean year — the two worlds approach so closely that
Nocturne is almost torn to pieces. This is the **Time of Trial**. Vast waves crash across the
seas, thousands of volcanoes explode, further blotting out the weak haze of the sun, and
earthquakes constantly ravage the land. All life is sent reeling, towns collapse, and many people
die. Then a terrible winter sets in for the next quarter of a year: the young freeze and most, if
not all, of the livestock die, unable to withstand the cold as they had the
heat.[^lex-nocturne]

Fenris's catastrophe arrives with the seasons; Nocturne's arrives on a fifteen-year clock and is
survivable only because everyone knows when it is coming. What the Salamanders built out of that
predictability is the most humane arrangement in this Volume.

Although most of Nocturne's populace is tribal, there are seven major population centres, and
they are called the **Sanctuary Cities**. During the Time of Trial they throw open their gates
and give refuge to the vast pilgrimages of tribes fleeing the elements.[^lex-nocturne] Each city
contains a Chapter Bastion of the Salamanders and is dedicated to one of the Chapter's
Companies.[^lex-nocturne]

Their siting is a small history of how knowledge accumulates in the Imperium. The cities stand
on the most seismically stable areas of Nocturne, positions **first divined by tribal shamans and
latent psykers through commune with the earth**; later, geological surveyors improved the
accuracy of those divinations; and finally, on the arrival of the Outlander — the
Emperor — great void shields and vast walls of adamantium and ceramite were
added.[^lex-nocturne]

Three technologies, in order: psychic intuition, survey instruments, and void shields, each
building on the last rather than replacing it. It is the only passage in this Volume where the
Imperium is shown *improving* on inherited knowledge instead of merely preserving or losing it.
Compare the Mechanicus of [[II.T2.Ch5]], whose whole method is recovery.

And then the practice that distinguishes the Chapter entirely. Because of the Salamanders' ethos
of protecting humanity, they **live among the people of Nocturne**: with the exception of
Neophytes and the First Company Firedrakes, most seasoned Salamanders maintain private domiciles
throughout Nocturne and the Sanctuary Cities. In times of war the companies are gathered by a
host of sacred teleportation translation points across the
planet.[^lex-nocturne]

Set this against everything in this Chapter. On Terra the population has feared all Space Marines
for ten thousand years. On Fenris the Chapter lives above unclimbable cliffs and never appears
openly among the tribes. On Baal there is one building on the whole world. On Nocturne the
Astartes have houses in the towns, and are teleported out when there is a war. The Salamanders
have solved the problem every other Chapter's geography embodies, and they solved it by moving
in.

The world's spiritual centre is a volcano. **Mount Deathfire** is the largest and deadliest,
described as the heart of the world and its lifeblood, of profound sacred significance to natives
and Chapter alike, and simultaneously the single biggest threat to the continuation of life on
Nocturne.[^lex-nocturne] Beneath it lies a vast undercroft of caverns and tunnels dating from
before Vulkan's time, which **no explorer or scholar of the current era has been able to map in
full** — though it is said Vulkan alone navigated its whole extent, leaving mysterious chambers
locked in its depths.[^lex-nocturne] Deep in its base is the **Pyreum**, a vast cavern created by
the first Master of the Forge and girded in ceramite, where the Chapter's dead are returned to
the mountain: chained to a ceramite-coated pyre slab and lowered into the lava flow by two
Astartes who pass red-hot chains through their hands until the symbols of the forge — flame, anvil
and hammer — are branded into them.[^lex-nocturne]

The mountain that will eventually kill the planet is also the crematorium, the seminary and the
reliquary. The Salamanders' funeral rite requires the pallbearers to be burned. Vulkan is
[[VIII.T2.Ch5]]; the Promethean Creed, [[IV.T4.Ch15]].

Nocturne's recent history is a shorter list than Baal's but no gentler. The Death Guard invaded
during the Horus Heresy in the Battle of Nocturne.[^lex-nocturne] In 761.M41 the daemon engines
of the Soulsmelter assaulted the world and its Great Crucible, and in response the Chapter Master
sanctioned the opening of his master reliquaries.[^lex-nocturne] In 975.M41 Nocturne and
Prometheus came under attack by the Dragon Warriors.[^lex-nocturne]

That middle entry is the Chapter in one line. Faced with daemon engines, the Salamanders'
recorded response was to unseal the vault and hand out the relics. Elsewhere in the Imperium a
relic is an object of veneration; on Nocturne it is a tool, and the distinction between the two
is the Promethean Creed.
