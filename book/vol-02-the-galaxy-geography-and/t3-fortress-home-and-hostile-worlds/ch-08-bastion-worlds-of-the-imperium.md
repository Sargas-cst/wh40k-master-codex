# Bastion Worlds of the Imperium

## Cadia — A Society Built as a Weapon {#cadia}

Cadia *was* a terrestrial Fortress World on the edge of the nebula known as the Eye of Terror,
situated in the Cadian Gate — the only stable, permanent route into and out of the Eye large
enough to allow the passage of battlefleets. For that reason it was heavily fortified against
Chaos and a frequent target of Abaddon's Black Crusades.[^lex-cadia] Everything about the
planet, down to the way its children were raised and its dead were buried, follows from that one
sentence of geography.

Its most unusual feature predates humanity entirely. **Five thousand, eight hundred and ten
intact pylons** were spread across Cadia's surface, with a further two thousand either buried or
in various states of disrepair. No two were identical in design, and each was machined with slim,
winding tunnels no wider than a man's head.[^lex-cadia] Attempts by the Adeptus Mechanicus to
study them failed miserably: their surfaces were totally impenetrable to auspex scanning, and
efforts to map the tunnels using servitors resulted in missing probes. It was nonetheless widely
believed that they were the reason the Cadian Gate existed at all.[^lex-cadia] Access was
generally forbidden, and being allowed to visit was a rare privilege for anyone below sufficient
military rank.[^lex-cadia]

Nearly eight thousand alien structures on a planet the Imperium had fortified as its front door,
which its own tech-priests could not scan, whose interiors swallowed every probe sent in, and
which the Imperium correctly suspected were holding the Eye of Terror shut. Its response was to
build a fortress on top of them and restrict the tours. The pylons' Necron origin is
[[VI.T1.Ch2]]; the material itself, [[I.T1.Ch4]].

    Each pylon rose **precisely half a kilometre in height and extended a quarter-kilometre
    into the ground**, its tunnels exactly two hundred and fifty metres
    long.[^lex-cadia] Elsewhere the pylons are **one kilometre tall, half a kilometre under
    the earth, and half a kilometre on each side**.[^lex-pylon]

    Every dimension in the first set is exactly half its counterpart in the second, and both
    trace to the same chapter of the same novel; the first carries a second citation as well.
    One reading is a mistranscription and nothing available here settles which. The codex
    treats the dimensions as unresolved and uses the pylon **count** — which only the Cadia
    article gives — without reference to their size.

There is a detail about the pylons that turns them from architecture into machinery. During the
Thirteenth Black Crusade, as the warp storm **Baphomael** expanded to engulf the edges of the
Cadian System, the pylons simultaneously began resonating with an almost imperceptible
vibration, and microscopic fractures appeared on their surfaces. They were found to be
resonating **at an amplitude similar to that produced by a Gellar Field** in their effort to
fight back against the encroaching storm.[^lex-cadia]

The Gellar Field is the thing that keeps a single ship's crew from being eaten alive in transit;
[[I.T1.Ch3]] holds it. Cadia's pylons were doing the same job for a star system, automatically,
without anyone operating them — and cracking under the load. The Imperium had spent ten thousand
years garrisoning a planet without understanding that the planet was the shield.

Cadia's society is the most complete example in this codex of a civilisation with one purpose.
As a Fortress World, **its entire population belonged to some branch of the Imperial
military**[^lex-cadia] — 71.75% of the total under arms, with a birth rate equal to the
recruitment rate.[^lex-cadia] Cadian children were taught to strip, reassemble and shoot a
lasgun before they were taught to read. Before puberty they were sent to survive alone on the
isolated islands of the Caducades Sea during the **Month of Making**, and by the age of ten most
could perform infantry field drills and were proficient in killing.[^lex-cadia] All Cadians
trained in the defence forces, joining the Youth Armies — the **Whiteshields** — to drill
alongside regulars and fight mock battles in the wilderness.[^lex-cadia]

A birth rate set equal to a recruitment rate is a population functioning as a supply line. The
figure to the second decimal place is the tell: somebody was measuring. And the Cadian Shock
Troopers this produced are considered among the finest soldiers in the entire Imperium, earning
the respect even of the Adeptus Astartes, and are widely emulated for their discipline,
marksmanship and stoic sense of self-sacrifice.[^lex-cadia] They are treated in [[IV.T5.Ch17]].

The institutions match. Cadia was under **permanent martial law**.[^lex-cadia] The role of
planetary governor was a military one — the **Governor Primus**, also head of Cadian High
Command — and in emergencies, if he were incapacitated, a special rank of **Lord Castellan of
Cadia** existed which by common consent could take over as
commander-in-chief.[^lex-cadia] There were **no Arbites on Cadia**: policing fell to the Cadian
Interior Guard.[^lex-cadia] One in every ten Cadians, regardless of their skills, was assigned
to that Interior Guard, with the result that some of the finest soldiers spent their entire
careers at home.[^lex-cadia] And because of the Eye's proximity, Cadia also maintained an
**Internal Guard** of Ordo Malleus Inquisitors permanently seconded to the Cadian military, who
made frequent use of Sanctioned Psykers.[^lex-cadia]

Look at what those four provisions do together. The Adeptus Arbites — the Imperium's own police
force, treated in [[IV.T1.Ch2]] — was simply not present, its function absorbed by the army. The
Inquisition was not an external oversight body but a permanent unit in the order of battle.
Social class on Cadia was dictated by military rank, which made the upper echelons of command
highly classist, with weight placed on descent from a military family as much as on
competence.[^lex-cadia] The world the Imperium chose as its shield against Chaos had no civilian
authority of any kind, and its officer corps had turned into an aristocracy.

The militarisation reached into things that had no military purpose. Civilian clothing
incorporated camouflage patterns, available in a range of urban and wilderness colourings, and
the dwellings of both rich and poor were armoured against attack.[^lex-cadia] Cities — **Kasrs**
in the local dialect — were fortresses in their own right, each protected by void shields capable
of withstanding days upon days of punishing orbital bombardment, encircled by rings of massive
earthworks studded with gun emplacements, separated by an inner moat crossed by hydraulic bascule
bridges each guarded by a heavily armed barbican.[^lex-cadia] Most Cadians have purple eyes, the
genetic heritage of the planet.[^lex-cadia]

And then there is the practice that says most about the place. The constant death toll from Chaos
incursions, combined with limited land, produced the **Law of Decipherability**. Every graveyard
on Cadia was maintained by local priests who routinely checked the tombstones of the honoured
dead for legibility. When a section of a cemetery was deemed illegible, those graves were exhumed
and the bones added to a communal pit — because their names were no longer known, the need to
honour their memory had passed, and the space could be reused.[^lex-cadia]

That is a formal legal instrument for forgetting the dead, administered by clergy, justified by
land shortage. It is the Imperium's attitude to its own people rendered in statute: remembrance
is a service with a maintenance requirement, and when the lettering wears out the obligation
lapses. Cadia produced the finest soldiers in the galaxy and could not spare the ground to keep
them in.

The end came in a single move. During the final stages of the Thirteenth Black Crusade, the Magos
Belisarius Cawl worked with the Necron Lord Trazyn to **fully activate the pylons and push the
Eye of Terror back**. Abaddon collided the debris of the Blackstone Fortress *Will of Eternity*
into Cadia's surface, destabilising the pylons as they were awakening; in a catastrophic chain
reaction the pylons were destroyed, and Cadia was dragged into the Warp.[^lex-cadia]

The planet was not conquered. It was used as a component, and the component was broken in the act
of being switched on for the first time in ten thousand years. Cadia's fall is
[[III.T5.Ch22]] and [[VII.T2.Ch7]]; what it opened, [[II.T1.Ch2]].

!!! disputed "Recorded conflict — cadia-destruction-extent"

    How thorough Cadia's destruction was is unclear. The world has been graphically depicted
    as an asteroid field, and has produced at least one named asteroid — yet some presumably
    significant portion of its mass must have remained intact in order to form the daemon
    world it became.[^lex-cadia]

    The article raises this against itself. The codex records it as an open question rather
    than choosing a reading, because both depictions are in current use.

What remains is not a ruin but an inversion. As a daemon world, Cadia's remains were radically
altered into an ever-shifting wilderness of daemonic beasts and horrific swamps **so repulsive
that even the Iron Warriors found them overly grotesque**. The Black Legion built a fortress
there called the Oubliette to rule from, and the Iron Warriors claimed it after their own
conquest of the planet.[^lex-cadia]

The world whose entire population was raised from birth to hold the line against Chaos is now a
Chaos possession that has changed hands between two traitor Legions. Daemon worlds are
[[V.T2.Ch7]]; the Iron Warriors, [[V.T3.Ch9]].

## Armageddon — The World That Cannot Stop {#armageddon}

Armageddon is a Hive World, the fourth planet of the Armageddon System, and the site of some of
the largest wars in the Imperium's history.[^lex-armageddon] To the Orks it is known as
**Armour-Geddem**.[^lex-armageddon] Where Cadia was a fortress that fell once, Armageddon is a
world that has been fought over four times and is being fought over now.

Its origin is a secret the Imperium keeps from itself. Unknown to the greater Imperium,
**Armageddon was in fact originally the world of Ullanor** — made famous by the Ullanor Crusade in the
Emperor's own time. Fifteen hundred years after the Horus Heresy, during the War of the Beast,
Ullanor became the capital world of The Beast and was thought destroyed by the Mechanicum. In
truth the Mechanicum simply **teleported Ullanor into the Armageddon
System**.[^lex-armageddon]

The site of the Great Crusade's greatest triumph was moved across the galaxy and given a new
name, and the Imperium at large does not know. Ullanor is where Horus was raised to Warmaster;
[[III.T3.Ch13]] holds the Crusade and [[VII.T3.Ch10]] the War of the Beast. That the Imperium's
most notorious battlefield of the 41st Millennium is the same rock as its proudest of the 31st is
the kind of fact this setting buries in a subsection.

The planet's physical state is a working definition of a hive world. Its surface holds three main
bodies of land — the Fire Wastes around the north pole, the Deadlands around the south, and one
large equatorial landmass split into **Armageddon Prime** in the west and **Armageddon Secundus**
in the east, meeting in a region dominated by a massive jungle — separated by a world
ocean.[^lex-armageddon] The atmosphere is thick and has been contaminated by thousands of years
of industrial by-product, along with volcanic hydrogen sulphides and carbon oxides from decaying
organic matter and a lack of plant life. **An average human requires mechanical assistance to
breathe** — though the thicker atmosphere does counteract the planet's weak magnetic field,
protecting Armageddon from cosmic radiation.[^lex-armageddon]

A world where humans cannot breathe unaided, and whose industrial pollution is the only thing
shielding it from cosmic rays. The damage is now load-bearing. Compare Terra in [[II.T2.Ch4]] and
Mars in [[II.T2.Ch5]]; Armageddon is the pattern those two worlds set, applied to a planet with
no throne and no forges to justify it. Hive worlds are [[IV.T2.Ch4]].

The **First War** is the one the Imperium has worked hardest to erase, and the reason is the
aftermath rather than the fighting. In 444.M41 cultists worshipping Khorne rebelled during a
warp storm; a great space hulk, the *Devourer of Stars*, appeared over the planet carrying
**Angron, the Daemon Primarch of the World Eaters**. The daemonic legions pushed the defenders
back in an orgy of bloodshed until the Space Wolves and the Grey Knights arrived; with the Wolves
bolstering the defences, the Grey Knights teleported into the centre of the horde and banished
Angron and his army from the material world.[^lex-armageddon]

Then the Imperium turned on the survivors. To preserve the secret of the Daemon Primarch and the
Grey Knights, **the entire population of Armageddon, and all the surviving soldiers, were
rounded up, sterilised and sentenced to work camps for the rest of their lives**, while a new
population was brought in to resettle the planet.[^lex-armageddon] Several thousand Guardsmen
slipped through the containment, so the Inquisition escalated: many Imperial Guard troopships
were destroyed as they departed, by Grey Knights strike cruisers — and in the Tremayne sector
**three entire worlds were put to the sword** to ensure the silence of a single company of Storm
Troopers who had fought at Helsreach Hive alongside the Grey Knights.[^lex-armageddon] Logan
Grimnar, the Great Wolf, has never forgiven the Inquisition for that betrayal of the men and
women who fought for their world.[^lex-armageddon]

Three planets destroyed to keep one company quiet about a battle they won. That is the arithmetic
of the Imperium's information policy, and it is the sharpest illustration in this Volume of what
the Ordo Malleus of [[IV.T7.Ch25|Volume IV Title 7]] exists to do. Note also what it does to the previous Section: the
Armageddon that fought the later wars is not the same population as the Armageddon that fought
the first. The census figure the sources carry is explicitly flagged as taken from a hive census
of 690.M37 — grossly out of date, and at best an approximation of the population **before** the
First War, after which the population was replaced.[^lex-armageddon]

The **Second War**, in 941.M41, is the one the Imperium tells. A huge Ork force under the Warboss
**Ghazghkull Mag Uruk Thraka** arrived to find the Overlord, Herman von Strab, completely
unprepared — his pride and incompetence making matters worse, his defence piecemeal, and his
refusal to contact the wider Imperium for help absolute. When a Commissar named **Yarrick** defied
him and sent an astropathic call for aid, von Strab had Yarrick exiled to Hades
Hive.[^lex-armageddon]

The exile decided the war. Yarrick led a heroic defence of Hades that distracted Ghazghkull from
the planetary battles, infuriating the Ork into sending more and more forces against it until he
considered the fight a matter of personal pride and took command of the assault himself. Relief
came when Commander Dante of the Blood Angels led an Imperial army — Salamanders, Blood Angels and
Ultramarines alongside the Imperial Guard — took control of the defence, and **had von Strab
arrested for crimes against the Imperium**. Hades Hive fell, but the time Yarrick's stalemate
bought allowed a counterattack that broke the Ork invasion.[^lex-armageddon]

The planet was saved by an officer its governor had removed for insubordination, in the hive he
was banished to, because the enemy warlord took the insult personally. Yarrick is
[[VIII.T2.Ch7]]; Ghazghkull, [[VIII.T4.Ch11]]; Dante, [[VIII.T2.Ch6]].

The **aftermath** shows the Imperium learning the wrong lesson competently. Armageddon was
devastated, and fifty years later many hives still bore extensive damage — but because of its
strategic significance the Adeptus Terra ordered an extensive set of projects to rebuild and
reinforce the whole system. Ground and void defences were bolstered, the sector's naval command
was transferred to nearby St. Jowen's Dock and refitted to handle the largest battleships, and
three space stations were built to monitor the system's
edges.[^lex-armageddon] Rumours that Ghazghkull had fallen proved false: he retreated to the
Golgotha Sector and spent the next fifty years building his Waaagh! anew while Imperial officials
believed him utterly defeated.[^lex-armageddon] Pockets of Feral Orks remained on Armageddon
itself, plaguing the equatorial jungles and the ruins of fallen hives for years.[^lex-armageddon]
Von Strab escaped his jailers, and would later return.[^lex-armageddon]

Governance changed too, and the change is worth noting because it is the opposite of Cadia's.
Since the Second War the planet has been ruled by a **military council** of high-ranking officers
of the Astra Militarum, Navy and Munitorum, representatives of the Mechanicus and Ecclesiarchy,
and the governors of the major hives.[^lex-armageddon] Cadia's answer to permanent war was a
single military governor; Armageddon's was a committee of every Imperial institution present.
Both are what the Imperium does when a planet becomes too important to govern normally.

The **Third War** came fifty years after the Second, and the sources' summary of its outcome is
the most telling sentence in the article. The Imperium retained the world, but fighting continued;
Ghazghkull left and went on to plague neighbouring worlds, pursued by Yarrick with a full Black
Templars crusade behind him. **The Orks have come to regard Armageddon as a kind of Valhalla,
where they can always come to find a good fight** — and the Imperium has had to keep sending
troops to hold the planet while Ork reinforcements continue to flood
in.[^lex-armageddon]

Armageddon is no longer a strategic objective for either side. It is a venue. The Imperium
garrisons it because the Orks come, and the Orks come because the garrison is there, and neither
condition can end without the other. The Orks are [[VI.T3.Ch10]].

The rest is escalation. After the Great Rift opened, Armageddon found itself directly in the path
of the **Blood Crusade**, a vast legion of Khorne's daemons pouring into realspace; the continued
fighting attracted the World Eaters' battleship *Conqueror* at the head of a Khornate host, and
**Angron manifested a second time**, through the Red Angel's Gate deep in the Fire Wastes,
forcing a major intervention by Grey Knights, Space Wolves, Black Templars and Imperial Guard
before he was pushed back through it.[^lex-armageddon] Shortly afterwards Ghazghkull launched the
**Fourth War**: the shock of a Speedwaaagh! under Wazdakka Gutsmek ahead of the main force has
taken **four out of nine major hives on Armageddon**, and though the Imperium mounts a determined
resistance under Yarrick — with the arrival of Operation Imperator buying enough time to prevent
total loss — the war continues.[^lex-armageddon]

Angron came back to the same planet through a permanent Warp aperture in its northern wastes.
Whatever the Grey Knights did in 444.M41, and whatever the Imperium destroyed three worlds to
conceal, it did not close the door. The wars themselves are [[VII.T2.Ch5]].

## Vigilus — The Sentinel of the Gauntlet {#vigilus}

Vigilus is an Imperial world that is a key route through the Great Rift and has, as a result,
become heavily contested. Orks, Genestealers and Eldar as well as the forces of Chaos have
embedded themselves on the planet, and **it stands on the verge of
collapse**.[^lex-vigilus] Its classifications are stacked: Hive World, War World, Sentinel World
at once, in the Nachmund sub-sector of Segmentum Obscurus, with a recorded population of roughly
**167,000,000,000** and a tithe grade of Solutio Extremis.[^lex-vigilus]

Before the Cicatrix Maledictum it was simply a productive world: a vital source of manpower and
material, made up of vast hive cities and colossal forges broken by dust-bowl wastelands and
fortified reservoirs holding its scarce potable water.[^lex-vigilus] Its export was defensive
technology — thanks to an **STC device** found by local tech-priests, it could produce
bastion-class force fields, which became popular across the Imperium and remain a cornerstone of
its own defence.[^lex-vigilus] Those fields are psychically charged, and render comatose anyone
attempting to breach their boundaries, **enabling the defenders to slaughter them at
leisure**.[^lex-vigilus]

A working Standard Template Construct fragment is one of the most valuable objects in the
setting — treated in [[III.T2.Ch7]] — and Vigilus's happens to produce shields. The world that
became the Imperium's only door through the Rift was already, by accident of archaeology, the
world best equipped to hold one.

Its pre-Rift politics are worth recording because they are the Imperium's institutional rivalries
in miniature, on a single planet. Vigilus was ruled by Governor Lucienne Agamemnus IX, of a
dynasty that had overseen its affairs for millennia and had long ago signed a treaty with the
Mechanicum. Despite that power the dynasty was constantly subverted by the **Ecclesiarchy**, which
gained influence over the planet's PDF — the Vigilant Guard and Vigilant Creedsmen — and over local
Imperial Guard garrisons, and which brought in Adepta Sororitas enforcers to ensure its power was
not tested. All-out war between dynasty, Mechanicum and Ecclesiarchy was avoided only by a
combined body, the **Aquiliarian Council**.[^lex-vigilus]

Three Imperial institutions on one world, one of them importing armed nuns to deter the other
two, and a standing committee whose sole function is to stop them fighting. Nothing external was
required. Set that beside Mars in [[II.T2.Ch5]], where Imperial and Mechanicum troops exchanged
fire over a custody dispute: the Imperium's internal frictions are structural, and they follow
its institutions onto every planet they share. The Ecclesiarchy is [[IV.T3.Ch8]]; the Sororitas,
[[IV.T3.Ch11]].

After the Great Rift, Vigilus fell inside Imperium Nihilus and became one of the only stable
routes from one side of the Rift to the other, making it strategically
vital.[^lex-vigilus] What that means on the ground is refugees: the world has become a sanctuary
for those crammed into its hive-sprawls hoping to traverse the Nachmund
Gauntlet.[^lex-vigilus] It also became a popular Mechanicum stronghold of the Forge World Stygies
VIII, owing to its proximity to the Stygius Front.[^lex-vigilus]

Water is the measure of everything on Vigilus, and the sources record it as a caste system.
The planet is an arid dust bowl where water is extremely scarce, and **even the consumption of
water is a status symbol**. The poor drink a yellow, foul-tasting water drawn from deep
underground, *aqua subterra*. The elite drink water from melted icebergs, *aqua glacius*. The
Mechanicum population drinks *aqua meteoris*, harvested from orbiting asteroids. The Ecclesiarchy
drinks *aqua sanctus*, purified with holy oils.[^lex-vigilus]

Four grades of drinking water, one per class, with the two great institutions supplying their own
from off-world and from the chapel respectively. It is the most economical description of Imperial
society in this codex: a hierarchy so complete that it reaches the glass in your hand, on a
planet where everyone is thirsty. Its sole moon, **Neo-vellum**, holds an Administratum Fortress
and a population of 17.2 billion.[^lex-vigilus]

Because of its position, Vigilus has fallen victim to a large number of invasions — Orks,
Genestealer Cults, Dark Eldar, Saim-Hann Eldar and Chaos.[^lex-vigilus] That list is the
Chapter's closing point. Cadia held one enemy at one door for ten thousand years and broke. On
Armageddon two enemies return on a cycle neither side can stop. Vigilus is besieged by five
different powers at once, and the Imperium cannot abandon it, because it is the way through. The
War of Beasts is [[VII.T4.Ch14]]; the Gauntlet it guards, [[II.T1.Ch2]].
