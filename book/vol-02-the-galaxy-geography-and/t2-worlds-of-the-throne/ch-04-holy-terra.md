# Holy Terra

## A Planet Made of Government {#hive-world}

Terra is the homeworld of Mankind, the resting place of the Emperor, and the most holy and
revered place in the Imperium — a world to which pilgrims flock from across the galaxy, where
even the barren soil the pilgrims tread is considered holy. The sources describe it as
effectively a temple the size of a planet.[^lex-terra] Known in antiquity as Earth, it is also
the capital world, and home to the main headquarters of the Adeptus Terra, the Administratum
and the Departmento Munitorum.[^lex-terra]

The physical facts are less devotional. Terra is classified a **hive world**, stripped long ago
of all forms of resources; its soil is utterly barren and its atmosphere a fog of pollution.
Massive labyrinthine edifices of state sprawl across the vast majority of the surface. Its
oceans boiled away long ago, though the Emperor created new artificial ones after the
Unification Wars.[^lex-terra] Many mountain ranges have been levelled — perhaps all of them
except the Himalayas, which remain all but untouched because of the laboratories said to lie
beneath them and the chambers of the Astronomican that course through the whole
range.[^lex-terra]

Read that sequence in order and the Imperium's relationship to its own homeworld becomes
plain. The seas were boiled off and replaced. The mountains were flattened. The one range left
standing was spared not out of reverence but because there is machinery inside it. Terra is not
a preserved birthplace; it is a site that has been consumed and then rebuilt as
administration. That the Imperium calls this holy is the theology of [[IV.T3.Ch8]] applied
to a strip-mined rock.

It is, despite the Heresy and the long Age of Strife before it, probably the most vastly
populated and built-up hive world in the Imperium — and beneath countless layers and millennia
of urban accretion, catacombs hold older cultures completely different from those on the
surface.[^lex-terra] Cultures, plural, and different in kind: humanity's homeworld contains
strata of humanity that the surface has forgotten.

!!! disputed "Recorded conflict — terra-population"

    The population of Terra is given as **200 billion** in one source and as
    **quadrillions** in two others.[^lex-terra]

    This is not a rounding disagreement — it spans four orders of magnitude. The lower figure
    comes from a video game's main menu; the higher from two novels. The codex records both
    and asserts neither, because nothing in the material lets the two be reconciled.

What the numbers cannot convey, one economic detail can. Most of Terra's population lives in
the most terrible squalor, their greatest hope that one of their offspring might be accepted
into the Adeptus Terra as a Menial, an adept of the lowliest sort.[^lex-terra] And a square
metre of land on Terra costs more than a palace on any other hive world; only the wealthiest
can afford to own even a small section of it.[^lex-terra]

Both halves of that are worth holding onto. The highest ambition available to a Terran is a
clerical job at the bottom of the bureaucracy — for a child, not for oneself. And the ground
itself is priced beyond reach, because it is holy. Sanctity has been converted into a property
market, and the people who live on the sacred soil cannot afford to stand on it.

## The Imperial Palace {#imperial-palace}

The Imperial Palace is both the palace of the Emperor and the capital city of the Imperium,
divided into the Inner and Outer Palaces.[^lex-imperial-palace] Seen from orbit it consists of
connected kilometres-high monolithic and pyramidal structures, marked with landing pads and
studded with defence weaponry.[^lex-imperial-palace] It is the seat of the Adeptus Terra,
overseen by the Adeptus Custodes.[^lex-imperial-palace]

Scale first, because the scale is the argument. The Palace's macro-habs and space ports break
through the atmosphere and rise into the void, while its sub-levels dig into Terra's bedrock,
in places reaching a depth of hundreds of miles. Its vaults, corridors, fastnesses, plazas and
chambers are so multitudinous that **no single record remains to list them all**, and the
techno-urbanic serf tribes, clan holdings and societal sub-nations dwelling within its walls
could populate entire star systems.[^lex-imperial-palace]

That last clause deserves emphasis. The Palace does not have inhabitants; it has *sub-nations*.
People are born, live and die inside a building without leaving it, in populations large enough
to settle worlds. And the building's own inventory has been lost — the Imperium cannot list the
rooms of the house its god lies in. Set that beside [[I.T1.Ch1]]: an institution that cannot
count its planets also cannot count its corridors.

The defences are recorded with unusual precision. The walls surrounding the Outer Palace are up
to **1,000 metres in width** and, in places, **1,500 metres in height** — taller than the
greatest structures of the rest of Terra. Gun-citadels run every hundred metres along their
length.[^lex-imperial-palace] Over them lies the **Aegis**, a multi-layered self-repairing void
shield web designed by the Adeptus Mechanicus, perhaps the most sophisticated of its kind in
the Imperium, encompassing the whole of the Palace.[^lex-imperial-palace]

A wall a kilometre thick is not a fortification against any weapon. It is a fortification
against time, and against the memory of a siege. The scars of the Horus Heresy were still
visible on Terra's ancient infrastructure ten thousand years later,[^lex-terra] and everything
about the Palace's defences is a response to one week in the 31st Millennium — treated at
[[III.T3.Ch16]].

!!! disputed "Recorded conflict — imperial-palace-extent"

    The Palace **covers the better part of the northern hemisphere**,[^lex-terra] or it is
    **built across a large part of the Asian continent**.[^lex-imperial-palace]

    A hemisphere and a continent differ by roughly an order of magnitude in area. Both
    statements are current on the wiki, in adjacent articles, each with its own citation. The
    Inner Palace is separately located over the ruins of
    Kathmandu,[^lex-imperial-palace] which is consistent with either.

There is one architectural distinction that governs everything else about the place. The Outer
Palace is as heavily urbanised and populated as any hive city — adepts numbering in the
billions work and reside there, alongside a large population of destitute
non-adepts.[^lex-imperial-palace] Beyond the Ultimate Wall, the Inner Palace's security is the
exclusive domain of the Adeptus Custodes.[^lex-imperial-palace] And an oddity: ancient laws
prohibit construction too close to the Outer walls, so the ground around them stands
**empty** — an extreme rarity on Terra.[^lex-imperial-palace]

On the most crowded world in the Imperium, where a square metre outprices a palace elsewhere,
there is a ring of vacant land around the Palace that no one may build on. It is the clearest
single illustration of what Terra's economy actually optimises for. Land is priceless, and the
most valuable land is deliberately left bare.

At the centre stand three things. The **Sanctum Imperialis**, the massive chamber housing the
Golden Throne and the Emperor's body, guarded by a select group of three hundred
Custodes.[^lex-terra] The **Eternity Gate**, the largest entry to it, approached by a
mile-long passage lined with the banners of thousands of long-dead Imperial heroes — the end of
the galactic pilgrimage trail and itself the most important pilgrimage site in the
Imperium.[^lex-terra] And the **Great Chamber of the Senatorum Imperialis**, the most famous
meeting place of the High Lords of Terra.[^lex-terra] The Throne is [[III.T3.Ch17]]; the Lords
are [[IV.T1.Ch1]].

Three hundred guards for the Emperor of Mankind, behind a wall a kilometre thick. The
Imperium's security model is entirely perimeter.

## The Institutions of the Throneworld {#institutions}

Terra is the heart of the Imperium and the home of many of its most important
organisations,[^lex-terra] and the pattern of where those organisations put themselves is more
revealing than the list.

Two of them sprawl across continents. The **Ecclesiarchal Palace**, headquarters of the
Ecclesiarchy, covers almost the entirety of Terra's southern continent, and is also home to one
of the two great convent-fortresses of the Adepta Sororitas.[^lex-terra] After it and the
Imperial Palace, the third largest site on Terra is **Skhallax City**, a Mechanicum
enclave.[^lex-terra] So the three largest structures on humanity's homeworld belong to the
state, the church, and the Machine Cult — and in that order of size. [[IV.T3.Ch11]] and
[[IV.T6.Ch19]] hold the latter two.

Two of them hide. The **Fortress of the Inquisition** is a sprawling complex built beneath the
south polar ice caps — deep underground, heavily warded, equipped with psyker-dampening devices
and null-fields. Only members of the Inquisition and sparse numbers of doomed prisoners may
enter. It is overseen by a senior Inquisitor known as **The Castellan**, a post that changes
every year to ensure no single man or woman can establish a powerbase over the
facility.[^lex-terra] The **Officio Assassinorum** is likewise based on Terra: its School of
Assassins and most of its temples are said to be there, though their actual locations are
closely guarded secrets.[^lex-terra]

The Castellan's annual rotation is the detail to keep. The Inquisition, an organisation whose
whole premise is that it answers to no one, has built into its own headquarters a term limit
designed to stop any of its members accumulating power there. It does not trust itself, and
the countermeasure is bureaucratic. That instinct runs through [[IV.T7.Ch23]].

Two of them exist to move things that are not physical. The **City of Sight** is the
headquarters of the Adeptus Astra Telepathica, and within it the **Conduit** is the centre of
Imperial transgalactic communication.[^lex-terra] The **Scholastia Psykana**, where Sanctioned
Psykers are trained, is also on Terra.[^lex-terra] Every astropathic message in the Imperium
passes through one building on one planet — the single point of failure described in
[[IV.T9.Ch29]].

And one runs *through* the planet. The **Forbidden Fortress** is built throughout the
Himalayas, and a single peak is carved to form the **Chamber of the Astronomican**, where ten
thousand psykers continuously power the beacon with their own life energies. Without it,
long-distance Warp travel would be impossible and the Imperium would
disintegrate.[^lex-terra] Connecting that machinery to the Golden Throne is the **Trench of the
Astronomican**, an enormous, seemingly bottomless trench lined with the machinery of the
Mechanicum, mounted with void shield arrays and gun batteries against attack or
sabotage.[^lex-terra] It has an ominous reputation on Terra.[^lex-terra]

This is why the Himalayas were the one range not levelled. The mountains that survived the
industrialisation of Terra survived because a cable runs through them from ten thousand dying
psykers to a dying man on a throne, and everything in [[II.T1.Ch1]] — the segmenta, the
sectors, the routes, the border where Navigators refuse to sail — is downstream of that cable.
Imperial geography has a physical origin point, and it is a trench under a mountain.

Two smaller entries complete the picture, and they concern who is *not* welcome. Terra is
nominally the homeworld of the Imperial Fists, but the Chapter rarely visits it, if at all,
basing its operations instead from its star fortress *Phalanx*.[^lex-terra] The reason is
recorded: during the Battle of Terra the Emperor's Children were particularly vicious towards
the population, and in the ten thousand years since, Terrans have retained a terror of **all**
Space Marines, loyalist and otherwise. For that reason only a handful are on Terra at any
time — among them the Wolfblade, a small unit of Space Wolves assigned as bodyguards to House
Belisarius of the Navis Nobilite.[^lex-terra]

An atrocity committed by one Legion in a single siege has kept the Emperor's own soldiers off
the Emperor's own planet for ten millennia. Institutional memory in the Imperium does not
distinguish between the traitors and the loyal; it remembers only the armour. The Navigators
who need those bodyguards keep their own ghetto on Terra — the **Navigator's Quarter**, said to
be on a massive island[^lex-terra] — and belong to [[IV.T9.Ch30]].

## Terra in the Age of the Rift {#noctis-terra}

Since the Heresy, Terra has largely stood as the untouched centre of the Imperium, though it
saw violence during the War of the Beast, the Beheading, the Siege of the Eternity Gate, the
Years of Madness and the Age of Apostasy.[^lex-terra] Ten thousand years after Horus's
betrayal it faced its greatest challenge since.

The Great Rift took the Astronomican dark, and on Terra the Noctis Aeterna lasted a little over
a month.[^lex-terra] What that month did to the planet is the best case study in this Volume of
what [[II.T1.Ch2]] meant at ground level. Most travel to Terra was paralysed and Warp energies
began to manifest on the planet, leading to widespread riots, starvation and madness. Though
formidably defended, Terra's defensive forces could not contain the social upheavals outside
key structures such as the Palace, and many hives fell into full-scale anarchy. Chaos Cults
began to operate openly and recruit from the desperate masses.[^lex-terra]

The Imperium's response was to give up most of the planet. At the behest of Inquisitorial
Representative Kleopatra Arx, the High Lords issued the **Arx Doctrine**, which saw much of
Terra ceded to mob rule while remaining Imperial forces reinforced the Palace, the Forbidden
Fortress and other key structures. Some **one quarter of Terra** was temporarily lost to
anarchy.[^lex-terra]

Note what the doctrine chose to hold: the Palace, and the mountain with the beacon in it. Faced
with losing the throneworld, the Imperium defended the Throne and the Astronomican and
abandoned the population. On the evidence of the previous Section that is not a failure of
priorities but an accurate reading of them — the two structures that make the Imperium possible
were held, and the people who make it holy were not.

It was not enough to prevent the worst. The Custodes and other forces ventured outside the
Palace walls to contain the heretics but could not stop the summoning of a great Khornate army
onto Terra's soil, and in the **Battle of Lion's Gate** the Throneworld faced its second major
invasion. The legions of Chaos were turned back by the Custodes, the Sisters of Silence, the
Grey Knights and the reborn Roboute Guilliman.[^lex-terra]

Twice in ten thousand years an enemy has stood on Terra, and both times the enemy came out of
the Warp rather than across space. The kilometre-thick walls have never been the relevant
defence.

What followed was not a victory parade. Guilliman took the mantle of Lord Commander of the
Imperium on Terra and oversaw a massive purge of the Imperial bureaucracy known as the
**Primarch's Scourge**, exposing many xenophile and Chaos cults as collateral damage spiralled
across the planet.[^lex-terra] Even so, it took many months to secure Terra outside the Palace
and other key zones. The demands of the Indomitus Crusade left most of the fighting to the
over-extended Custodes and Arbites, and the whole business proved very controversial among the
High Lords.[^lex-terra]

That controversy is the note to end on. The Imperium's capital had been overrun by daemons, a
quarter of it surrendered to mobs, and its bureaucracy purged by a returned demigod — and the
recorded institutional reaction was that the High Lords found the handling
*controversial*.[^lex-terra] Guilliman's return is [[III.T5.Ch24]]; the Scourge and the
politics of it, [[IV.T1.Ch1]]. There have also, recently, been Genestealer Cults spotted on
Terra, beginning the **Unthinkable War**[^lex-terra] — which is to say the throneworld is now
also an infestation site, and belongs to [[VI.T4.Ch15]] as much as to this Chapter.
