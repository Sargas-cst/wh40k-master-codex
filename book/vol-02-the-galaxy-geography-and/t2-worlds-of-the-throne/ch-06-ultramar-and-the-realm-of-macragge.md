# Ultramar & the Realm of Macragge

## An Empire Within an Empire {#five-hundred-worlds}

Ultramar is a quasi-autonomous realm of the Ultramarines and part of the greater Imperium of
Man. Unlike most Space Marine Chapters, the Ultramarines do not rule over a single world or
fortress-monastery but an entire sub-sector of space consisting of approximately five hundred
habitable planets — a realm the sources themselves describe as **"an empire within an
empire"**.[^lex-ultramar]

Known as the **Five Hundred Worlds** — not merely five hundred worlds but five hundred
*inhabited* ones — it lies in the southern reaches of the Eastern Fringe, within the Ultima
Sector of Ultima Segmentum, and is one of the farthest realms from the light of the
Astronomican, south of the T'au Empire and Ichar IV.[^lex-ultramar]

That last clause is the geographic fact that shapes the rest. Ultramar sits near the edge of
the beacon's reach described in [[II.T1.Ch1]], on the Eastern Fringe, adjacent to a hostile xenos
power. The most stable and prosperous human region in the Imperium is also one of its most
remote — and the two facts are connected. Distance from Terra is what made the autonomy possible.

The prosperity is documented in terms that are grim in context. Ultramar is considered a
progressive and prosperous region of the Imperium, and until recent disasters **the average
human life expectancy even managed to reach the mid-30s**.[^lex-ultramar]

That figure is the single most useful number in this Volume. The best-governed human realm in
the galaxy — self-sufficient, disciplined, protected by ten Space Marine Chapters — got its
citizens to their mid-thirties. Whatever the Imperium's other regions look like, this is the
ceiling. Every claim about Ultramar's exceptionalism should be read against it.

The society itself is described as unlike other Imperial manufacturing centres. Ultramar is
composed primarily of industrial worlds, but they lack the nightmarish toxic wastelands common
elsewhere.[^lex-ultramar] The population, raised in a warrior society that disdains those who do
not pull their weight, is disciplined, loyal and productive, living in balance with the local
ecosystem.[^lex-ultramar] With sprawling cities surrounded by extensive farmlands and oceans
teeming with fish, each planet is completely self-sufficient in raw materials and food, and is
the envy of many other worlds.[^lex-ultramar] Civil unrest is unusually rare compared to other
Imperial sectors, attributed to the high standard of living and to the discipline of each
citizen.[^lex-ultramar]

Set that beside Terra in [[II.T2.Ch4]] and Mars in [[II.T2.Ch5]], both of which have been
industrialised into poison. Ultramar proves the Imperium's ruin is a policy outcome rather than
a physical necessity. Worlds can be manufacturing centres and remain habitable. Most are not,
because most are not governed like this.

## Macragge {#macragge}

Macragge is the homeworld of the Ultramarines and the capital world of the stellar realm of
Ultramar,[^lex-macragge] and it holds a constitutional distinction found nowhere else. **Apart
from Terra and Mars themselves, it is the only planet in the Imperium allowed dominion over
other planets.**[^lex-macragge]

Three worlds in the galaxy may rule other worlds. Two of them are in the Sol System and hold
the Throne and the forges. The third is a Space Marine homeworld on the Eastern Fringe. That is
the whole of the exception, and the rest of this Chapter is an account of how it was earned and
what it costs.

Its history begins, like Mars's, with survival. Before the Great Crusade, Macragge was one of
humankind's lost colonies, governed by a pair of officials known as Consuls — a bleak but not
inhospitable world, part of a decayed star empire of ages past, inhabited since the Dark Age of
Technology.[^lex-macragge] Its industries survived the Age of Strife relatively intact and its
people retained an authoritarian but cohesive society. It had preserved a number of antiquated
short-range warp-capable craft usable for near-stellar transit, and continued building sub-light
spacecraft even during the most intense warp storms. This let Macragge maintain contact with
several neighbouring human-settled systems despite the storms — and so **retain the knowledge
that it was not alone in the darkness**.[^lex-macragge]

That is the origin of Ultramar, and it predates Guilliman. A world that never entirely lost
interstellar contact during the Age of Strife had neighbours it already knew and already traded
with. When the Primarch arrived and Ultramar formed, the network was there to be formalised. Set
against [[III.T2.Ch9]], where the rest of humanity was cut off world by world, Macragge is the
counterfactual: an Imperium built by a place that never went dark.

The Primarch's own story on the planet is the setting's clearest political parable, and it is
worth recording as the sources give it. The infant Roboute Guilliman landed on Macragge after
being taken from Terra by the Gods of Chaos, and was found and adopted by one of the Consuls,
Konor — becoming a prodigy, a skilled administrator and a military genius.[^lex-macragge] While
Guilliman was suppressing a bandit revolt in the mountains his father was usurped and poisoned
by the other Consul, Gallan. Konor, though a member of the nobility, had devoted himself to the
common people, instituting sweeping reforms that benefited the masses and undermined the
position of the idle rich.[^lex-macragge] Guilliman returned, defeated Gallan's forces, was
elected sole Consul, and following his father's example gradually rebuilt Macragge into a
meritocracy where everyone worked for the common good.[^lex-macragge]

The Emperor found him by accident. The Great Crusade carried the Emperor to Espandor in a
neighbouring system, whose people spoke of the astounding son of Consul Konor; he knew he had
found one of his sons and set out at once, and was surprised on arrival to find a society
prosperous, advanced and well defended.[^lex-macragge] The Primarchs are [[III.T3.Ch12]];
Guilliman himself, [[VIII.T2.Ch5]].

The planet's physical character matches its politics. Macragge is bleak but beautiful, dominated
by enormous mountain ranges uninhabitable for its human population, most of whom live in the
lowlands — yet revered as one of the most magnificent planets in the Imperium.[^lex-macragge] Its
two continents, Westenland and Guillimanos, rise above a **dust ocean covering four-fifths of
the surface**, home to species such as dust-krill; the dust seas absorb high amounts of moisture
and can be deadly to swim in, requiring protective gear even to approach
safely.[^lex-macragge] Precipitation is non-existent, daytime temperatures are very high and
fall to freezing overnight, and in warmer months the oxygen percentage drops due to increased
activity of oceanic bacteria.[^lex-macragge] Its recorded population is
**400,000,000**.[^lex-macragge]

The infrastructure is the tell. Compared with the complex and ancient cityscapes of Terra,
Macragge's is far more regulated, adhering to a system created by Guilliman himself: fertile land
allocated to agriculture, the population resident in designated centres, academic institutions
and martial training centres providing education, manufactorum complexes producing
munitions.[^lex-macragge] Fortifications around the poles and equator are complemented by
orbital platforms, defensive silos, void mines and patrolling warship fleets.[^lex-macragge]

A planet laid out to a plan by one man, and still running on that plan ten thousand years later.
Terra grew by accretion until no record of its rooms remained; Macragge was designed. The
difference between the two is the difference between the Imperium as it is and the Imperium as
Guilliman intended, and it is visible from orbit.

At the centre, atop the highest range — the Crown Mountains — stands the **Fortress of Hera**, the
Ultramarines' fortress-monastery, itself situated in the middle of Magna Macragge Civitas, the
capital and the grandest Imperial city in the eastern galaxy.[^lex-macragge] Within its walls
lies the **Shrine of Guilliman**, where the Primarch sat upon a marble throne inside a stasis
field until his resurrection in 999.M41 — one of the holiest pilgrimage sites in the
Imperium.[^lex-macragge] The Fortress also holds the Library of Ptolemy, the Sword Hall, the Hall
of Maxellus and Gallan's Rock.[^lex-macragge]

A rock named after the man who murdered the Primarch's adoptive father, kept inside the
Primarch's own fortress. Ultramar does not forget its founding crime; it files it.

## Government by Tetrarchy {#tetrarchy}

Ultramar's constitutional arrangements are the most elaborate in the Imperium outside Terra, and
they exist because the realm was twice resized.

On assimilation into the Imperium during the Great Crusade, Ultramar became a state within a
state, enjoying a large degree of autonomy that continues to this day. While all within it are
subjects of the Emperor, the Supreme Leader of Ultramar was always Guilliman.[^lex-ultramar]
Its type of government is recorded plainly as **military government**.[^lex-ultramar]

Below the Master of Ultramar sit the **Tetrarchs**. When the realm was five hundred worlds there
were four, operating from four key worlds; after the Heresy and Ultramar's drastic reduction the
post was abolished, and it has recently been reinstated by the reborn
Guilliman.[^lex-ultramar] The Stellar Realm is now divided into five sectors — North, South, East,
West and Centre — four of them ruled by a Tetrarch appointed by Guilliman personally, each with a
capital world.[^lex-ultramar]

- **Northern Sector**, based on the Forge World **Konor**; Tetrarch Severus Agemman, First
  Captain of the Ultramarines.[^lex-ultramar]
- **Southern Sector**, based on **Andermung**; Tetrarch Portan, Second Captain of the Genesis
  Chapter.[^lex-ultramar]
- **Western Sector**, based on **Protos**; Tetrarch Balthus, Captain of the Doom
  Eagles.[^lex-ultramar]
- **Eastern Sector**, based on **Vespator** — previously ruled by the Sotharan League, which
  Guilliman reconstituted around Vespator after Sotha was destroyed by Hive Fleet Kraken;
  approximately 86 planets, Tetrarch Decimus Felix, a Primaris Ultramarine and Guilliman's
  Equerry.[^lex-ultramar]
- **Central Sector**, under the direct rule of Macragge and its Lord, Marneus
  Calgar.[^lex-ultramar]

Note the composition. Two of the four Tetrarchs are not Ultramarines — they are officers of the
Genesis Chapter and the Doom Eagles. The governance of Ultramar is shared among the Chapters
descended from the Ultramarines rather than reserved to the Chapter itself, which makes the
realm a federation of successor Chapters as much as a Chapter's fief. Successor Chapters are
[[IV.T4.Ch15]].

Below the Tetrarchs are Imperial Commanders with connections to the Ultramarines. Each world of
Ultramar is ruled by feudal lords loyal to the Chapter Master of the Ultramarines — which is why
he is also styled **Lord Macragge**[^lex-ultramar] — and the most common form of government on
Ultramar's worlds is a feudal system led by noble houses.[^lex-ultramar] The government of each
world, besides supplying the Ultramarines with men and material, is directed towards the
prosperity of its populace.[^lex-ultramar] Above them all sits the **Council of Greater
Ultramar**, of which all current Tetrarchs are members.[^lex-ultramar] Internal security and
suppression of rebellion falls to a secretive agency, the **Vigil Opertii**, and the
**Praecental Guard**.[^lex-ultramar]

Two things are worth holding together there. Ultramar's worlds are directed towards the
prosperity of their people — an instruction the Imperium issues nowhere else in this codex — and
Ultramar maintains a secret police to suppress rebellion. Both are true, and the realm's low
civil unrest is presumably a product of both. This is the most benevolent government in the
setting, and it is still a military government with an intelligence service.

The current leadership, as of M42: Master of Ultramar, Roboute Guilliman; Lord Macragge and
Chapter Master, Marneus Calgar; Regent of Ultramar and Tetrarch of Konor, Severus
Agemman.[^lex-ultramar] Calgar is [[VIII.T2.Ch6]].

## The Exemption {#exemption}

There is one provision that explains Ultramar's autonomy better than any of its titles, and it
is fiscal.

**The worlds of Ultramar do not pay the usual tithes**. They contribute directly to the upkeep
of the Ultramarines Chapter instead.[^lex-ultramar] Macragge's own tithe grade is recorded as
Adeptus Non.[^lex-macragge] Each world maintains its own Planetary Defence Force, the
well-disciplined and equipped **Ultramar Auxilia**, similarly exempt from the wider tithe system
and contributing to the Auxilia directly[^lex-ultramar] — though the Auxilia can number billions
of soldiers,[^lex-ultramar] and Ultramar's efficiency and prosperity are such that several
hundred regiments are still maintained to join the Imperial Guard when
needed.[^lex-ultramar] Space and orbital defence rests on the Ultramarines' own fleet and the
**Ultramar Defence Fleet**, and no fewer than six massive star fortresses — the largest being
*Galatan* — stand sentinel over shipping lanes and critical positions, each protected by void
shields and carrying enough firepower to destroy a moon.[^lex-ultramar]

The tithe is the Imperium's fundamental relationship with its subjects: [[IV.T1.Ch2]] is
essentially the machinery of collecting it. Five hundred worlds are outside that machinery. They
pay a Space Marine Chapter directly and keep their own army and navy. Whatever the charts say,
this is not administratively part of the same state as the worlds in [[IV.T2.Ch4]].

Recruitment works the same way, by pride rather than by levy. Though the need has diminished
since the days of the Legion, each world still provides the Chapter with new recruits, and it is
a point of pride for a family to have provided one — especially among older aristocratic
families, with many communities proudly displaying statues of the Ultramarines born
there.[^lex-ultramar] For a family that has produced a renowned hero or a Chapter Master, the
fame can last generations.[^lex-ultramar]

Compare the Black Ships of [[I.T2.Ch9]] and the tithe of [[IV.T2.Ch5]]. Elsewhere the Imperium
takes what it needs from populations that cannot refuse. In Ultramar the same extraction is
organised as an honour, and families compete for it. It is the only place in this codex where the
Imperium's demands and its subjects' wishes are recorded as pointing the same way.

Since the Plague Wars the protection has been formalised. Guilliman ordered that **ten Chapters
be permanently stationed within Ultramar**, so the reformed Five Hundred Worlds would be under
the permanent protection of about ten thousand Astartes at all times.[^lex-ultramar] These
**Shield Chapters of Ultramar** — among them the Scythes of the Emperor, the Novamarines, the
Doom Eagles, the Silver Skulls and the Praetors of Ultramar — are all descended from the
Ultramarines and operate from worlds inside the realm.[^lex-ultramar] The buildup was also
intended to enable operations such as the Vengeance Campaigns against the Scourge Stars and
other overrun regions.[^lex-ultramar]

One percent of the Imperium's Space Marines, permanently garrisoned in one sub-sector. Against
the roughly five hundred Chapters unaccounted for after the Noctis Aeterna recorded in
[[II.T1.Ch2]], the concentration is striking: as the Imperium lost track of half its Astartes,
Ultramar acquired a standing garrison of ten Chapters.

## Four Invasions {#invasions}

Ultramar's reputation is for stability, and its history is a list of invasions. Reading them in
order shows what the stability is actually made of.

**The Heresy.** Ultramar and the Ultramarines were left largely unscathed by the Horus
Heresy,[^lex-ultramar] but the qualifier hides a great deal. It was invaded by the Word Bearers,
producing the Battle of Calth, and **about one fifth of the Five Hundred Worlds were destroyed by
the Word Bearers and World Eaters during the Shadow Crusade**.[^lex-ultramar] In the aftermath
the Word Bearers erected the Ruinstorm, severing Ultramar from the rest of the Imperium, and
Guilliman — fearing the Imperium itself was lost — sought to make Ultramar the heart of a
contingency empire, **Imperium Secundus**.[^lex-ultramar] The Heresy is [[III.T3.Ch16]].

A hundred worlds destroyed counts as "largely unscathed" only by comparison with the Legions
that were annihilated. And note the precedent Imperium Secundus sets: when the Imperium went
dark, the response was a second Imperium centred on Ultramar. Ten thousand years later, when the
Rift cut the galaxy in half, the response was a second Imperium centred on Baal — the Regency of
[[II.T1.Ch2]]. The institution has done this before.

**The Tyranids.** In 745.M41 Ultramar was invaded by Hive Fleet Behemoth, the first Tyranid Hive
Fleet the Imperium encountered.[^lex-macragge] In the final battle Marneus Calgar took the fight
to the Hive Ship in space, leaving the Ultramarines First Company to garrison Macragge's polar
fortresses. The strategy worked and Behemoth was repulsed — but the Ultramarines suffered horrific
casualties and **the First Company was completely wiped out**.[^lex-macragge] Behemoth's mark on
the realm included the complete destruction of the world of Prandium.[^lex-ultramar] Hive Fleet
Behemoth is [[VII.T2.Ch6]]; the Tyranids, [[VI.T4.Ch14]].

**Chaos, twice.** The Iron Warriors Warsmith Honsou assembled an enormous warband to invade
Ultramar in revenge against Captain Uriel Ventris; the Chaos forces were beaten back, but the
War for Ultramar saw the greatest loss of life since Behemoth.[^lex-ultramar] Then, after the
Thirteenth Black Crusade, the Black Legion invaded under Abaddon personally, hoping to prevent
an Imperial–Aeldari expedition from resurrecting Guilliman. As the Fortress of Hera was breached,
Guilliman was reborn and led the counterattack that decimated the invaders — then left for Terra
on the Terran Crusade.[^lex-macragge][^lex-ultramar]

**Nurgle.** The forces of Nurgle then began to overwhelm Ultramar in the **Plague Wars** and the
Invasion of Konor. Led by Mortarion, the traitors initially made headway despite determined
resistance; with the return of Guilliman and his Primaris reinforcements the invasion was fought
to a standstill, and with supply lines cut and the Scourge Stars imperilled by the War in the
Rift, the Nurglites retreated.[^lex-ultramar] Macragge itself was never hit by a concentrated
invasion, but suffered probing attacks by Plague Drones and cultists, and mutinies by the
Ultramar Auxilia erupted in the Illyrium region.[^lex-macragge]

The mutinies are the detail that matters. Ultramar's own defence force rebelled during the
Plague Wars — on the capital world, in the realm with unusually rare civil unrest. Prosperity and
discipline hold up to a point, and the point is a Nurgle invasion. Mortarion is
[[VIII.T3.Ch9]]; the Plague Wars belong to [[VII.T4.Ch12]].

What followed is the state Ultramar is in now. Guilliman declared that the original borders of
the Five Hundred Worlds be restored, and **many resistant planets had to be forcibly
reconquered**.[^lex-ultramar] There was a period of rebuilding, decontamination and fortification,
and Guilliman greatly increased the realm's military strength against future
invasions.[^lex-ultramar] But many worlds were lost, occupied or independent following the
formation of the Great Rift and the Plague Wars, and Guilliman has declared the **Ultramarian
Reclamation** to reclaim them.[^lex-ultramar]

That first clause should not slide past. After the Heresy Guilliman concluded that Space Marines
should no longer directly rule over humanity and removed most of Ultramar's surviving worlds
from Ultramarine control, reducing the realm to a fraction of its size — roughly a dozen
worlds.[^lex-ultramar] Ten thousand years later the same man reversed his own decision and
retook them, in some cases by force, from populations that had governed themselves in the
interval and did not wish to return.

Ultramar is the best-run human territory in the galaxy, and its current borders were restored
against the wishes of some of the people inside them. That is the Imperium's central problem
appearing in its single most favourable instance: even here, even under the Primarch who
distrusted Astartes rule on principle, the answer to a subject world's refusal was reconquest.
