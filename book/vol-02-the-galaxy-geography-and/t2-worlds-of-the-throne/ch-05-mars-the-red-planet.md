# Mars, the Red Planet

## The First Colony {#first-colony}

Mars — Sacred Mars, or simply the Red Planet — is the fourth planet of the Sol system, and the
domain, headquarters and principal Forge World of the Adeptus Mechanicus.[^lex-mars] Its
classification is **Forge World**, its production grade **I-Maximus**, and its tithe grade
Aptus Non.[^lex-mars] It is the linchpin of the Mechanicus and the greatest repository of
technical knowledge in the Imperium.[^lex-mars]

Its history begins earlier than the Imperium's and, unusually for this setting, with a date.
In the early **22nd Century** Mars became the first planet to be terraformed by humans. Made
habitable, it was settled by industrial cartels and soon developed into the first Hive
World and a centre of industry and scientific advancement. Through the Dark Age of
Technology, the empires of Mars and Earth peacefully coexisted.[^lex-mars]

Three firsts in one paragraph: the first terraformed world, the first hive world, humanity's
first colony. Set against [[II.T2.Ch4]] the relationship is clear enough — Terra and Mars are
the two halves of the original human polity, and every hive world in the Imperium descends
from a template laid down on the neighbouring planet before the Imperium existed. What Terra
is to Imperial religion, Mars is to Imperial industry: not a subsidiary but the elder partner.

The peaceful coexistence is worth flagging too. The Imperium's two central worlds spent the
Dark Age of Technology as separate empires that got along. That arrangement is the exception
in ten thousand subsequent years of Imperial history, and it belongs to [[III.T2.Ch6]].

Some physical consequences of the low gravity persist. Martians are generally tall compared to
the baseline human average taken across many other Forge Worlds, with delicate bones lengthened
over millennia — traits that extend to the vat-born humans created there.[^lex-mars] Humanity's
first colony has, quietly, stopped producing quite the same species.

## How the Machine God Was Born {#omnissiah}

The most important thing Mars did for the Imperium it did while cut off from it, and the
sequence deserves to be given in order because it is the origin of a galactic religion.

During the Age of Strife, Mars endured long centuries of isolation as complete anarchy ripped
through Terra.[^lex-mars] Cut off by chronic Warp storms, the planet's atmospheric shields gave
way, exposing it to deadly radiation that devastated it.[^lex-mars] High radiation and a
build-up of pollutants drove the survivors underground. The world was plagued by mutants, rogue
psykers, abominable intelligences and petty warlords.[^lex-mars]

Then the crucial step. Fallout shelters represented the only hope of survival on the Red Planet.
**Technology, now responsible for one's very existence, soon became revered, and the worship of
the Omnissiah began in earnest.**[^lex-mars] Any humans remaining outside the shelters devolved
into cannibal mutants or were wiped out by the insane automata and artificial intelligences
stalking the surface.[^lex-mars]

That is a complete and sufficient account of how the Cult Mechanicus came to exist, and it does
not require mysticism. A population trapped underground in machines that kept it alive began
worshipping the machines. The theology of [[IV.T6.Ch19]] is the formalisation of a survival
reflex from one specific catastrophe on one specific planet — and the enemies outside the
shelters were the thinking machines of [[III.T2.Ch8]], which is why the Cult's central
prohibition is against artificial intelligence. The Mechanicus does not forbid the Abominable
Intelligence on doctrinal grounds. It forbids it because the last time it existed on Mars it ate
everyone who was not in a bunker.

What followed shows the recovery was self-directed. Long before the Terran Unification Wars the
survivors had developed their own culture, guided by the Cult Mechanicus, and after battling
many horrors — including the Cy-Carnivora — they reunited the planet and founded the
Mechanicum. In time Mars began sending expeditions to Earth to reclaim lost technology *there*,
and sent fleets beyond the Sol System to found new Forge Worlds.[^lex-mars]

Note the direction of travel. Mars recovered first, and salvaged Terra. When the Emperor
eventually united Terra he established a union with Mars — the Treaty of Olympus — bringing the
Red Planet back into the fold,[^lex-mars] and during the Great Crusade the Titan Legions of
Mars marched alongside the newly created Space Marines.[^lex-mars] The word in the sources is
*treaty*, not conquest. The Mechanicus joined the Imperium as a negotiating party, and the
peculiar semi-independence that gives it in [[IV.T6.Ch19]] dates from that document.

## The Fabricator-General's Domain {#fabricator-general}

Martian society is described as highly stratified, and the strata are worth setting out because
they are unlike anything else in the Imperium.

At the lowest level are unaugmented human citizens who labour en masse at simple tasks. Most
citizens aspire to gain status by joining the **Skitarii** — in the process receiving their
first bionics. Above these menials and soldiers is the ruling order, the **Tech-Priests**. The
planet is ruled directly by the **Fabricator-General** of the Mechanicum,[^lex-mars] and its
ruling body — the ruling body of the entire Cult Mechanicus by extension — is the **Synod of
Mars**, also called the Great Synod, which he leads.[^lex-mars]

The ladder is the point. Social advancement on Mars is measured in how much of your body has
been replaced. An unaugmented human is at the bottom; the first promotion comes with the first
bionics; the ruling class is the most heavily rebuilt. Every other Imperial society sorts people
by birth, or by piety, or by psychic aptitude. Mars sorts them by how far along they are in
ceasing to be flesh. [[IV.T6.Ch20]] holds the Tech-Priests and Skitarii themselves.

The military and industrial weight concentrated on the planet is easy to understate. Mars is
home to the mightiest fleet in the Imperium, **Battlefleet Solar**, and above the equator vast
space docks float in geostationary orbit.[^lex-mars] It serves as the Segmentum Fortress of
Segmentum Solar and is the homeworld of several Titan Legions, including the Legio
Ignatum.[^lex-mars] Every day trillions of tons of cargo are ferried down to the surface or up
to the gigantic orbiting manufactorums by void-lifts.[^lex-mars]

That first item resolves the oddity noted in [[II.T1.Ch1]]. Segmentum Solar's fortress is Mars
rather than Terra, and this is why: the fleet, the docks, the Titan Legions and the manufacturing
capacity are all on the Red Planet. Terra holds the Throne and the beacon; Mars holds the
shipyards. The Imperium's spiritual capital and its military-industrial capital are two
different planets in the same system, ruled by two different institutions, and the arrangement
has never been reconciled — only treatied. The Titan Legions are [[IV.T6.Ch21]].

Its geography records that industry as a directory of temples. **Olympus Mons** — also the
largest mountain in the entire Sol System — is the Temple of the Fabricator-General, and holds
the **Temple of All Knowledge**.[^lex-mars] Elsewhere: the **Olympica Fossae** Titan
construction yards; **Magma City**; the **Temple of the Frictionless Piston**; the **High Altar
of Technology**; the **Librarium Omnissiah**; the **Grand Temple of the Omnissiah**; and the
**Vaults of Moravec**.[^lex-mars]

A mountain that is a temple that is also an office. On Mars the distinction between a factory,
a shrine and a seat of government does not exist, which is the clearest expression of the Cult
Mechanicus there is: to build is to worship, so the workshop *is* the cathedral.

## A Planet of Rust {#rust}

Millennia of industry have reduced the surface of Mars to toxic, barren desert.[^lex-mars] The
planet has millions of factorums and hives covering its surface like a burning spider's web of
light,[^lex-mars] its hives reaching miles into the Martian sky with foundations delving deep
towards the core, while orbital factories circle the equatorial belt, their ceaseless industry
forming a glowing halo around the planet.[^lex-mars] Mars is home to untold billions of
servitors alongside the Tech-Priests.[^lex-mars]

One detail here is the best single image in this Volume. In the distant past the planet's red
hue was almost entirely due to its desert. By M41, **the red comes primarily from the rust
coating the vast metallic structures on its surface**.[^lex-mars]

The Red Planet is still red, and the colour means something completely different. Mars was named
for iron oxide in sand; it is now the same colour because of iron oxide on machinery. Ten
thousand years of industry have replaced the natural cause of a planet's appearance with an
artificial one that happens to look identical from orbit. Nothing about the view has changed and
nothing about the planet is the same — which is as good a description of the Imperium's
relationship to its own past as this codex will find. Compare Terra in [[II.T2.Ch4]], where the
oceans were boiled off and replaced with artificial ones.

The population figure recorded is **20,000,000,000** — twenty billion.[^lex-mars] Against
Terra's disputed hundreds of billions or quadrillions, twenty billion is a small number for a
world of millions of factorums, and it is worth noting what fills the gap: servitors in untold
billions,[^lex-mars] which the census does not count as people.

## The Schism and What Is Buried {#schism}

Two things sit under Mars's history that the Imperium has never resolved. One is documented; the
other is rumour that the sources record as rumour.

The documented one is that **Mars fell**. During the Horus Heresy it became a key battleground
as the loyalist Mechanicus and Dark Mechanicum split, and in the resulting **Schism of Mars**,
which devastated the planet, **the Dark Mechanicum emerged victorious**.[^lex-mars] After the
traitors took the world, the Imperial Fists blockaded it for several years, only for it to be
"liberated" by Horus's traitor forces during the final stage of the Solar War.[^lex-mars] Mars
was reclaimed by the Imperium only after Horus was defeated at the Battle of
Terra.[^lex-mars] The Heresy is [[III.T3.Ch16]]; the Dark Mechanicum, [[V.T3.Ch11]].

The Imperium's principal source of technical knowledge and heavy industry spent the Heresy in
enemy hands and was recovered because the war was won elsewhere. Ascraeus Mons, fortress of the
Legio Tempestus, was destroyed in the Schism.[^lex-mars] And the relationship never fully
healed: fifteen hundred years later, during the War of the Beast in M32, relations between the
Adeptus Terra and the Mechanicum degenerated over the scheming of the then Fabricator-General,
Kubik, and a crisis erupted over custody of a single Tech-Priest — ending in the Fists Exemplar
exchanging fire with Skitarii before Kubik backed down.[^lex-mars] Mars was a battlefield again
in the North-South Mars Conflict of M34.[^lex-mars]

Imperial troops and Martian troops shooting at each other over a custody dispute, fifteen
centuries after the Heresy, is the Treaty of Olympus showing its age. Mars is not a province.

The undocumented one is the more famous. **Some rumours tell that the C'tan known as the Void
Dragon resides deep under Mars's surface.**[^lex-mars] Another rumour holds that the Primarch
Ferrus Manus survived the Isstvan V massacre and now resides on Mars, though this is strictly
rejected by the Iron Hands.[^lex-mars]

The codex records both as the sources do — as rumour, attributed and unconfirmed. But the first
is worth sitting with, because of what the previous Sections established. Mars is the birthplace
of the Machine God's worship, and the story that will not go away is that something is buried
under it: one of the star gods of [[III.T1.Ch2]]'s prehistory, sleeping beneath the planet whose
whole culture is built on revering technology it does not understand. Whether or not it is true,
the Mechanicus's own theology gives it nowhere to be filed.

There is a footnote that gives the rumour an odd resonance. In M41 Mars came under attack by a
bizarre suicidal Necron raid: five Shroud Class Light Cruisers somehow penetrated the world's
formidable defences, and defence ships pursued them to a mining complex in the northern
region known as the **Noctis Labyrinthus** before destroying them at great cost. One ship
actually landed on the soil of Mars before being vaporised. **What the Necrons hoped to gain by
this remains a mystery.**[^lex-mars] The threat was neutralised and the landing site
quarantined.[^lex-mars]

The Necrons are the servants and jailers of the C'tan. Five of their ships committed suicide to
put one hull on the surface of Mars, and the Imperium quarantined the spot and recorded that it
does not know why. The sources draw no connection between that raid and the Void Dragon, and
neither does this codex. It notes only that both entries appear on the same page, and that the
Imperium's answer to the second is a cordon and a question mark.
