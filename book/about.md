# About This Codex

## What it is

A reference work covering the setting of Warhammer 40,000, written out-of-universe and
analytically. It explains the setting to a reader rather than speaking from inside it,
which means it can discuss editions, retcons and contradictions openly — something an
in-universe voice cannot do.

Where this codex describes Imperial doctrine, it is describing what the Imperium
*believes and enforces*, which is frequently not what is true. Where those diverge, it
says so.

## How it is sourced

Sources are ranked, and the ranking is visible in the
[Bibliography](appendix/bibliography.md).

| Tier | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Official Games Workshop web pages | Authoritative, used where reachable |
| 2 | Lexicanum | The working ceiling for this project |
| 3 | Fandom's Warhammer 40k wiki | Leads only — corroborated at tier 2 before use |
| — | Reddit, forums, fan essays | Never used as a source |

**Printed codices, rulebooks and Black Library novels were not consulted.** They are the
authoritative sources for this setting and they exist only in print. Everything here
therefore rests on what web-reachable sources *claim* about them.

This produces a distinction that is maintained rigorously throughout:

- *What we read* — a specific wiki article, on a specific date, recorded with its URL.
- *What that article cites* — a printed book and page number, reproduced for the
  reader's benefit and **explicitly labelled unverified**, because nobody here opened
  that book.

Anything surviving only at tier 3 or below is omitted or marked uncertain. A claim that
cannot be sourced at all is cut, not softened.

## Why contradictions are recorded instead of resolved

Games Workshop's own position is that everything is canon but not everything is true.
In-universe sources are propaganda, Imperial records are censored, and contradictory
accounts of the same event have been published on purpose for forty years. Successive
editions have also revised each other repeatedly.

So "accurate" cannot mean "the one true answer" — for much of this setting there isn't
one. It means: every load-bearing fact traced to a retrieved source, current material as
the baseline, earlier-edition differences noted where they matter, and **conflicts stated
in the text rather than laundered out of it**. The
[Disputed Facts Register](appendix/disputed-facts.md) collects them.

Some entries in that register record something subtler: a term in wide use that **no
source actually defines**. Those are marked as undefined and left that way. An
authoritative-sounding definition invented to fill the gap would be worse than the gap.

## How it is organised

The book crosses three organising axes — place, chronology, and institution — so the
same subject naturally surfaces in several volumes. Without discipline that becomes
contradiction, so one rule governs the whole work:

> **Every subject is treated in full in exactly one chapter.** Everywhere else gets a
> framing sentence and a cross-reference, never a retelling.

Cadia is the standard illustration. One chapter owns the world, another owns its
destruction, a third owns the siege. Each points at the other two instead of repeating
them. The [Master Index](appendix/master-index.md) records where every subject lives.

Definitions work the same way: each term is defined once, in the
[Consolidated Glossary](appendix/glossary.md), and referenced from the prose. One
correction propagates everywhere.

This is enforced mechanically, not by memory. Two chapters claiming the same subject
fails the build, as does any cross-reference that does not resolve or any citation
pointing at a source that was never recorded.
