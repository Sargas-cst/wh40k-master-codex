# Magnus's Folly & the Terra Breach

## The Librarian Crisis {#crisis}

Before the breach there was a trial, and the trial is where the Imperium made the decision that
cost it the war.

**The young Imperium was wary of the development of psychic potential among the population; the
means to reasonably successfully monitor and control such mutant developments did not yet exist**,
and there was deep distrust and bias among the various factions that saw, or did not see, a
benefit to allowing those with psychic powers **to remain unchained — or even
alive**.[^lex-council-of-nikaea]

Many psykers were nonetheless put to use, **the most important being the Navigators who made safe
space travel possible** — and **even these psykers were barely tolerated by
some**.[^lex-council-of-nikaea] That is [[III.T2.Ch6]]'s engineered caste still on probation
centuries later.

**Any grouping of psykers was bound to draw comment, and so it proved with the Thousand
Sons.**[^lex-council-of-nikaea] The Legion Magnus inherited **were rife with psychic mutations,
being based on Magnus's genes**; he took them in as his own and began training them in the ways
of the psyker, and when individuals within the Imperium began to voice their fears openly,
**Magnus silenced them**.[^lex-magnus]

The problem was structural, not disciplinary. [[III.T3.Ch12]] records that each Legion was grown
from its Primarch's genetic template. Magnus was a psyker, so his Legion was a Legion of psykers —
not by policy but by construction. The Imperium could no more separate the Thousand Sons from
sorcery than it could separate the Space Wolves from Fenris.

Magnus's own history with the Warp was already compromised. **Despite the warnings of his Father to
beware the horrors of the Empyrean, Magnus lost his right eye in a failed bargain** with what he
would later discover to be **Choronzon**.[^lex-magnus] Throughout the Crusade **he came into
contact with long-isolated cultures where psychics had been allowed to flourish** and — **although
warned by the Emperor to shun such matters** — **began to gather arcane lore from across the
galaxy**.[^lex-magnus]

Those isolated cultures are [[III.T2.Ch9]]'s pocket empires: the worlds that survived Old Night,
some of which had done so by tolerating psykers rather than suppressing them. Magnus was
collecting the knowledge of the minority that had made the opposite choice from the Imperium's,
and the Imperium's founding evidence said that choice was fatal.

## The Edict of Nikaea {#edict}

The **Council of Nikaea** was **a great convocation called by the Emperor in 001.M31 to discuss
the use of psychic powers in the Imperium, particularly within the Legiones
Astartes**.[^lex-council-of-nikaea] It came in the waning years of the Great Crusade and was
**one of the last notable gatherings of historically important personages before the outbreak of
the Horus Heresy**.[^lex-council-of-nikaea]

The name it was given by those on trial tells you what kind of proceeding it was. **While termed a
'council', the methodology of the debate and the persecution they felt they suffered because of it
led the Thousand Sons to refer to the event as the Trial of the Thousand Sons, or the Trial of
Magnus the Red.**[^lex-council-of-nikaea]

The defence offered was measured, and it came from a junior officer of another Legion. **A junior
White Scars marine called Targutai Yesugei spoke up, saying that there was nothing inherently evil
about a Librarian** — an Astartes psyker being **akin to an athlete**, a gifted
individual.[^lex-council-of-nikaea]

The verdict went the other way, and it went further than the charge. **The Emperor issued what
would become known as the Edict of Nikaea, decreeing that the Legiones Astartes, beyond the use of
Navigators and Astropaths, would no longer employ psykers. They were to disband their Librarius
departments, the Librarians re-deployed to the battle companies, and the Primarchs themselves were
to refrain from using any psychic powers they possessed.**[^lex-council-of-nikaea]

And on the actual accusation: **this of course applied to Magnus, whose use of sorcery was declared
not proven**.[^lex-council-of-nikaea]

Read those two sentences together, because the sequence is the finding. Magnus was acquitted, and
the Emperor then abolished the entire discipline across all eighteen Legions. The trial cleared
the defendant and banned the practice. To Ahzek Ahriman, the speech **seemed to be directly
intended for Magnus himself**[^lex-council-of-nikaea] — a verdict of not proven delivered as a
warning to the man it exonerated.

## What Actually Happened at Nikaea {#hidden}

The sources record something the participants did not know, and it changes what the Council was.

Present among the concealed Space Wolves contingent was **Kasper Hawser**, a Skjald of the Third
Company. Leman Russ had told the Chief Custodian that the Space Wolves suspected Hawser **was a
Hidden One of the Thousand Sons: a deep-cover intelligence agent, unaware of his own status as a
scouting conduit** — which made his presence a serious security risk even given the psi-blocking
of the Sisters of Silence and the inert materials the Nikaea complex was built
from.[^lex-council-of-nikaea] Russ allowed it precisely in order to test the
possibility.[^lex-council-of-nikaea]

During the deliberations, an apparent attempt was made to remove the compromised spy: **Amon,
Master of the Hidden Ones and Equerry to Magnus the Red, made a sorcerous attack** on the Custodian
supervising Hawser.[^lex-council-of-nikaea]

Except that it was not Amon. **Tragically for everyone concerned, it would later be revealed that
the 'Amon' that attacked was not in fact Amon of the Thousand Sons, but an aspect of the Primordial
Annihilator masquerading as the Master of the Hidden Ones** — part of a convoluted scheme **to
remove the threat presented by both the Space Wolves and the Thousand Sons to the grand chaotic
plan that would result in the Horus Heresy**. **The real Amon, and all the Thousand Sons present
including Magnus, had no idea of what had transpired in their
names.**[^lex-council-of-nikaea]

So the evidence that condemned the Thousand Sons at the trial that acquitted them was manufactured
by Chaos, in their name, without their knowledge — and its purpose was to set the Space Wolves
against them. That is the same method as Davin in [[III.T3.Ch14]]: not a temptation but an
orchestration, arranging events so that loyal parties destroy each other. Erebus impersonated a
dead friend to turn Horus; something impersonated Magnus's equerry to turn Russ.

## The Breach {#breach}

Magnus went home. **Magnus returned to Prospero, intent on pursuing his sorcerous experiments in
secrecy.**[^lex-magnus]

And then he saw the war coming. **He peered into the Warp, and saw a vision of Horus's revolt and
the roles all the Legions would play, except his own.**[^lex-magnus] He entered his brother's mind
while it was under the Chaos ritual on Davin and **attempted to persuade his brother away from
heresy and remain loyal to their father**.[^lex-magnus]

That is [[III.T3.Ch14]] from Magnus's side. The one Primarch who tried to stop the Heresy before it
began was the one the Imperium had just put on trial, using a power the Imperium had just
forbidden him to use.

When persuasion failed he tried to raise the alarm, and the obstacle was infrastructural.
**Magnus decided to warn the Emperor via an astral projection spell, because a Warp storm was
blocking astropathic messages to Terra.**[^lex-magnus]

The Imperium's communications had failed, exactly as they fail in [[II.T1.Ch2]] ten thousand years
later. Magnus's sorcery was not indulgence at this point; it was the only channel left.

**As his astral form blazed through the Warp, he came across a Webway corridor that led to Terra
and decided to take a shortcut through it. Magnus did not know that this particular corridor was
built by the Emperor, and was part of his top secret Webway
project.**[^lex-magnus]

There is the cost of the secrecy recorded in [[III.T3.Ch13]]. The Emperor withdrew to Terra and
told no one what he was building. Magnus, hurrying to warn him, could not recognise the thing he
was about to break.

**Magnus tried in vain to breach the wall of the corridor, but then an anonymous voice from within
the Warp offered Magnus the extra power he needed, and the overconfident Magnus accepted without
question.**[^lex-magnus]

Accepted without question — from an anonymous voice in the Warp, having already lost an eye to
exactly such a bargain with Choronzon. The sources are not kind to him here and neither is this
codex: the man carrying the warning took power from the enemy he was warning about.

**Magnus tore a breach in the wall and followed the corridor to Terra, bursting through the portal
beneath the Golden Throne. The breach allowed daemons to invade the Webway and ruin the Emperor's
Webway Project. Enraged, the Emperor did not listen to Magnus's warning and banished him from his
presence.** The breach became known as **Magnus's Folly**.[^lex-magnus]

Every clause there is a disaster. The Webway Project of [[I.T3.Ch12]] — the Emperor's route to a
human Webway, and the thing he had abandoned the Crusade for — was ruined permanently. The warning
about Horus was not delivered, because the messenger's arrival was itself the catastrophe. And the
Emperor's response to the only intelligence anyone brought him about the coming civil war was to
throw the source out.

## The Burning of Prospero {#prospero}

**The Emperor dispatched Leman Russ and his Space Wolves to arrest Magnus and bring him to
Terra.**[^lex-magnus]

Arrest. That was the order.

**Leman Russ was subsequently contacted by Horus, who convinced him of the need to exterminate the
Thousand Sons as opposed to simply returning Magnus to Terra.**[^lex-magnus]

The Warmaster — already turned, as [[III.T3.Ch14]] establishes — intercepted the Emperor's arrest
warrant and converted it into an extermination order. And [[III.T3.Ch12]] records what the Space
Wolves were built for: an insurance policy for use against other Space Marine Legions, possibly
already used against the two erased Legions. Horus did not have to invent an instrument. He
pointed an existing one.

**In the ensuing Burning of Prospero, Magnus watched in horror as the great libraries and arcane
archives he had worked so hard to create were burned to the ground.** Engaging Russ in combat, he
and his forces **disappeared into the Warp thanks to a ritual he had helped prepare with
Ahriman** just as Russ was about to strike the final blow.[^lex-magnus] **There, Magnus found what
he had wanted: unrestricted psychic powers and an opportunity for vengeance.** Giving himself to
Chaos, he **took all that he had from Prospero, from the Imperium, into the Warp
forever**.[^lex-magnus]

His physical form was destroyed in the battle against Russ, and he now exists **as a being of
corporeal Warp energy in the Eye of Terror alongside his Legion** — though **in truth he had been
shattered into many shards which acted independently of the
other**.[^lex-magnus]

The Imperium lost the Thousand Sons, their libraries, and their Primarch in a punitive expedition
launched on a forged escalation of a legitimate arrest order — and it lost the archive of arcane
knowledge that might have told it something about the enemy it was about to fight. Compare
[[III.T2.Ch7]]: the Imperium burned the largest collection of Warp-lore in human hands, and has
spent ten thousand years since unable to explain the Warp to itself.

## The Cost of the Edict {#cost}

The Edict of Nikaea outlived the trial and did its damage in the war.

**The Emperor's Decree would prove to be a severe handicap to the Loyalist Legions that fought for
him during the Horus Heresy.** Not all Legions abided by it — **most notably the Alpha Legion**,
which continued to use Librarians in secret.[^lex-council-of-nikaea]

The traitors, of course, were not observing it at all. That is the asymmetry: a prohibition on
psychic warfare binds only the obedient.

The clearest illustration comes from Calth. When the Word Bearers summoned hordes of daemons,
**Guilliman ruefully considered that Nikaea had deprived the Ultramarines of their best weapon
against denizens of the Warp, the Librarius** — and resolved that if they survived, **their first
order of business would be to see the Decree abolished**. He also noted **that the timing was so
convenient that it almost seemed like someone had planned
it**.[^lex-council-of-nikaea]

Guilliman's suspicion is well founded and the previous Sections support it. A Council whose
decisive incident was staged by Chaos in the Thousand Sons' name, producing an edict that disarmed
the loyalists against daemons two years before daemons were unleashed on them, is either the
luckiest coincidence in the setting or it is not a coincidence.

Enforcement was inconsistent even during the war. Malcador had Nathaniel Garro recruit **Tylos
Rubio, a former Codicier of the Ultramarines**, and later **gave Rubio free rein to use his
psychic abilities** without indicating whether this was a step towards abolishing the Decree —
while the Custodian **Khorarinn warned Garro that he considered the Decree fully in effect, and
threatened to execute Rubio instantly** if he used his abilities in his
presence.[^lex-council-of-nikaea]

Two arms of the Emperor's own household, in the same war, operating opposite policies on the same
man.

And afterwards it simply lapsed. **Despite the Emperor's decision, in the aftermath of the Horus
Heresy enforcement of Nikaea ceased. By M41, the choice of whether to have Librarians was left to
individual Chapters** — the Salamanders considering Nikaea outdated, the Marines Malevolent
retaining a healthy distrust of psykers and Warp-seers.[^lex-council-of-nikaea]

So the Edict destroyed a Legion, disarmed the loyalists at Calth, cost the Imperium its greatest
Warp archive, and was then quietly dropped. The Librarians of [[IV.T4.Ch13]] exist today because
nobody bothered to keep enforcing a law nobody had repealed — which is as good a description of
Imperial governance as this codex will offer.
