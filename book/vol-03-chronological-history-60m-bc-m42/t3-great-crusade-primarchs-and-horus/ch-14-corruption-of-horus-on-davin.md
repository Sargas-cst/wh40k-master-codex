# Corruption of Horus on Davin

## An Unremarkable World {#davin}

**Davin was once blessed with a progressive and civilised culture until the isolation of the Age
of Strife brought anarchy.** The civilisation quickly descended into barbarism and became a
collection of savage feral tribes.[^lex-davin]

That is [[III.T2.Ch9]]'s regression happening to one specific planet, and it is worth noting that
Davin's ruin has the same cause as Terra's. Its deserts still contain ruins **that indicate it
could once have supported a more advanced culture**.[^lex-davin]

What filled the vacuum is the detail that matters. Davin came to be ruled by the **Warrior
Lodges** — semi-religious societies focused on the veneration of various beasts: **the Lodge of
the Bear, the Lodge of the Hawk, the Lodge of the Serpent, the Lodge of the Crow, and the Lodge
of the Hound** were a few among many. These Lodges **were led by dark priest-like monks that
performed the arcane rites of the sects**.[^lex-davin]

And then the sources make an observation about the Imperium's attention that reads, in hindsight,
as an indictment. **Such a culture could only be tolerated under the nascent Imperium's rule due
to being just one of many hundreds of thousands of unremarkable worlds spread across the
galaxy.** Had Horus not been treated there, **they likely would have remained largely unknown and
ignored**.[^lex-davin]

Hundreds of thousands of worlds, and this one had a functioning cult of beast-worshipping monks
performing arcane rites, and the Imperium's records show that nobody thought it worth a second
look. The Great Crusade of [[III.T3.Ch13]] exterminated species and bombarded human worlds
judged too mutated; it walked past this one because it was boring. Davin is the argument against
the Imperium's whole model of compliance — an empire that big cannot inspect what it takes.

## Compliance, and What the Legion Took Away {#compliance}

**Davin's known history only covers the brief time of (supposed) planetary compliance to
Imperial rule.**[^lex-davin] The parenthesis is the sources' own.

The campaign was trivial. **The indigenous warriors briefly attempted to resist Imperial takeover,
but quickly surrendered, completely outclassed in military terms.** The warrior tribespeople were
**allowed to remain mostly intact** after surrendering, because **they had impressed Horus with
their battlefield courage and their willingness to learn and adapt to a new way of
life**.[^lex-davin]

Horus spared them because he admired them. That is a decision made on the character of a
Primarch — engineered for charisma, as [[III.T3.Ch12]] records, and evidently susceptible to it
in others. A different Primarch would have destroyed the Lodges as the religion they plainly
were.

Then the sentence on which the Imperium turns. **The actual military campaign on Davin was short,
and the Luna Wolves left soon after the surrender of the Davinite warriors, taking with them
their concept of the warrior-lodge.**[^lex-davin]

The Legion adopted the institution. Not the beliefs — the *form*: a fraternal society inside a
military body, with initiations and secrecy and its own hierarchy. Horus's own Legion imported an
organisational structure from a feral world's beast cult, and thought it a harmless piece of
soldiers' culture.

What that made possible is documented from the other side. **Unknown to Horus, his brother Lorgar,
along with his entire Legion of Word Bearers, had secretly fallen to Chaos and conspired to make
Horus the Ruinous Powers' champion against the Emperor. For many years before acting openly, the
Word Bearers usurped the loyalist hold on their Legion by establishing Warrior
Lodges.**[^lex-horus-lupercal]

The Lodges were the delivery mechanism. Davin did not corrupt Horus by itself; it supplied a
social technology that a compromised Legion then used to hollow out others from within. And the
Imperium handed Davin's re-education to that very Legion: **the re-education and shepherding of
the people into the light of the Imperial Truth was left to a detachment of the Word Bearers led
by Kor Phaeron**, while governorship went to **Commander Eugen
Temba**.[^lex-davin]

Lorgar's Legion — already turned, as [[III.T3.Ch13]] records, decades before the Crusade's
end — was given custody of a planet of Chaos cultists and told to teach them the Imperial Truth.

## The Wound {#wound}

**Sixty years later, Horus and the 63rd Expedition returned to Davin at the behest of Chaplain
Erebus of the Word Bearers**, who reported that Commander Temba and his forces had gone renegade
and holed up on Davin's Moon.[^lex-davin]

Erebus is the same First Chaplain who, in [[III.T3.Ch13]], destroyed the Imperium's negotiations
with the Interex and left with the Anathame. He is now the source of the intelligence that brings
Horus back to Davin.

The moon had changed. Originally charted as similar to Davin in atmosphere and climate, **the
warping effects of the Chaos powers, particularly Nurgle's, grossly altered the ecosystem**: most
forestation vanished, and much of the moorland turned to **noxious, fog-bound swamp which
eventually would conceal the mass graves of the Imperial garrison**.[^lex-davin]

**Horus led an assault force to the moon personally, where his forces were confronted by the
reanimated remains of Temba's garrison. Horus slew the grossly altered Temba himself, but was
gravely wounded doing so.**[^lex-davin] The weapon was **a blade of Nurgle wielded by the
Chaos-corrupted Eugen Temba**.[^lex-horus-lupercal]

That blade is the Anathame from the Hall of Devices — the Kinebrach sword Erebus stole from the
Interex while sabotaging the Imperium's talks with the one human civilisation that understood
Chaos. The theft in [[III.T3.Ch13]] and the wound here are the same operation, sixty years apart.
Horus was struck down with a weapon taken from people who had tried to warn him.

## The Lodge {#lodge}

**Horus fell unconscious, and under the advice of Word Bearers First Chaplain Erebus the natives
of the world helped to heal him. However, unknown to the desperate Mournival, the healers and
Erebus himself had all long since become corrupted by Chaos and were orchestrating the entire
series of events.**[^lex-horus-lupercal]

*Orchestrating the entire series of events.* The renegade governor, the report that brought Horus
back, the wound, and the only available cure were one operation. Horus was not ambushed; he was
routed through a sequence.

**Having his dying body moved to the Serpent Lodge, Horus was soon subjected to an ancient Chaos
ritual by the Davin priests, while Erebus entered his mind disguised as the deceased Hastur
Sejanus.**[^lex-horus-lupercal] He was **'healed' in the Temple of the Serpent Lodge on Davin
proper**, and **this act, overseen by Erebus, corrupted Horus and set the stage for the Horus
Heresy**.[^lex-davin]

The impersonation is precisely chosen. Sejanus was a dead member of the Mournival, Horus's own
inner circle — a voice Horus trusted and could not check, because its owner was dead.

## What He Was Told, and What Was True {#visions}

The content of the temptation is on record, and it is the most important passage in this Title.

**This image of Sejanus showed Horus horrifying visions of the future, where the Emperor ruled as
a god and had discarded the Primarchs once they had outlived their
usefulness.**[^lex-horus-lupercal]

Now read that against [[III.T3.Ch12]]. Malcador the Sigilite claimed that he and the Emperor
deliberately set the Primarchs against one another to purge the unmanageable ones — *just as they
had the earlier Thunder Warriors* — leaving humanity rather than superhumans in charge. And the
Emperor told Arkhan Land that he had no emotional attachment to the Primarchs because **they were
tools and weapons**.

Erebus's vision and Malcador's account describe the same policy. The codex asserts neither: the
vision is a daemon's inducement and Malcador declined to confirm his own story. But the
convergence is on the record, and it means the case against Horus's judgement is narrower than
the Imperium's version allows. He was not shown a lie about being disposable. He was shown
something that at least two other sources independently suggest was the plan.

What sits beside it in the same speech *is* plainly false. **Erebus also told Horus that the Gods
of Chaos were peaceful beings with little interest in the Materium, and that it was the Emperor
who was intent** on war.[^lex-horus-lupercal] Everything in [[III.T1.Ch4]] and [[V.T1.Ch1]]
contradicts that. Horus was given one claim that may have been true and one that certainly was
not, from a source he had reason to distrust.

Because he did distrust it. This is the detail that makes the Heresy a choice rather than a
deception: **despite realising early on that this Sejanus was but an impostor, Horus nonetheless
accepted these revelations for unknown reasons.**[^lex-horus-lupercal]

He knew. The sources say plainly that he identified the impersonation and accepted the message
anyway, and they decline to explain why. That is where the Imperium's account of a noble son
tricked by sorcery breaks down, and it is also where any account of simple ambition breaks down.
Whatever happened in the Serpent Lodge, Horus was not fooled.

The grievances the visions landed on were real and pre-existing. **Horus's bitterness towards the
Emperor** was **already growing from his father's isolation and Secret Project on Terra, as well
as the formation of the Council of Terra — essentially subordinating the Primarchs to mortal
humans**.[^lex-horus-lupercal]

Both of those are [[III.T3.Ch13]]'s documented facts, not the daemon's inventions. The Emperor
went home to work on something he would not describe, and installed clerks over his sons. Erebus
did not need to manufacture a grievance; he needed only to supply an interpretation.

## Afterwards {#afterwards}

**After his experience on Davin, Horus agreed to align with the powers of Chaos in order to
overthrow the Emperor**, whom he had become convinced was **a corrupt dictator bent on achieving
godhood and forsaking his sons in favour of mortal
rulers**.[^lex-horus-lupercal] He then began converting the Legions under his direct command:
**Angron of the World Eaters, Fulgrim of the Emperor's Children, and Mortarion of the Death
Guard**, and eventually recruited **Konrad Curze** of the Night Lords and **Alpharius
Omegon**.[^lex-horus-lupercal]

Every name on that list appears in [[III.T3.Ch13]]'s catalogue of feuds, or in
[[III.T3.Ch12]]'s account of the rediscovery. Angron was the Primarch abducted against his will;
Curze had already tried to kill Dorn. Horus did not create the traitors. He collected them.

One attempt was made to stop it. **The only attempt to stop the conversion of Horus came from his
brother Magnus the Red, who entered Horus's mind but was unable to interfere with the powerful
rituals of the Davin cultists or convince Horus to remain loyal to the
Emperor.**[^lex-horus-lupercal]

And the retaliation for that attempt is the subject of the next Chapter. **Horus dealt with Magnus
afterward by manipulating Leman Russ into launching an all-out purge of
Prospero.**[^lex-horus-lupercal]

That is the causal order, and it inverts the story the Imperium tells. [[III.T3.Ch15]] is usually
read as Magnus being punished for sorcery. On this account, the Burning of Prospero was Horus's
revenge on the one brother who tried to save him — executed using the Legion that
[[III.T3.Ch12]] records was built as an insurance policy against other Legions.

Davin itself did not survive its role. **Following the corruption of Horus, the corrupted populace
of Davin spread throughout the Imperium to spread the influence of Chaos.** During the formation
of the Ruinstorm the world became **the nexus of the Warp Storm and was enclosed by a massive
sphere made of the bones of trillions of organisms**.[^lex-davin] Years later Sanguinius,
Guilliman and Lion El'Jonson — with the captive Konrad Curze — **stood at the same temple where
Horus had been corrupted**, discovered Davin to be the realm of the daemon **Madail**, fought a
massive battle there, and **destroyed Davin with Cyclonic
Torpedoes**.[^lex-davin]

Its infobox now records its class as **Dead World**, former Feral World, and its population as
**None**.[^lex-davin] Three loyal Primarchs stood in the room where the Heresy began and then
sterilised the planet — which changed nothing, because what left Davin had already gone
everywhere.
