# Engineering the 20 Primarchs

## What They Were Built For {#design}

The **Primarchs** were **the twenty artificially engineered, gene-wrought creations of the
Emperor of Mankind** that served as both the gene-sires and supreme commanders of their
respective Space Marine Legions.[^lex-primarch]

The specification is exact. **The Emperor used his own DNA in their creation**, and they were
designed to be far superior to the average human: **immensely larger, stronger, hardier, faster
and more intelligent**. They were also **incredibly charismatic, as their main role was to be
generals and leaders of the Imperial military** — and **each had their own purpose and
specialty**, allowing them to serve specific functions the Emperor's forces would need in
reconquering the galaxy.[^lex-primarch]

Note that charisma is listed as an engineered property alongside strength and speed. The
Primarchs were not merely built to fight; they were built to be followed. And the specialties
were assigned in advance, which connects directly to [[III.T3.Ch11]]: the twenty Thunder
Warrior Legions already existed, with matched specialisms, before the Primarchs were made. The
sons were designed to fill slots.

The Emperor's own account of what they were to him is on record, and it is the coldest thing in
this Volume. While Arkhan Land helped him investigate Angron's Butcher's Nails, **the Emperor
explained that he had no emotional attachment to the Primarchs because they were tools and
weapons**.[^lex-primarch] Asked why he then allowed them to call him father, he answered with a
story:

> There was once a writer, a penner of children's stories who told the tale of a wooden puppet
> that wished to be reborn as a human child. And this puppet, this automaton of painted, carved
> wood that sought to be a thing of flesh and blood and bone — do you know what it called its
> maker?[^lex-primarch]

He is not denying that they are his sons. He is observing that a manufactured thing naturally
calls its manufacturer father, and that this tells you nothing about the manufacturer's
feelings. Every tragedy in [[III.T3.Ch14]] through [[III.T3.Ch16]] proceeds from twenty beings
engineered for charisma and loyalty discovering, at various speeds, what they were to the man
who made them.

## The Laboratory {#laboratory}

The Primarchs were created **in a secret underground laboratory on Terra, under the tightest
security**.[^lex-primarch] Perturabo would later estimate his own birth at **792.M30**, perhaps
giving a date for the project.[^lex-primarch]

What went into them is where the sources become genuinely disquieting, and every account of it
comes from a hostile or compromised witness — which the codex flags rather than smooths over.

According to **Ingethel the Ascended**, the Primarchs were created **with the aid of forbidden
sciences and arcane lore derived from the Warp**.[^lex-primarch] **Eldrad Ulthran** also states
that the Emperor **utilised dark powers** to construct them.[^lex-primarch] It is speculated
that they were **imbued with energies and knowledge the Emperor gained in a Warp Portal on
Molech**.[^lex-primarch]

Weigh the witnesses. Ingethel is a daemon; Eldrad is an Aeldari farseer with his own reasons;
the Molech connection is marked *speculated*. Not one of these is a neutral source, and the
codex will not upgrade them into fact. What is nonetheless striking is that two independent
hostile parties, of different species, describe the same method — and that the Emperor's project
to abolish religion because it opened doors to Chaos was, on this account, built using the Warp.

One further ingredient is recorded without hedging. **The genetic material of the powerful
Perpetual Erda was used in the creation of the Primarchs, and she viewed them as her
"sons"**.[^lex-primarch]

So the Primarchs have two parents, and the second one matters for the next Section. They are
not solely the Emperor's; a second immortal contributed to them and regarded them as her
children.

## The Scattering {#scattering}

Then the event that made the Imperium's entire history possible and doomed it in the same
moment.

**The Gods of Chaos somehow managed to spirit them away just prior to their maturation with a
great Warp vortex** — and it is speculated that they also **managed to tamper with the infant
Primarchs**.[^lex-primarch]

!!! disputed "Recorded conflict — primarch-scattering-cause"

    Three accounts of who scattered the Primarchs, and they are not compatible.

    **The Chaos Gods did it**, spiriting them away with a Warp
    vortex.[^lex-primarch]

    **It was a causal loop.** Argel Tal and his men, sent back in time inside the Eye of
    Terror to the Emperor's laboratory, were convinced by Ingethel the Ascended to destroy the
    Gellar Fields protecting the infants from the touch of the Chaos Gods — and almost
    immediately after those generators were disabled, the Primarchs were sucked into a Warp
    vortex.[^lex-primarch]

    **Erda did it.** The Perpetual whose genetic material went into them **created the Warp
    vortex that scattered them, out of anger over the fate the Emperor planned for her
    "children"** — though according to Erebus she was **unwittingly manipulated by the Chaos
    Gods** into the act.[^lex-primarch]

    The codex records all three. Note that they are not simply rival claims: the causal-loop
    account has Chaos acting through humans travelling backwards in time, and Erebus's version
    has Chaos acting through Erda without her knowledge. Both can be read as mechanisms for the
    first account rather than alternatives to it — which may be the point, or may be three
    incompatible stories the setting has never reconciled.

What the scattering did, mechanically, was create the Space Marines. **Scattered across the
galaxy, beyond the Emperor's reach**, the Primarchs each landed on a different world, vastly
separated from their father. **The Emperor could sense that the infant Primarchs were alive, but
was unable to reach them. Therefore he used their previously collected DNA samples to create the
Space Marines** — the genetic material of each Primarch founding a single Legion, twenty in
total.[^lex-primarch]

The Adeptus Astartes exist because the Primarchs were stolen. Had the theft not happened, the
Emperor would have had twenty generals and no Legions built from them; [[IV.T4.Ch12]]'s
gene-seed is a contingency measure. It is the single most consequential accident in the setting,
and by two of the three accounts above it was not an accident at all.

And the worlds they landed on made them who they became. **The new homeworlds of the infant
Primarchs, and their subsequent experiences on those worlds, would influence and mould them to
a large extent.** They quickly grew to adulthood and often rose to power, ruling, leading and
helping the inhabitants.[^lex-primarch]

That is why [[II.T3.Ch7]] matters to this Volume. Guilliman was shaped by Macragge's
meritocracy, Russ by Fenris's shifting ice, Vulkan by Nocturne's Time of Trial. The Emperor
engineered twenty beings to specification and then lost control of the variable that determined
their characters.

## The Rediscovery {#rediscovery}

The Great Crusade of [[III.T3.Ch13]] was, among other things, a search. How the found reacted is
recorded with useful variation.

**Most Primarchs somehow recognised the Emperor on sight, and immediately pledged their
allegiance on the spot.** A few — **Leman Russ, Ferrus Manus and Vulkan** — only followed him
after being bested in contests. **Angron refused, and was abducted from his homeworld, something
he never really accepted.** The last to be found was Alpharius.[^lex-primarch]

Three categories: recognition, defeat, and abduction. The Imperium's official register of
loyalty begins with a man taken by force who never accepted it, and [[III.T3.Ch16]] is partly
the bill for that. Angron's abduction is not a later grievance invented to justify treason; it
is in the founding record.

!!! disputed "Recorded conflict — first-primarch-found"

    **Traditional accounts state that the first Primarch found was Horus**, who became the
    Emperor's favourite. **However a dubious account given by Alpharius states he was the
    first**, and landed on Terra itself following the
    scattering.[^lex-primarch]

    The article's own word is *dubious*, and the codex keeps it. Alpharius is the Primarch
    whose defining characteristic is deception, and a claim by him to have been first — and to
    have landed on Terra, of all worlds — is exactly the claim he would make whether or not it
    were true. It is recorded because unverifiable claims by unreliable narrators are part of
    this setting's texture, not despite it.

## Two Erased, and One Account of Why {#erased}

Two of the twenty are missing from the record, and the manner of their absence is more
informative than any surviving detail about them.

**Little is known about the two unknown Primarchs, the IInd and the
XIth.**[^lex-primarch] Rogal Dorn, describing the plinths on which their statues stand, says
those plinths **should be vacant** — and asks: **'no one ever spoke of those two absent
brothers. Their separate tragedies had seemed like aberrations. Had they, in fact, been warnings
that no one had heeded?'**[^lex-primarch]

Lorgar makes several references, **saying that the Emperor ordered that they be
forgotten**.[^lex-primarch] Shown the Emperor's gene-labs by the daemon Ingethel, five Word
Bearers captains speak of the two lost Legions, **mentioning how they were erased from Imperial
records and how Roboute Guilliman's Ultramarines Legion mysteriously swelled in numbers each
time one of the two Legions was "erased"**.[^lex-primarch] Their remarks suggest **the fate of
the lost Legions was relatively common knowledge though kept hushed
up**.[^lex-primarch]

Read the Ultramarines detail against [[II.T2.Ch6]]. That Chapter records the Ultramarines
becoming the largest of all the Legions, and Ultramar the only Space Marine realm permitted
dominion over other worlds. This passage offers an explanation the Imperium has never given: the
Legion grew each time another was deleted. The implication the sources draw is that Chapters
descended from the Ultramarines may in fact carry the gene-seed of the lost
Legions.[^lex-primarch]

And there is a suggestion about the instrument. In *Prospero Burns* it is suggested that **Leman
Russ and his Space Wolves had been used by the Emperor to cull or pacify the two Legions**, and
that **the Emperor created the Wolves for just such an event** — using their barbaric, untamed
nature as **a sort of insurance policy for use against other Space Marine
Legions**.[^lex-primarch] The unleashing of the Wolves on the Thousand Sons in
[[III.T3.Ch15]] is offered as further demonstration.[^lex-primarch]

Fenris in [[II.T3.Ch7]] produced a Chapter that recruits the dying and lives above unclimbable
cliffs. This account makes that deliberate: the Emperor built one Legion specifically to destroy
the others, and used it at least once before Prospero.

Which brings the Chapter to the account that reframes everything, and which the sources
themselves refuse to certify. According to **Malcador the Sigilite**, he and the Emperor
**deliberately agitated the Primarchs and sought to turn them against one another** — the
resulting war intended to purge **certain unmanageable Primarchs and their Legions, just as they
had the earlier Thunder Warriors**. This would leave **humanity, not bio-engineered superhumans,
as the rulers of the galaxy**. The Ruinous Powers intervened before the plan could be fully
formulated, leading to disaster in the Horus Heresy.[^lex-primarch]

The article's caveat is explicit and the codex reproduces it: **this account may be either a
partial or total lie, as Malcador himself seems to keep its validity
ambiguous**.[^lex-primarch]

If it is true, then the Horus Heresy was a botched version of a purge the Emperor intended, and
the Primarchs were always disposable in exactly the way [[III.T3.Ch11]] records the Thunder
Warriors were. It would make the pattern a policy: build superhuman instruments, use them, and
have them destroy each other when their usefulness ends. Malcador's own phrase — *just as they
had the earlier Thunder Warriors* — asserts precisely that continuity, which is why
[[d:thunder-warriors-fate|the register keeps that conflict open]] rather than settling it.

The codex asserts none of it. What it records is that the second-most-powerful man in the
Imperium said this, that he declined to confirm whether he meant it, and that his account is
consistent with a documented earlier precedent. On the evidence available, that is as far as
anyone can honestly go.
