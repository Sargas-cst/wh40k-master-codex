# Aftermath & the Golden Throne

## What the Throne Was Before {#origins}

The **Golden Throne** is **an ancient and arcane device located in the Imperial Palace on Terra**,
which **currently houses and sustains the remains of the Emperor of
Mankind**.[^lex-golden-throne]

Its origin is not Imperial. **The Golden Throne was thought to be an ancient relic dating back to
at least the Dark Age of Technology or possibly even before, which the Emperor discovered in sunken
ruins within Asia's deserts** — although **even the Emperor Himself was unsure whether the idea was
His, or to what extent the devices he discovered had served as
inspiration**.[^lex-golden-throne]

That uncertainty is worth registering. The Emperor did not know whether his greatest work was his
own invention or something he had copied from a machine he dug up. Set that beside
[[III.T2.Ch7]]: the being who founded the Imperium was, on this point, in exactly the position of
the Adeptus Mechanicus — working from a recovered template he could not fully account for. A second
similar device, **Dark Glass**, likely used to test the technologies by whoever made it, was found
during the Great Crusade and he **set about restoring it as well**.[^lex-golden-throne]

Its original purpose was not life support. **The Emperor went to work to restore the device for his
own ends, providing human access to the Eldar Webway.**[^lex-golden-throne]

The Throne was a door. That is the fact the Imperium has forgotten and the fact that explains
everything about the Emperor's condition. [[I.T3.Ch12]] holds the Webway Project; this was its
terminal.

Its installation is described physically, and the scale is instructive. Once installed under the
Imperial Palace it **took the form of a bulky machine-like chair suspended over gigantic mechanised
doors made of gold metal** — doors **large enough for a Warhound Scout Titan to walk through
unbowed** — the chair linked to the portal **by huge bundles of cables, wires and conduits**. It
stood at one end of **a vast hall big enough to house up to six fully equipped Space Marine
Companies**: the Emperor's main laboratory, at the centre of his underground complex, the **Imperial
Dungeon**, still littered with other huge machines and storage crates and worked by **hundreds of
red-robed technicians**.[^lex-golden-throne]

A chair above a gate wide enough for a Titan, in a laboratory the size of a parade ground. The
Golden Throne of [[II.T2.Ch4]] is usually pictured as a reliquary; it was built as the control
station of a portal.

!!! disputed "Recorded conflict — golden-throne-construction-date"

    In *Realm of Chaos: The Lost and the Damned*, **the Golden Throne was constructed *after* the
    Emperor's mortal wounding by Horus.** Though he was **"dead by any ordinary understanding"**
    there were enough living cells in his body **for his spirit to animate it** — allowing him to
    speak while his body was still fresh, and **supervise the construction of the Golden
    Throne**.[^lex-golden-throne]

    Later sources, such as *Master of Mankind*, **have the Golden Throne completed and in use well
    before the Horus Heresy**.[^lex-golden-throne]

    The article records this among its own conflicting sources. The stakes are large: on the
    older account the Throne is a purpose-built life-support device the Emperor designed for
    himself while dying, and on the later one it is a Webway portal control he was forced onto.
    This codex follows the later account, because the Webway Project and the War Within the
    Webway depend on the Throne already existing — but the earlier version is recorded, not
    discarded.

## The Door That Cannot Be Left {#the-door}

The Heresy's consequence for the Throne begins with the breach in [[III.T3.Ch15|Magnus's Folly]].

**When Magnus used his sorcerous powers to warn the Emperor of Horus's treachery, he inadvertently
created huge holes in the Emperor's psychic shield. Daemons poured into the human-built portion of
the Webway and slaughtered thousands of Adeptus Mechanicus workers there.** The Legio Custodes and
Sisters of Silence were left **fighting a desperate battle to prevent the daemons from reaching the
portal and breaching Terra itself**, and eventually **the Imperial forces had to abandon the Webway
and retreat back into the Imperial Palace**.[^lex-golden-throne]

Then the sentence that defines the next ten thousand years. **The portal was closed, but only the
psychic power of the Emperor was enough to keep it that way — so he was forced to remain on the
Golden Throne, or find a suitable replacement.**[^lex-golden-throne]

He was on the Throne before Horus ever reached Terra, and he was on it because Magnus's warning had
opened a hole that only he could hold shut. The Emperor of [[III.T3.Ch13]] who withdrew from the
Crusade to work on a secret project became a man who could not leave the room, and the reason is
the intervention of the son trying to save him.

A contingency was installed. **Vulkan arrived in the throne room, where at the Emperor's guidance
he installed the Talisman of Seven Hammers**, which acts as a dead man's
switch.[^lex-golden-throne]

And when the Emperor did leave, briefly, someone had to sit in his place. **When the Emperor was
forced to battle Horus on board the *Vengeful Spirit*, his place on the throne was taken briefly by
Malcador the Sigillite. Malcador perished performing this
endeavour.**[^lex-golden-throne]

That is the price of the duel in [[III.T3.Ch16]], and it was paid before the duel began. The
Emperor could only go to fight his son because Malcador agreed to hold the door, and holding it
killed him. The Collected Visions account in [[III.T3.Ch16]] has Malcador's charred body still in
the chair when the Emperor is carried back.

**The mortally wounded Emperor was reinstated upon the Throne after his battle with Horus. At his
instruction, it was then enhanced and converted into the life-sustaining form it currently
bears.**[^lex-golden-throne] His physical form is kept alive **through advanced stasis fields and
psi-fusion reactors that have become beyond the understanding of the
Imperium**.[^lex-golden-throne]

Note "at his instruction" — which is the Collected Visions reading of
[[d:emperors-final-words|what he said at the Throne]], and which the other account cannot support,
since in it he says one ambiguous syllable and nothing else.

## The Tether {#tether}

Malcador's own account of what the Throne does is the most important passage in this Chapter, and it
inverts the Imperium's understanding entirely.

> Rogal and Constantin believe they are saving him. They think that the throne is his only chance.
> In fact, the reverse is also true. **He is the throne's only chance.** [...] Yes, the throne may
> stabilise him and suspend him, as it did me, but that's beside the point. **He is the only one who
> can stabilise it**, for I can no longer perform that task. And thus it will tether him here, to
> this seat, to this room, to this reality, on the brink of death and the verge of life, both
> wounded and whole, unborn and yet reborn, ended yet unending, now and forever. **It was never his
> intended plan. But it was a contingency.**[^lex-golden-throne]

Read that against the Imperial Cult of [[IV.T3.Ch8]], which holds the Emperor's interment to be a
divine sacrifice sustaining humanity by his will. Malcador — musing as a disembodied consciousness
moments before his death — describes something closer to mutual hostage-taking. The Throne keeps the
Emperor from dying; the Emperor keeps the Throne from failing; and the arrangement **tethers him
here** indefinitely.

And it was not the plan. **My King-of-Ages knew it might come to this, if the permutations of the
future aligned in this particular way** — he **had me believe he would be ready, if there was no
other choice**.[^lex-golden-throne] A contingency, foreseen as a possibility, entered because there
was no alternative left.

The Imperium's central religious fact is, on this account, a fallback position.

## Twenty Per Cent {#malfunction}

The Throne has been failing since the day it took him, and the figures are recorded.

**As a consequence of the Siege of Terra, the Throne's systems, alongside the infrastructure of the
Imperial Dungeon, were catastrophically damaged. All system processes were disrupted and the Throne
was reduced to 20% of its full operation.** The **Adnector Concillium** began remedial works, **but
the time frame of their conclusion was something the Adnector's members could not
estimate**.[^lex-golden-throne]

One fifth of function, from the first day, with no estimate for repair. Everything the Imperium has
done for ten thousand years — the Astronomican, the Emperor's psychic protection of the Warp routes,
the closed portal — has run on a machine at twenty per cent that nobody could schedule a fix for.

The decline since is documented in two steps. **By late M36, the Golden Throne began to require more
and more sacrifice of psykers to remain functional. By early M41, four times the amount of psykers
were required to maintain optimal power levels.**[^lex-golden-throne] And then: **in the last year
of M41, Tech-Priests discovered failures in the mechanisms of the Golden Throne that are far beyond
their ability to repair.**[^lex-golden-throne]

Quadrupled consumption, and then failures beyond repair. This is the true meaning of the psyker
tithe of [[I.T2.Ch9]] — the Black Ships do not merely feed the Astronomican; they feed a failing
machine whose appetite is growing. The thousand psykers a day is a maintenance bill.

## The Bargain {#bargain}

What the Imperium did about it is the most damning episode in this Volume, and it happened within
living memory of the present.

**Faced with this cataclysmic scenario, several high-ranking members of the Imperium decided to
act.** Shortly before the formation of the Great Rift, **Lord Inquisitor Adamara Rassilo attempted
to smuggle a Dark Eldar Haemonculus onto Terra**, in cooperation with **three members of the High
Lords of Terra**: the Fabricator-General **Oud Oudia Raskian**, the Speaker for the Chartist
Captains **Kania Dhanda**, and the Master of the Astronomican **Leops
Franck**.[^lex-golden-throne]

Three of the twelve High Lords of [[IV.T1.Ch1]] and a Lord Inquisitor conspired to bring a Drukhari
torturer to the Throneworld. The Haemonculus **betrayed the conspirators, disappearing beneath the
Imperial Palace and conducting grotesque killings and experimentations** until cornered and slain by
Lord Inquisitor Erasmus Crowl and Custodes under Navradaran, and **Rassilo's cabal was shattered
with it**.[^lex-golden-throne]

But the scheme succeeded anyway. **Despite this setback Leops Franck was able to send out a
delegation of Tech-Priests and Xanthite Inquisitors** for the final
arrangement.[^lex-golden-throne] And the terms are on record.

**The bargain saw the Mechanicum give up billions of humans as slaves, and access to the Golden
Throne by the Dark Eldar, in exchange for a technical solution to its
malfunction.**[^lex-golden-throne]

Billions of human beings sold to Commorragh, and the Emperor's own life-support opened to the
Drukhari, by the Imperium's own Fabricator-General and Master of the Astronomican. Compare
[[II.T3.Ch10]]: the Imperium's holiest object is now maintained under contract by the species whose
city runs on the stolen suns of [[II.T3.Ch10]] — and whose own crisis is the reason they agreed.

Because the trade was mutual. **In truth the bargain was beneficial for both parties, for it allowed
the Dark Eldar to begin development of their own Golden Throne** — the **Black Throne** — **in an
attempt to stop the destructive daemonic incursions that had erupted into Commorragh**, which have
only intensified since the Great Rift.[^lex-golden-throne]

That is the Dysjunction of [[II.T3.Ch10]] seen from the other side. Vect's city is being eaten by daemons
and is amputating districts into sub-dimensions to survive; the Black Throne is the Drukhari
attempt at the Emperor's solution, learned from the Imperium's own machine. Two dying powers
exchanged the only thing each had.

And the outcome is a deferral, not a fix. **According to Inquisitor Erasmus Crowl, the Mechanicum
has only delayed the destruction of the Golden Throne by a few centuries through their dealings with
the Dark Eldar.**[^lex-golden-throne]

A few centuries. That is the horizon on which the entire Imperium sits at the point where
[[III.T4.Ch18]] picks up the story — the Throne bought time with slaves, the tether Malcador
described holds for a while longer, and the being on it has been, in the sources' own words, on the
brink of death and the verge of life, **now and forever**, since the day his son died asking to be
killed.
