# Rise of the Emperor & Unification

## The Timing {#timing}

By M29, Terra had been engulfed in the chaos and anarchy of the Age of Strife for several
thousand years — **a violent cauldron divided between city-states, techno-barbarian warlords
and various other polities**.[^lex-unification-wars] That is the world [[III.T2.Ch10]]
described.

Then a single sentence gives the whole strategic logic of the Imperium's founding. **As
Slaanesh was close to its birth at the epicentre of the Eldar Empire, the Warp storms plaguing
humanity's homeworld becalmed themselves. Sensing the time was right, a being known as the
Emperor finally began his campaign to reclaim Earth.**[^lex-unification-wars]

Read the word *finally*. He was already there, and he had been waiting. [[III.T2.Ch9]]
established that the Age of Strife ended because the Aeldari Empire fell; this Chapter
establishes that the Emperor knew it was ending and had prepared accordingly. His campaign is
not a response to opportunity but a plan held in reserve until the weather changed.

That is also the answer to a question the Imperium never asks itself: why now, and not five
hundred years earlier, while billions starved on Terra? The sources give the answer without
comment. He moved when the storms lifted, not when the suffering peaked, because a unified
Terra was only useful if the galaxy was reachable. [[III.T2.Ch10]] records the same timing from
the other direction — the conquest was undertaken **in anticipation of the dispersal of the
Warp storms**, one hundred years before Slaanesh woke.

**Appearing openly for the first time**, the Emperor led his Thunder Warriors in a series of
campaigns against all other warlords and techno-barbarians of Old
Earth.[^lex-unification-wars]

*Appearing openly for the first time* is doing quiet work. It presupposes that he existed
beforehand and had not been open about it. The being who unified Terra had been on the planet,
unannounced, for an unstated period — long enough to have built secret laboratories and grown
an army in them.

## The Thunder Warriors {#thunder-warriors}

The **Thunder Warriors** — also the Thunder Legion, or *Legiones Cataegis* — were the
genetically engineered warriors of Terra, created by the Emperor to unite humanity's homeworld
beneath his rule in M30. **Wrought to be living weapons**, they were **physically stronger,
more savage and more potent in combat than the later Astartes, though not as
long-lived**.[^lex-thunder-warriors] They were created **in secret
laboratories**.[^lex-unification-wars]

Stronger than a Space Marine. That is the specification, stated plainly, and it reframes the
Adeptus Astartes of [[IV.T4.Ch12]] as a step *down* in raw capability. The engineering used on
the Thunder Warriors, **whilst creating superior fighters, was not as efficient** as what came
later — **nor was the technology as advanced, or the geneticists as entirely
willing**.[^lex-thunder-warriors]

"Nor the geneticists as entirely willing" is the phrase to keep. Humanity's first
super-soldiers were built by coerced scientists, in secret, on a starving planet, by a being
who had not yet told anyone what he was.

Their defects were severe and known. **The Thunder Warriors' genetic template was unstable and
they were prone to fits of bloodlust and instability. Short-lived and rife with both mental and
physical health issues, Thunder Warriors were known to suddenly drop dead or stop following
orders.**[^lex-thunder-warriors] Unlike the later Legions, **they lived as warlords and
relished battle** — and, notably, **their human emotions were not expunged from them, and they
were known for their dry humour**.[^lex-thunder-warriors]

They were organised into **twenty regiments of no more than a few hundred warriors each, named
by the Emperor himself**[^lex-thunder-warriors] — and into **twenty Legions, each led by a
Primarch**, though those Primarchs **were not physically different from the warriors they led**:
simply superlative warriors promoted to the position by the
Emperor.[^lex-thunder-warriors] The correspondence runs deeper still: the 4th Legion of Thunder
Warriors was known as the *Iron Lords* and specialised in siegecraft, **closely resembling the
subsequent Iron Warriors**.[^lex-thunder-warriors]

Twenty Legions, twenty Primarchs, matched specialisms. The structure of [[IV.T4.Ch13]] existed
before the Primarchs of [[III.T3.Ch12]] were found — which means the Emperor did not design his
Legions around his sons. He designed the Legions first, ran them once with promoted men, and
then fitted the Primarchs into slots already cut.

!!! disputed "Recorded conflict — thunder-warriors-fate"

    Imperial history records that the Thunder Warriors **all died in the final battle of the
    Unification Wars, the Battle of Mount Ararat**, and that the Legiones Astartes were
    created to replace them.[^lex-thunder-warriors]

    A surviving Thunder Warrior, **Arik Taranis**, gives a different account: the Emperor
    **did not replenish their numbers, or may in fact have massacred** the hyper-violent and
    short-lived Thunder Warriors to make way for his stable, mass-produced Astartes —
    **fabricating the tale of Mount Ararat as a cover-up**, or using the battle to expend the
    last of them. They were suitable for conquering Terra but did not fit his vision of
    warriors who would take the entire galaxy.[^lex-thunder-warriors]

    The purges are implied to have been carried out by the **Templi Veritas** of the Legio
    Custodes.[^lex-thunder-warriors]

    The codex records both. Note what makes this conflict unusual: it is not two texts
    disagreeing but an official history contradicted by a witness who should not exist. If
    Imperial history is right, Taranis cannot be testifying.

## The Conquest {#conquest}

The forces that took Terra were a coalition, and the composition matters because the Imperium
still runs on it.

The Emperor's forces included **not only the Thunder Warriors but also the Custodian Guard**,
formations that would become the early Imperial Army — **the Geno Five-Two Chiliad** and the
**Inferallti Hussars** — and eventually the proto-Astartes. **His forces utilised the advanced
weapons of the Terrawatt Clan after he secured an alliance with
them.**[^lex-unification-wars] One of his earliest major campaigns was against the **Maulland
Sen Confederacy**.[^lex-unification-wars]

Two things follow. The Custodian Guard of [[IV.T8.Ch27]] is older than the Space Marines — it
was there for the conquest of Terra — and the Imperial Army of [[IV.T5.Ch16]] descends from named
techno-barbarian regiments. And the alliance with the Terrawatt Clan, one of the warlord
polities listed in [[III.T2.Ch10]], establishes the method: the Emperor did not defeat all
comers. He absorbed one, took its weapons, and used them on the rest.

The result was decisive and its sequel was immediate. **After capturing Terra and Luna, and
winning the allegiance of the Mechanicum of Mars, the Emperor launched his Great
Crusade** — and over the next two hundred years spearheaded the Astartes Legions in reconquering
much of the galaxy and finding the lost Primarchs.[^lex-unification-wars]

That is the Treaty of Olympus in [[II.T2.Ch5]] appearing as one clause in a list of
conquests — Terra, Luna, and *the allegiance of Mars*. The distinction between the first two and
the third is the whole of the Mechanicus's later position: two worlds were captured and one was
won over.

## The Imperial Truth {#imperial-truth}

The doctrine the Emperor imposed is one of the setting's great ironies, and its content is worth
setting out exactly.

The **Imperial Truth** was the doctrine preached during the years of the Great
Crusade.[^lex-imperial-truth] Its content was **the removal of religion, faith and superstition**
from the human worlds Terra recontacted; in their place, **Iterators** offered **the cold
clarity of logic and science**.[^lex-imperial-truth] Its other key aspects were **the manifest
destiny of mankind to rule the stars, the exceptionalism of humanity, and the danger posed by
all xenos**.[^lex-imperial-truth]

Three of those four survive intact into the 41st Millennium. Manifest destiny, human
exceptionalism, and the danger of all xenos are the Imperium's operating assumptions in
[[IV.T1.Ch1]]. Only the first — the removal of religion — was reversed, and it was the one the
Emperor cared most about.

The enforcement was physical. **As part of his campaign to spread the Imperial Truth, the
Emperor's Thunder Warriors destroyed all churches on Terra**, believing it necessary that
humanity accept science and reason over superstition and
barbarism.[^lex-imperial-truth]

And the actual reason was not philosophical. **In truth, the Emperor sought to wipe out
superstition and religion as they were easy doors from which the powers of Chaos entered the
minds of men.**[^lex-imperial-truth]

That single sentence resolves what otherwise looks like ideology into engineering. The Imperial
Truth was not atheism for its own sake; it was a Warp-defence measure, applied to a species
that [[III.T2.Ch9]] had just shown to be catastrophically vulnerable through belief. Faith
attracts attention, as [[I.T1.Ch3]] describes; the Emperor's answer was to abolish faith.

Its failure is total and specific. **The Imperial Truth began to disintegrate due to the Horus
Heresy and encounters with the Dark Powers of the Warp.** Many either turned traitor as a result
or embraced the growing movement of the **Lectitio Divinitatus**, which proclaimed that the
Emperor was a god.[^lex-imperial-truth] With that theological shift, **the "Imperial Truth"
itself had its meaning twisted, becoming simply one of the many other ways to refer to the
burgeoning Imperial Cult**.[^lex-imperial-truth]

The phrase survived and now means its own opposite. A doctrine created to abolish religion is
today a synonym for the state church of [[IV.T3.Ch8]]. The Emperor set out to close the door
Chaos came through, and the institution built in his name flung it open by deifying him — which
is the Heresy's deepest consequence and [[III.T3.Ch17]]'s subject.

## What Unification Did Not Finish {#unfinished}

The Unification Wars are usually described as complete. They were not, and the exception
records something about the Emperor that the Imperium's later conduct does not.

Despite the Emperor having ended the Unification Wars, **there were still numerous feudal
states, religious adherents, remote nations, stubborn autocrats and royal houses that refused
to end their independence**. They posed no risk to his rule of Terra, but resisted every effort
to make them join the Imperium.[^lex-unification-wars]

And his response was patience. **The Emperor went to great lengths to resolve these conflicts
without using violence, if it was possible.** The Imperium's dealings with these independent
groups **continued well into the Great Crusade** — **but there was a limit to the Emperor's
patience. When it was reached, He would unleash His wrath on any group that still refused to
join.**[^lex-unification-wars]

The worked example gives the limit a number. The royal house **Yeselti of Boeotia** was
destroyed when it would not end its nation's independence — **even after the Emperor gave them
one hundred and fifty years to do so**.[^lex-unification-wars] Over a decade later, the Imperium
waged war against the **N Brigade** in Ossetia for the same
refusal.[^lex-unification-wars]

A hundred and fifty years of forbearance, and then annihilation. Both halves are real and the
codex reports both: this is not a regime that killed on contact, and it is not a regime that
took no for an answer. Boeotia appears in [[II.T2.Ch4]]'s list of Terran nations for exactly
this reason — the Imperium's own records remember it as a place that was patiently and then
completely destroyed.

That pattern is the Great Crusade in miniature, three centuries early, and it is
[[III.T3.Ch13]]'s subject. Compliance was genuinely offered. Refusal was genuinely fatal. The
Ringers of Saturn in [[III.T2.Ch10]] got the short version.
