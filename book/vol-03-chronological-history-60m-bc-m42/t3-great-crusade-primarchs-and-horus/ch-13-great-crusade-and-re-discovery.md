# Great Crusade & Re-discovery

## The Shape of the Undertaking {#shape}

The **Great Crusade** — roughly **798.M30 to 005.M31** — was a brief age of rebuilding and
reunification following the complete regression of mankind during the Age of Strife. **It was a
time when the Emperor still lived in the conventional sense and led his race in
person.**[^lex-great-crusade] It ran from the conquest of Earth's moon to the Battle of Isstvan
III and the beginning of the Horus Heresy.[^lex-great-crusade]

*Brief* is the sources' word, and it is accurate: two hundred years, against the five thousand
of Old Night and the ten thousand of the Imperium that followed. Everything the Imperium is
was decided inside that window.

The parenthetical about the Emperor is the more important framing. **He still lived in the
conventional sense** — which is the sources acknowledging, in the definition of the age, that
what sits on the Golden Throne afterwards does not. [[III.T3.Ch17]] holds that transition.

The instrument was not the Space Marines alone. The Legions were created from the gene-seed
samples that survived the scattering, and would play the major role **alongside the Imperial
Army, Titan Legions, Legio Cybernetica, Sisters of Silence, and Custodian
Guard**.[^lex-great-crusade]

Note the **Legio Cybernetica** in that list, and set it against [[III.T2.Ch8]]. The Imperium
banned artificial intelligence after the Cybernetic Revolt, and then conquered the galaxy with a
formation whose name announces what it is — alongside a Legion equipped with Battle-Automata
salvaged from the Men of Iron themselves. The prohibition was never absolute; it was a licensing
regime.

And the timing repeats the pattern of [[III.T3.Ch11]]. **The subsiding of the warp storms
coincided with the end of the Age of Strife**, and with warp travel feasible again the Emperor
could begin — but **even before the warp storms and the Age of Strife had ended, the Emperor had
begun to make plans**, including the creation of a far more advanced form of power armour for
the conditions of deep space and alien worlds.[^lex-great-crusade]

He was designing equipment for a galactic campaign while Terra was still cut off. That is the
third time in two Titles the sources record him preparing for a window before it opened.

## How It Was Fought {#method}

The method is described without euphemism, and the codex reproduces it in the sources' own
order because the order is the argument.

Throughout the following millennium the Emperor **gradually reclaimed his Primarchs, and slowly
united humanity under the Pax Imperialis. World after world fell to the Emperor's
forces.**[^lex-great-crusade] **Tyrants and false-rulers of thousands of worlds were overthrown
by Imperial forces, who quickly outlawed religion and dogma by decree of the Emperor
himself.**[^lex-great-crusade]

Then the xenos. **Orks, Eldar, Hrud, Slaugth, hideous Warp entities, and many other lesser alien
races were all defeated and driven back in a seemingly unstoppable tide of
conquest.**[^lex-great-crusade] Over the course of the Crusade **hundreds of alien races were
exterminated and their empires left in ruins**.[^lex-great-crusade]

And then the humans. **Worlds whose human population had become too corrupted or mutated were
simply exterminated from orbit.**[^lex-great-crusade]

Read those three paragraphs as one policy, because they are. The Crusade overthrew tyrants,
abolished religion, exterminated hundreds of species, and destroyed human worlds whose
populations had changed too much during the isolation the Imperium had not prevented. The
abhumans of [[III.T2.Ch9]] evolved during Old Night through no fault of their own; the Crusade's
standing order was to kill the ones that had gone too far. The Imperium's later hatred of the
mutant, treated at [[IV.T2.Ch7]], is not a decline from Crusade-era enlightenment. It is Crusade
policy, retained.

The scale is documented. The Crusade lasted around two hundred years, and **at its peak had over
4,300 Expeditionary Fleets involved in galactic conquest**.[^lex-great-crusade] It was supplied
through **Void Harbours** — combinations of space station, warehouse and dock sitting on major
warp routes, holding cargo offloaded from macro-haulers and passing it to waiting warships **so
that giga-volumes of munitions, humans, abhumans, Astartes and all they brought with them could
be supplied at a relentless rate**.[^lex-great-crusade]

Four thousand three hundred fleets, fed by depots on the warp routes. This is the point
[[II.T1.Ch1]] makes about Imperial geography, demonstrated at its origin: the Crusade did not
expand into space,
it expanded along routes, and the Void Harbours are where the subsector-as-cluster pattern comes
from.

Command was divided three ways at first — **one thrust commanded by Horus, another by Ferrus
Manus, and the last by the Emperor himself** — and as each Primarch was discovered he was given
control of his own Legion. **As more of the Empire was carved, the Emperor's forces were spread
more and more thinly.**[^lex-great-crusade]

## The Interex {#interex}

One encounter is worth a Section of its own, because it is the test case for what the Crusade
was actually for.

The **Interex** were **a highly advanced human civilisation** during the time of the Great
Crusade[^lex-interex] — humans originating from Terra who had built their own
civilisation.[^lex-interex] They were tall, with **one genetically engineered difference: large
bat-like ears** that helped them use a **unique language based on music and sounds**, though
they still retained an evolved human dialect of Terra and used it
rarely.[^lex-interex]

They had also solved, independently, the problem the Imperium existed to address. **At some point
they made contact with the Eldar, who told them about the dangers of Chaos and the threat that
the inhabitants of the Warp pose to humanity.**[^lex-interex] They were **devoted to fighting
Chaos** — which they called **'Kaos'** — and they regarded the Imperial 63rd Expedition
sceptically, **as they thought they might be tainted by it**.[^lex-interex]

Stop on that. A human civilisation that had survived Old Night, learned the truth about Chaos
from the Aeldari, organised itself around fighting it, and then met the Emperor's Crusade and
suspected the newcomers were compromised. They were right. Set it against [[III.T3.Ch11]]: the
Imperial Truth denied the existence of the very thing the Interex had correctly identified, and
did so as a Warp-defence measure. Two human societies had arrived at opposite strategies from the
same threat, and the Interex's was better informed.

They had also made peace with aliens. After a long war the **Kinebrach** were defeated and
**subsequently entered their society, becoming an inextricable part** of it; the Interex then
encountered, defeated and contained the aggressive **Megarachnids** on the planet the Imperium
called **Murder** — *Urisarach* to the Interex, meaning **Spiderland** — after they had infested
eight Interex systems.[^lex-interex]

An integrated xenos population, inside a human polity, at the exact moment the Crusade was
exterminating hundreds of species. Whatever the Interex were, they were not a problem the
Imperial Truth had vocabulary for.

How it ended is the point. The Interex **were in talks with Horus and his peers** when somebody
infiltrated the **Hall of Devices** — the Interex museum of artefacts and weapons — **sabotaged
the building to explode**, and stole **a valuable and dangerous Kinebrach sword known as an
Anathame**. Fighting broke out between Space Marines and Interex forces until Horus was
evacuated.[^lex-interex] The saboteur was **later discovered to be First Chaplain Erebus of the
Word Bearers**.[^lex-interex]

So the Imperium's negotiation with the best-informed human civilisation it ever met was destroyed
by a traitor inside its own delegation, who stole a weapon in the process. That weapon is the
Anathame, and what it was used for is [[III.T3.Ch14]]. Erebus appears again in
the account of Erda in [[III.T3.Ch12]]. The Crusade did not fail to absorb the Interex because
compliance was refused; it failed because someone wanted a war.

## Ullanor, and the Emperor's Withdrawal {#ullanor}

**The Great Crusade reached its apex with the great victory by Horus against a massive Waaagh! of
Orks in the Ullanor Crusade. After this victory the Emperor declared Horus Warmaster, or acting
Supreme Commander in his stead. Having seen most of humanity reunited the Emperor returned to
Terra, where he began his secret project.**[^lex-great-crusade]

Four sentences, and each is a hinge.

Ullanor is the planet [[II.T3.Ch8]] identifies as Armageddon — teleported into the Armageddon
System during the War of the Beast and renamed, its origin unknown to the Imperium at large. The
site of the Crusade's greatest triumph is the site of the 41st Millennium's longest war, and
nobody making war there now knows it.

The Warmastership is the delegation that destroys the Imperium. Horus is made acting Supreme
Commander in the Emperor's stead — the favourite of [[III.T3.Ch12]], promoted above nineteen
brothers.

And the withdrawal is the act that makes everything after it possible. **The Emperor returned to
Terra** to begin the Webway Project. [[I.T3.Ch12]] holds what that project was. What matters
here is what it meant to the Legions: the being they had followed in person for two centuries
left the front, delegated to one of their number, and did not explain why. The Crusade's
commander went home to work on something he would not describe.

## The Feuds {#feuds}

The Crusade's end is a political story, not a military one, and the sources set out the
mechanism precisely.

Towards the end came **a period of political consolidation**. The Legions were dispersed across
the galaxy, **many acting as garrison forces rather than campaigning armies** — the Iron Warriors
especially **struggling under the monotony of siege warfare and garrison
duties**.[^lex-great-crusade] A large bureaucracy overseen by **Malcador** and the **Council of
Terra** formed to manage the conquered territories, and **this bureaucracy often clashed with the
Astartes military establishment**. Tensions ran high **when it became apparent that the Emperor
intended to have Malcador's Terran Council rule over the nascent Imperium, and not the Legiones
Astartes**.[^lex-great-crusade]

That is the Heresy's political cause, stated plainly: the Legions conquered the galaxy and were
then told that clerks would run it. And it is exactly what Malcador's own account in
[[III.T3.Ch12]] claims was intended — humanity, not bio-engineered superhumans, as the rulers of
the galaxy. Whether or not that account is a lie, the policy it describes is documented here
independently.

Then the personal collapse, and note its stated cause. **Without the Emperor's guidance the
Primarchs, who had previously had friendly rivalries, were beginning to develop dangerous
feuds.**[^lex-great-crusade]

The record is specific. Some Primarchs **had reached the point of intolerance with one
another**; others, like **Magnus the Red**, **had simply become isolated in the confusion of the
Great Crusade**. **Lorgar** had **secretly turned against the Emperor decades before the
Crusade's end**, after the destruction of his "perfect city" of **Monarchia** by the
Ultramarines. **Leman Russ clashed with both Angron and Lion El'Jonson.** **Perturabo was shunned
by most due to the nature of his hand in the Crusade and was in turn envious of Rogal Dorn.**
And **Konrad Curze** had a disagreement with Dorn **that escalated into a brutal surprise attack
which left Dorn badly injured and unconscious**. **This mistrust and hatred would form the
linchpin of the Horus Heresy.**[^lex-great-crusade]

Every item on that list is a consequence of a decision made in [[III.T3.Ch12]]. Twenty beings
engineered for charisma and dominance, raised on twenty unrelated worlds, given armies and
sent out under a commander who then left. Lorgar's turn is the clearest: the Emperor had
Monarchia destroyed for the offence of worshipping him, which is the Imperial Truth enforced
against its own object, and it cost him a Legion decades before Horus fell.

Magnus's isolation is treated in [[III.T3.Ch15]], and Horus's corruption — begun with a stolen
Kinebrach sword — is the next Chapter's. What this Chapter establishes is that the Heresy did not
begin with a betrayal. It began with a management structure.
