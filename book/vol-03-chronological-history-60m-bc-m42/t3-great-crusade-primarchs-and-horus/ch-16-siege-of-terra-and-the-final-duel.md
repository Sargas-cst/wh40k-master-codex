# Siege of Terra & the Final Duel

## Who Was Not There {#absent}

The **Siege of Terra** was the penultimate battle of the Horus Heresy: waged on humanity's
homeworld, it **decided not only the fate of the Horus Heresy but also that of
humanity**.[^lex-siege-of-terra]

The most useful way into it is the order of battle, because the defence of Terra was determined by
who could not reach it.

Following the Drop Site Massacre, Horus **had crippled three loyalist Legions**. The Raven Guard
and Salamanders were nearly destroyed; the Iron Hands survived in larger numbers **but were left
leaderless**. The fragmented warbands of these **Shattered Legions** kept hitting traitor forces
during the advance on Terra **but were unable to contribute to the defence of the Throneworld
itself**.[^lex-siege-of-terra]

And the three great loyalist Legions that remained intact were elsewhere. **The Ultramarines,
Space Wolves, and Dark Angels were either too far away or bogged down in their own
campaigns.**[^lex-siege-of-terra] [[III.T3.Ch13]] records where: the Ultramarines mired at Calth
against Kor Phaeron, the Space Wolves finishing Prospero, the Dark Angels spread across the
southern and western borders.

**Only the Imperial Fists, Blood Angels, and White Scars Legions were in the Sol System in
force.**[^lex-siege-of-terra]

Three Legions out of nine loyal ones, to hold humanity's homeworld. And note what put two of the
absentees where they were: the Ultramarines were fighting the Word Bearers, whose treachery began
on Davin in [[III.T3.Ch14]]; the Space Wolves were on Prospero because Horus had converted an
arrest order into an extermination in [[III.T3.Ch15]]. The Heresy's opening moves were not
attacks on Terra. They were arrangements to ensure Terra would be under-defended when the attack
came.

## The Eternity Gate {#eternity-gate}

By the time the siege reached its crisis, **nothing could stop the tide of traitors pouring into
the Inner Palace** from the parts of the Ultimate Wall broken open by the Legio Mortis. Even
**Bhab Bastion, Dorn's command centre, became besieged with the Imperial Fists Primarch still
inside**, and the traitor armies **had lost much of their cohesion, turning into a barbaric horde
that swept forward murdering all in its path**.[^lex-siege-of-terra]

Two simultaneous assaults were underway, and the second was invisible. **The Emperor's psychic
shield was near breaking point thanks to a constant immaterial assault by the debased form of
Magnus in the Webway** — and **Vulkan was dispatched into the Webway to find and kill Magnus before
he could succeed**.[^lex-siege-of-terra]

That is the breach of [[III.T3.Ch15|Magnus's Folly]] being used as a door. Magnus tore the corridor open while
carrying a warning; the corridor is now the axis of a psychic attack on the Emperor from inside
his own project.

**Horus himself appeared over the traitor communications network for the first time in weeks to
give a single order: the capture of the Eternity Gate.**[^lex-siege-of-terra]

The defence assembled there is worth stating as a number. **Only some 70,000 defenders mustered on
the Delphic Battlement** — mainly Blood Angels and Imperial Army, with Imperial Fists, White
Scars, Legio Ignatum and Mechanicum loyalists also represented — against a horde of traitor
Astartes, Imperial Army, Beastmen, mutants, daemons and Titans.[^lex-siege-of-terra] Corswain
offered to send the forces defending the Astronomican, **but Sanguinius declined, due to the vital
nature of the Dark Angels Paladin's mission**.[^lex-siege-of-terra]

Seventy thousand, and the Great Angel turned down reinforcements to keep the Astronomican
covered — the beacon of [[IV.T9.Ch31]] that [[II.T1.Ch1]] shows the whole Imperium is built on. He
chose the institution over his own survival, and the choice was correct.

What the traitors did before the assault is recorded plainly and belongs in the record. **At dawn
the traitors erected the mutilated but still-living loyalist prisoners they had nailed to banners,
crosses, vehicles, or whatever else they could find, to try to break the spirit of the
defenders.** A Reaver Titan of the Legio Mordaxis advanced holding the tortured form of Blood
Angels Captain Idamas in its power claw, and **issued Horus's conditions: that any who fled the
coming battle would be granted a pardon by Horus, new Emperor of
mankind**.[^lex-siege-of-terra]

Sanguinius's answer was to release them. He **stated that he would stand and fight but all others
were free to go where they wished** — and **it is believed that no loyalists took the opportunity
to leave**, all present instead pledging themselves to him.[^lex-siege-of-terra]

The battle itself brought the daemon he had already beaten once. **Ka'Bandha**, bested by
Sanguinius during the Signus Campaign, **vowed to reap 500 Blood Angels in the presence of their
Primarch** and nearly succeeded before Sanguinius intervened; their airborne duel ended when
**Sanguinius broke the daemon's back with the hilt of his sword**, and Ka'Bandha fell to be
consumed by lesser daemons taking advantage of his weakness.[^lex-siege-of-terra]

Ka'Bandha's name appears again in [[II.T3.Ch7]] — his sigil arranged from Tyranid skulls on Baal
Primus, ten thousand years later, on the Blood Angels' own moon.

!!! disputed "Recorded conflict — sanguinius-armour-chink"

    In the Horus Heresy artbook, **Sanguinius was able to crack a small chink out of Horus's
    armour, which the Emperor later exploits**. **This is not present in the Black Library
    novelisation.**[^lex-siege-of-terra]

    The article records this among its own list of changes between accounts. It matters because
    the chink is the detail that makes Sanguinius's death meaningful in the older telling — his
    defeat is what enables his father's victory. Remove it and the Great Angel dies having
    bought time and nothing else.

## Lupercal's Court {#lupercals-court}

**When the Emperor, accompanied by Caecaltus Dusk, Garviel Loken and Leetu, finally reached
Lupercal's Court and found Sanguinius's mauled strung-up corpse, the Master of Mankind showed no
visible emotion.**[^lex-siege-of-terra]

He **ordered Sanguinius's corpse taken down, then ignored Lupercal's taunts and proclamations of
supremacy, instead addressing the Gods of Chaos observing the battle directly and ordering they
stand down. The Emperor stated that the Gods had killed his son — which Horus soon came to
understand was a reference to himself as well, and not just
Sanguinius.**[^lex-siege-of-terra]

That is the Emperor's assessment of the Heresy delivered to its authors, in front of the son who
started it: Horus was already dead, and had been since Davin. Whether it is grief or accounting is
left to the reader; the sources note only that he showed no visible emotion at the body of the son
who had just died holding the gate.

The duel that followed was not primarily physical. **Fully empowered by The Four, Horus was able
to match and then surpass the Emperor's own psychic might.** What began as a contest of sword and
maul **escalated into a clash across time as they found themselves assailing one another on
Cthonia, ancient Terra, Ullanor, Gorro, and even future Cadia**. They **began to attack one
another in their own memories**, and finally the battle **manifested as a card game with the
Imperial Tarot** — Horus using a deck **whose creation was aided by the Chaos Gods**. At the end
**the Emperor played his ultimate card, *Death*, but Horus countered with *The
Despoiler***.[^lex-siege-of-terra]

Note *future Cadia* in that list. The duel touched the world whose destruction in
[[III.T5.Ch22]] opens the Great Rift — ten thousand years ahead of itself. And note that the
Emperor lost. **The Emperor was defeated in the metaphysical**, and in the material realm was
overwhelmed.[^lex-siege-of-terra]

Horus then **dragged the Emperor towards the fifth throne he had constructed alongside those of
the four Chaos Gods**, proclaiming he would **punish the Emperor by forcing Him to be his
slave**.[^lex-siege-of-terra] The Emperor absorbed some of Horus's dark power for a
counterattack — **only a small portion, in order to avoid corruption** — and was overpowered again:
Horus **punctured his throat with a talon and nailed his father back to the throne using his own
sigil-blades**, then **created a crown of red energy and placed it upon the Emperor's head,
completing his tableau**.[^lex-siege-of-terra]

!!! disputed "Recorded conflict — who-stood-before-horus"

    **Previously it was ambiguous as to who actually stands between Horus and the Emperor's
    broken body, with some stating it was a Custodian, a Space Marine, or the lone Guardsman
    Ollanius Pius. The Black Library novelisation reveals all three to have been true at
    different points.**[^lex-siege-of-terra]

    This is the rare case of a long-standing contradiction the sources have deliberately
    resolved by making every version true in sequence. It is recorded here as **resolved**,
    because the resolution is itself the notable fact: three incompatible accounts of the
    setting's most famous act of defiance were reconciled by staging all three.

The sequence as the current account gives it: **the lone Custodian Caecaltus Dusk** refused to
back down and distracted Horus long enough for Leetu to act;[^lex-siege-of-terra] then **Ollanius
Persson arrived**, teleported in with John Grammaticus, and while John used **a single line of
Enuncia** to blow the Warmaster back, Ollanius **tried to wake the Emperor and give him the
Athame blade**. John fled; **Ollanius stood his ground and unloaded his lasgun into Horus**, who
**contemptuously swiped his talon, reducing Ollanius to red mist — but giving the Emperor the time
He needed**.[^lex-siege-of-terra]

A man with a lasgun, against a being who had just beaten the Emperor of Mankind. He achieved
nothing except a delay, and the delay was sufficient.

Then Garviel Loken, and the trap. Loken **pleaded with Horus to prove he was always the better man
and not a slave to Chaos**, pointing out that **the Emperor had refused to fully drink from the
Warp to become the Dark King** while Horus was the one being controlled — and **appealed to Horus's
pride, convincing him to briefly shed his Warp powers and finish the Emperor as a mere
Primarch**.[^lex-siege-of-terra] Horus agreed, **since the battle was over anyway**, and crushed
the Emperor's skull with *Worldbreaker* **as the Chaos Gods and daemons around them angrily
shouted and protested, trying to warn Horus of something**.[^lex-siege-of-terra]

**Loken and the Emperor's corpse had both been illusions.**[^lex-siege-of-terra]

The Emperor won by persuading his son to give up the only advantage he had, using an appeal to the
one virtue Horus had left. The Chaos Gods saw it coming and could not make him listen.

## The Mercy {#mercy}

What followed was physical and terrible. **The Warmaster was able to gouge out the Emperor's eye,
mutilate his arm, crush his spine, and set his hair alight.** The Emperor struck back **using the
power of the reactivated Astronomican to shine its light directly into Horus's
brain**.[^lex-siege-of-terra]

The beacon Sanguinius died to protect was used as a weapon at the decisive moment. Corswain's
mission, and Sanguinius's refusal of reinforcements, paid at exactly this point.

And then the Chaos Gods disciplined their champion. **Horus fell into agony as he desperately
tried to cloak himself in Warp power once more. However, much to Horus's horror, he discovered
that the Chaos Gods were now teaching him a lesson of sorts by deliberately denying him their
power. Horus came to the realisation that he had never been in control, and his Chaos masters were
treating him as they would a disobedient slave.** That realisation, with the brief absence of Warp
corruption over his soul, **saw the old Horus the Emperor had once loved manifest once
more**.[^lex-siege-of-terra]

He had been told the opposite on Davin. [[III.T3.Ch14]] records Erebus assuring him that the Gods
of Chaos were **peaceful beings with little interest in the Materium**. Horus learned what they
were in the last minutes of his life, by being switched off.

**Horus pleaded with the Emperor to kill him before the Chaos Gods could fully reassert control.
The Emperor initially hesitated, but granted Horus a mercy and stabbed His son through the chest
with the Athame blade Ollanius had given him. Stating that he forgave Horus and would wait for
him, the Emperor then channelled all of His might into the blade, obliterating Horus's form and
reducing him to a mere skeleton inside charred armour.**[^lex-siege-of-terra]

The Imperium's founding atrocity is officially a father executing a traitor. The sources record it
as a father granting a dying son's request, and forgiving him. The Ecclesiarchy of [[IV.T3.Ch8]]
has never taught it that way.

**With the deed done, the Emperor immediately collapsed.** Furious that their pawn was dead, **the
Chaos Gods instantly retreated back into the shadows**, and the Warp-madness that had plagued
Terra and the *Vengeful Spirit* went with them.[^lex-siege-of-terra]

## What the Emperor Said {#final-words}

The most consequential moment in the setting's history is the one its sources least agree about,
and the disagreement is not about detail but about whether the Emperor spoke at all.

!!! disputed "Recorded conflict — emperors-final-words"

    **There are two versions of the Emperor being placed upon the Golden Throne at the end of the
    Siege of Terra**, and the article sets them out side by side.[^lex-siege-of-terra]

    In **Horus Heresy: Collected Visions**, Dorn and the Custodians find him alive, and he speaks
    at length — a valedictory address naming *"Poor, brave Malcador the Hero"*, explaining that
    his psychic powers will return but his body will never heal, that he is **bound to this
    machine for all time**, and instructing Dorn and Jaghatai to fight on: **"This is not the end
    of our struggle. This is just the beginning of our crusade to save
    Humanity."**[^lex-siege-of-terra]

    In **The End and the Death: Volume III**, he **does not speak at all** during the
    deliberation. It is **Leetu, looking at an Imperial Tarot deck, who first suggests the
    Throne**. And Dorn's own later account of the only thing the Emperor said is: **"He said one
    word to me as I took him to the seat. 'Now,' I think He said. Or it might have been
    'No.'"**[^lex-siege-of-terra]

    The codex does not choose. Note precisely what is at stake: in one account the Emperor
    consents to the Throne and issues orders that found the Imperium's mission; in the other he
    may have been refusing it, and the man carrying him could not tell.

Everything else about the ascension differs with it. In the older account **Malcador's charred form
sits on the Throne still**, the tech-priests perform the exchange, **the last flicker of life
leaves Malcador's body and it turns to dust** — and at the instant of Malcador's death the Emperor
awakens, sustained by a fragment of strength Malcador had reserved for
him.[^lex-siege-of-terra] Khan **questions how such a horrific device could save the Emperor**,
and Dorn answers that **they do not know how the Throne works, but it was built by the Emperor and
they must trust him**.[^lex-siege-of-terra]

Dorn's answer is the founding act of Imperial faith, and it is an admission of ignorance. Nobody
present understood the machine. They used it because the Emperor had made it, and hoped. What that
machine has done for ten thousand years is [[III.T3.Ch17]]'s subject, and [[IV.T9.Ch30]] holds the
Throne itself.

In the newer account Malcador is already dead — **viewing the scene posthumously**, seemingly as a
deceased soul, his physical body having died hours before — and his private musing supplies the
detail that reframes the whole scene: **"He is the throne's only chance. I know it, and my friend
knows it. This is what we were trying to tell them."**[^lex-siege-of-terra]

So in one version the Throne was the Emperor's instruction and in the other it was a hint two dead
or dying men managed to plant in a tarot deck, because nobody living thought of it. The Imperium
was founded on the Golden Throne either by command or by guesswork, and the sources give both.

The codex records the divergence and asserts neither. What both agree on is the only thing that
mattered afterwards: he went onto the Throne, and he never came off it.
