# Age of Strife & Warp Isolation

## The Causes, in Order {#causes}

The **Age of Strife**, also known as **Old Night**, was a destructive, anarchic and
regressive period before the forming of the Imperium. It followed the Dark Age of
Technology — which by all accounts was an age of great prosperity — beginning in **M23 or
M25** and ending in **M30**.[^lex-age-of-strife]

Five millennia, give or take two. The uncertainty in the start date is the same uncertainty
[[III.T2.Ch8]] records about the Cybernetic Revolt, and for the same reason: the period
begins in a collapse that nobody was in a position to date at the time.

The sources are careful to present the causes as multiple rather than singular, and the
ordering is worth following because the Imperium's later doctrine picks one and forgets the
rest.

**There were multiple factors.** One of the earliest was **the revolt of the Men of Iron,
which devastated the human race and heralded the end of the Age of
Technology**.[^lex-age-of-strife] But **the first major event that many claim kickstarted the
Age of Strife was the rise of psykers on human worlds**, the danger of which was **compounded
by the eruption of massive and persistent Warp storms** around the Sol System and elsewhere.
In addition, records report the great war with the machines, humanity battling vast robotic
armies.[^lex-age-of-strife]

Three causes, interacting: machines, psykers, storms. Note that the sources hedge attribution
even here — *many claim* the psykers were first. What the Imperium retained from this is the
psyker cause and the machine cause, both of which became prohibitions, and it retained
nothing actionable about the third, because warp storms cannot be legislated against.

## The Psyker Catastrophe {#psykers}

The psychic emergence of [[III.T2.Ch6]] arrived in a civilisation that had no framework for
it, and the sources record the two policies humanity tried.

Psykers had first appeared towards the end of the Age of Technology. **While persecuted as
witches on many planets, the most enlightened and progressive worlds at first accepted
psykers and allowed these powerful psychics to explore and develop their
powers.**[^lex-age-of-strife]

Then the finding that decided ten thousand years of Imperial law. **It was quickly discovered
that the uncontrolled use of psychic energies proved a dangerous threat to humanity, and many
worlds fell under hellish dominance as the minds of these psykers became the gateways through
which Warp entities were able to enter the material universe. Only worlds which had
rigorously suppressed psykers were able to avoid this
fate.**[^lex-age-of-strife]

That is the most uncomfortable sentence in this Volume, and the codex will not soften it. The
tolerant worlds were destroyed. The repressive worlds survived. Whatever else is true of the
Imperium's persecution of psykers — the culling, the Black Ships of [[I.T2.Ch9]], the
sanctioning apparatus of [[IV.T9.Ch29]] — it is a policy with an empirical basis, arrived at
by observing which worlds were still there afterwards.

The mechanism is the one [[III.T1.Ch4]] set out in detail: a psyker's mind as a door. Sixty
million years earlier the Enslavers had used exactly that route against the Old Ones' engineered
psychic species. Humanity walked into the same trap with no knowledge of the precedent, and
the sources here do not name the Enslavers — they say *Warp entities*, which may or may not be
the same thing.

The collapse that followed was fast. The Age of Technology **ended catastrophically, as
widespread insanity, daemonic possession, and inter-human conflicts suddenly took hold
throughout the human worlds**.[^lex-age-of-strife]

## Isolation {#isolation}

The storms did the structural damage, and the mechanism was economic before it was military.

**With the emergence of Warp storms through most of the galaxy, travel through the Warp
became more and more dangerous until all but impossible, isolating many colony planets for
millennia.** Soon the planets dependent on importing goods to feed their enormous
populations — **particularly Terra** — suffered massive famine and widespread
revolts.[^lex-age-of-strife]

Terra is named first because Terra was worst placed. [[II.T2.Ch4]] describes a planet stripped
of resources with its soil barren and its oceans gone, feeding two hundred billion or more
from elsewhere. Cut the shipping and it starves, and it did: **without access to the wider
galaxy, Earth could no longer acquire sufficient food or resources to accommodate its
population, and untold billions died to starvation and resource
wars**.[^lex-age-of-strife]

Untold billions, on the homeworld, from a supply-chain failure. Not war, not Chaos — freight.

That weakness was then exploited. The famine **left humanity incredibly vulnerable to
external threats**, which several xenos races — the Orks among them — took advantage of,
executing brutal raids that devastated many human-colonised
worlds.[^lex-age-of-strife] The composite picture the sources give of the period's opening is
worth quoting for its accumulation: **mankind at the dawn of the Age of Strife gripped in
bitter civil war, while simultaneously battling hordes of powerful daemons and seemingly
endless xenos invasions**.[^lex-age-of-strife]

Civil war, daemons and invasion at once, in a starving civilisation that had just lost its
machines. And within the Sol System itself, **control shifted constantly between Terra and
Mars during the first half of the Age of Strife**.[^lex-age-of-strife] The two worlds of
[[II.T2.Ch4]] and [[II.T2.Ch5]] — which the Dark Age had left as peaceful partners — spent
two and a half thousand years fighting each other over the wreckage of the Solar System.

The regression was total. The Age of Strife was **a time of anarchy, destruction, and
regression, lasting thousands of years. Mankind's successes in the Age of Technology were
lost, and many human worlds regressed to the level of
barbarity.**[^lex-age-of-strife]

That is the sentence that explains the entire Imperium. Every feral world, every feudal world,
every population that has forgotten it came from somewhere else — all of it is the residue of
this. The Great Crusade of [[III.T3.Ch13]] was not conquest of unknown space; it was the
rediscovery of colonies that had spent five thousand years forgetting.

And humanity itself changed. **Over the long period of isolation new species of humans began
to evolve, adapting to suit their new environment**, and became the **Abhuman** races: the
**Ratlings**, stunted by thousands of years of inbreeding on worlds even more conducive to
human life than ancient Terra; the **Ogryns**, from harsh, cold and barren worlds; and the
**Squats**, from the barren high-gravity worlds towards the galactic
core.[^lex-age-of-strife]

The abhumans of [[IV.T2.Ch7]] are therefore not mutants in the sense the Imperium fears. They
are what happens to isolated human populations over five millennia, and the Ratlings'
recorded cause is the mildest imaginable: their worlds were *too* hospitable.

## What Survived {#survivors}

Not everywhere fell, and the pattern of survival is instructive.

**Some isolated pockets of human civilisation managed to survive and even thrive** — mainly
those worlds and star systems which were, or became, **completely self-sufficient in all
vital aspects**.[^lex-age-of-strife]

Self-sufficiency, not strength. The worlds that came through were the ones that did not need
anybody, which is precisely the inverse of Terra's position. Macragge in [[II.T2.Ch6]] is the
worked example: industries intact, contact retained, nothing imported that mattered.

Travel did not stop entirely. **Sub-light travel was still possible**, and some star systems
had several colonies to trade with and share resources; **warp jumps could be made on
occasion, as the storms waxed and waned**. A few fortunate systems were close enough together
for tiny **"pocket empires"** to form — among them the domain of the **Interex** and the Squat
homeworlds.[^lex-age-of-strife]

Those pocket empires matter more than their size suggests. They are the reason the Great
Crusade encountered organised human polities rather than only barbarians, and the Interex in
particular is the case that tests what the Crusade was for: a functioning human civilisation
that had survived Old Night on its own terms, which the Imperium met and did not absorb
peacefully. That episode belongs to [[III.T3.Ch13]].

## Why It Ended {#ending}

The Age of Strife did not end because humanity solved it. This is the causal link
[[III.T2.Ch6]] flagged, and the sources state it directly.

**The Warp storms continued to increase for a reason: the birth of Slaanesh, caused by the
Fall of the Eldar. As Slaanesh was finally born the Warp storms ended with a single storm of
massive proportions.**[^lex-age-of-strife]

Read the causality in both directions, because it runs both ways. The storms that isolated
humanity for five thousand years were the Aeldari Empire's decline registering in the
Immaterium — [[III.T2.Ch6]] dates that decline's beginning to M18, the same millennium as the
Warp Drive. And they ended in the single catastrophic event of
[[s:fall-of-the-eldar|the Fall itself]]. Humanity spent Old Night suffering the symptoms of
another species' decadence, and was released by that species' destruction.

What that release made possible is the rest of this Volume. **As the Warp began to calm the
Emperor was able to lead his Thunder Warriors, and later twenty Space Marine Legions, from
unified Terra to reunite humanity, beginning the Great Crusade that would create the
Imperium.**[^lex-age-of-strife]

The sequence is worth pinning down precisely, because the Imperium's own account blurs it.
The Emperor did not end the Age of Strife. The Fall of the Eldar ended the Age of Strife, and
the Emperor moved into the window it opened. His conquest of Terra is [[III.T3.Ch11]]'s
subject and the Crusade is [[III.T3.Ch13]]'s — and the sources are careful about which of
them began the new age: **the Age of the Imperium officially began with the Emperor's conquest
of Terra in the Unification Wars, but in the view of many, truly began with the Great
Crusade.**[^lex-age-of-strife]

That distinction is not pedantry. One reading makes the Imperium a Terran state that expanded;
the other makes it a galactic project that happened to start on Terra. The sources decline to
choose, and so does this codex — but the choice determines whether Mars's Treaty of Olympus in
[[II.T2.Ch5]] was an annexation or a founding partnership, and the Imperium has never settled
that question either.
