# The Technological Golden Age

## Before the Warp Drive {#sublight}

The Imperium remembers the **Dark Age of Technology** as a lost paradise whose relics it
worships. It is worth beginning with what preceded it, because the sequence explains why
the achievement mattered and why it was fragile.

In the prior age — the **Age of Terra** — humanity had already taken its first tentative
steps into the greater galaxy. Using slower-than-light travel it began slowly to explore,
and to encounter alien races: diplomacy was initiated with some and war with others, the
Orks among them.[^lex-dark-age-of-technology] Those early colonies **had to be
self-sufficient, and were isolated from Terra by long and dangerous
journeys**.[^lex-dark-age-of-technology]

From **M15 through M18** — the **First Expansion** — mankind began colonising the stars by
sub-light spacecraft. The ships sent to nearby systems **had to survive as independent
states**, separated from Earth by **up to ten generations of
travel**.[^lex-dark-age-of-technology]

Ten generations is the figure to hold. A colony ship's founders never arrived; their
great-great-great-grandchildren did. Every early human colony was therefore founded by
people who had never seen Terra, from a mission brief handed down orally and in archives
across three centuries of flight. Humanity's first galactic culture was not an empire but a
diaspora of self-governing strangers, and the Imperium's chronic problem — that a world out
of contact drifts — is not a symptom of the Age of Strife. It is the original condition.

The pattern shows in what those expeditions left behind. Autonomous probes were sent to
scout worlds suitable for habitation, and pioneering fleets of **Long March** settlement
ships travelled to them **carrying instructions on how to found human settlements in
hostile environments, along with instructions on how to repurpose their ships into combined
construction-defence platforms** — now known as **Knights**. Those colonies later evolved
into the Knight Houses and Knight Worlds.[^lex-dark-age-of-technology]

A colony ship designed to be dismantled into war machines, with the instructions included,
is a revealing artefact. The Knights of [[IV.T6.Ch21]] are not weapons the Imperium
developed; they are the disassembled hulls of humanity's first colony fleet, still walking.
Similarly, mining fleets sent from Terra towards the Galactic Core settled there, aided by
their **Ancestor Cores**, and from that sect arose the Leagues of Votann and the
Kin.[^lex-dark-age-of-technology]

## The Age of Exploration {#exploration}

Then one invention changed the scale of everything.

**The Warp Drive is invented in M18**, and interstellar faster-than-light travel becomes
possible. The **Age of Exploration** begins in earnest: many federations and empires are
founded, and the first **Alien Wars** are fought.[^lex-dark-age-of-technology]

But not safely, and not at once. **For a period of time Navigators did not
exist**[^lex-dark-age-of-technology] — and the sources record what that meant plainly:
**gruesome accidents and tragic missteps were known to have occurred as the strain of
humanity known as Navigators did not yet exist**.[^lex-dark-age-of-technology]

There is a period, then, in which humanity had warp travel and no one who could see where
they were going. [[I.T1.Ch3]] describes what the Warp does to a ship without protection;
this is the era in which that was learned empirically, by loss.

The fix was genetic. Between M18 and M19 **the Navigator gene was fashioned**, leading to
the Navigators — mutant humans who could see the currents of the Warp and steer a course
through them. This allowed longer and more accurate warp jumps, **and humanity proceeded to
colonise the stars at a furious rate**.[^lex-dark-age-of-technology]

Note the verb: *fashioned*. The Navigators of [[IV.T9.Ch30]] are not a natural mutation the
Imperium learned to exploit. They are an engineered instrument, built for a purpose, and
their hereditary Houses are the direct descendants of that programme. Within a millennium
the instrument had become a power in its own right: by M19 or M20 **Navigator families had
become powers in their own right, controlling the vast majority of industrial and trade
cartels**, forming Houses and surveying the systems that would later become the
Imperium.[^lex-dark-age-of-technology]

Humanity solved its navigation problem by creating a hereditary caste, and the caste
promptly acquired the economy. Ten thousand years later the Navis Nobilite still holds that
position, and this is where it was won.

!!! disputed "Recorded conflict — psykers-before-navigators"

    Psykers appeared across human populations **before** the Navigators were
    created,[^lex-dark-age-of-technology] or **after** warp travel and psykers had already
    become widespread.[^lex-dark-age-of-technology]

    The article devotes a section to this and reasons about the sources rather than simply
    listing them, which is worth reproducing. The Deathwatch Core Rulebook and *Horus
    Heresy: Collected Visions* carry identical text stating Navigators came after psykers —
    but the article notes that one of these may be an **in-universe Inquisition document**,
    judging by the artistic style of the page, and therefore potentially fallible. Rulebooks
    written as out-of-universe resources put widespread psykers in M22, after Navigators.
    A further source puts the rapid appearance of psykers in M25, after Navigators, warp
    drives and Gellar fields alike.

    The codex does not order the two. What every account agrees on is that both existed
    before the collapse, and that the psykers were the more dangerous development.

The Navigators themselves are not the only uncertainty. Some sources cite the first
scientifically proven psykers appearing between M18 and M22; others place it in the latter
portions of the age. What few Imperial records remain show the first mentions of psykers at
the **end of M22**, and by late M23 psykers had become **near ubiquitous across humanity's
worlds**.[^lex-dark-age-of-technology] By the end of M24, psykers and other mutations were
appearing in human populations across the galaxy.[^lex-dark-age-of-technology]

And one line records the consequence in six words: **the dread powers were drawn towards
humanity alongside this psyker emergence**.[^lex-dark-age-of-technology]

That is [[III.T1.Ch4]] happening again, to a different species, sixty million years later.
The Old Ones engineered psychic soldiers and drew the attention of the Warp. Humanity
evolved into a psychic species and drew the same attention. Nobody involved knew the
precedent, and the outcome was comparable.

## What the Golden Age Actually Was {#character}

The Imperium's memory of this era is of unity and wisdom. The sources describe something
considerably less comfortable, and the distinction matters because the Imperium's entire
technological theology rests on the misreading.

Humanity entered **a golden age of enlightenment as scientific and technological progress
accelerated**; human worlds united, and non-aggression pacts were secured with dozens of
alien races.[^lex-dark-age-of-technology] World after world was colonised at a rapacious
pace.[^lex-dark-age-of-technology]

But politically it was never one thing. **Stellar empires would rise and fall during these
times. The human race splintered and reformed time and again into warring or competing
power blocs and planetary empires** — and while there is evidence of many wars and conflicts,
**none seemed to threaten the destabilisation of human space as a
whole**.[^lex-dark-age-of-technology]

That is the honest description of the golden age: a permanently fragmenting civilisation
whose wars were survivable. Not peace — merely wars that did not matter enough to end
anything. The Imperium's ambition of a single unified humanity under one authority has no
precedent in the era it venerates.

The genetic engineering is where the era's reputation and its record part company most
sharply. Humanity's mastery of and belief in science let it experiment widely with genetic
engineering, modifying the genome to ever greater degrees and producing **armies of tailored
gene-troopers**. **Scientist-kings and technodemagogues would wage wars that consumed entire
star systems.** Bloody uprisings of genetically altered humans erupted within populations,
**many altered to a state beyond recognition and sanity**.[^lex-dark-age-of-technology]

Read that against the Imperium's horror of mutation. The Adeptus Mechanicus reveres this
period as the age of perfect knowledge, and the Ecclesiarchy of [[IV.T3.Ch8]] burns mutants
in its name. Both are looking at the same civilisation, which built gene-troopers by the
army and lost whole star systems to what it made. The Space Marine programme of
[[IV.T4.Ch12]] is not a departure from Dark Age practice. It is the one surviving
continuation of it.

The technological legacy is stated without hedging, and it is the foundation of everything
the Mechanicus is. **Technologies of the Dark Age were far more advanced than what the
Imperium has today, and indeed essentially all Imperial technology is based on acquired STCs
from the period.** In the superstition and religious fervour that has taken hold since the
Horus Heresy, Dark Age technology is viewed with a mixture of awe and fear, and its relics
are sought fanatically by the Adeptus Mechanicus.[^lex-dark-age-of-technology]

"Essentially all" is the operative phrase, and it is [[III.T2.Ch7]]'s subject. The Imperium
does not build; it copies, from templates it does not understand, made by a civilisation
whose politics it would consider heresy.

## The Aeldari Above It All {#aeldari}

One more fact frames the whole period, and it is easy to miss because humanity's records do
not mention it.

**During this age, and well prior to humanity's first steps into the stars, the Aeldari were
the undisputed rulers of the galaxy** — and the Aeldari Empire **cared little for the
goings-on of the lesser races**.[^lex-dark-age-of-technology]

Humanity's golden age happened inside somebody else's empire, and that empire did not
consider it worth attention. The Long March fleets, the Navigator Houses, the
gene-troopers, the stellar empires rising and falling: all of it beneath the notice of the
power that had inherited the galaxy in [[III.T1.Ch5]].

And that power was already rotting. **The Eldar Empire's gradual descent from ennui to
excess began in M18**[^lex-dark-age-of-technology] — the same millennium humanity invented
the Warp Drive.

The two curves are worth holding side by side, because they cross. As humanity began
expanding, the Aeldari began declining, and both processes were driven by the same
medium: humanity was becoming psychic and attracting the attention of the Warp, while the
Aeldari were indulging themselves into a psychic catastrophe. Neither civilisation knew it
was in a race.

The endings interlock exactly. Humanity's collapse into the Age of Strife came with the most
ferocious warp storms in its history; and **humanity's Age of Strife ended with the
culmination of the Fall of the Eldar Empire in M30, resulting in the creation of the Eye of
Terror, the birth of Slaanesh, and the calming of the tempestuous tumult in the
Warp**.[^lex-dark-age-of-technology]

That is the single most important causal link in this Volume, and it deserves stating
directly: **the Age of Strife ended because the Aeldari Empire fell.** The warp storms that
cut humanity into isolated worlds for five millennia subsided when Slaanesh was born and the
Warp's turbulence resolved into the Eye of Terror. Humanity did not recover through its own
efforts, and the Emperor's Great Crusade of [[III.T3.Ch13]] became possible because another
species destroyed itself at the right moment. [[s:fall-of-the-eldar|That destruction]] is treated
with the Aeldari; the collapse it ended is [[III.T2.Ch9]]'s.
