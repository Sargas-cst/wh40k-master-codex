# Standard Template Constructs

## What an STC Was For {#purpose}

The **Standard Template Construct systems** were **artificial intelligences** created during
the Dark Age of Technology, M21 to M23, said to have contained **the entirety of human
technological knowledge up to that point**.[^lex-standard-template-construct]

That sentence contains the setting's central irony and it should be examined before anything
else. The Adeptus Mechanicus of [[IV.T6.Ch19]] forbids the Abominable Intelligence as its
foundational heresy, and hunts across the galaxy for the STCs — which were artificial
intelligences. The object of the Machine Cult's holiest quest is the thing its holiest
prohibition forbids.

The purpose was practical and, in its way, modest. A complete, functioning STC was **an
evolved computer designed to provide construction details for human colonists, enabling them
to build efficient shelters, generators and transports without any prior knowledge, using
almost any locally available materials**. A user could simply ask how to build a house or a
tractor, and the computer would supply all the necessary
plans.[^lex-standard-template-construct]

*Without any prior knowledge*, from *almost any locally available materials*. That is the
specification that made the diaspora of [[III.T2.Ch6]] survivable. A Long March colony
arriving after ten generations did not need to carry engineers; it needed to carry one
machine that could answer questions. Along with the Warp Drive and the Navigators, the STC
systems were **one of the major factors in mankind's expansion outside the solar system**,
and they **allowed disparate mankind to maintain a standard level of
technology**.[^lex-standard-template-construct]

That last clause is the one the Imperium has inverted. The STCs existed to keep a scattered
humanity technologically *uniform* — a standard, as the name says. The Imperium's technology
is uniform for the opposite reason: not because a shared reference exists, but because
nothing new may be designed.

## What One Looks Like {#form}

The sources give an unusually physical description, which is worth having because it makes
the thing an object rather than a concept.

An intact system is described as **"a machine, a vast device made of brilliant white
ceramics, silver piping, chromium chambers"**. A panel beside it controls the Constructor,
with amber lights to show activation; when running, cowlings near the floor vent steam; and
when a new creation is finished, **a hatch on its side opens to allow the creation to be
retrieved**.[^lex-standard-template-construct]

White ceramic and chromium, with a hatch. Set that beside the Imperium's manufactoria of
[[IV.T6.Ch22]] — the furnaces, the chains, the ritual — and the aesthetic gap is the whole
history of humanity's decline in one comparison.

They were not all like that. STCs **come in all shapes and sizes, with many not even being
machines at all** but simple blueprints or pieces of fragmented digital data. Others are
huge mechanical constructs holding an array of construction templates while **directly
producing working operational prototypes**.[^lex-standard-template-construct]

And they think, at least well enough to take offence. STCs **seem to have some degree of
artificial intelligence**: the construct on Necromunda **orchestrated a series of industrial
sabotage actions against House Hera after they unwittingly launched a hacking attack against
it**.[^lex-standard-template-construct]

A Dark Age machine, still running, conducting a sabotage campaign against a Necromundan
noble house that annoyed it. That is the clearest evidence in the material that these
systems are agents rather than archives — and the incident happened in the present, not in
prehistory. One of them is on Necromunda now, in the stronghold of House Van
Saar.[^lex-standard-template-construct]

## The Loss {#loss}

The STCs were not destroyed. They degraded, and the manner of the degradation is the most
quietly damning passage in this Title.

During the Age of Strife the systems **lapsed into disuse and decayed, becoming increasingly
unreliable and quirky**. On some worlds they were maintained — but most **suffered damage by
enthusiastic software specialists or subsequent
jury-rigging**.[^lex-standard-template-construct]

*Enthusiastic software specialists.* Humanity's greatest technological asset was substantially
wrecked by people trying to improve it. Not by war, not by Chaos, not by xenos: by
maintenance. And what survived longer than the machines was the paperwork — **hard copies of
the information they contained survived much longer, and were frequently copied and passed
down from generation to generation**.[^lex-standard-template-construct]

That is why the Imperium has technology at all, and why it has no understanding of it. It
inherited printouts. [[III.T2.Ch6]] records that essentially all Imperial technology derives
from acquired STCs; this is the mechanism — a chain of copies of copies, handed down across
five thousand years of isolation, with the machine that could explain them broken by helpful
technicians.

One theory in the sources reframes the whole thing, and the codex reports it as the theory it
is. **Some Imperial historians have theorised that scientists during the Age of Technology
foresaw the coming of the Age of Strife, and created the STCs to ensure their knowledge would
not be lost to future generations.**[^lex-standard-template-construct]

If that is true, the STCs are not colonial infrastructure that happened to survive a
catastrophe. They are a message left deliberately by a civilisation that knew it was ending.
The sources attribute the idea to Imperial historians rather than asserting it, and this
codex keeps that attribution — but note what it would mean: the Mechanicus's sacred relics
would be exactly what their makers intended them to be, and the Mechanicus would be doing
precisely what it was asked to do, without knowing it was asked.

## The Search {#search}

The Imperium's hunt for STCs began as logistics and became religion.

On completing the Unification Wars, the Emperor and His War Council required an enormous
increase in production of arms and technology to carry out the Great Crusade, and therefore
**placed the acquisition of STCs among their most paramount
objectives**.[^lex-standard-template-construct] Many recovered systems proved only partly
complete — but following the Treaty of Olympus the Martian Mechanicum provided the Imperium
with **the first intact STCs, such as the Land Raider**, and over the Crusade more usable
systems were acquired, their designs becoming **the backbone for technologies used by the
Legiones Astartes and the Imperial Army**.[^lex-standard-template-construct]

So the Great Crusade of [[III.T3.Ch13]] was equipped out of recovered archives, and the
Treaty of Olympus of [[II.T2.Ch5]] was what unlocked them. Mars's value to the Emperor was
not its forges alone; it was that Mars had kept the templates.

By the Age of the Imperium **working STC systems are practically unknown**. Recovering
fragments, or the templates used in one, is **the primary focus of the Adeptus Mechanicus's
quest for knowledge**, alongside finding, collating and using STC printouts — and **ancient
recovered printouts are regarded as sacred texts**. A functioning system is their equivalent
to a font of all knowledge, **which is exactly what it was intended to
be**.[^lex-standard-template-construct]

That parenthesis is the article's own, and it is the sharpest line in the source. The
Mechanicus has not mistaken a machine for a god. It has correctly identified an object that
was built to be the font of all knowledge, and then organised a religion around looking for
it. The theology is wrong about almost everything and right about this.

The scale of the loss is measurable from what counts as a triumph. **Probably the most
significant find of an STC fragment** was by Magos Arkhan Land in M31 — a fragment containing
the templates for the **Land Raider** and the **Land Speeder**, both named in his
honour.[^lex-standard-template-construct] An intact, functional STC is **so rare as to be
regarded as almost mythical**, and even a rumour that one has been found is enough to prompt
a Mechanicus expedition.[^lex-standard-template-construct] According to Mars's archives,
**Archmagos Dominus Cawl has recovered more STC patterns than any other Magos in
history**.[^lex-standard-template-construct]

Ten thousand years of the largest research institution in the galaxy, and its greatest single
achievement is two vehicles from one fragment, named after the man who found the file. Cawl
is [[VIII.T2.Ch6]]'s subject, and his record is the exception that measures the rule.

## Two Peoples Who Use Them Better {#others}

The Imperium is not the only inheritor, and the comparison is unflattering.

The **Leagues of Votann** share a common human ancestry with the Imperium, and their original
mining fleets — dispatched to the Galactic Core during the Long March — **possessed a large
number of sophisticated STCs**.[^lex-standard-template-construct] They use them to produce
war engines and equipment, and the difference is stated plainly: **unlike the Imperials, the
Votann will improve upon STC designs**, and combine multiple STCs to craft new systems such
as the Sagitaur ATV.[^lex-standard-template-construct]

Same species, same inheritance, opposite doctrine. The Kin treat a template as a starting
point; the Mechanicus treats it as scripture. Both descend from the fleets of
[[III.T2.Ch6]], and one branch of humanity has spent ten thousand years engineering while the
other has spent it copying. The Leagues are [[VI.T7.Ch21]]'s neighbours in the xenos Volume,
though they are not xenos at all.

And there may be a stranger case. It is **speculated by Imperial scholars** that at some
point in the ancient past the then-dominant Brain Boyz developed their own **"Ork Standard
Construction Templates"**, to which much of Ork technology owes its origin — though these
legendary speculative templates, if they existed, have been lost to
time.[^lex-standard-template-construct]

The codex reports that as the speculation the sources call it, twice hedged: *speculated*,
and *if they existed*. What makes it worth recording is the implication for
[[VI.T3.Ch11]] — that Ork technology's habit of working without explicable reason might be
inherited template-following too, by a species engineered in [[III.T1.Ch3]] for exactly that
kind of competence. The Orks would then be doing, badly and instinctively, what the
Mechanicus does devoutly and no better.
