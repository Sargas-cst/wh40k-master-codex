# Descent of Holy Terra

## The Homeworld Reduced {#reduced}

Terra spent the Age of Strife becoming the thing the Imperium now calls holy, and the process
is worth following because nothing about it was sacred.

**Terra and the rest of the Solar System were surrounded by terrible Warp storms, isolating
the human homeworld for several thousand years.**[^lex-age-of-strife] The consequences of
that isolation were set out in [[III.T2.Ch9]]: no imports, famine, and **untold billions dead
to starvation and resource wars**.[^lex-age-of-strife]

Then the reduction, dated. **By the 28th Millennium almost all traces of civilisation on Earth
were long gone.** What remained was **a battleground of deserts whose brute savages scavenged
for what scarce arable land and water remained**, amid the domains of warlords **which fought
over the scraps of the ancient culture**.[^lex-age-of-strife]

Set that against [[II.T2.Ch4]]. The Terra of the 41st Millennium is a hive world of continental
palaces, its oceans artificial, its soil barren, its air unbreathable without assistance. The
Terra of M28 was a desert where people fought over water. The modern planet's condition is
usually read as the cost of ten thousand years of Imperial administration; this Chapter records
that the ruin came first, and the Imperium built its capital on top of it.

The scarcity is the detail to keep. **Arable land and water** — humanity's homeworld, which had
governed a galaxy-spanning civilisation two millennia earlier, reduced to a competition over
farmland. Not high technology, not orbital infrastructure. Fields.

## The Techno-Barbarians {#techno-barbarians}

The people who fought that competition have a name, and their equipment is the reason they
matter to everything that follows.

**Techno-barbarian** is the name given to the warlords and warrior hordes which battled over
the ruins of Terra during the Age of Strife.[^lex-techno-barbarian] They formed the troops of
the warlords who dominated the planet, and were **equipped with a primitive form of powered
armour** — which **would become the basis for the developments of Space Marine
armour**.[^lex-techno-barbarian]

There is the through-line from a desert warlord's retinue to the Adeptus Astartes of
[[IV.T4.Ch12]]. Power armour is not an Imperial invention and not a Dark Age relic recovered
by the Mechanicus. It is a scavenger technology, developed by barbarians on a dying Terra out
of what they could still make work, and then refined. The most iconic object in the setting
began as the field kit of men fighting over wells.

The compound name is doing precise work too. These were not barbarians in the sense of people
who never had technology; they were people who had technology and had lost the civilisation
that produced it. Every techno-barbarian warlord was operating salvaged Dark Age equipment
with no ability to replace it — which is exactly the Imperium's own relationship to its
machines, as [[III.T2.Ch7]] describes, arrived at three thousand years early.

The Emperor's rise happens among them and out of them. **The Emperor arose in the later part of
the Age of Strife to reunify Terra**, and under his leadership his army **conquered the other
warring techno-barbarian factions**.[^lex-techno-barbarian]

*The other* factions. The sources place him inside the category rather than above it: a
techno-barbarian warlord who won. That framing is repeated elsewhere — **a great warlord, later
to be known as the Emperor, used the first genetically modified humans — little more than a
unified force of techno-barbarians — to conquer the
planet**.[^lex-age-of-strife] [[III.T3.Ch11]] holds his rise; what this Chapter establishes is
the world he rose in.

The timing is recorded with unusual precision, and it is worth pinning because of what
[[III.T2.Ch9]] established about why the age ended. This happened **one hundred years before
the waking of Slaanesh**,[^lex-age-of-strife] and the conquest of Terra by the Thunder
Warriors was undertaken **in anticipation of the dispersal of the Warp storms around the
planet**.[^lex-age-of-strife]

*In anticipation.* The Emperor unified Terra before the storms lifted, expecting them to lift.
Whatever else is true of him, he knew the Fall of the Eldar was coming and timed a planetary
conquest to be finished when the galaxy reopened. The Thunder Warriors are treated in
[[III.T3.Ch11]] and the Primarchs in [[III.T3.Ch12]] — and the sources note here that the
Primarchs were created and then **spirited away by the Chaos Gods**, who, unable to destroy
them, scattered them across the galaxy; the Emperor retained their DNA to continue producing
engineered warriors.[^lex-age-of-strife]

## The Names That Survived {#names}

What the sources preserve of Old Night on Terra is almost entirely a list, and the list itself
is the historical finding.

**Little information remains from this dark time** — but the names of warlords who ruled are
known: the **Adedeji**, **Belot**, **Cardinal Tang**, **Daival Shan**, **Dame Venal**,
**Dalmoth Kyn**, the **Great King of Akkad**, **Gotha**, **Kalagann**, **Kibuka**, **"the
half-mad half-genius" Narthan Dume**, the **Priest-King of Maulland Sen**, **Uilleam the Red**,
and the **Unspeakable King**.[^lex-age-of-strife]

Alongside them, the polities: the **Achaemenid Empire**, **Akkad**, **Albia**, **Albyon**,
**Boeotia**, the **Caucasus Wastes**, the **Franc**, **Gyptus**, **Jermani**, **Merica**, the
**Maulland Sen Confederacy**, the **Nordafrik Conclaves**, **Nordyc**, **Oseania**,
**Oxitania**, the **Panpacific Empire**, the **Saragorn Enclave**, **Scandian**, the
**Terrawatt Clan**, old **Ursh**, and the **Yndonesic Bloc**.[^lex-age-of-strife]

Read those names as data rather than colour. They are corrupted Terran geography — Jermani,
Merica, Franc, Nordyc, Albyon, Gyptus, Oseania, Scandian — the continents and nations of ancient
Earth surviving as sounds after the civilisations that named them were gone. And beside them
sit **Akkad** and the **Achaemenid Empire**, names from humanity's earliest recorded history,
back in use.

That is what five thousand years of isolation does to a planet's memory, and it is the most
concrete illustration in this Volume of the regression [[III.T2.Ch9]] describes. The names did
not survive as knowledge. They survived as sounds, reattached to whatever was standing. The
High Gothic and Low Gothic of [[I.T1.Ch2]] are the same phenomenon in language: a
degraded descendant of something, spoken by people who cannot read the original.

One of these polities is worth a note because [[II.T2.Ch4]] mentions it still. The
**Terrawatt Clan** ruled from the Ural Mountains, and the Himalayas of the modern Imperial
Palace stand where they stood. The warlords' domains are the substrate of the Imperial capital.

## Mars Took the Other Road {#mars}

The Solar System's other world faced the same isolation and produced the opposite outcome, and
the comparison is the point of including it here. The full account belongs to
[[II.T2.Ch5]]; what matters for this Chapter is the divergence.

Mars **too struggled with anarchy and starvation**, but suffered a catastrophic failure of its
own: **routine maintenance on their Solar Shields went undone and the shield disintegrated
entirely**. Solar radiation destroyed the ecosystem and vegetation humanity had cultivated over
millennia, and the survivors of the anarchy were plagued by radiation — most dying soon after
exposure, others **reduced to mutants: gibbering, cannibalistic
zombies**.[^lex-age-of-strife]

And then, from that, an institution. **With the destruction of Mars on the brink of
possibility, a new idea arose: the Cult of the Machine God.** Its devotees sought out the
scattered technology needed to build temporary radiation shelters, and under their Tech-Priests'
direction set about restoring their world — recovering **oxygen generators, food processing
machines, and yet larger shelters** through selfless dedication and personal
sacrifice.[^lex-age-of-strife]

Two planets, one catastrophe, two responses. Terra produced warlords who fought over the
scraps. Mars produced a religion that rebuilt the atmosphere. The Mechanicus's veneration of
machinery, which [[IV.T6.Ch19]] treats as theology, began as a public works programme that
worked.

The Martians' verdict on Terra is the detail that should be remembered whenever the Imperium
speaks of its holy homeworld. The Tech-Priests **visited Terra, occasionally raiding it for
remnants of technology** — and **they were appalled at the destruction of Terra, and saw nothing
worth saving**.[^lex-age-of-strife]

Nothing worth saving. That is Mars's contemporaneous assessment of the planet that would
shortly become the spiritual centre of the human species, delivered by the civilisation that
would become its industrial partner under the Treaty of Olympus.

What Mars did instead was study the problem. The Martians **studied the Warp and, after many
lifetimes, learned to detect lulls in the Warp storms** — and at the same time **the immense
fighting machines known as Titans were created**.[^lex-age-of-strife] For over a thousand years
the Cult Mechanicus watched and waited, and whenever a break occurred an expeditionary force
was sent, complete with a full Titan Legion, thousands of servitors and Tech-Priests. Some
were lost in the Warp or perished on faraway worlds; others **succeeded in establishing Forge
Worlds — replicas of Mars**. Broken messages were transmitted home, but **not until the time of
the Great Crusade would the various Forge Worlds and Mars be
reunited**.[^lex-age-of-strife]

So the Forge Worlds of [[IV.T6.Ch22]] are not Imperial foundations. They are Martian colonies,
planted during Old Night by a civilisation that had taught itself to read the storms, and
reunited with their parent world only when the Emperor arrived. Mars did not wait to be
rescued; it was already expanding. The Titan Legions of [[IV.T6.Ch21]] were built for that
programme, not for the Crusade.

## The Outer System {#outer-system}

One more fragment completes the Solar System, and it matters because it shows the Imperium's
founding was a negotiation in places.

**Little is known about the state of the outer Solar System during this period.** What is known
is that **a polity existed on the moons of Saturn whose military arm was the Saturnine
Fleet**. Its exact extent is unknown, but **it joined the Imperium at the end of the Age of
Strife** — and notwithstanding that union, **the Saturnine Fleet retained a separate hierarchy
and its own unique traditions up to the time of the Horus
Heresy**.[^lex-age-of-strife]

A Saturnian state that joined rather than fell, and kept its own command structure for
centuries afterwards. The Imperium of [[IV.T1.Ch1]] presents itself as a single hierarchy;
here at its founding is a member polity with its own hierarchy, persisting for the whole of the
Great Crusade. Set that beside Mars's Treaty of Olympus in [[II.T2.Ch5]] and Ultramar's
autonomy in [[II.T2.Ch6]] — the pattern of the Imperium absorbing intact states on their own
terms is present from the first day.

Not all of Saturn agreed. **Another Saturn-based culture, that of the Ringers, opposed Imperial
rule and were defeated by the Space Marines.**[^lex-age-of-strife]

That is the whole of the recorded history of the Ringers: they existed, they refused, and they
were destroyed by the Legions. It is a single sentence, and it is the template for the Great
Crusade that [[III.T3.Ch13]] describes — compliance offered, refusal answered. The first world
to receive that choice was a moon of Saturn, before the Crusade had left the Solar System at
all.
