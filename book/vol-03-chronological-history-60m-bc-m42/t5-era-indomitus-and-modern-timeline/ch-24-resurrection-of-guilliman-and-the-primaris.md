# Resurrection of Guilliman & the Primaris

## Two Instructions {#instructions}

[[III.T5.Ch22]] ended with Abaddon learning that Cawl had something aboard his ship and not knowing
what. The answer was set in motion ten thousand years earlier.

**Sometime before the Battle of Thessala, Guilliman tasked the Tech-Priest Belisarius Cawl with two
deeds: the resurrection of Guilliman should he fall, and the creation of a next generation of Space
Marines.**[^lex-roboute-guilliman]

Read that as what it is: a contingency plan for his own death and the obsolescence of his own
Legions, filed by the man who had just written the Codex Astartes. [[III.T4.Ch18]] records Guilliman
breaking the Legions into thousand-strong Chapters so that no commander could threaten Terra again,
and monitoring gene-seed more closely because accelerated cultivation had produced the flaws Chaos
exploited. At the same time he commissioned a replacement for the Astartes and an insurance policy on
himself.

Then the wound. **In the Battle of Thessala against the Emperor's Children, Guilliman would be
grievously wounded.** Fulgrim **had been to the Eye of Terror, reaping the terrible powers therein,
and had been elevated by Slaanesh to a mighty and fell daemon prince, no longer resembling a man**
but **a serpentine creature of immense stature, and multi-limbed**.[^lex-roboute-guilliman]

Fulgrim is one of the six daemon primarchs whose blessings Abaddon gathered before the Thirteenth
Black Crusade, per [[III.T4.Ch20]]. The wound he inflicted at Thessala is the one that had to be
healed before Guilliman could return to fight the Crusade Fulgrim endorsed.

## Ten Thousand Years in Stasis {#stasis}

**For the next ten thousand years Guilliman's mortal body remained in stasis, on the Shrine of
Guilliman deep within the Temple of Correction, one of the holiest places in the entire
Imperium.**[^lex-roboute-guilliman]

[[II.T2.Ch6]] describes that shrine from Macragge's side — inside the Fortress of Hera, atop the Crown
Mountains, one of the holiest pilgrimage sites in the Imperium. What pilgrims went to see is the
subject of a dispute the sources record without resolving.

**Some pilgrims claimed that the Primarch's wounds were slowly recovering, a feat credited to the
power of the Emperor. Others deny the phenomenon, and point out the sheer impossibility of change
within the stasis field. Yet enough believed the stories to come and witness for themselves the
miracle of the Primarch.**[^lex-roboute-guilliman]

That is the Imperial Cult of [[IV.T3.Ch8]] in one paragraph, and it is worth noticing that the
sceptics are recorded too — and that their objection is technical rather than theological. A stasis
field, by definition, admits no change; therefore the wounds cannot be healing; therefore the
miracle is not occurring. The Imperium contains people who reason like that, and the sources note
that enough people ignored them to sustain a pilgrimage for ten millennia.

The irony is that the miracle was real and its author was a Tech-Priest. What eventually healed the
wounds was **a suit of power armour that regenerated the injuries inflicted by
Fulgrim**[^lex-roboute-guilliman] — the **Armour of Fate**, which Guilliman
wears.[^lex-roboute-guilliman] The pilgrims were right that he would recover and wrong about who was
arranging it.

## The Resurrection {#resurrection}

The route to Macragge ran through the Webway, and it ran through an alliance.

**Shortly after Abaddon the Despoiler's Thirteenth Black Crusade and the destruction of Cadia, the
Tech-Priest Belisarius Cawl and Saint Celestine led a group of Imperial survivors into the Webway
with the aid of their new Eldar allies. The group arrived at
Macragge**[^lex-roboute-guilliman] — where **Cawl revealed that he had in fact lived for the last ten
thousand years and was ordered by Guilliman himself to complete two tasks in the event of his
downfall**.[^lex-roboute-guilliman]

The Webway is the Old Ones' construction from [[III.T1.Ch3]], the network the Emperor died trying to
give humanity access to in [[III.T3.Ch17]], and the thing Magnus broke in [[III.T3.Ch15]]. The
Imperium's survivors of Cadia reached Macragge through it as guests of the Aeldari.

And then the act itself. **One of these was to revive Guilliman, a feat that Cawl could now
accomplish thanks to Eldar aid. Using a suit of power armour that regenerated the wounds inflicted by
Fulgrim, and with his soul restored to his body by Ynnead, Guilliman was restored just as Black
Legion forces invaded Macragge.**[^lex-roboute-guilliman]

Take that sentence apart, because the Imperium cannot afford to. The body was mended by Mechanicum
engineering. **The soul was restored by Ynnead** — the Aeldari god of the dead, whose partial awakening
in [[II.T3.Ch10]] caused the Dysjunction that is currently destroying Commorragh, and whose waking
is one of the five candidate causes of the Great Rift in [[I.T1.Ch4]].

The Imperium's greatest living hero, Lord Commander of a state founded on the principle that all
xenos are a danger, was returned to life by an alien god, in a procedure arranged by a Tech-Priest
who had concealed a ten-thousand-year lifespan, with a route provided by Harlequins. Not one element
of it is lawful under the doctrine of [[III.T3.Ch11]] or the Creed of [[IV.T3.Ch8]].

His first act was to fight the invasion that arrived in time to witness it — the Black Legion assault
on Macragge that [[II.T2.Ch6]] records as the fourth invasion of Ultramar, and which Abaddon had
launched **hoping to prevent an Imperial–Aeldari expedition from resurrecting Guilliman**. He
arrived too late by the length of a ceremony.

## What Cawl Was Building {#primaris}

The second instruction took the whole ten thousand years, and its product is the Imperium's largest
military reform since the Codex Astartes.

**Primaris Space Marines are the next step in the evolution of the Emperor's Space Marines** —
**larger and more physically powerful than their standard cousins, in addition to having a more
stable gene-seed**.[^lex-primaris-space-marines] They **appeared in the closing days of the 41st
Millennium following the Thirteenth Black Crusade and resurrection of Roboute
Guilliman**,[^lex-primaris-space-marines] and **pre-Primaris original template Space Marines are now
referred to as "Firstborn"**.[^lex-primaris-space-marines]

*A more stable gene-seed* is the phrase that connects this to [[III.T4.Ch18]]. Guilliman's own
post-mortem on the Heresy was that accelerated gene-seed cultivation created flaws Chaos exploited.
The Primaris are the engineering answer to that finding, commissioned by the man who made it.

The programme's inputs are extraordinary. Cawl was given **the Sangprimus Portum, which allowed the
Archmagos access to information regarding all twenty
Primarchs**[^lex-primaris-space-marines] — including, necessarily, the two erased from the record in
[[III.T3.Ch12]]. He **also used several unique STC-technologies during the
process**.[^lex-primaris-space-marines]

So the Primaris Marines were built using Dark Age Standard Template Constructs — the artificial
intelligences of [[III.T2.Ch7]] whose recovery is the Mechanicus's holiest quest and whose nature is
its holiest prohibition — and genetic data on all twenty Primarchs, two of whom the Emperor ordered
forgotten. **The task would take ten thousand years to complete.**[^lex-primaris-space-marines]

And the scale was ready before anyone was told. **Tens of thousands of Primaris Marines were secretly
created by Cawl over the millennia, while many more were made after the rebirth of Guilliman. Many
of these were produced and held in stasis within the *Zar-Quaesitor*, Cawl's
flagship.**[^lex-primaris-space-marines] **While in stasis, they were nonetheless trained and
indoctrinated through virtual simulation programs beamed directly into their
brains.**[^lex-primaris-space-marines]

That is the artefact Abaddon's seer detected in [[III.T5.Ch22]] and could not identify: a ship
carrying tens of thousands of superior Space Marines, asleep, being taught in their sleep, for ten
thousand years. Abaddon captured the vessel and did not find them.

Guilliman's own justification is the quotation the sources open with, and it is worth ending on
because of how much it conceals. **"They were forged for Mankind's darkest hour — and that hour is
upon us."**[^lex-primaris-space-marines]

He is describing a decision he made a hundred centuries earlier, executed in secret by a Tech-Priest
using forbidden technology and proscribed genetic archives, against a possibility he had correctly
foreseen. The Imperium of [[IV.T1.Ch1]] would have executed everyone involved. It accepted them
because the alternative was losing, which is the same reasoning that put a Necron on Cadia's null-array
in [[III.T5.Ch22]] and an Aeldari god in Guilliman's chest.
