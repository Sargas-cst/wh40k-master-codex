# Indomitus Crusade Reconquest

## Whose Idea It Was {#origin}

The **Indomitus Crusade** was launched by the resurrected Guilliman **after he declared himself Lord
Commander of the Imperium's armies during the Thirteenth Black
Crusade**.[^lex-indomitus-crusade] Its aim is **reconquest**: to liberate the worlds embattled by
Chaos following the creation of the Great Rift.[^lex-indomitus-crusade] **The undertaking was so
grand that it echoed the Great Crusade of old.**[^lex-indomitus-crusade]

But the sources record a doubt about its authorship, and it is the most interesting fact in this
Chapter. **It was not until Guilliman departed from the Throneroom of the Imperial Palace after
meeting with the Emperor that he declared the need for the Indomitus Crusade, leaving some to
speculate it was the Emperor's will as opposed to his
own.**[^lex-indomitus-crusade]

Nobody knows what was said in that room, and it matters enormously. [[III.T3.Ch17]] establishes that
the thing on the Golden Throne has been silent since the Siege, sustained by a machine at twenty per
cent — and [[III.T3.Ch16]] records that the sources cannot agree whether he spoke at all at the end.
Guilliman went in, came out, and ordered the largest military undertaking in ten thousand years. The
Imperium cannot establish whether its founder gave that order.

## What It Cost to Muster {#muster}

The Crusade's assembly is described with a candour that Imperial accounts usually avoid, and it makes
the reconquest look less like a liberation than a triage.

Resources came from **many worlds capable of producing vast material output** — **Terra, Mars,
Jupiter, Armageddon, Bastrophol and Shenjin** among
them.[^lex-indomitus-crusade] Armageddon on that list is worth a pause: [[II.T3.Ch8]] has it fighting
its fourth war against Ghazghkull with four of nine hives fallen, and it was still contributing
materiel to the Crusade.

The manpower came from somewhere worse. **Military forces were garnered through withdrawing forces
from Imperium Sanctus systems left untenable due to the impact of the Great
Rift.**[^lex-indomitus-crusade]

*Left untenable.* The Crusade was crewed by stripping the garrisons off worlds that had been judged
already lost. And the people asked to abandon them objected: **this process spawned a great deal of
resentment, and several mutinies were done by forces unwilling to abandon their
posts**.[^lex-indomitus-crusade] **In the end, the withdrawals succeeded in providing vast numbers of
warriors and war machines.**[^lex-indomitus-crusade]

Imperial soldiers mutinied rather than leave the populations they were defending, and were overruled.
The Crusade that liberated worlds was assembled by evacuating the defence of others, and the sources
record the mutinies without condemning them.

Its composition was everything the Imperium has. **Troops from over a dozen Space Marine Chapters led
by the Imperial Fists**, joined by **Imperial Guard, Mechanicum, Custodes, Sisters of Battle, Imperial
Navy, Skitarii, Legio Cybernetica, Collegia Titanica, Knights, a small contingent of Sisters of
Silence, and the newly created Primaris Space Marines**.[^lex-indomitus-crusade]

The Custodes are in that list, and they should not be. [[III.T4.Ch19]] records the Adeptus Custodes
sealing themselves in with the Emperor's body for seventy years and not learning what Vandire was
doing. Their presence in a galactic crusade is a measure of how far the Imperium's normal
arrangements have been suspended.

## Short Jumps {#method}

One sentence explains why the Crusade took as long as it did. **With the Astronomican in a state of
disarray due to the formation of the Great Rift, the Indomitus Crusade risked only short jumps
through the warp.**[^lex-indomitus-crusade]

Everything in [[II.T1.Ch1]] about the beacon defining the practical limits of Imperial space applies
here in miniature. The Imperium's largest fleet in ten millennia moved across the galaxy in short
hops because its navigation aid was unreliable — and its earliest objectives were infrastructural
rather than territorial: **the recapture of Warp Nexuses around Terra, such as those at
Vorlese**.[^lex-indomitus-crusade]

The first operations of the reconquest were fought to secure the road. Compare
[[III.T3.Ch13]]'s Void Harbours — the Great Crusade was supplied along the warp routes too, and the
Indomitus Crusade had to take them back before it could go anywhere. One early action has a
characteristic solution: **the Grey Knights cleansed Jupiter's moon of Ganymede of daemonic taint,
subsequently transforming it into a major Hub-Fortress for the
Crusade**.[^lex-indomitus-crusade]

## The Transition {#transition}

The Crusade's first phase ended after roughly **twelve years**,[^lex-indomitus-crusade] and what
happened then reshaped the Adeptus Astartes permanently.

**The Unnumbered Sons were finally broken up. When the vast holds of the *Zar-Quaesitor* were
emptied, Archmagos Cawl departed, for he had many more secret vaults to activate in order to complete
the Ultima Founding.**[^lex-indomitus-crusade]

*Many more secret vaults.* [[III.T5.Ch24]] recorded tens of thousands of Primaris held in stasis
aboard one ship; this establishes that the ship was not the whole of it. Cawl had caches elsewhere,
and the Ultima Founding of [[IV.T4.Ch15]] is their activation.

The consolidation method is worth recording because it is how the Imperium's map changed. **Once
deployed, new Chapters remained after the initial conflicts were won, seeking to consolidate the
crusade's gains. In many cases, they did this by establishing their own new Chapter
planets.** In this way **the crusade not only freed worlds from the tyranny of the Dark Gods, but also
strengthened their defences against further attacks that were sure to
come**.[^lex-indomitus-crusade]

New Chapters, on new homeworlds, garrisoning reconquered space. That is the fortress-monastery
arrangement of [[II.T3.Ch7]] being created wholesale in the present rather than inherited from
M31 — and it is the largest expansion of Astartes territory since the Second Founding of
[[III.T4.Ch18]].

Guilliman's own priority then shifted homeward. **After hearing of serious developments in the Plague
Wars of Ultramar, Guilliman could no longer ignore his home and decided to
return.**[^lex-indomitus-crusade] The Plague Wars have their own Chapter in [[VII.T4.Ch12|Volume VII]], and count as the fifth
invasion in [[II.T2.Ch6|Ultramar]]. **After the Battle of Raukos, Guilliman held a great Triumph and announced the dispersion of
the Crusade throughout the Imperium.**[^lex-indomitus-crusade]

## Into Nihilus {#nihilus}

The second half of the undertaking was the one the Crusade had been declared for, and it required a
door.

**After the conclusion of the Plague Wars in Ultramar, Guilliman announced his intention to venture
into the Imperium Nihilus at long last in order to reassert Imperial rule
there.**[^lex-indomitus-crusade] But **with the Nachmund Gauntlet too beleaguered, he was forced to
find another** route; **a second path dubbed the Attilan Gate was discovered near the Pit of Raukos
Warp Rift, but was too unstable for safe travel**.[^lex-indomitus-crusade]

That is the Nachmund Gauntlet of [[II.T1.Ch2|the Sundered Imperium]] under siege and the Vigilus of
[[II.T3.Ch8|the bastion worlds]] beset by five powers at
once. The Imperium's only reliable crossing was contested, so **Guilliman entered Imperium Nihilus
through the Attilan Gate while he left Cawl to attempt to stabilise it with Necron Pylon
technology**.[^lex-indomitus-crusade]

Read that carefully. The Imperium's route into its own lost half was stabilised using the technology
of the species whose pylons Abaddon spent ten thousand years destroying — and Cawl succeeded
**around 15 years after the start of the Crusade** by **acquiring Necron data from a derelict World
Engine**, just as Raukos was assailed by the forces of
Vashtorr.[^lex-indomitus-crusade]

The pylons of [[II.T3.Ch8]] held the Cadian Gate open for ten millennia and nobody understood them.
Fifteen years after Cadia's destruction, the Imperium is deliberately using pylon technology to hold
a warp passage open, having learned how from a Necron wreck. That is the single clearest measure of
how much the Era Indomitus has changed what the Imperium will countenance.

The crossing succeeded, and produced the arrangement [[II.T1.Ch2]] records. **Guilliman eventually
succeeded in making it through Imperium Nihilus. He arrived at Baal as it was beset by both Tyranids
and Chaos, and helped save the Blood Angels and their Successor Chapters from destruction. After the
battle Guilliman met with Dante, who he appointed as Regent of Imperium Nihilus and gave the
resources to bring some semblance of order to the besieged realm. He then returned to the Imperium
Sanctus to continue his work.**[^lex-indomitus-crusade]

[[II.T3.Ch7]] describes what he arrived at: Baal reduced to one settlement, Baal Primus rendered
barren, the Chapter saved only by his arrival. And the Regency is the second Imperium Secundus — the
same instrument Guilliman himself raised around Ultramar when the Ruinstorm cut it off, per
[[II.T2.Ch6]]. He has now done this twice, ten thousand years apart.

The Chapter ends where the Imperium currently stands, which is not in triumph. **Sometime after the
Devastation of Baal, Abaddon and Vashtorr launched their Arks of Omen Campaign. During the subsequent
wars, the entire Indomitus Crusade Fleet Quartus was corrupted or destroyed by a vast Khornate horde
led by Angron.**[^lex-indomitus-crusade]

An entire Crusade fleet lost — corrupted *or* destroyed, the sources decline to say in what
proportion — to a daemon primarch who had manifested twice on Armageddon in [[II.T3.Ch8]]. The
Indomitus Crusade is not a completed reconquest. It is an ongoing operation that has already lost one
of its fleets, and [[III.T5.Ch26]] is what arrived while it was busy.
