# 13th Black Crusade & the Fall of Cadia

## The Plague of Unbelief {#prelude}

[[III.T4.Ch20]] established that the Black Crusades were a demolition programme aimed at the
pylons. This is the one that reached the objective, and it opened not with a fleet but with an
epidemic.

The first signs, as M41 closed, were **numerous sightings of Space Hulks emerging from the Warp
around the Cadian Sector**. Those that got past the Imperial blockade **struck strategically
important worlds and naval bases throughout the sector, crippling the region's naval
forces**.[^lex-13th-black-crusade] Then the Nurgle vessel *Plagueclaw* appeared, and Admiral Quarren's
hunt for it discovered something worse: **the Chaos fleet was led by the dreaded *Terminus Est*,
flagship of Typhus of the Death Guard**.[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

What followed was not conquest but contagion. **Typhus's Plague Fleet spread pestilence throughout
the Sector and the Officio Medicae were helpless to stop it.** As the epidemic spread,
**apocalyptic sects began appearing on afflicted worlds, preaching that the Emperor's wrath had
descended upon them and was a punishment for their sins.** In what became the **Plague of
Unbelief**, **Chaos spread throughout the sector as the fanatical sects rioted against Imperial
authorities** — and **Plague Zombies soon emerged from the bodies of those struck down by
disease**.[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

The mechanism is worth naming precisely, because it is the Imperium's own theology used as a weapon.
A plague arrives; the population concludes it is divine punishment for insufficient faith; the
resulting religious frenzy turns against the Imperial authorities who cannot stop the plague.
The creed of [[IV.T3.Ch8]] teaches that suffering indicates sin, and Typhus only had to supply the
suffering.

It scaled. **Cults preaching that the Imperium had forsaken the teachings of the Emperor grew in
number**, and **Imperial authorities, crippled by plague, could do little to stop the ensuing chaos
and mob rule**.[^lex-13th-black-crusade] On the world of **Lelithar** a figure arose **proclaiming
himself the Voice of the Emperor**, whose cult spread and **encouraged rebellion against Imperial
rule**; **attempts by the Officio Assassinorum to dispose of the Voice of the Emperor proved
unsuccessful**.[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

And on Cadia itself the machinery began to fail. **The world's mysterious pylons erupted into life
and vibrated intense psychic energy. The pylons appeared to be fighting to hold back the powers of
the Warp that were pouring from the Eye of Terror at an unprecedented level — but they were slowly
destroying themselves in the process.**[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

[[II.T3.Ch8]] recorded this from the pylons' side: they resonated at an amplitude comparable to a
Gellar Field, and microscopic fractures appeared. The Imperium watched its own shield crack and did
not know that was what it was watching.

## Betrayal at Tyrok Fields {#tyrok}

**Despite the deteriorating security situation, Cadia remained a beacon of
stability.**[^lex-13th-black-crusade] What broke it was a parade.

To counter increasing Chaos activity, **the Cadian High Command issued an order that every regiment
of the Cadian Shock Troops were to muster on Cadia itself**, and hundreds of extra landing fields
were assembled at **Kasr Tyrok**. Millions had already assembled when **the Volscani Cataphracts
regiment, considered by many to be the most hardened fighters in the sector, landed on
Cadia** — and **in honour of the reputation of the Volscani regiment, the Cadian High Command itself
gathered at Tyrok Fields to personally greet the arriving
forces**.[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

**Exchanging their Cadian and Imperial banners for those of Chaos, the Volscani forces opened fire
on the Cadian assembly on Tyrok Fields and annihilated most of the Cadian High Command in one fell
swoop.**[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

The entire command structure of the Imperium's most militarised world, killed at a welcome ceremony
by a regiment it had summoned. [[II.T3.Ch8]] describes Cadia as a society in which every citizen
belonged to a branch of the military and social class was determined by military rank; that
structure put its whole leadership in one field to greet a unit nobody had screened.

**Only a skilful counterattack by Lord Castellan Ursarkar E. Creed, who managed to take control of
the reeling Cadian forces, prevented further calamity.**[^lex-13th-black-crusade] Creed became
Governor **due to the murder of the previous holder of the position** and **ordered the Cadian
forces to dig in as he dispatched requests for aid throughout the whole of the
Imperium**.[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

The Lord Castellan office of [[II.T3.Ch8]] existed precisely for this: a rank held in reserve for
when the Governor Primus is incapacitated. It worked. It is one of the few Imperial contingencies in
this codex that functioned as designed.

Elsewhere the picture was worse. The Agripinaa Sector was ravaged by Typhus; **only an intervention
by the Howling Griffons, Jouran Dragoons and Legio Astorum prevented the total collapse of Imperial
rule** there.[^lex-13th-black-crusade] Lucius the Eternal butchered Belisar; Night Lords warbands
moved on Scarus; and **cells of Alpha Legion revealed themselves across Segmentum Obscurus, badly
delaying Space Marine reinforcements in protracted
struggles**.[^lex-13th-black-crusade] **Soon, every world within a thousand light years of the Eye
of Terror became embroiled in conflict.**[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

The Alpha Legion's role is the tell. [[III.T4.Ch18]] recorded them as one of the three traitor
Legions that never withdrew into the Eye, operating inside Imperial space for ten thousand years.
Their contribution to the Thirteenth was not battle but delay — holding up reinforcements at the
moment they were needed.

## Seventy-Seven Minutes {#quarren}

The Imperium then made the same mistake it had made after the first wave of every previous Crusade.

**After several months of fighting the Imperium had reestablished its hold on the Fortress World and
many considered the worst of the crisis to have passed.** Most Kasrs had been retaken, Chaos fleets
scattered — **such was the confidence of many Imperials that victory celebrations were already being
actively planned**. **However in truth, the attacks so far were merely the first wave in Abaddon's
grand campaign.**[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

Then the armada. Quarren's picket fleet **detected a massive Chaos armada exiting Warp space near
the Cadian system** — **of unparalleled size**, including the *Terminus Est*, and **worst of all a
massive Blackstone Fortress, the *Will of Eternity*, aboard which Abaddon now made his command
centre**.[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

That is the twelfth Black Crusade's prize from [[III.T4.Ch20]], arriving as the thirteenth's flagship
nine hundred years later. The Gothic War was not a raid the Imperium repelled; it was the
acquisition of the weapon that would break Cadia.

**While Quarren led a valiant defence from the Emperor Class Battleship *Might of the Faithful*, the
Imperials were annihilated after only 77 minutes.**[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

Seventy-seven minutes for the fleet defending the most important world in the Imperium.

Creed's response exposed the deeper problem. He convened a council of war with a Wolf Lord, a Dark
Angels Master, an Inquisitor, a Canoness and a Magos — and **despite Creed's best efforts to create a
unified command, the various Imperial factions present still proved too stubborn and rebellious.
Creed lamented that he did not have one true army from which to defend Cadia, but many smaller
independent forces with their own agendas.**[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

That is the Codex Astartes of [[III.T4.Ch18]] delivering exactly what it was designed to deliver.
The Legions were broken into autonomous Chapters so no commander could ever again control an army
large enough to threaten Terra. At Cadia, no commander could control an army large enough to defend
it either.

## The Necron Who Saved Cadia {#trazyn}

Two outsiders arrived because they had independently detected the same thing, and neither was
human.

**Trazyn the Infinite's collection on Solemnace was devastated in upheavals. This provoked him into
action, and using the Celestial Orrery he traced the disturbances to the Eye of Terror. Trazyn was
moved to resolve the crisis by going to Cadia.**[^lex-13th-black-crusade] At the same time,
**Belisarius Cawl was instructed by the Harlequin Shadowseer Sylandri Veilwalker to investigate a
long-abandoned planet devastated in the 4th Black Crusade. On the world he discovered the presence
of Pylons much like Cadia** — and was then **instructed by Veilwalker to head to
Cadia**.[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

A Necron overlord and an Aeldari Harlequin worked out what Abaddon was doing to the pylons, and one
of them had to point the Imperium's greatest surviving technologist at the evidence. The Crimson
Path of [[III.T4.Ch20]] was legible to two xenos powers and not to the species whose homeworld was
the target.

What happened next the sources state as flatly as it deserves. Abaddon positioned the *Will of
Eternity* over Cadia to destroy its Kasrs with the fortress's Warp gun. Magos Klarn had not
completed repairs to the **Null-array** that powered Cadia's planetary shield. Abaddon fired — **and
he was surprised when, as the weapons charged for their full barrage, the planetary shield
manifested. Trazyn had interceded, supplying more than sufficient power to the array**, to the
astonishment of Klarn, **who chose not to report in that moment the xenos-tech which infested the
null-array**.[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

And then the sources render the verdict themselves: **Cadia was saved not through the bravery of the
Space Wolves nor through the labours of the Adeptus Mechanicus, but through the intercession of one
curious Necron.**[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

The Imperium's official account of Cadia is a story of human defiance. The record says the shield
that held Abaddon's first barrage was powered by a member of the species from [[III.T1.Ch5]] whose
king judged that the time of all flesh would pass, acting because his private museum had been
disturbed — and that the Tech-Priest who noticed decided to keep quiet.

The alliance became formal. As the situation worsened, **such was the desperate situation for the
Imperium that they even agreed to a temporary alliance with the Necron**, and Cawl **laid out his
plan to amplify the Cadian Pylons, pushing back the Chaos horde and perhaps even weakening the Eye
of Terror itself**.[^lex-13th-black-crusade] Trazyn revealed himself to Cawl and **offered aid with
the Cadian Pylons, admitting that he too did not wish to see the galaxy ripped asunder by the
Warp**.[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

Trazyn also fought. He **secretly aided the Imperial defenders with his own Deathmarks** and
**disabled a Chaos Baneblade discreetly**;[^lex-13th-black-crusade] when Abaddon came for Cawl
directly, Trazyn **used his many Tesseract Labyrinths to summon a whole army before him, including
the Heresy-era Lieutenant Commander Cerantes of the Ultramarines** — and Abaddon **was forced to kill
Cerantes** before finding that both scientists had escaped.[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

The Tesseract Labyrinths of [[III.T1.Ch5]] hold shattered C'tan. They also, it turns out, hold a
collection of people, and Trazyn spent it defending Cadia.

## The Protocols, and the End {#evacuation}

The most damning documents in this Chapter are Imperial, and they concern the evacuation.

**The Imperial evacuation plans had been worked out at the time of the 2nd Black Crusade** — and
**were often completely outdated, confused, and just not
feasible**.[^lex-13th-black-crusade] The second Crusade was 597.M32, per
[[III.T4.Ch20]]. Cadia's evacuation plan was over eight thousand years old.

Worse, there was a plan not to evacuate at all. **The Inquisition had secretly drafted the "Extremis
Protocols", effectively forbidding an evacuation of Cadia due to the risks of corruption being
spread by its overly Chaos-exposed defenders.**[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

The population raised from birth to fight Chaos at the Imperium's front door was, by that fact,
classified as too contaminated to rescue. And Creed disobeyed: **Creed had covertly worked against
the "Extremis Protocols", and augmented as well as updated the old evacuation plans, which he
triggered** after the *Will of Eternity* crashed.[^lex-13th-black-crusade] **An attempt by
high-ranking Inquisitor Talia Daverna to prevent the entire evacuation was overruled by Greyfax, and
Daverna was later shot by Cadians when she continued her blocking
efforts.**[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

An Inquisitor shot by the people she was trying to condemn. The Cadians of [[II.T3.Ch8]] had no
Arbites and policed themselves through the Interior Guard; at the end they applied that authority to
an Inquisitor obstructing their survival.

The Imperium's own recommendation had been worse still. With Kasr Kraf briefly secure, **the
Inquisition judged the situation doomed and recommended virus bombing Cadia to spare it from the
Archenemy**.[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

The Chapter's other deaths are worth recording. Sven Bloodhowl led a boarding action onto the *Will
of Eternity*, was mortally wounded by Abaddon — and had **fallen into the Wolf Lord's trap as the
*Phalanx* appeared and unleashed a punishing salvo**, forcing Abaddon to teleport away and
**disabling the *Will of Eternity***.[^lex-13th-black-crusade] Creed's standard bearer **Jarran Kell
gave his life staying behind to hold back the enemy**, falling **personally battling
Abaddon**.[^lex-13th-black-crusade] And Saint Celestine defeated the Khornate champion Urkrathos,
who **welcomed his temporary banishment** knowing the Warp gun would annihilate his
killers.[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

Urkrathos's objective is the one that mattered. His assault's **main objective was to disable the
Pylons emitting null-fields around Kasr Kraf, which would interfere with the Warp Gun** — and he
**finally reached the Null-Array of Kasr Kraf, slaughtering Mechanicum troops before him and
destroying the infernal device which was poison to his Daemonic
kin**.[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

Then the end, in one sentence. **Meanwhile, with the pylons failing, Celestine was back at her
maximum power and led a withdrawal of Imperial forces from doomed Cadia. As Creed and parts of his
Cadian 8th held the line against the returned Daemonic legions, the Imperials fled Cadia as the
planet was dragged into the Warp.**[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

[[II.T3.Ch8]] records what [[II.T1.Ch3]] adds: Abaddon collided the debris of the *Will of Eternity*
into the surface, destabilising the pylons as they were awakening, and the chain reaction destroyed
them. The planet the Imperium had garrisoned for ten thousand years without understanding was
consumed by the thing it had been holding back.

One coda points at the next Chapter. Aboard the *Vengeful Spirit*, **Abaddon was told by one of his
seers that Cawl has a precious artifact aboard his vessel. What the artifact was was not known, but
Abaddon considered it of immense importance** — and the Black Legion **assailed the Imperial
evacuation fleet just as it was about to jump into the Warp**, capturing Cawl's ship. Cawl and
Greyfax were **guided by Celestine's vision to seek salvation on the outer planet of the system,
Kalisus**.[^lex-13th-black-crusade]

Abaddon knew Cawl was carrying something and did not know what. [[III.T5.Ch24]] does.
