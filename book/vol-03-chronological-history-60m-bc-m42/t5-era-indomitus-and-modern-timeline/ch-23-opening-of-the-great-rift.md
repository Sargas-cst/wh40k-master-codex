# Opening of the Great Rift

## Guilliman's Explanation {#guilliman}

The Rift's geography is [[II.T1.Ch2]]'s and the competing theories of its origin are
[[I.T1.Ch4]]'s, where five are recorded and none can be chosen between. This Chapter is about the
event, and it begins with the one explanation offered by someone in a position to know.

**This new tear in reality was created at the end of the 41st Millennium during the Thirteenth
Black Crusade, following the destruction of Cadia.**[^lex-great-rift]

**Guilliman speculates that the Rift formed after Abaddon strategically destroyed worlds rich in
blackstone over ten thousand years, with the destruction of Cadia as its
trigger.**[^lex-great-rift]

That is the Crimson Path of [[III.T4.Ch20]], reconstructed after the fact by the Lord Commander of
the Imperium. Set the two together and the sequence is complete: twelve Black Crusades that took no
territory but destroyed pylon-bearing worlds one by one; a thirteenth that broke the largest
concentration of them; and a tear across the galaxy as the accumulated barrier gave way.

The word the sources use is *speculates*, and this codex keeps it. But note what distinguishes
Guilliman's account from the other four theories in [[I.T1.Ch4]]: it is the only one that explains
why the Crusades were fought the way they were. The others identify a moment — Magnus's sorcery,
Ynnead waking, bloodshed in the Damocles Gulf, the Ruinous Powers reacting to Guilliman's own
resurrection. His identifies a programme.

What is not in doubt is the effect. **All that is truly known is that its emergence was a
figuratively galaxy-shattering event that divided the Imperium in half and ushered in new wars
across nearly every world in the Emperor's Empire.**[^lex-great-rift]

And the physical consequences reach further than the division. **So powerful and far reaching was
this Warp Storm that the very laws of physics have begun to fray**, and the time fluctuations **once
largely localised to larger storms such as the Eye of Terror have spread across the entire known
galaxy** — some worlds feeling **centuries go by in an instant**, others **all but frozen in
time**.[^lex-great-rift]

That is the fact that makes the Era Indomitus unlike every previous crisis in this Volume. The Age of
Strife in [[III.T2.Ch9]] cut worlds off from each other; the Rift has cut them off from a shared
present. Two Imperial worlds in the same sector may no longer be in the same year, which is why
[[I.T1.Ch2]]'s check numbers — the digit recording how reliably a date can be related to time on
Terra — went from bureaucratic pedantry to the only honest way to write history.

## What a Thousand Cultures Called It {#names}

The Rift's names are worth a Section because the list is a survey of who was hurt by it.

**The Great Rift is variously known by the cultures of the galaxy as the Crimson Path, the Mouth of
Ruin, the Warpscar, the Dathedian, Gork's Grin** — **and a thousand other names
besides**.[^lex-great-rift] The Space Wolves and the folk of Fenris named it
**Everdusk**.[^lex-great-rift] To the T'au it is **the Greater Axial Rift**, or **Mont'yhe'va, the
Devourer of Hope** — **for its stellar storms killed millions of T'au and left many colonies
devastated**.[^lex-great-rift] **To the Imperium, it is the Cicatrix
Maledictum.**[^lex-great-rift]

Two of those names carry arguments. **Crimson Path** is Abaddon's own term for his programme, per
[[III.T4.Ch20]] — which means one of the galaxy's names for the Rift is the name of the plan that
made it, in use by cultures who do not know that. And *Devourer of Hope* is the T'au Empire of
[[II.T3.Ch10]] recording civilian losses: the expanding, diplomatic power that terraforms dead worlds
lost millions of people and whole colonies to a wound in reality that human politics produced.

Its appearance depends on where you stand. **It appears to those on the western side of Segmentum
Solar as a tainted scar across the sky**[^lex-great-rift] — and the sources' sentence about those on
the eastern side is where the division of [[II.T1.Ch2]] becomes visible from the ground rather than
on a chart.

## The War in the Rift {#war-in-the-rift}

Two responses to the Rift close this Chapter, and they belong to opposite sides.

The Imperium's is a crusade. **In response to this new catastrophe, the Primarch Roboute Guilliman,
now Lord Commander of the Imperium's armies, has launched the Indomitus Crusade** — its aim **to
liberate those Imperial worlds that now find themselves embattled by the forces of
Chaos**.[^lex-great-rift] Guilliman **tasked many crusade elements, Torchbearer fleets and other
assets, such as a Rogue Trader Katla Helvintr, to find a stable route through the Rift, capable of
ensuring the mass-movement of men and material**; at least one was exploited, **near the Eye of
Terror in the Nachmund Gauntlet**, and **some years later a second route dubbed the Attilan Gate was
discovered**.[^lex-great-rift]

That is [[II.T1.Ch2]]'s Gauntlet and [[II.T3.Ch8]]'s Vigilus arriving as the consequence of a search
ordered here. The Crusade itself is [[III.T5.Ch25]].

The other response is Chaos's, and it is not an offensive. **Inside the Rift itself, the Gods of
Chaos have embarked on an extension of the Great Game, battling one another for supremacy in what has
become known as the War in the Rift.**[^lex-great-rift]

The four powers of [[V.T1.Ch5]] opened a wound across the galaxy and then began fighting each other
inside it. That is the same pattern [[III.T1.Ch2]] recorded of the C'tan — a victory so complete
that the victors turned on one another for want of anything else to do — and it is the reason the
Rift has not simply swallowed the Imperium. [[II.T2.Ch6]] notes the Plague Wars ending partly because
the Scourge Stars were imperilled by the War in the Rift: Chaos's internal war is, functionally, the
Imperium's most effective ally.

One last claim is recorded because a named Imperial figure made it, and because it is either the
most important fact in the Era Indomitus or wishful thinking. **According to Mathieu, the Forces of
Chaos made a grave mistake by opening the Great Rift, for it is also empowering the Emperor and
allowing for his eventual return.**[^lex-great-rift]

The codex records that as Mathieu's view, attributed. Weigh it against [[III.T3.Ch17]]: the thing on
the Golden Throne has run at twenty per cent since the Siege, its psyker consumption has quadrupled,
and its Mechanicum custodians bought it a few more centuries by selling billions of people to
Commorragh. Against that, one man's belief that the Rift is feeding it. Whether Mathieu is right is
the open question the setting currently rests on, and this codex does not answer it.
