# Nova Terra & the Age of Apostasy

## The Time of Twin Empires {#interregnum}

Between the Codex Astartes and the Age of Apostasy the Imperium split in half, and the sources
give the episode two thousand words less than it deserves.

**The Nova Terra Interregnum** is **also known as the time of twin Empires, and lasted for nine
centuries**.[^lex-nova-terra-interregnum] **The Ur-Council of Nova Terra dismissed the authority of
the High Lords and claimed rule of the Segmentum
Pacificus.**[^lex-nova-terra-interregnum]

Nine hundred years, and an entire segmentum — one of the five fleet zones of
[[II.T1.Ch1]] — governed by a rival council that simply declared the High Lords irrelevant. The
Imperium was two states for longer than it had been one.

The split spread through the Imperium's institutions rather than along a front. **Eventually the
civil conflict spread to the Adeptus Mechanicus, resulting in the Moirae
Schism.**[^lex-nova-terra-interregnum] Then the church intervened: **during the Cataclysm of Souls
in 975.M35 the Ecclesiarchy tried to unite the Imperium as a religious state. Their efforts ended
in massive religious wars against the Ur-Council in a civil war unseen since the Horus
Heresy.**[^lex-nova-terra-interregnum]

That last comparison is the sources' own. A civil war on the scale of the Heresy, and the Imperium
records it in a paragraph. The Heresy gets libraries because the Emperor was in it.

One episode is worth noting for what it says about the Dark Angels. **During the fighting, the Dark
Angels made a daring attack upon Nova Terra upon suspecting that the mysterious Cypher was the
member of the Ur-Council known as the Saviour of Praxus, though they failed to secure any
prisoner.**[^lex-nova-terra-interregnum] A Chapter conducting its own operation, inside a galactic
civil war, in pursuit of a private obsession.

And one world came through untouched. **Far from Nova Terra, it is said that the Realm of Ultramar
was strengthened by the Interregnum and was free of the turmoil that beset the rest of the
Imperium.**[^lex-nova-terra-interregnum] That is the point [[II.T2.Ch6]] makes again: the realm that pays
no tithe and needs nothing from anyone gains when the centre fails.

## How the Ecclesiarchy Came to Rule {#ecclesiarchy}

The **Age of Apostasy** was **one of the largest and most destabilising periods in the history of
the Imperium**, occurring in M36, five millennia after the Horus Heresy — and it **grew out of the
power struggle between two Imperial organisations, the Ecclesiarchy and the
Administratum**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

The mechanism by which a church acquired the state is described with unusual clarity, and it turns
on a misunderstanding nobody corrected.

The Ecclesiarchy **spread into the lives of every citizen and servant of the Imperium, from the
most humble miner and scribe to the greatest heroes and the most influential officials, including
the High Lords of Terra**. And **the High Lords, believing like every other citizen that the
Ecclesiarchy was speaking for the Emperor, would often follow their words in directing the
Imperium**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

Then the sentence that makes it fraud rather than error: **though they did not actually speak the
words of the Emperor, the Ecclesiarchy did little to retract this
image**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

Soon they were **indirectly controlling the fate of the Imperium, influencing legal changes,
military deployments and organisation, threat priority decision-making, and where Imperial
resources should be directed — often towards themselves**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

That is the Imperial Cult of [[IV.T3.Ch8]] arriving at temporal power by never denying an
assumption. And it is precisely the outcome the Emperor's Imperial Truth in [[III.T3.Ch11]] existed
to prevent: he abolished religion because faith opened doors, and five thousand years later a church
was running the state by letting people believe it spoke for him.

The opposition assembled from the institutions the church had displaced. **Eventually the power of
the Ecclesiarchy grew too much, and the Fabricator-General of the Adeptus Mechanicus, a group of
Space Marine Chapter Masters, and eventually the Administratum rallied together to fight against
the control of the Ecclesiarchy.**[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

## Vandire {#vandire}

**Goge Vandire is the name most closely associated with the Age of Apostasy, and the Reign of
Blood.** He was **the 361st High Lord of the Administratum** with **a reputation as a staunch
resistor to the dominance of the Ecclesiarchy**. **It is said he used assassins to gain his
position** — and **any who spoke out against him mysteriously disappeared or were found dead, so
none dared oppose him**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

His method was to control both sides. **Shortly before his rise, Vandire was instrumental in the
rise of Ecclesiarch Paulis III, an incompetent who was easily controlled by Vandire and his
followers.**[^lex-age-of-apostasy] Then, once he had the Administratum, **he used his powers to
undermine the Ecclesiarchy, while he led a group of Imperial Guard officers into the Ecclesiarchal
Palace and overthrew Paulis III in what can only be described as a military coup. He declared
Paulis a heretic and had him shot** — and took control of the Ecclesiarchy himself, **effectively
controlling both at once as High Lord of the Administratum and
Ecclesiarch**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

One man holding the two most powerful offices in the Imperium, having installed a puppet in one in
order to depose him. The Imperium's institutional checks, as described in [[IV.T1.Ch1]], did not
slow this down at all.

The purge that followed had a piece of luck that Vandire turned into doctrine. Those Cardinals who
had not fled **gathered onto a single ship and set off for Ophelia VII in an attempt to escape**;
**their ship was engulfed by a gigantic warp storm and never seen again just as they entered the
warp**. **Vandire used this as evidence to claim that it was the Emperor's will that they should
die, and that he himself should lead the faith of the
Imperium.**[^lex-age-of-apostasy] He then elected Cardinals of his own choosing, **including many
weak-minded fools and several brilliant geniuses who were just cruel enough to follow
him**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

What his rule was actually like is recorded in more detail than most Imperial atrocities, because
he insisted on it. **As time went by it became increasingly clear that Vandire was insane. He was a
paranoid megalomaniac who saw traitors and conspiracy everywhere** and **enjoyed personally
torturing people, declaring to onlookers that he was purging their souls for the
Emperor**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

**He had his every word recorded for posterity and had a long trail of scribes whose job was to
note anything he said, and to note any particularly ingenious torture
methods.**[^lex-age-of-apostasy] He would fall into trance-like states during which, **according
to him, he was receiving the words of the Emperor** — while **actually mumbling arguments with
himself and occasionally shouting out loud for no reason**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy] And he kept **a
huge map of the galaxy plotting up-to-date warp storm movements, so that whenever a new planet was
revealed he could** reach it.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

The specific orders are worth listing because the sources list them, and because they were carried
out. He had the hive world of **Calana VII** bombarded **without reason**; ordered the farmlands of
**Boras Minor** invaded and **every female child under twelve years of age** enslaved; and had the
ice caps of **Jhanna** melted with orbital defence cannons, **drowning nearly four million
people**. **All of his orders were meticulously recorded for posterity, including the deaths of
millions.**[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

The rot reached the armed forces. **In the Imperial Navy, it became a generally accepted practice
for officers to murder superiors to speed up their promotion.**[^lex-age-of-apostasy] With the
Departmento Munitorum also under his control, **many Astra Militarum regiments enforced his tyranny
on countless worlds**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

For **seventy years** this was the government of the human species.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

## The Brides of the Emperor {#brides}

The most consequential thing Vandire did was acquire a bodyguard, and how he did it is the single
best illustration in this codex of what Imperial faith actually rests on.

His spy network reported **a small cult of perhaps only five hundred members on the little known
agri world of San Leor** — the **Daughters of the Emperor**, **open only to women, who devoted
themselves to the worship of the Emperor through inner purity** and **studied the arts of war
tirelessly, honing their skills throughout their entire
lives**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy] Vandire was **furious**: people were worshipping the Emperor in a
way he did not control — **but the more he heard the more he began to covet this
knowledge**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

His visit was staged as propaganda. He took **almost 100 soldiers and servants**, his agents moving
ahead to force the population to line the streets; **those who resisted were executed as heretics,
no matter whether they were or not**, and **even newly born babies and the aged were forced to
go**, supplied with laurels and gifts to present **at gun point**, with **holo-vids of the
proceedings sent across the galaxy**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

At the temple **he found the gates barred against him and was informed by a young Daughter that he
was not recognised by the cult**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy] What he did next he had planned.

With a small bodyguard he entered, **clutched his Rosarius while kneeling to the Emperor, praying
for protection** — then **stood and ordered one of his guards to shoot him**. The officer refused
at first, **unwilling to endanger himself**, but Vandire persuaded him. **The officer loaded his
laspistol and shot Vandire in the chest. There was a blinding flash of light and when it faded,
Vandire was still standing, unhurt. As one, the warriors in the temple fell on their knees to
him.**[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

And then the admission, recorded by his own scribes: **later, Vandire boasted to his scribes that
he assumed the Daughters would not have heard of a Rosarius and the conversion field it
created.**[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

A force-field trinket, a compliant gunman, and a congregation that had never seen the equipment.
That is how the Adepta Sororitas of [[IV.T3.Ch11]] came into Imperial service — the most devout
military order in the galaxy, recruited by a conjuring trick its perpetrator wrote down as a joke.

They served him completely. Renamed the **Brides of the Emperor**, elevated to Ecclesiarchal
bodyguards, **trained by the best soldiers of the Imperial Guard and given the most modern
weaponry**, they **would perform any action that their lord wanted, at any
time**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy] They were also his **companions and servants** — tasting his food,
feeding him when ill, nursing his frail body, entertaining him — **and even though they were used in
this way, they were still hardened fighters**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy] When the Ecclesiarchy tried to
assassinate him, **the Brides entered the meeting chamber of the traitorous Cardinals and locked
the doors behind them; one hour later they emerged with the heads of every cardinal
present**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

## Sebastian Thor, and How It Ended {#thor}

Vandire's insanity, the sources note, **was only ever directed outwards**. While other planets
raised spires to him, **the Palaces on Terra were falling into decay again** — whole wings
collapsing, chandeliers left unlit, **dust at knee depth in some places**, tapestries **ripped and
chewed by rats, who used the priceless carpets for nests**. When rain cleaned the windows and light
poured in, **Vandire would retreat to his chambers and sit in silence for days at a
time**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

The end came from a world nobody had heard of. **Dimmamar had denounced Vandire as a traitor to
humanity, and one name was to be heard many times across the Segmentum Obscurus: Sebastian
Thor.**[^lex-age-of-apostasy] **No one knew where this man had come
from**[^lex-age-of-apostasy] — **many suspected him to be a daemonic entity, but he was just a man,
born in a Dimmamar Schola Progenium habitat**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

**Thor claimed also to have had visions from the Emperor and warned that humanity was falling. He
had an eloquence and charisma that none could match or understand and could reach into the hearts
and minds of the people.**[^lex-age-of-apostasy] Within three months **eighty planets had fallen to
the new Confederation of Light**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

The decisive event was a second warp storm, and it is impossible to read as coincidence. Vandire
sent **the most trusted of the Templar Frateris to raze Dimmamar to the ground** — and **soon after
the war fleet jumped into the warp from the base on Clax, it was destroyed by a gigantic warp
storm**. The last astropath reported **white arcs of light tearing the ships apart, literally, as
well as the soldiers on board**, and the Clax system has been cut off ever since, **by the Storm of
the Emperor's Wrath**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

**With the death of a vast fleet, the whole of Segmentum Obscurus burst into
rebellion.**[^lex-age-of-apostasy] Thor's own passage was equally unimpeded: **though the rest of
the galaxy was still covered by warp storms, Thor's ships had no hindrance as they moved from planet
to planet**, and **the Paternova of the Navigators dubbed him *Abstracta Preomnis*, Master of the
Warp**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

The codex records these as the sources give them and draws no conclusion. But note the symmetry:
Vandire justified his rule by a warp storm that killed his enemies, and was destroyed by a warp
storm that killed his fleet. Both sides read the Immaterium as verdict. [[I.T1.Ch4]] describes what
warp storms actually are, and the Imperium's habit of treating them as judgement is the same
epistemology in both mouths.

The state collapsed institutionally before it collapsed militarily. The Space Marines and Adeptus
Mechanicus, **kept distant by the problems with warp travel**, found **their worlds had become
bastions of the old faith and were protecting the people from the invasions of Vandire**; Chapter
Masters praised the Confederation of Light, and **the Adeptus Mechanicus called for the High Lords
to account for themselves and to execute Vandire as a traitor**. Vandire **dissolved the High
Lords** and ordered attacks on the revolting Space Marines and Mechanicus; **many of his officers
refused and were hanged or burnt as heretics** — **but by this time Vandire had been revealed as the
fraud he was**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

The Fabricator-General **Gastaph Hediatrix ordered his Martian Tech-Guard to Terra**, joined by the
**Imperial Fists, Fire Hawks, Soul Drinkers and Black Templars**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy] And the
siege that followed inverted the founding image: **images were set up relating to the Horus Heresy,
except this time the other way around — the heretics within the
palace**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy] The Brides, **numbering around 10,000 by now, defended with all
their might**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

**Eventually, however, it was to be from within that the greatest threat
came.**[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

The Adeptus Custodes had **maintained their vigil over the body of the Emperor** but **closed
themselves off for protection**, and **only when the Space Marines and Adeptus Mechanicus moved on
the Imperial Palace did the Custodes learn of the full extent of Vandire's
treachery**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

Sit with that. The Emperor's own bodyguard, in the same building, did not know what was happening
for seventy years, because they had sealed themselves in with him. [[IV.T8.Ch27]] holds the
Custodes; this is the cost of their singular focus.

**The defences were nothing to the Adeptus Custodes, as they knew every pathway and hidden passage
in the building**, and a small unit **led by Longinus** infiltrated to **the inner sanctum of
Vandire**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy] There the Centurion met a unit of Brides and **attempted to show
them that they were fighting for evil, not the good of the Imperium**. **The Brides were not to be
swayed** — so, **leaving his men as hostages**, he **led the leader of the Brides of the Emperor
away into the tunnels under the palace**.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

**No record exists of what happened there, but it is commonly believed he led her to the Golden
Throne of the Emperor himself, and upon seeing his blessed form came to realise the truth in the
matter. It is a matter of record that after whatever may or may not have occurred with the
Centurion, the leader of the Brides personally executed the traitor
Vandire.**[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

The Reign of Blood ended because a Custodian showed one woman the thing on the Throne. The sources
are careful — *no record exists*, *commonly believed* — and this codex keeps that care. What is
matter of record is only the killing.

The settlement afterwards created two institutions the Imperium still runs on.
**Sebastian Thor was tried as a traitor to the Imperium, found innocent and named
Ecclesiarch.**[^lex-age-of-apostasy] **The Ecclesiarchy was forbidden from retaining men at
arms.**[^lex-age-of-apostasy] And **the Brides of the Emperor were re-formed into the Adepta
Sororitas — the Sisters of Battle — becoming the military wing of the Ecclesiarchy without violating
the letter of the prohibition against "men under
arms."**[^lex-age-of-apostasy] The **Holy Synod of Terra** was created by
Thor,[^lex-age-of-apostasy] and the **Wars of Vindication** followed within the Officio
Assassinorum.[^lex-age-of-apostasy]

The Imperium responded to a church seizing the state by forbidding that church an army, and then
approving an army for it on a technicality about gender. That loophole is the founding charter of
the Adepta Sororitas of [[IV.T3.Ch11]], and it has held for five thousand years — which tells you
what Imperial law is for.
