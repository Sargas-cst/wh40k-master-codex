# Abaddon's Black Crusades (1st – 12th)

## What a Black Crusade Is {#definition}

A **Black Crusade** is **an incursion that is formed when the usually disparate forces of Chaos
unite under a particularly strong Chaos Champion and go forth en masse to wage war against the
Imperium of Man**.[^lex-black-crusade]

The definition turns on the word *disparate*, and the reason is [[III.T4.Ch18]]. After the Great
Scouring drove the traitors into the Eye of Terror, **the followers of Chaos began to fight among
each other for domination and territory, and their vast armies eventually
fractured**.[^lex-black-crusade]

So the Imperium's containment worked, and its success is what created the problem. Herding the
traitor Legions into one warp rift did not destroy them; it put them somewhere they would fight
each other, which kept them harmless — until someone could stop them fighting each other.

That is rare. **Once or twice within a millennium an exceptional Chaos Champion rises who is strong
enough to bring his fellow warlords and their hosts under his sway. When this happens ancient
rivalries are set aside and uneasy alliances are forged, clearing the way for what is called a
Black Crusade.**[^lex-black-crusade]

What assembles is everything the Eye contains: **the multitudinous hordes of the Lost and the
Damned, ancient and towering Chaos Titans, the bizarre war machines of the Dark Mechanicum and the
daemons of the warp** — led into the realm of man by **the vengeful warbands of the Chaos Space
Marines**.[^lex-black-crusade]

And the failure mode is built in. When a crusade launches, **whole worlds fall prey to the rampaging
hordes and the Dark Gods themselves divert their attention to the material realm, delighting in the
sacrifices made in their names** — but **the nature of Chaos is fickle, and unless it is repelled by
the defenders, the crusade is bound to splinter into separate hosts and warbands who pursue their
own ambitions**.[^lex-black-crusade]

Read that carefully, because it is the strangest strategic fact in the setting. A Black Crusade
dissolves *if it is not stopped*. Victory disperses it; only resistance holds it together. The
Imperium's defence of Cadia in [[II.T3.Ch8]] for ten thousand years was, on this logic, the thing
that kept giving Abaddon an army.

## The Twelve {#the-twelve}

**The most infamous Black Crusades are those led by Abaddon the Despoiler, Warmaster of the Black
Legion and successor to Horus.** He has led thirteen, **which ranged from small raids to battles
that spanned entire sectors**.[^lex-black-crusade]

The sources give them as a dated list, and the list read in order is more informative than any
single campaign. What follows is that list, with what Abaddon took from each.

| # | Date | Name | What he took |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 781.M31 | The First Battle of Cadia | The daemon sword **Drach'nyen** |
| 2 | 597.M32 | The Cursing of Corona | A curse sunk into the core of Belis Corona |
| 3 | 909.M32 | The Desecration of Gerstahl | Destroyed the remains of **Saint Gerstahl** |
| 4 | 001.M34 | The Death of the Kromarch | Destroyed the Grand Citadel of Kromarch |
| 5 | 723.M36 | The Scouring of Elysia | Summoned **Doombreed**; destroyed the Warhawks and Venerators Chapters |
| 6 | 901.M36 | Drecarth's Folly | Brought the Sons of the Eye to heel |
| 7 | 811.M37 | The Ghost War | The **gene-seed** of Blood Angels slaughtered at Mackan |
| 8 | 999.M37 | The Skullgather | Appeased Tzeentch with **sequences of death forming a mathematical equation** |
| 9 | 573.M38 | The Starving of Cancephalus | Got several fleets **out of the Eye undetected** |
| 10 | 001.M39 | The Conflict of Helica | Fought the Iron Hands at Medusa |
| 11 | 301.M39 | The Doom of Relorria | Abducted thousands of **Orks for experiments on their use of the Warp** |
| 12 | 139.M41 | The **Gothic War** | The last two **Blackstone Fortresses** |

[^lex-black-crusade]

Nothing on that list is territory. Not one of the twelve is recorded as having taken and held a
world. What Abaddon extracted was: a daemon weapon, a curse, a saint's relics, a fortress, a
summoned daemon prince, the submission of a rival warband, a Chapter's gene-seed, an appeasement
to Tzeentch, an undetected fleet movement, a grudge match, a sample of Orks, and two Blackstone
Fortresses.

Several of those are shopping. The seventh took Blood Angels gene-seed — the genetic material of a
Chapter drawn from [[II.T3.Ch7]]; the gene-seed itself is treated in [[IV.T4.Ch12]]. The eleventh
abducted Orks specifically to study **their use of the Warp**, the Waaagh field of
[[VI.T3.Ch10]]. The twelfth took the
Blackstone Fortresses, which [[VII.T3.Ch11]] holds and which are the same Necron
technology — noctilith, per [[I.T1.Ch4]] — as the pylons of [[II.T3.Ch8]].

The eighth deserves its own note because of what it says about the enemy. Abaddon **appeased the
Changer of Ways with sequences of death creating a mathematical equation of terrible and profane
perfection**.[^lex-black-crusade] A campaign fought so that the pattern of the casualties would
constitute a formula. Tzeentch is [[V.T1.Ch3]].

And the ninth is the one an Imperial strategist should have noticed. Its recorded achievement is
that **several Chaos fleets left the Eye of Terror undetected** during the Antecanis
Massacre.[^lex-black-crusade] The Imperium counted that as a massacre survived. It was a test of
whether the Cadian Gate could be slipped.

## The Crimson Path {#crimson-path}

The Imperium fought twelve Black Crusades as twelve raids. The sources record that they were one
operation.

**The Black Crusades of Abaddon have a secret and even more sinister purpose. Throughout the
millennia he has identified ancient monoliths holding back the tides of the warp. Using his Black
Crusades to sometimes conceal his true objective, Abaddon has targeted and destroyed many of these
structures as part of a plan known as the Crimson Path. The ultimate objective of Abaddon's Black
Crusades is to expand the Eye of Terror to envelop Terra
itself.**[^lex-black-crusade]

Every element of the Imperium's ten-thousand-year misreading is in that passage.

The monoliths are the pylons of [[II.T3.Ch8]] — Necron blackstone structures which
[[I.T1.Ch4]] establishes strengthen the barrier between realspace and the Warp, and which the
Adeptus Mechanicus could not scan and did not understand. Cadia's five thousand eight hundred and
ten intact pylons held the Cadian Gate open, and the Imperium had garrisoned the planet for
millennia without knowing that the planet *was* the mechanism.

Abaddon knew. He had been identifying and destroying these structures across eleven Crusades before
the twelfth took the Blackstone Fortresses, which are made of the same material. The raids that
looked like trophy-hunting were demolition, and the trophies were cover.

That reframes the whole of this Chapter's list. The Imperium repelled twelve Black Crusades and
recorded twelve victories. What it was actually experiencing was a single engineering programme
being carried out in front of it, over nine thousand years, by an enemy whose apparent
disorganisation was the disguise. [[III.T5.Ch22]] is what happens when the programme reaches its
objective, and [[III.T5.Ch23]] is the result.

One detail completes the picture and it belongs here rather than later. The thirteenth Crusade began
with Abaddon **gathering the blessings of the daemon primarchs** — Fulgrim, Perturabo, Angron,
Mortarion, Magnus and Lorgar — before launching on the Cadian
system.[^lex-black-crusade] Six of the Primarchs of [[III.T3.Ch12]], every one of them a figure
from [[III.T3.Ch13]]'s catalogue of feuds, endorsing the successor to the brother who recruited
them. The Long War of [[V.T3.Ch9]] has continuity of command that the Imperium's own institutions
cannot match: Abaddon has been running the same plan, with the same backers, since 781.M31.
