# Reorganization & the Codex Astartes

## The Great Scouring {#scouring}

The **Great Scouring** was the Imperium's counter-offensive against the rebel armies of Horus
following the defeat of the Heresy in c.M31.[^lex-great-scouring] Horus's campaign to strike at
Terra for a quick and decisive victory had failed; the rebels were broken and in retreat, and
Imperial forces now had the job of reacquiring all the territory they had
captured.[^lex-great-scouring]

But it was not only a reconquest. **The Great Scouring saw the first mass galaxy-wide purge
carried out by the Imperium as it sought to erase the taint of Horus's treachery from its
realm.**[^lex-great-scouring]

*The first.* The Imperium's characteristic instrument — the purge applied at galactic scale — is
dated here, to the years immediately after the Siege. Every later example in this codex, from the
Age of Apostasy in [[III.T4.Ch19]] to the Primarch's Scourge in [[II.T2.Ch4]], is a repetition of
a method invented in the seven years after Horus died.

The military result was incomplete, and the sources are precise about it. **It took seven years
for the Imperium to drive most of the Traitors into the Eye of Terror** — dated 015.M31 to
021.M31 — **but the bulk of the Night Lords, Emperor's Children, and Alpha Legion continued to
operate in Imperial space far beyond this date. Large-scale battles are known to have been waged
against Traitor holdouts for over a century.**[^lex-great-scouring]

So the traitors were not expelled; three Legions' worth of them simply stayed. The Eye of Terror
of [[II.T1.Ch3]] became the traitors' home because most of them were driven there, not all — and
the Long War of [[V.T3.Ch9]] has no clean start date on the Imperial side either.

## Who Was Left in Charge {#authority}

The political situation after the Siege is the reason for everything else in this Chapter.

**In the aftermath of the Horus Heresy, the Emperor had been incapacitated, and the Space Marines'
numbers had been decimated by defections to Chaos and battle losses. With the Emperor confined to
the Golden Throne the High Lords of Terra found themselves the new leaders of the Emperor's
domain.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

That is the outcome [[III.T3.Ch13]] identified as the Heresy's political cause. The Legions
rebelled in part because the Emperor intended Malcador's Terran Council to rule rather than the
Astartes. They lost, and the Council took over anyway — with the added authority of the Emperor's
silence. The High Lords of [[IV.T1.Ch1]] hold power today because of a war fought partly to stop
them holding it.

The reaction to victory was fear. **Both the High Lords and surviving Primarchs became paranoid of
the possibility of such events repeating themselves. The armies of the Emperor once again scoured
the galaxy seeking out all forms of heresy and removing even the slightest chance of
rebellion.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

And one Legion emerged dominant. **The Ultramarines were left as the largest loyal Legion, and
Guilliman assumed the title of Lord Commander of the
Imperium.**[^lex-codex-astartes] [[III.T3.Ch16]] records why they were largest and intact: they
were mired at Calth and never reached Terra. The Legion that missed the Siege inherited the
Imperium — and [[III.T3.Ch12]] notes that the Ultramarines had already grown each time one of the
two erased Legions was deleted.

## Breaking the Legions {#second-founding}

**Concerned that once again there could be a threat from such a terrible force, each Legion was to
be divided into smaller Chapters, and a new set of regulations would be created to govern
them.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

The document that did it was Guilliman's. **In order to give form to the newly established Adeptus
Astartes, Roboute Guilliman gathered his thoughts in the treatise known as the Codex
Astartes.**[^lex-codex-astartes] It was **largely a work of two parts**: the first laying out **the
organisational structure of the Chapters, their insignia, officers and role**, the second **a
collection of strategic and tactical theses intended to provide guidance to future
leaders**.[^lex-codex-astartes] It contained **everything a member of the Astartes would need to
know** — recruitment, organisation, tactics and beyond — and **became a sacred text to the majority
of the Chapters**.[^lex-codex-astartes]

The central provision is a number. **Where the Legions could number more than 10,000 strong, the
Chapters would be limited to about a thousand.** Each original Legion would be **divided as many
times as needed into successor Chapters**, a division called the **Second Founding**, **seven years
after the Horus Heresy, in 021.M31** — the remaining thousand bodies of each original Legion
surviving as a new Chapter of the same name.[^lex-codex-astartes]

A tenfold reduction in the largest unit of force any commander could hold. That is not a military
reform; it is a constitutional one, and its purpose is stated plainly — no single Astartes commander
should ever again control an army capable of threatening Terra. [[IV.T4.Ch13]] holds the structure
it produced, and [[IV.T4.Ch15]] the Foundings that followed.

The second decree is subtler and reaches into the gene-seed itself. **Space Marine recruitment and
training would advance slower than during the Heresy, and a Chapter's gene-seed stocks would be
monitored more closely for purity.** The reason: **previously the Legions had used accelerated
gene-seed cultivation techniques to quickly expand their numbers. However, this process created
genetic flaws in the Legionnaires that were exploited by Chaos. By slowing down the process,
Chapters would be able to more easily recognise and excise
impurities.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

Read that as a post-mortem. The Imperium's own conclusion about why half its Astartes turned
traitor is that they were mass-produced too fast and the resulting defects were exploitable. The
Space Marines of [[IV.T4.Ch12]] are slow to make because the fast ones broke — which is the same
lesson [[III.T3.Ch11]] records about the Thunder Warriors, learned a second time at
catastrophically greater cost.

And the newly divided Chapters diverged. **While these newly created Chapters claimed their
founders' origins, such as their Primarch, they would also come to develop their own culture and
customs.**[^lex-codex-astartes] That divergence is why [[II.T3.Ch7]] can describe three Chapters
with three incompatible relationships to their own populations.

## The Near Second Civil War {#dorn}

The reform was not accepted quietly, and how close it came to another war is not widely told.

**Rogal Dorn initially rejected the Codex Astartes and enmity developed between him and Guilliman.
Dorn called Guilliman a coward, citing his lack of participation in the defence of the Imperial
Palace. Guilliman accused Dorn of being a rebel for refusing the
Codex.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

Both charges land. Guilliman was absent from the Siege, as [[III.T3.Ch16]] records. And Dorn was
refusing a lawful order from the Lord Commander of the Imperium.

**This enmity quickly involved other Space Marine Legions and a rift developed**: Leman Russ and
the Space Wolves stood by the Imperial Fists, while Jaghatai Khan's White Scars and Corax's Raven
Guard supported the Ultramarines. **A second civil war appeared likely when the Imperial Fists
strike cruiser *Terrible Angel* was fired upon by the Imperial Navy in connection with the Codex
crisis.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

Four Legions taking sides and the Navy shooting at one of them, within a decade of the Siege of
Terra. The Imperium came within an incident of repeating the Heresy over a book about
organisational structure.

How it ended is worth quoting for what it says about the new order. **Dorn ultimately relented
after spending seven days meditating in the pain glove. There, he concluded that the Legion could
no longer serve the Emperor who had been and must serve the Emperor who was, which involved
accepting the new order of which the Codex was a part.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

*The Emperor who had been* and *the Emperor who was*. That is the theological distinction on which
the whole Imperium now rests, formulated by Rogal Dorn in a torture device, seven years after the
Siege. The living commander of [[III.T3.Ch13]] was gone; what remained was the thing on the Throne
in [[III.T3.Ch17]], and loyalty had to be re-pointed at it. The Imperial Cult of [[IV.T3.Ch8]] is
that re-pointing, carried to its conclusion.

## A Book That No Longer Exists {#no-original}

The final fact about the Codex Astartes undermines its own authority, and the sources state it
without apparent discomfort.

**There is no such thing as a single version of the Codex Astartes, and the original text no
longer exists in any form.**[^lex-codex-astartes] The earliest known copy is the **Apocrypha of
Skaros**[^lex-codex-astartes] — which **may or may not be the
first**.[^lex-codex-astartes]

**Over the millennia, the work has been added to by successive hands so extensively that various
competing versions have emerged. In some cases these are so divergent that Chapters have gone to
war over differing interpretations of the Primarch's
will.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

Chapters killing each other over readings of a text whose original is lost. That is the Imperium's
relationship to every inherited document, and [[III.T2.Ch7]] describes the same pattern in
technology: copies of copies, venerated, with the source unavailable for consultation.

The sources' own assessment is admirably balanced and worth reproducing in full. **Although the
doctrines presented in all are invaluable, and collectively the existing versions are perhaps the
single greatest work on the art of war in the Imperium, attribution of each individual part of the
text is nigh impossible — even with the help of its putative
author.**[^lex-codex-astartes]

That last clause is the sharpest thing in the article. Guilliman has returned, as
[[III.T5.Ch24]] records. He is available to ask. And even with the author present, nobody can
establish which parts of the Codex Astartes he actually wrote.
