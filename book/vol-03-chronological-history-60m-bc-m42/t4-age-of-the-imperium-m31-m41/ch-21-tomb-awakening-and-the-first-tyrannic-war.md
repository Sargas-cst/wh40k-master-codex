# Tomb Awakening & the First Tyrannic War

## The Tombs Open {#awakening}

[[III.T1.Ch5]] recorded the order: sleep for sixty million years, then reawaken. The waking did not
go to plan, and how it failed shapes the present.

**When the Tomb Worlds did begin to reawaken, it was not simultaneously. Some awoke to see the
Great Crusade, others during the Age of Apostasy. To avoid chaos, these prematurely awoken Necrons
were organised under the Awakened Council.** Most, however, **awoke during the later years of
M41 — but even still, billions of Necrons lay dormant**.[^lex-necron]

Early risers during the Great Crusade and the Age of Apostasy, and a committee formed to manage
them. That is [[III.T1.Ch5]]'s attrition arriving on schedule: sixty million years is long enough
for the timers to drift, and the sources already record Tomb Worlds lost to malfunction and Eldar
raids.

What the Imperium does not know is stated plainly, and it is the most alarming assessment in this
Title. **What the Imperium cannot know is that, should the Necrons ever fully wake and unite, they
would face a foe as numerous as themselves. For now, the Imperium has had but a taste of the
Necrons' might, and it is fortunate for Mankind that the Necrons remain divided by madness and
conflicting agendas.**[^lex-necron]

*As numerous as themselves.* Every Necron incursion the Imperium has repelled — [[VI.T1.Ch1]]'s
subject — has been fought against a fraction of a force that could match the human species in
numbers, and the only thing preventing that is that the sleepers woke up mad and disagreeing.

And the division is being repaired. **These are but the first stumbling steps of a giant as it
gathers pace, and even now powerful leaders like Anrakyr the Traveller, Imotekh the Stormlord and
the Silent King are uniting their people under a common cause in order to re-establish the Infinite
Empire.**[^lex-necron]

## Why the Silent King Came Back {#szarekh-returns}

The most important date in this Chapter is not 745.M41. It is the year before.

**In 744.M41, the Silent King ended his self-imposed exile and returned to the galaxy after an
encounter with the Tyranids within the intergalactic void.**[^lex-necron]

Szarekh had left in [[III.T1.Ch5]] to find whatever measure of solace or penance he could, having
destroyed the command protocols that bound his people to him. He came back because he met something
outside the galaxy — and **he discovered the Tyranids were just one of many threats facing the
Necrons**: other alien races had swarmed over their Tomb Worlds, and **the Warp now seeped across
the Galaxy**.[^lex-necron]

The Imperium's first contact with the Tyranids is 745.M41, at Tyran. The Necrons' first contact was
earlier, in the void between galaxies, and it was sufficient to end a sixty-million-year exile. The
being who judged in [[III.T1.Ch5]] that the time of all flesh would pass came back because
something was coming that he could not outwait.

His method since is worth recording, because it explains why the Imperium has never identified him.
**He has begun a journey across the galaxy with a band of his loyal Triarch Praetorians to reawaken
Tomb Worlds that still slumber so they may unite against the Tyranids.** And **the returned Silent
King is careful not to reveal his true identity, even to fellow Necrons** — working **primarily
through Triarch Praetorians or unwitting Crypteks and Necron Overlords**, and having **steadily
influenced the galaxy from one side to the other**.[^lex-necron]

The last Silent King is moving through the galaxy anonymously, waking his own species, and
concealing himself from it. He destroyed the protocols that let him command; what he has instead is
influence exercised through people who do not know who he is.

## The Warnings Nobody Read {#warnings}

**Hive Fleet Behemoth was the first Tyranid Hive Fleet encountered by the Imperium, and also the
first contact the Imperium had with the Tyranids as a race.**[^lex-behemoth] It **made a juggernaut
rush into the galaxy from the Eastern Fringe and proceeded into Ultramar, resulting in what became
known as the First Tyrannic War**.[^lex-behemoth]

But the Imperium had been told. **Early warning signs came from an Explorator census of the
planetary systems on the Eastern Fringe surrounding Tyran, undertaken in the preceding decade
before Behemoth's invasion. Planets known to have been full of life were discovered reduced to
lifeless, barren rock without even any living bacteria. These mysterious new Dead Worlds were
reported to the Explorator General — but nothing came of it. The outpost on Tyran also filed its own
reports of discovered dead worlds, but no help was sent.**[^lex-behemoth]

A decade of advance notice, filed twice, and acted on never. Set that against [[II.T1.Ch1]]: the
Imperium's astrography is a logistics scheme that acquired the appearance of geography, and reports
from the far edge of Ultima Segmentum travel through it into an archive. Compare
[[III.T4.Ch20]] — the ninth Black Crusade's real achievement was moving fleets undetected, and the
Imperium recorded a massacre. Twice in this Title the Imperium receives the decisive intelligence and
does nothing with it.

The first attack is dated. **The first documented attack was upon the outpost of Tyran in 745.M41.
Hive Fleet Behemoth overran the isolated Adeptus Mechanicus Explorator Base with overwhelming
force, and stripped the world of all life.**[^lex-behemoth]

What saved the Imperium from total surprise was one man and a buried archive. **A data-codex
recording the invasion was stored deep below the base's command bunker and was recovered almost a
year later by Inquisitor Kryptman, a far-sighted Inquisitor who had been dispatched to investigate
the reports of unexplained dead worlds. Analysing the codex, Kryptman realised the grave threat that
was entering the galaxy and became determined to warn the
Imperium.**[^lex-behemoth]

And even then the warning nearly failed, for infrastructural reasons that will be familiar from
[[II.T1.Ch2]]. Kryptman **attempted to send a message via the Telepathica Matrix** orbiting Thandros
I, **but he couldn't get through**; in desperation he went to Thandros **and found it destroyed**,
salvaging the Matrix so it could project the data-codex with his own report. The Ordo Xenos replied
**with orders to travel to Macragge and assist in mounting a
defence**.[^lex-behemoth]

The species was warned by a man who physically retrieved a broken transmitter from a devoured system
because the Imperium's communications did not work. Kryptman is [[VIII.T2.Ch7]]; the Ordo Xenos,
[[IV.T7.Ch24]].

The naming is a small revealing detail. **Being the first time the Imperium had encountered a new
alien race, they were named after the planet Tyran**[^lex-behemoth] — and the Ordo Xenos, **as
dictated by Imperial tradition, named the invading alien fleet after an ancient and forbidding
legend: *Behemoth***.[^lex-behemoth]

The Imperium's procedure on meeting an extragalactic species that eats biospheres was to name it
after the place it ate and to reach for scripture for the fleet. The Tyranids of [[VI.T4.Ch13]] are
called that because of an outpost nobody had heard of.

!!! disputed "Recorded conflict — macragge-battle-date"

    Behemoth was destroyed at the Battle for Macragge in **745.M41**, or in
    **746.M41** — **depending on the source**.[^lex-behemoth]

    The article flags the discrepancy itself. It matters more than a single year usually would,
    because the same article dates the first attack on Tyran to 745.M41 and has Kryptman recovering
    the data-codex **almost a year later**. On the earlier date, the entire war — first contact,
    Kryptman's investigation, the astropathic failure, the voyage to Macragge, and the battle — has
    to fit inside one year, which the narrative cannot accommodate.

## Behemoth {#behemoth}

What Behemoth was, tactically, is best understood by what later Hive Fleets were not.

It **attacked from the galactic south-east of the Eastern Fringe without warning and ploughed wildly
into the galaxy, ruining hundreds of systems and devouring everything in its path**. And **compared
to later Hive Fleets which adopted different strategies, Behemoth showed no subtlety in its berserker
advance**.[^lex-behemoth]

That comparison is the Hive Mind's learning curve made visible, and it is [[VI.T4.Ch13]]'s subject.
The first fleet the galaxy met was the crude one. Every fleet since has been better.

Behemoth's advance into Ultramar is the material [[II.T2.Ch6]] records from the defenders' side.
**Prandium, known as "the jewel in Ultramar's crown", was an easy conquest.** The Ultramarines under
Marneus Calgar mounted a defence in support of the local Prandium Defence Auxilia — **however, the
luscious agri-world was stripped of all life**, the Tyranids **so thorough that even Prandium's
atmosphere was consumed for whatever biomass it
contained**.[^lex-behemoth]

An entire atmosphere eaten for its trace organics. That is the distinction between the Tyranids and
every other enemy in this codex: Chaos wants souls, the Orks want a fight, the Necrons want the
galaxy emptied. Behemoth wanted the mass.

Calgar's response to losing Prandium is recorded, and it is unusually candid for an Imperial
commander. **For a week Marneus Calgar went without food or water while meditating on the matter.
When he came out of his trance-like state, he told the Ultramarines that they had been guilty of the
sin of pride and that they would have to adapt and develop new strategies and tactics, even
weapons.** Hit-and-run raids were abandoned.[^lex-behemoth]

A Chapter Master of the Legion that wrote the Codex Astartes concluding that the Codex's tactics did
not work and that the fault was pride. Set that against [[III.T4.Ch18]]: Chapters have gone to war
over interpretations of that text, and here its authors' own successors set it aside because
something arrived that it had no answer for.

How the war ended is the fact that has misled the Imperium ever since. **Eventually its onslaught
used more biomass than it could replace, and it all but consumed itself in the process of breaking
the Imperial defence at the Battle for Macragge.**[^lex-behemoth]

Behemoth was not defeated so much as spent. [[II.T2.Ch6]] records the cost on the other side: the
Ultramarines' First Company annihilated to the last man holding the polar fortresses, and Prandium
destroyed permanently. The Imperium recorded a victory at Macragge, and what actually happened was
that an unsubtle first attempt ran out of fuel against the best-defended human realm in the galaxy,
having eaten hundreds of systems to get there.

And the timing closes the Title. Behemoth arrived one year after the Silent King came back from the
void because he had met the Tyranids and concluded the Necrons needed to unite against them. Two
powers older than humanity had already assessed this threat and changed sixty-million-year plans
over it. The Imperium filed the reports.
