# Bio-Engineered Races

## Life as a Weapon {#young-races}

The Old Ones had believed that all life was useful. Losing a war turned that belief into a
military programme, and the galaxy the Imperium inhabits is the result.

In the millennia after the C'tan reversed the war, **desperate for some retribution as the
star gods toyed with their galaxy, the Old Ones genetically engineered life into new
forms with strong links to the Warp** — psychic **Young Races** such as the Eldar, the
K'nib and the Rashan.[^lex-war-in-heaven-necron]

The design criterion is the whole point, and it is stated outright: strong links to the
Warp. The Old Ones had already been seeding species for their own sake — they are known to
have brought about the rise of numerous new species and impregnated thousands of worlds
they made their own.[^lex-old-ones] What changed was purpose. Having lost to a power made
of living metal, they built soldiers out of the one thing that power could not touch.

The full roster of their creations, as the sources record it, is: **Aeldari, Jokaero,
K'nib, Krork, Mankind, Rashan** and **Hrud**.[^lex-old-ones] Every one of them still
matters sixty million years later, which is a remarkable return on a weapons programme run
by a civilisation that was about to be exterminated.

It worked, briefly and enormously. These new races fought the Necrons in every corner of
the galaxy in **the bloodiest episode of the war, unleashing the power of the Warp**. This
overwhelmed the C'tan, putting them and their immortal Necrons on the back foot, and
**victory by the Old Ones and their Young Races seemed all but
assured**.[^lex-war-in-heaven-necron]

Hold that sentence, because [[III.T1.Ch4]] is entirely about what it cost. The Old Ones
did not lose the war for want of a working weapon. They found one, deployed it, and were
winning with it. The Warp is not a resource that can be used at that scale without
consequence, and the consequence is the next Chapter.

## The Second Generation {#second-generation}

When the Warp turned on them, the Old Ones went back to the laboratory, and what they
built the second time was different in kind.

During the closing days of the war, **the then-desperate Old Ones brought forth newer
creations to defend the last of their strongholds** — the green-skinned **Krork** and the
technology-mimicking **Jokaero**.[^lex-jokaero] The reason was specific: denizens of a
much more turbulent Warp had clustered around those strongholds, riled by the violence of
the war.[^lex-krork]

Notice the change in specification. The first generation was psychic, built to fight
machines. The second was built to fight things that come *out of* the Warp — so the Old
Ones this time engineered brute resilience and technical facility instead of psychic
reach. It is a species-scale admission that the first design had backfired.

The **Krork** carry one property that explains the Orks of the 41st Millennium better than
almost anything else in the setting. They were **the only one of the Young Races to escape
the Nightbringer's fear of death**.[^lex-krork]

That is a deliberate exemption from the single most consequential act of psychic
engineering in this history. The Nightbringer — the C'tan of [[I.T4.Ch14]] — imprinted a
terror of mortality so deeply into the young races that it persists in their descendants as
instinct. The Krork alone escaped it. Everything characteristic of their descendants
follows: a species that does not fear death fights differently, and cannot be deterred by
threatening it. The Orks are [[VI.T3.Ch10]].

Their scale is recorded in fragments, and the fragments agree. The Krork are remembered by
the Eldar Harlequins as **fearsome foes at the apex of their might**,[^lex-krork] and an
unnamed Necron Lord from Naogeddon remarked on their spread through the galaxy as being
**as plentiful as the Humans**.[^lex-krork] Trazyn keeps a Krork specimen in his
collection, **with mountainous shoulders**[^lex-krork] — and this may be the same creature
as the **twelve-metre-tall Orkish monstrosity** Fabius Bile saw, with an exoskeleton and
complex internal organs serving as armour far in advance of Imperial battleplate, which
Bile speculated may have been a Krork.[^lex-krork]

Twelve metres, armoured internally beyond anything the Imperium can manufacture. The
sources keep that identification speculative — Bile *speculated* — and so does this codex.
What is not speculative is the pattern: every surviving witness, Aeldari, Necron and human,
describes the Krork as considerably more formidable than what walks the galaxy now.

The **Jokaero** are the stranger success. *Pongo ingenio*: an alien race, ape-like in
appearance, **capable of tremendous technological and logical feats, specialising in
miniaturisation technology**.[^lex-jokaero] Regardless of their creators' efforts, the Old
Ones were doomed once their intergalactic network was breached by the psychic spawn of
their own creations — yet **despite the destruction of their creators, the Jokaero
continued to live on** into the Age of the Imperium, where there was much speculation about
their origins.[^lex-jokaero]

And one observation the sources make that is worth reproducing precisely because they raise
it themselves: **the physical resemblance of the Jokaero to the orangutans of ancient Terra
may not be coincidental**.[^lex-jokaero]

Two of the Old Ones' war-engineered species look like each other's cousins, and one of them
is us. The article states the resemblance and declines to draw the conclusion; so does this
codex. But it sets up the next Section, which is the claim the Imperium would least like to
hear.

## Humanity on the List {#mankind}

**Mankind appears on the list of species the Old Ones bio-engineered.**[^lex-old-ones]

That is given in the sources without hedging, as an item in the roster of the Old Ones'
creations, and it deserves setting out plainly rather than burying, because of what the
rest of this codex is about.

The Imperium of [[IV.T1.Ch1]] rests on the proposition that humanity is the galaxy's
rightful inheritor and that other species are abominations to be purged. Read this material
straightforwardly and humanity is a xenos artefact — engineered by reptiles, sixty million
years ago, as materiel in somebody else's war, alongside the Orks and the Aeldari and the
ape-like Jokaero it now hunts.

The Old Ones' fate makes the position more awkward still. In the power vacuum following the
war, **the lesser creations of the Old Ones such as Mankind were known to have continued to
develop, but in unforeseen ways**.[^lex-old-ones]

"Lesser creations" and "unforeseen ways" are the operative phrases. Humanity is recorded
here as a minor product of the programme that did not turn out as designed — a description
no Imperial source would accept, and which this codex reports because the material says it.
What the Imperium says about its own origins is treated in [[III.T2.Ch6]] and
[[III.T3.Ch11]], and the two accounts do not meet.

The mantle of galactic dominion did not pass to humanity in any case. It went to the Eldar,
who had fought alongside the Old Ones during the war; that species survived where the Old
Ones did not, and would see the Necrons depart into the Great Sleep.[^lex-old-ones]
Humanity's turn came far later, and is [[III.T3.Ch13]]'s.

## The Slanni Problem {#slanni}

There is one more inheritance from the Old Ones, and it is the one the sources cannot
resolve.

The **Slanni** — also written **Slann** — are a sentient reptilian or amphibian species
described as those who **are, or were, or have inherited the legacy of** the Old
Slann.[^lex-slanni] Before the Imperium, and even before the Aeldari, the Slanni ruled a
mighty galactic domain.[^lex-slanni] They **claim to be, and may actually be, the oldest of
all the races in the galaxy**, and in the heyday of their old empire they discovered and
nurtured many primitive species, encouraging their evolution while eradicating or
relocating more dangerous ones, and seeding many worlds with livestock.[^lex-slanni]

That description is the Old Ones' programme, in the Old Ones' own terms. Which is exactly
the difficulty.

!!! disputed "Recorded conflict — slanni-old-ones-identity"

    The Slanni **are** the Old Ones, **were** the Old Ones, or have merely **inherited
    their legacy**.[^lex-slanni]

    The article gives all three readings in a single clause, each separately cited, and
    does not choose between them. "Old Slann" is used elsewhere as a plain synonym for the
    Old Ones, while the Slanni are simultaneously described as a species still extant —
    which the Old Ones are not, having been hunted to destruction.

    The codex records the ambiguity rather than resolving it. Where the Old Ones are meant
    it says Old Ones, and it does not silently treat the Slanni as survivors of a species
    the sources elsewhere call extinct.

What the Slanni are credited with teaching is more useful than what they are. When the
Eldar first encountered the Old Slann — **the greatest of all spacefaring peoples** — they
learned many secrets of the universe from them: **the principles of warp-tunnel
engineering, the nature of the Warp, and the practice of calling the sentient creatures of
the Warp the Powers of Chaos**.[^lex-slanni]

Three transmissions, and each shapes the setting permanently. Warp-tunnel engineering is
the Webway of [[I.T3.Ch10]], which the Aeldari still live inside. The nature of the Warp is
the body of knowledge the Imperium spent ten thousand years failing to recover. And the
naming of the Powers of Chaos means the galaxy's vocabulary for its own gods was inherited
rather than discovered — [[V.T1.Ch1]] uses terms handed down from a civilisation that had
already lost a war to them.

The teaching was not one-sided in tone. While the Old Slann taught the Eldar the dangers of
the Warp, **they also taught them its many positive aspects**: how the mind of a living
creature passes at death into the Warp, where it may — if that mind has achieved
power — remain whole and immortal as a spirit. It was the Slann belief that **the object of
life was to perfect the mind**, and thereby achieve conscious immortality.[^lex-slanni]

Set that against everything else in this Title. The Old Ones held that a sufficiently
perfected mind survives death intact within the Warp. The Necrontyr, offered immortality by
the C'tan, took the opposite route: they abandoned their souls to acquire imperishable
bodies, as [[III.T1.Ch2]] records. Two civilisations, both terrified of death, both offered
a way out — one through the soul, one through the machine. The one that chose the soul is
extinct. The one that chose the machine is still here, and wishes it had chosen otherwise.
