# Warp Corruption & the Enslavers

## What the War Did to the Warp {#corruption}

The Old Ones were winning. They lost anyway, and not to the Necrons — they lost to the
weapon they had built, working exactly as designed at a scale nobody had modelled.

The mechanism is recorded plainly. In this era the Old Ones had engineered a number of
species with a strong connection to the Warp to serve as warrior races of psykers against
their enemy, and **the growing pains of these Young Races disturbed the peaceful Empyrean,
turning it into a hellish environment filled with now-deadly Warp
predators**.[^lex-enslaver]

The chain from [[III.T1.Ch3]] is now complete and it is entirely causal. The Old Ones
needed soldiers who could reach the Warp. Soldiers who reach the Warp leave marks in it.
Millions of years of war fought by psychic species, dying in psychic agony in astronomical
numbers, did to the Immaterium what any sufficiently large disturbance does to any medium:
it changed its weather permanently.

The other account of the same moment approaches it from the souls rather than the species.
**The maelstrom of souls of the Young Races, unleashed into the Warp still experiencing the
carnage of war, attracted the rending claws of predatory Warp entities** — the most
disastrous of which were the Enslavers.[^lex-war-in-heaven-necron]

Both accounts agree on the shape: the war's psychic byproduct was not waste but bait.
[[I.T1.Ch3]] describes the Warp as fuelled by the emotions and souls of sentient beings.
This is that principle applied to a galactic war, and the result is that the combatants fed
the thing that ate them.

!!! disputed "Recorded conflict — empyrean-before-the-young-races"

    The Empyrean was **peaceful** before the Young Races disturbed it,[^lex-enslaver] — or
    it already held what the Old Slann taught the Eldar to call **the sentient creatures of
    the warp**, and warned them of **the dangers of the warp** in the same
    breath.[^lex-slanni]

    Both cannot be simply true. The Old Ones' own spiritual doctrine — that a perfected
    mind survives death in the Warp *if it can resist* the Powers of Chaos — presupposes
    predatory Warp intelligences already present, and it is described as something they
    taught rather than something they discovered late.

    The codex records both. What can be said without choosing is narrower and still
    substantial: whatever was in the Warp beforehand, the war made it very much worse, and
    the Enslavers specifically are dated to the war rather than before it.

## The Enslavers {#enslavers}

The **Enslavers** — *spiritus subjugator*, also known as Psyrens, Krell, dominators or
puppeteers — are **biological entities that are native to the Warp**.[^lex-enslaver] They broke the
boundaries between dimensions, and among all the Warp-spawned creatures the war produced
they were the most proficient.[^lex-enslaver]

Physically they are described with an oddly clinical precision for something that ended a
galactic civilisation. They appear as barrel-shaped or basic spheroids roughly **two metres
in height**, with tough leathery skin they can change the tone of at will, typically
leathery brown. A **single large sensory organ** — red, pink or orange, sometimes called an
eye — sits at the very top of the body, and its actual function is
unknown.[^lex-enslaver] Around the upper body is a cluster of eight to twelve pale
tentacles, each about a metre and a half long, two of them longer and ending in suckered
pads; these serve as the primary sensory and manipulative organs.[^lex-enslaver] They have
no legs. They move by floating, up to three metres high, using the tentacles when they wish
to move precisely or fast — able to change direction or speed comparably to a human, but
unable actually to fly.[^lex-enslaver]

They possess physical bodies, but appear to exist in Warp space for at least part of their
life cycle; their bodies can also exist naturally in the material realm, and their
corporeal forms **can be felled by weapons fire**.[^lex-enslaver]

That last clause is worth noting, because it is unusual. Most Warp-native threats in this
setting require specialised means. An Enslaver can be shot. The reason they were
catastrophic anyway is in the next Section.

Two negative findings about them are more unsettling than the physical description.
**Mankind has been unable to determine whether the Enslavers are intelligent in human
terms** — they act rationally and make reasonable decisions, but they have never
communicated with another race, and whether that is inability or refusal is
unknown.[^lex-enslaver] And they have never been observed to use tools or equipment of any
kind. An Enslaver is theoretically capable of using a weapon and has never been seen to do
so; **manual labour is conducted by their psychically dominated
slaves**.[^lex-enslaver]

A species with no technology, no recorded language, and no demonstrated interest in either,
which nonetheless ended the reign of the galaxy's first civilisation. It did not need tools
because it uses people.

How they travel explains why nowhere is safe from them. They can transcend normal space,
which lets them traverse the galaxy and perhaps beyond, moving on the currents of the
Immaterium **by using the psychic vibrations of other creatures**, which draw them
close.[^lex-enslaver] The strongest and most easily traced emanations come from unprotected
human psykers, and such vibrations **can be detected as far away as ten light
years**.[^lex-enslaver]

An unprotected psyker is a lighthouse visible across ten light years to something that
travels by following the light. This is the most compelling justification the setting ever
offers for the Imperium's psyker policy — the culling, the sanctioning, the Black Ships of
[[I.T2.Ch9]] — and it is worth registering that the justification is real even where the
policy is monstrous.

The sources supply the case in a single quotation, from an Ordo Xenos Inquisitor, and it is
the best short statement of the threat in the material. One unsanctioned witch caused the
destruction of **Hive Skorpios** when one of those things used her brain as a gateway.
Within three days the entire hive's population was reduced to drooling mindslaves; within
three weeks an entire continent was at war. **And all because the governor thought his
family should be exempt from the psyker cull and refused to give his daughter to the Black
Ships.**[^lex-enslaver]

## A Body Made Into a Door {#gateway}

The mechanism of domination is bounded and almost modest. Through mental control an
Enslaver can take over the minds of other races, **a single Enslaver able to dominate up to
ten creatures**, whose victims retain all their abilities, knowledge and physical
attributes — all now under the Enslaver's command.[^lex-enslaver] Victims become helpless
and complete puppets, performing even the most self-destructive actions without
hesitation.[^lex-enslaver] The command range is **limited to about fifty
metres**.[^lex-enslaver]

Ten minds at fifty metres is a tactical nuisance, not an apocalypse. The apocalypse is what
the Enslaver does to a psyker, and it is the most grotesque process in this Volume.

The bond with the host exists to **create a Warp Gate** through which more Enslavers may
enter reality.[^lex-enslaver] The victim may be unaware at first, but in time gradual
changes are forced on their body chemistry. They become lethargic and tired, their skin
discoloured, and then over a period of **fifty to seventy-five hours** their form swells as
body tissue disintegrates and reforms into something new: **an arch of ruptured flesh**
that serves as the conduit for a Warp Gate linking Materium and Immaterium, through which a
trio of Enslavers travels unrestricted. With its body serving that new purpose, the victim
perishes — and the Enslavers it ushered through move quickly to enslave more psykers as an
infestation.[^lex-enslaver]

That is why an enemy that can be shot is nonetheless an extinction event. Each Enslaver
does not need to win a fight; it needs to reach one psyker and convert them into a
permanent door, three more come through, and each of those goes looking for another
psyker. The growth is not military but epidemiological, and the sources call it exactly
that: a virus-like action, breaching the Immaterium in epidemic numbers.[^lex-enslaver]

Sanctioning helps, and only for a while. Enslavers cannot possess sanctioned psykers as
easily, because of the preparation those minds have undergone, and can be overwhelmed if
enough minds are present — but **the protection is temporary, and with enough pressure a
sanctioned mind will break**. Once an infestation has begun, even protected psykers such as
Astropaths become victims once the Enslavers reach reality, turned into further
gateways.[^lex-enslaver]

And the recorded endpoint is the Imperium's most extreme measure, presented as routine.
With enough Enslavers, or one of extraordinary power, an entire hive of humans can be
controlled and the infestation spread across the planet — **after which the only acceptable
plan of action is Exterminatus**.[^lex-enslaver]

## The Fall of the Old Ones {#fall}

Against that, the Old Ones did what [[III.T1.Ch3]] describes: they engineered the Krork and
the Jokaero to defend their last strongholds. It was not enough, and the reason is
infrastructural rather than martial.

As Warp-spawned plagues overtook their **intergalactic networks, places of power, and great
works**, the Old Ones were vulnerable.[^lex-war-in-heaven-necron] The arrival of the
Enslavers brought the final downfall of their reign **as their own creations fell victim to
the Enslavers' command**.[^lex-enslaver]

There is the whole tragedy in one clause. The Old Ones' advantage had always been the
Webway — the network that let them appear anywhere, which the Necrons had breached in
[[III.T1.Ch2]] and which now carried a plague. And the army they had built to save
themselves was psychic, which is to say it was the Enslavers' preferred raw material. Every
asset they had became a liability in the same event.

The C'tan reacted to the Enslavers as an existential threat too, and their response was
characteristic: they **concocted a plan to cede the galaxy to the Enslaver threat, sealing
themselves away for millennia while their Necrons protected
them**.[^lex-war-in-heaven-necron] The Necrons, as their masters plotted, did not relent
but advanced while their foes faltered, and the Old Ones — scattered and defeated — were
hunted and destroyed by the C'tan and the Necrons.[^lex-war-in-heaven-necron]

So the Old Ones were finished off by the Necrons, but they were *beaten* by the Enslavers.
The distinction matters for reading the rest of this Volume: the Necrons did not defeat the
galaxy's first civilisation in open war. They inherited a collapse.

What the Enslavers did next was take everything. **Breaching the Immaterium in epidemic
numbers, the Enslavers would take what was left of the galaxy** while the C'tan were
shattered by their own Necron slaves.[^lex-enslaver] And then the strangest turn in the
whole history: **the galaxy became a wasteland until the psyker swarm died off, and allowed
the galaxy to emerge with new life**.[^lex-enslaver]

The Enslavers won, and then starved. Their entire method depends on psykers to travel
towards and bodies to convert; having consumed the psychic species of the galaxy, the
plague ran out of hosts and collapsed. Nobody defeated them. The galaxy was simply emptied
enough that they could not sustain themselves — which is the only recorded instance in this
setting of a threat ending because it succeeded too thoroughly.

That emptied galaxy, slowly refilling, is the stage for everything from [[III.T2.Ch6]]
onward. The Aeldari inherited it, having survived by means the sources do not
explain,[^lex-war-in-heaven-necron] and the Necrons went to sleep beneath it, which is
[[III.T1.Ch5]].

## Still Here {#still-here}

The Enslavers were never destroyed, and this is the point on which the Chapter should end,
because it changes the Enslavers from history into a standing condition.

By the Age of the Imperium **the origins of these creatures are largely unknown to Mankind
and remain a complete mystery to them**.[^lex-enslaver] Descriptions are rare and
fragmentary.[^lex-enslaver] The Imperium fights, occasionally, a species that ended the
galaxy's first civilisation, and does not know what it is.

Major infestations have occurred over the millennia. The Storm Wardens combated a notably
large one spanning several worlds in late M36, in the event called **the Nemesis
Incident**.[^lex-enslaver] Numerous Enslavers were encountered by Inquisition forces in the
Scintilla System in 260.993.M41 and destroyed — and the evidence revealed that the
creatures were **embedded within a rogue-psyker smuggling ring** headed by criminal
organisations, most notably the Faxlignae.[^lex-enslaver] Both the Ordo Malleus and the
Ordo Xenos claim an interest in them, for their Warp origins and their existence as an
alien species respectively.[^lex-enslaver]

A smuggling ring that trafficks unsanctioned psykers is, from an Enslaver's perspective, a
delivery service. That is the Hive Skorpios logic operating as an industry: every psyker
hidden from the Black Ships is a potential door, and someone is moving them around for
profit. The Ordos are [[IV.T7.Ch24]] and [[IV.T7.Ch25]].

Two recent notes complete the picture. In early M42, Enslavers were drawn to and assaulted
the *Blackened Tower*, one of the Black Legion's Arks of Omen, **heavily damaging
it**.[^lex-enslaver] And Genestealer Cults are equally vulnerable, sharing a basic psychic
link — Enslavers sometimes pursue genestealer populations on space hulks, playing cat and
mouse with the hybrids and following the hulk as it moves, while the more psychically
gifted hybrids are hidden in stasis tubes to avoid their notice.[^lex-enslaver]

The species that broke the Old Ones is currently inconveniencing the Black Legion and
hunting Genestealer Cults through drifting wrecks. It has no allegiance in the galaxy's
wars because it is older than all of them, and it is still doing the only thing it has ever
done. Trazyn the Infinite keeps **the ossified husk of an Enslaver** among his
relics[^lex-enslaver] — which is, characteristically, the closest thing the galaxy has to a
museum of the event that made it.
