# Humanity's Golden Conduit

## The Emperor's Great Work {#great-work}

Humanity tried to take the Webway, and the attempt is the hinge on which the whole setting
turns. Almost nothing else in this codex explains as much.

The Webway Project — also called the Imperial Webway, and the Emperor's Great Work — was a
highly secret undertaking of the Emperor's during the Great Crusade and probably long
before it. The sources are unambiguous about its priority: it was to be the culmination of
his ambition, and its completion was his greatest concern above all
others.[^lex-webway-project]

The immediate aim was transport. The project sought to replace Warp travel with access to
the Webway, which was more efficient and — crucially — bypassed the depredations of Chaos
within the Warp.[^lex-webway-project] Read against [[I.T1.Ch3]], that is the Emperor
identifying the single greatest structural weakness in his own empire and attempting to
engineer it away.

But transport was the means, not the end. The ultimate objective was for the Emperor to
sever humanity from the Warp *in its entirety*, depriving Chaos of its power, while guiding
the species through its evolution into a fully psychic race and so assuring its ascendancy
in the galaxy.[^lex-webway-project]

That deserves restating plainly, because it recasts everything the Imperium later became.
The Emperor's plan was not to defeat Chaos. It was to disconnect from it — to make humanity
unreachable, and then to complete the psychic evolution that [[I.T2.Ch5]] describes as the
source of every psyker. The Imperium of the 41st Millennium, with its Black Ships and its
sanctioned witches and its beacon fuelled by burned souls, is what remains after that plan
failed. Every institution in Volume IV is a workaround.

The provenance of the technology is deliberately left open. Asked directly, in a psychic
exchange with the Custodian Diocletian Coros, the Emperor said he had either improved on
technology from the Dark Age of Technology or had conceived the idea himself — and refused
to give a definite answer.[^lex-webway-project] The setting's most consequential engineering
project has no stated origin, and the one person who knew declined to say.

## Engineering Beneath the Palace {#engineering}

The mechanism was a portal, and the portal required a person.

Using a psyker of great power, an opening into the Webway could be created from the Golden
Throne — and the psyker originally intended for the role was likely Magnus the
Red.[^lex-webway-project] The Emperor made such a portal deep within the Imperial Palace on
Terra, and it was there that he began the work of expanding human access into the
labyrinth.[^lex-webway-project]

Construction fell to a purpose-made caste. The Emperor created a new order of Mechanicum
tech-priests, the Unifiers, to build fresh Webway tunnels for human
use.[^lex-webway-project] Here the project departed fatally from its model. Unlike the
original tunnels, the new ones were *not* made of psychically resistant material; instead
the Emperor's own psychic will, channelled through the Golden Throne, protected them from
daemonic intrusion.[^lex-webway-project]

That is the flaw, and it is worth being precise about it. [[I.T3.Ch10]] records that the
Webway is safe from the Warp because it was built to exclude it — the wards are in the
substance of the thing. The human extensions substituted a living psyker for that
substance. The tunnels were only as secure as the Emperor's attention, which made the whole
network a single point of failure with a person at it.

Secrecy compounded the risk. The project was mired in the greatest secrecy; not even the
primarchs knew why the Emperor had returned to Terra after naming Horus Warmaster at
Ullanor, and the silence bred resentment — Horus's especially.[^lex-webway-project] Part of
the motive may have been fear of how the Navis Nobilite would react to a threat to their
monopoly on interstellar travel.[^lex-webway-project] That fear was well founded. The
Paternova dispatched an agent to destroy Dark Glass, a prototype intended to link with the
Golden Throne.[^lex-webway-project]

That agent was the man named Veil, and he succeeded — detonating himself aboard the station
with two vortex charges.[^lex-veil] Readers who met him in [[I.T1.Ch4]] met him as a
curiosity: the only Veil the setting names, in a Chapter about a boundary nobody defines.
He turns out to have been the Navigators' instrument against the one project that would
have made Navigators unnecessary.

The Aeldari reacted no better. On the Imperium's first entry into the Webway, its previous
masters began sealing the portals to their own craftworlds against possible
invasion.[^lex-webway-project] Humanity's arrival in the network was read, immediately, as
an act of war by everyone with a stake in it.

## The Breach and the Throne {#the-breach}

The project ended in a single event, and the Imperium has been living inside the
consequences for ten thousand years.

Magnus the Red destroyed it — while trying to help. Attempting to warn of the treachery of
Horus and Erebus on Davin, Magnus used a forbidden psychic ritual to reach the Emperor
directly, deep inside the Imperial Palace. The ritual produced a massive psychic backlash
that set off a chain reaction through the wards the Emperor had so carefully constructed
across the Palace.[^lex-webway-project]

The result was catastrophic: multiple Warp rifts opened *inside the Webway itself*, known
afterwards as Magnus' Folly, and daemons poured through in
countless numbers.[^lex-webway-project] The Emperor was forced to contain the breach by
residing permanently on the Golden Throne for most of the Horus Heresy that followed, while
the Custodian Guard and the Sisters of Silence fought the War Within the Webway to stop the
daemons reaching the Palace and overrunning Terra.[^lex-webway-project] The rifts
themselves are the phenomenon described in [[I.T1.Ch4]]; the event belongs to
[[III.T3.Ch15]] and the war to [[III.T3.Ch16]].

In the end the Emperor sealed the portal within the Palace to save Terra, which doomed the
project.[^lex-webway-project] And here the sequence produces its final, bleakest turn: for
as long as the daemonic hordes press against the breach, the Golden Throne is the Emperor's
*prison*.[^lex-webway-project] He could leave it only by temporary expedients — sacrificing
psykers in enormous numbers, or substituting someone of comparable power, as Malcador
did.[^lex-webway-project]

That is what Malcador's death was. When the Emperor went to confront Horus aboard the
*Vengeful Spirit*, Malcador was left to maintain the Throne and hold the portal closed in
his place; the effort nearly killed him, and when the Emperor's broken body was returned,
Malcador gave his life, reducing himself to dust so that the Emperor could speak his final
instructions.[^lex-webway-project] The portal has remained closed for ten thousand years
since.[^lex-webway-project]

And the condition attached to that closure is the one every reader should carry forward:
should the Emperor die, the Imperial Palace is breached and Terra itself is overrun by
daemons.[^lex-webway-project] The corpse-god on the Throne is not being venerated out of
sentiment or preserved as a symbol. He is a door, and he is holding.
