# Inhabitants of the Labyrinth

## Craftworld Gates {#craftworld-gates}

The Webway is not empty. Two Aeldari civilisations live inside or alongside it, and a third
group guards something at its centre. Their relationships to the network are quite
different, and the differences explain much of what those cultures became.

A craftworld is a continent-sized vessel, self-sufficient, with its own culture, capable of
housing tens of millions.[^lex-craftworld] They were not built as refuges. During the
Aeldari Empire they were trading vessels, travelling both the void and the Webway, on
missions that could take centuries and range thousands of light years beyond Aeldari space
before turning home.[^lex-craftworld] They were built in titanic star docks, themselves the
work of the Bonesingers of Ceith-Cailli.[^lex-craftworld]

Their crews were often isolated from the Aeldari of the worlds the ships were named for,
which gave them something the rest of their species lacked: perspective. Each return
offered a clearer view of the civilisation's decline than those living inside it
had.[^lex-craftworld] In the final voyages before the Fall, seeing the ruin of their
society, the craftworlds opened their doors to those Aeldari still sane and most often fled
into the depths of space.[^lex-craftworld] Not all escaped — those that did not were
destroyed in the psychic overspill and consumed by Slaanesh.[^lex-craftworld]

So the craftworlds survived because they were merchant shipping that happened to be
mobile, crewed by the one part of the population positioned to see what was coming. The
Fall itself belongs to Volume III; the craftworlds as places to [[II.T3.Ch10]], and as a
society to [[VI.T2.Ch4]].

Their relationship to the Webway is one of connection rather than habitation. A craftworld
carries a Nexus of Webway Gates in its hull, tying it into the
network,[^lex-webway] and contains gates linking it both to other craftworlds and to
millions of planets — which is what gives a scattered civilisation any cohesion at
all.[^lex-craftworld] They also hold vast space docks housing fleets that carry Aeldari
armies through the Webway to the rest of the galaxy.[^lex-craftworld]

The distinction worth holding onto is this: craftworlds *use* the Webway. They are
moored to it, and can be cut off from it. When the Emperor began forcing his own way in,
their response was to seal their portals against possible Imperial invasion — a decision
covered in [[I.T3.Ch12]] and one only possible for people whose homes are outside the
labyrinth rather than within it.

## The Dark City {#commorragh}

Commorragh is the opposite arrangement. It is not connected to the Webway; it is *inside*
it, and it has been eating the surrounding network for millennia.

The Dark City is the capital of Drukhari society, located deep within the labyrinth — the
space between realspace and the Warp.[^lex-commorragh] Its origins are respectable enough:
in the days of the Aeldari Empire it was one of the greatest Webway port-cities, and Port
Commorragh was established in M18 as the primary nodal port in the whole
network.[^lex-commorragh]

Its rise followed from its position. Being able to move a fleet to any of the most vital
Aeldari worlds, and to reach the far-flung corners of realspace, made it the single most
important location in the Webway. That importance was precisely why it was granted
autonomy — it was judged too valuable to belong to any one part of the
empire.[^lex-commorragh]

The consequence was immediate and, in retrospect, entirely predictable. Autonomy placed
Commorragh outside the jurisdiction of the great Aeldari councils, and it therefore
attracted everyone with deeds to hide.[^lex-commorragh] The most strategically vital place
in the network became the one place its laws did not reach. What followed was expansion by
absorption: the city grew wealthy and influential, took in other port-cities, private
estates and sub-realms, and swelled with each acquisition on the plundered wealth of the
parts of the Webway it consumed.[^lex-commorragh]

By M31 the Fall shattered Aeldari civilisation, and the unrepentant survivors persisted
inside the Webway; between M31 and M32 Commorragh became a vast realm in its own right,
falling under the oppressive rule of the noble houses at its
heart.[^lex-commorragh] Structurally it is now less a city than a composite: a collection
of satellite realms and cities linked to one another by portals and hidden pathways, with
dimensional gateways acting as shortcuts that let the immense distances between sub-realms
be crossed in a single step.[^lex-commorragh]

Commorragh as a place belongs to [[II.T3.Ch10]], and the Drukhari as a society to
[[VI.T2.Ch6]]. What belongs here is the structural point: the Webway's most important
junction was ceded to whoever would hold it, and the people who took it built a city whose
geography is now indistinguishable from the network's own.

## Harlequins and the Black Library {#black-library}

At the centre of the labyrinth is a library, and everything about it is defended.

The Black Library of Chaos — usually just the Black Library — is the Aeldari repository of
forbidden lore.[^lex-black-library-of-chaos] Knowledge that it exists is held by few, and
fewer are admitted.[^lex-black-library-of-chaos] What it contains is the Aeldari study of
the Warp: tomes and writings describing every aspect of Chaos, its blandishments,
influences, forms, creatures, perils, promises and
horrors.[^lex-black-library-of-chaos] Behind psychically locked doors are black tomes of
daemon summoning, grimoires, and records of countless Chaos cults across the
galaxy.[^lex-black-library-of-chaos]

Its acquisition policy is the detail that makes it more than a collection. It does not hold
only Aeldari knowledge of Chaos — *any* knowledge of Chaos is taken and kept
there.[^lex-black-library-of-chaos] The library is not passive. Somewhere in the Webway
there is an institution whose standing purpose is to remove information about Chaos from
the rest of the galaxy and file it.

The guard is layered. The passages to it are watched by Harlequins, who come and go as
needed, though their Solitaires may pass as they
wish;[^lex-black-library-of-chaos] Harlequins hold no allegiance to any craftworld or kabal
with the single exception of the Black Library, whose routes they fiercely guard and
obfuscate both inside the Webway and beyond.[^lex-harlequin] The entrance itself is kept by
the Guardian-Scribes — said to be the most terrible of all the perils of the
Webway — and its records maintained by the White
Seers.[^lex-black-library-of-chaos] Obfuscation is doing as much work here as force: the
routes are not merely defended but deliberately confused, which given the geometry
described in [[I.T3.Ch10]] is a formidable defence on its own.

The Harlequins answer to Cegorach, the Laughing God: a trickster deity, the Great Fool,
who gave the Aeldari irony as a means of finding humour in themselves and so avoiding the
trap of hubris.[^lex-cegorach] He is described as mocking, sinister, vindictive and
enigmatic, and his jokes punish gods and mortals alike for the sin of
pride.[^lex-cegorach] During the War in Heaven, Cegorach and his Soul-Dancers persuaded the
C'tan to turn their hunger on one another and consume their own
kind.[^lex-cegorach] Despite the Harlequins' often sinister character, Cegorach represents
the soul of the Aeldari and is still worshipped on the craftworlds.[^lex-cegorach]

The Harlequins themselves are the subject of [[VI.T2.Ch8]], and the Aeldari pantheon of
[[I.T4.Ch16]]. What belongs here is the placement: the most dangerous archive in the galaxy
sits inside the one structure that is neither realspace nor Warp, guarded by servants of a
god whose defining act was making his enemies destroy each other.
