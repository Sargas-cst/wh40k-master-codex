# Alternative FTL Technologies

## Necron Inertialess Drives {#inertialess}

Three species cross the galaxy without entering the Warp at all, and each pays a different
price for it. The Necrons pay the smallest, because they are already dead.

Inertialess drives are the propulsion systems of the Necron fleet, capable of interstellar
travel without entering the Warp, using relativistic energy and leaving only minor spatial
anomalies behind them.[^lex-inertialess-drive] They travel at extraordinary speeds — one
source describes crossing the galaxy in "the blink of an
eye"[^lex-inertialess-drive] — and can also be used for short-range jumps on the
battlefield.[^lex-inertialess-drive]

The catch is decisive and it is biological. The drives exert immense accelerative forces on
the whole of the vessel, including passengers and crew, and those forces are almost
immediately lethal to mortal bodies. No flesh-and-blood species has any chance of surviving
travel by inertialess drive.[^lex-inertialess-drive]

That single fact explains why the best transport technology in the setting has had no
influence on anyone else's. It is not secret, not especially rare, and it works far better
than anything the Imperium possesses — and it cannot be used by anything alive. The Necrons
solved interstellar travel by being metal. Whether they solved it before or after that
transformation is a question for Volume III Title 1.

Human tech-priests who have studied the drives find them utterly incomprehensible
enigmas.[^lex-inertialess-drive] Set that against [[I.T1.Ch1]]: an institution that
preserves technology by ritual rather than understanding, confronted with a working
faster-than-light system it can neither use nor explain.

The Necrons also force entry into the Webway with Dolmen Gates, described in
[[I.T3.Ch10]] — which means they alone among the galaxy's powers can travel both without the
Warp *and* through the labyrinth. It has not made them dominant, which is a useful
corrective to the assumption that mobility decides wars. Their fleet is treated in
[[VI.T1.Ch3]].

## Tyranid Gravity Compression {#narvhal}

The Tyranid method is the strangest of the three, and the only one that damages the
destination before arrival.

Tyranids do not use Warp jumps. They rely instead on the Narvhal: a bio-ship that exists to
let every vessel of a hive fleet travel faster than light, and which is otherwise small,
innocuous, and almost entirely without weaponry or armour.[^lex-narvhal] The most important
ship in a hive fleet is the one least able to defend itself.

Its sensory apparatus does the work. Clustered along a Narvhal's bow are monofilament
spines able to interpret a wide range of input, including a broad spectrum of gravimetric
signals, allowing it to detect a planetary system at enormous
distance.[^lex-narvhal] It then harnesses that system's gravity to create a corridor of
compressed space along which the fleet travels toward it.[^lex-narvhal] The mechanism by
which it does so is not explained — the source says only that it *somehow* harnesses the
gravity, which is as much as anyone has recorded.

The trade-off against Warp travel is stated explicitly and is the reverse of the Imperium's
position: the method is slower than proper Warp travel, but *infinitely more
reliable*.[^lex-narvhal] Compare [[I.T1.Ch3]], where the Imperium accepts wild
unpredictability in exchange for speed. The Tyranids made the opposite choice, and it is
worth asking which is the better trade for a species that does not care how long anything
takes.

There is a limit near the end of every journey. Strong gravitational forces interfere with
the Narvhal's delicate senses, so on final approach the fleet can no longer use its
ability and must fall back on slower conventional propulsion — often delaying arrival by
years or even decades.[^lex-narvhal]

That delay is not merely a cost, because of what the compression does to the target.
Manipulating a system's gravity has disastrous side effects: the ensnared planets suffer
earthquakes, solar flares, tidal waves and other natural disasters throughout the interval
between the Narvhal taking hold and the fleet actually
arriving.[^lex-narvhal] For the Tyranids this is another weapon — the defenders spend the
intervening years disorganised and busy with their own world turning against
them.[^lex-narvhal]

So the propulsion system is also the bombardment. A world under Tyranid approach experiences
the journey as a decades-long natural catastrophe, and by the time the fleet is visible the
planet has already been softened by its own geology. Hive fleets belong to
[[VI.T4.Ch14]].

## The T'au Slipstream, and the Ork Exception {#slipstream-and-orks}

The T'au method is the newest, the most ambitious, and the only one to have gone
catastrophically wrong in a way the setting still lives with.

The AL-38 Slipstream Module is an experimental device for faster-than-light travel, reverse
engineered from Imperial vessels and Kroot technologies.[^lex-al-38-slipstream-module] It
was developed by the Earth Caste scientist Ka'buto after years spent studying Imperial FTL
and the wreckage of Kroot warspheres.[^lex-al-38-slipstream-module] Fitted to a ship, the
module creates a bubble of antimatter around it and propels it fast enough to pierce the
fabric of reality.[^lex-al-38-slipstream-module] Initial tests were extremely successful,
allowing a vessel to cross the entire T'au Empire in a few days where the journey had
previously taken months.[^lex-al-38-slipstream-module]

Then it was used at scale, against advice. Despite Ka'buto's warnings, the Ethereal Council
activated hundreds — possibly thousands — of AL-38 modules simultaneously at Numenar Point to
launch the Fourth Sphere of Expansion. The mass use of antimatter tore reality open and swept
the T'au fleet away.[^lex-al-38-slipstream-module] The Ethereals scrapped the entire
programme, and the modules were disassembled and returned to Earth Caste
storage.[^lex-al-38-slipstream-module]

The fleet was not destroyed. Decades later it emerged that it had been transported across
much of the galaxy, leaving behind the wormhole that became known as the Startide
Nexus.[^lex-al-38-slipstream-module] Nearly two-thirds of the Fourth Sphere fleet was lost
during its time in the Warp, and — paradoxically — the opening of the Cicatrix Maledictum is
what allowed the remainder to find a way back.[^lex-al-38-slipstream-module] The Great Rift,
which ruined the Imperium, saved them. Ka'buto and his team are presumed to have continued
improving the device in hope of a Sixth Sphere.[^lex-al-38-slipstream-module]

!!! disputed "Recorded conflict — tau-ftl-retcon"

    Whether the T'au had *any* faster-than-light capability before the slipstream module is
    disputed between editions. Earlier material gives them slow but working FTL in the form
    of gravitic drives and hooks; 8th Edition rewrote them as having had no true FTL at all
    until the AL-38.[^lex-al-38-slipstream-module] This is a deliberate change made between
    editions rather than an inconsistency inside the setting — the kind of revision Appendix
    A Title 1 exists to track.

A correction on the Orks, who are often listed among the non-Warp travellers and do not
belong there. Ork fleets use Warp engines for faster-than-light travel — volatile and
dangerous ones, but Warp engines nonetheless, comparable to human Warp
drives.[^lex-ork-fleet][^lex-warp-drive] What is distinctive is not the physics but the
navigation. Ramshackle Ork ships frequently enter the Warp with no particular destination in
mind, trusting that Gork and Mork will deposit them somewhere with a good fight
available;[^lex-ork-fleet] other fleets are steered by Weirdboyz, with highly variable
results.[^lex-ork-fleet]

So the Orks are not an alternative to Warp travel. They are Warp travel with the navigation
removed and faith substituted — which, given the Astronomican's cost as described in
[[IV.T9.Ch31]], is either the stupidest or the most economical arrangement in the galaxy.
Ork vessels are [[VI.T3.Ch11]]'s subject.
