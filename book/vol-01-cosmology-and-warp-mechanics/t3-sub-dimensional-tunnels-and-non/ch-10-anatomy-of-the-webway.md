# Anatomy of the Webway

## What the Old Ones Built {#old-ones}

The Warp is not the only way across the galaxy. There is a second route, older than any
species now using it, and it exists precisely because its builders wanted nothing to do
with the first.

The Webway is a labyrinth lying between the Materium and the Warp — part of both, and
existing in neither.[^lex-webway] Its origins trace to the Old Ones, who made it as a
conduit through which they could reach countless far-flung worlds without suffering the
tides of the Warp.[^lex-webway] They were the first race to cross the gulf between stars
in a single step and to learn the manipulation of alternate dimensions, and that mastery
gave them the Webway portals.[^lex-webway]

It is worth pausing on what that means, because it inverts the usual reading of this
setting. [[I.T1.Ch3]] describes Warp travel as humanity's only practical means of
interstellar movement, unreliable in principle and dangerous by nature. The Webway is
proof that the problem was solved once, by someone else, tens of millions of years ago —
and that the solution still exists. The galaxy's dominant civilisation is not using the
best available transport network. It is using the one it can reach.

The Webway was also a weapon. During the War in Heaven the Old Ones used it to
outmanoeuvre the Necrontyr so effectively that within a few centuries that race had been
reduced to a minor irritant.[^lex-webway] Mobility, not firepower, was what nearly ended
them. That changed when the C'tan entered the war on the Necron side: Nyadra'zatha, the
Burning One, had long wanted to bring his fire into the Webway, and taught the Necrons how
to pierce the boundary of that space beyond space by means of Dolmen
Gates.[^lex-webway][^lex-dolmen-gate] The Old Ones' greatest advantage was turned against
them. That war is the subject of Volume III Title 1; the gates themselves recur in
[[I.T3.Ch13]].

Knowledge of the Webway passed afterwards to the Aeldari, who were taught the
technologies that made it.[^lex-webway] From the secrets of the Warp the Old Ones gave
them, they built a pan-galactic network of navigable tunnels.[^lex-webway] The passages
are described as ancient constructs, older than even the Aeldari
know[^lex-webway] — which is the first of several places in this Chapter where the current
occupants turn out not to understand their own inheritance.

## Geometry Without a Map {#geometry}

The Webway resists description, and the sources are unusually careful about admitting it.

It is described as not being a true dimension at all, but a complex network of capillaries
and arteries: a maze of glowing tunnels forming a tapestry of hidden threads spread
between realspace and Warp space — ultimately a construct that spans
dimensions.[^lex-webway] The vocabulary is anatomical rather than architectural, and that
seems deliberate. It is treated as something grown and circulatory rather than laid out.

Its paths are not ordinal. Mapped onto the galaxy the Webway would not resemble a
spiderweb; the nodes would fall in an almost random pattern, with nexuses appearing only
sporadically — on craftworlds, on the Crone Worlds, and at the great port
cities.[^lex-webway] This is the single most important fact about it for any practical
purpose. Distance inside the Webway has no reliable relationship to distance outside it.
Two worlds close together in realspace may be far apart in the labyrinth, and the reverse.
Access is not a matter of proximity but of whether a gate happens to exist.

Viewed from a breach on the Warp side, its exterior appears golden.[^lex-webway] Its
construction includes complex psychic wards to prevent it being breached, and hyperspatial
pathways.[^lex-webway] The wards are the load-bearing detail: the Webway is safe from the
Warp not because of where it sits but because it was *built* to exclude it, which is why
every later attempt to open it by force — Necron, human — produces the failures described
below and in [[I.T3.Ch12]].

Its reality is more malleable than that of the Materium, though less so than the Warp's,
and the ancient Aeldari Empire used devices such as Reality Engines to manipulate its
fabric directly.[^lex-webway]

Time inside it is not dependable either. The Webway was theorised to stretch forwards and
backwards in time, but the Aeldari never experimented with that aspect, fearing the
consequences.[^lex-webway] The Harlequins speak of remote locations where the flow of time
reverses — Uigebealach — or does not flow at all, the Crossroads of
Inertia.[^lex-webway] One Inquisitor, Bronislaw Czevak, spent several decades inside and
came out with his body's ageing reversed by several centuries; he theorised that constant
Webway travel is partly responsible for Aeldari longevity.[^lex-webway] That is a single
attested case supporting a broad hypothesis, and is best read as such.

Present-day Aeldari do not fully understand the exact shape or form of the network they
live in.[^lex-webway] Knowledge of where a craftworld sits within it is a secret kept by
that craftworld's Seers.[^lex-webway] So the most complete map of the Webway is
distributed among people who guard it from each other.

## Degradation and Fractured Realms {#degradation}

The Webway is broken, and has been for a long time. What survives is a functioning
remnant of something larger.

In the shattered portions of the network there are dead ends, mazes that trap the unwary,
pathways abandoned or destroyed outright, and passages inhabited by
daemons.[^lex-webway] The doorways into those parts are sealed with runes of power,
specifically to keep whatever populates them from reaching a
craftworld.[^lex-webway] Note the phrasing of that danger: the sources do not always say
what is in there. The seals exist against unknown contents.

The response to intrusion is automatic and indifferent. Artificial portals such as those
the Dolmen Gates provide are neither stable nor easily controllable, and the Webway itself
somehow detects these breaches in its hallways and seals off the infected branches until
the danger has passed. Anyone travelling in those portions can be destroyed as the network
corrects the breach.[^lex-webway]

That is worth stating plainly, because it is the closest thing the Webway has to a
disposition. It does not defend itself in the sense of fighting; it quarantines. A
forced entry causes the surrounding tunnels to be amputated, with whoever is inside them
included — attacker and bystander alike. The mechanism has no way to distinguish a Necron
raiding party from an Aeldari trade route that happened to share a branch. Nothing in the
material describes who or what performs this sealing, or whether anything decides it at
all.

Movement through the network is also more constrained than its reputation suggests. Large
arterial passages can carry spacecraft, which is how Aeldari forces cross the galaxy to
make war. But most tunnels admit only small strike forces on foot, or at best allow
vehicles through their doorways.[^lex-webway] The Webway is not a highway system with
occasional footpaths; it is mostly footpaths, with a few passages wide enough for a fleet.

The gates that remain are held rather than owned. A craftworld carries a Nexus of Webway
Gates built into its hull, connecting it to the network.[^lex-webway] Storm Serpents
operate portable generators that power a wraithgate, letting forces arrive directly on a
battlefield, and the Drukhari use mobile portals to link their own
forces.[^lex-webway] Who holds which gate, and what they do with the ground behind it, is
the subject of [[I.T3.Ch11]].
