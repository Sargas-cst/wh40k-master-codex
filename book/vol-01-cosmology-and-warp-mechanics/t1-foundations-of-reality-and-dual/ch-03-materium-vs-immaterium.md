# Materium vs Immaterium

## The Asymmetry of Real Space {#real-space}

The setting is built on a pair of realities, and the pair is not balanced. One half is
documented at enormous length. The other barely exists as a subject.

[[g:real-space]] — the Materium — is the material universe: everything with mass and
duration, governed by physics as a reader would recognise it. The Imperium's term for it
is set explicitly against **Warp Space**, the parallel dimension of
[[s:the-warp]].[^lex-real-space] The two are described as converging at several places
in the galaxy, the Eye of Terror and the Maelstrom among them.[^lex-real-space]

That is very nearly the whole of it. Lexicanum's article on real space runs to a few
hundred bytes and carries a single citation.[^lex-real-space] There is no body of
material describing the physical laws of the Materium, no account of how they are
thought to operate, no discussion of the material universe as a thing with properties.
The article's function is almost entirely negative: it defines real space as *what the
Warp is not*.

The asymmetry is worth stating plainly rather than papering over, because it tells you
something real about how the setting is constructed. Warhammer 40,000 does not describe
a physical universe with a strange dimension attached. It describes a strange dimension,
and gestures at an ordinary universe as the place you are standing when you look at it.
The Materium is the setting's null hypothesis: assumed, unexamined, and interesting only
where the other reality intrudes on it.

That is a design choice with consequences for a reference work. There is no *Volume of
Physics* to write, because the sources supply none. Where this codex describes
realspace, it will nearly always be describing something the Warp is doing to it — which
is why the boundary between the two, treated in [[I.T1.Ch4]], carries so much more
weight than either side.

The naming is worth noting too. "Materium" is not a headword on Lexicanum: it redirects
to Real Space. The material universe does not get its own article title.

## The Nature of the Empyrean {#the-empyrean}

The Warp is a psychic dimension parallel to real space, and it is known by a great many
names: Warpspace, the Immaterium, the Empyrean, the Ether or Aether, the Sea of Souls,
the Othersea, otherspace, Sha'iel, and the Realm of Chaos.[^lex-warp] The multiplicity
is itself informative — the number of names a thing carries tends to track how many
cultures have needed to speak about it without agreeing on what it is.

It is described as a mirror dimension of pure energy, a mirror to the
Materium.[^lex-warp] Two properties do most of the work. First, its existence is fuelled
by the emotions and souls of every sentient being in the material
universe.[^lex-warp] Second, it has no form, and the laws of time and space do not apply
within it: size and time are simultaneously infinite and irrelevant, and its energies
are in constant turmoil.[^lex-warp]

The first property is the one with teeth. The Warp is born from the thoughts, dreams and
emotions of every psychically attuned sentient race in the galaxy; every emotion and
action in the Materium feeds it, and is reflected back, creating its own
reality.[^lex-warp] The consequence is stated directly: were the galaxy's races somehow
cut off from the Warp, its malevolent entities would ultimately weaken and
starve.[^lex-warp] The setting's central antagonist is sustained by its victims. It is
not an external invader but an emanation.

The sources are careful to present their descriptions as *analogies*, and this codex
keeps that framing. The common comparison is to a vast sea of energy subject to constant
movement, currents and undertows; the common metaphor for transit compares the Warp to a
fast-flowing stream whose motionless banks are real space, with a vessel as a leaf
dropped into the current. The article notes that this is useful for understanding but
that the Warp is far more complex than a linear stream, moving in endlessly
unpredictable and convoluted patterns.[^lex-warp] These are teaching images, not
mechanisms — a distinction it is easy to lose.

Every creature of flesh and blood also has an existence in the Warp: a
[[g:soul]], a shadow-self of pure energy from which mortals draw resolve, vigour and
determination.[^lex-soul] At death the soul is parted from the body and cast adrift in
the Warp,[^lex-soul] where most swiftly lose sentience and perceive only
oblivion.[^lex-warp] Souls are the final destination the setting offers, and they are
also food. The soul is treated in full at [[I.T2.Ch5]]; Chaos itself at [[V.T1.Ch1]].

## Warp Travel and the Gellar Field {#warp-travel}

The Imperium exists because the Warp can be crossed. The article is unambiguous about
the dependence: the Emperor's will may be mighty, but his reach is long only because
Warpspace can be crossed by his fleets.[^lex-warp] Vessels capable of voyaging thousands
of light years in days are what bind a million worlds into one state.[^lex-warp]

The method is to enter the Warp using a warp drive, and then to navigate between its
streams as one would an ocean — which is possible only because the Warp is a domain of
pure energy rather than of distance. The Imperium uses Navigators, a sub-race of humans,
for this purpose.[^lex-warp] The webway and the non-Warp alternatives are treated in
[[I.T3.Ch10]] and [[I.T3.Ch13]].

The unreliability is not incidental. Time proceeds a great deal slower — or faster — in
the Warp than in the material universe, producing time stasis, acceleration, reversal
and other violations of physics. The article's own summary is that while the solution is
effective, it is far from perfect.[^lex-warp] Long journeys take months or years, and
the timing is unpredictable because of the nature of the medium
itself.[^lex-warp] Nothing about this improves with better engineering; the variance is
a property of the Warp, not a tolerance in the drive. This is the direct cause of the
check-number scale described in [[I.T1.Ch2]].

What makes transit survivable at all is the [[g:gellar-field]]: a protective system used
by Imperial ships travelling within the Warp, whose main purpose is to protect the
vessel from daemonic incursion.[^lex-gellar-field] It was invented alongside the warp
drive, and the sequencing matters — the two are a single invention, because a warp drive
without a field is a means of dying somewhere unusual. The field protects ship and
occupants both from the hostility of the Warp itself and from the predations of warp
entities.[^lex-gellar-field]

On the spelling: readers will encounter both "Gellar" and "Geller". This is not a live
dispute. Lexicanum's article opens by giving both forms, attributing the variant to an
official Games Workshop web article.[^lex-gellar-field] This codex uses *Gellar field*,
matching the article title, and records the variant in the glossary rather than
relitigating it in prose.
