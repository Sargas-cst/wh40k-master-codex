# The Veil & Metaphysical Boundaries

## The Veil: A Term Without a Referent {#the-veil}

This Chapter is named for something that cannot be described, and the honest thing to do
is say so at the start rather than at the end.

"The Veil" is in wide circulation as a name for the boundary between the Materium and
the Immaterium. It appears in fan writing, in discussion, and in loose summary. What it
does not appear in is any source that defines it.

The position is starker than mere absence. There *is* a Veil in the setting — and he is a
man. Veil was a human vassal and magister of the Navis Nobilite during the Great Crusade,
sworn to House Achelieux. He survived the invasion of Herevail, was rescued by the White
Scars, and destroyed the Dark Glass station with two vortex charges on the Paternova's
orders.[^lex-veil] That is the only Veil the setting names. The cosmological one has no
entry anywhere.

!!! undefined "Undefined — the-veil-as-structure"

    Nothing anywhere describes the Veil as a structure with measurable properties. It has
    **no stated thickness, composition, location or mechanism**. Accounts that give it
    any of those are supplying what the setting does not.

What *is* attested is a barrier, referred to but never characterised. The Warp Rift
article describes a rift as a weakening of the barrier between real space and the Warp,
allowing the Warp's creatures into the material universe.[^lex-warp-rift] So the sources
posit something separating the two realities — they simply never say what it is, how
thick, where, or made of what. It is known only by its failures.

That is a defensible way for a setting to work, and worth distinguishing from sloppiness.
A boundary described only through its breaches is consistent with a cosmology whose whole
point is that reality is not securely sealed. The gap becomes a problem only when a
reference work fills it. Treat "the Veil" as a metaphor in common use, not a mechanism —
and be suspicious of any account that assigns it dimensions.

## Ruptures and Reality Tears {#ruptures}

If the boundary is undefined, its failures are documented in detail, and they come in two
kinds that are easy to conflate.

A [[g:warp-storm]] is a disturbance within the Warp itself, affecting both Immaterium and
Materium.[^lex-warp-storm] Storms can last days, months or centuries. A vessel caught in
one may be flung thousands of light years off course into uncharted space, or trapped in
the Warp entirely; a ship may travel briefly and emerge to find centuries have passed in
real space. Storms also sever Warp travel through whole regions, isolating systems from
the Imperium for months, years or centuries — and some systems have always been isolated
with no sign of changing.[^lex-warp-storm] Particularly severe storms during the Age of
Strife devastated humanity's interstellar realms, and dissipated only with the birth of
Slaanesh.[^lex-warp-storm]

A [[g:warp-rift]] is different in kind: a tear linking the two realities, through which
the Warp's inhabitants can pass.[^lex-warp-rift] The attested causes are worth
enumerating, because they are more varied than "sorcery":

- unpredictable movements of the Warp breaking into real space
- daemonic possession of a mortal
- deliberate summoning ritual
- a focus of suffering and death from a great war
- chance circumstance, such as a warp drive implosion
- most insidiously, the mind of an unprotected psychic creature[^lex-warp-rift]

Note the range. A rift can be opened by a battle, by an accident, or by a person simply
existing. Rifts last moments or centuries, and take forms including whirlpools of raw
magic, gates of green fire, clouds of impenetrable shadow and boiling lakes of
pus.[^lex-warp-rift] Stable variants exist: a Warp Portal connects a fixed point in real
space to a fixed point in the Warp, and is of use to the Imperium chiefly as an exit for
a ship whose drive has failed.[^lex-warp-rift]

One detail deserves highlighting, because it is the setting policing its own narrators.
Navigators say that to plough through a warp rift is little different from swimming in
acid, flaying away parts of a passenger's soul with every moment inside it. The article
calls this "partial truth" — such overlap regions are in fact calmer than storms, though
more violent than realspace.[^lex-warp-rift] An in-universe account, corrected in place.

The largest known example is the Great Rift, or Cicatrix Maledictum, which opened at the
end of the 41st Millennium and divided much of the galaxy.[^lex-great-rift] Its scale is
such that physical law has begun to fray and temporal inconsistency, once confined to
large storms, has spread across the known galaxy — some worlds experiencing centuries in
an instant, others near-frozen.[^lex-great-rift] It is the subject of [[II.T1.Ch2]] as
geography and [[III.T5.Ch23]] as event.

## Blackstone and the Suppression of the Warp {#blackstone}

There is one attested way to push the Warp back, and the Imperium did not invent it.

A [[g:pylon]] is a Necron-built structure capable of suppressing Warp phenomena in the
space around it.[^lex-pylon] The material is [[g:noctilith]], also called blackstone,
which interferes with the boundary between real space and the
Warp.[^lex-noctilith][^lex-pylon] Note what that implies: the only documented means of
stabilising a boundary the sources decline to define is a xenos technology, built by a
species that predates humanity, and used by the Imperium without being understood — the
pattern [[I.T1.Ch1]] describes, at cosmological scale.

The clearest evidence for the effect is negative — what happened when it stopped. The
Great Rift's origins are, in the source's own words, a mystery, and several competing
theories are recorded: the destruction of the Cadian pylons during the 13th Black
Crusade, sorcery by Magnus the Red at Fenris, the awakening of Ynnead, mass bloodshed in
the Damocles Gulf, and the Ruinous Powers reacting to Guilliman's
resurrection.[^lex-great-rift] Guilliman himself is recorded as speculating that Abaddon
destroyed blackstone-rich worlds across ten thousand years, with Cadia as the
trigger.[^lex-great-rift]

!!! disputed "Multiple accounts, none settled"

    Five theories, plus Guilliman's own speculation, and no way to choose between them.
    All that is stated as known is that the Rift divided the Imperium in
    half.[^lex-great-rift] The largest cosmological event in the setting's history has no
    agreed cause.

The pylons and noctilith are treated in full as xenos technology at [[VIII.T5.Ch14]], and
Cadia itself at [[II.T3.Ch8]]. What belongs here is the principle: the boundary can be
reinforced, the means are not human, and the one place it was reinforced at scale is the
place whose failure tore the galaxy.
