# The C'tan (Star Gods)

## Origin of the Star Vampires {#origin}

Every other power in this Volume derives from the Warp. The C'tan do not, and that single
fact makes them the most genuinely alien thing in the setting.

The C'tan — Yngir in Aeldari — are beings of pure energy described as forming a fundamental
constant of the universe, said to be the first beings in
existence.[^lex-c-tan] They are held to have been born during the creation of the universe
itself, formed from the insensate energies unleashed by the churning of primordial forces,
emerging as the earliest self-aware entities from seas of plasma and solar flares long
before the first planets had formed and cooled.[^lex-c-tan]

Note what that does to their category. Chaos gods, as [[I.T1.Ch1]] records, formed from the
pooled emotions of mortal beings and remain dependent on them. The C'tan predate mortality
altogether. They are not sustained by worship, cannot be starved of it, and have no stake
in anyone's inner life. Where the Warp powers are a reflection of sentient life, the C'tan
are simply older than it — which is why the setting's usual leverage against gods does not
apply to them.

They were also, initially, harmless. The sources describe them continuing a benign
existence with little conception of the universe around them until the intervention of the
Necrontyr.[^lex-c-tan] The star gods did not seek out the galaxy. They were found, and then
put to use.

The Necrontyr had studied the stars since their earliest times, seeking to understand their
baleful energies — looking both for a power to unleash on their enemies the Old Ones, and
for a cure to their own short lifespans.[^lex-c-tan] What they found was worse than what
they were looking for.

!!! disputed "Rival accounts — ctan-discovery-accounts"

    How the Necrontyr found the C'tan is recorded two different ways by two different
    peoples. The archives of Solemnace say it was an accident: a chance discovery by a
    stellar probe investigating a dying star. The Aeldari *Book of Mournful Night* claims
    the raw hatred the Necrontyr felt for the Old Ones sang out across space and served as
    a beacon that drew the C'tan in.[^lex-c-tan]

    Both are given. One is a Necron record and one an Aeldari one, and each flatters its
    keeper — an accident absolves, a beacon of hatred condemns.

What followed was a bargain. The Necrontyr persuaded the Nightbringer, by desperate pledges
of servitude, that there were species beyond number for it to destroy; and soon afterwards
they awakened many other star gods, becoming their willing servants in the war against the
Old Ones.[^lex-c-tan] The Silent King's own chronicle puts it without
consolation — *they came to us as gods, and we, like fools, took them at their
word*.[^lex-c-tan]

The name "star vampires" is earned in the aftermath rather than the meeting. What the C'tan
wanted, once awake, was worshippers and slaves. The War in Heaven belongs to
[[III.T1.Ch1]] and the bio-transference that made the Necrontyr into Necrons to
[[III.T1.Ch2]].

## Necrodermis {#necrodermis}

A being of pure energy cannot act on matter directly. The solution was metal, and it is the
reason the C'tan appear in the setting as figures rather than forces.

A necrodermis is the mechanical body of living metal that houses a Necron's
essence.[^lex-necrodermis] The same material is used to contain the compressed essence of a
C'tan when it manifests — and specifically, it is what allows the C'tan to *perceive* the
material world at all.[^lex-necrodermis] The metal is not armour. It is a sensory organ.

The material itself was developed by the Necrontyr for use in their
starships,[^lex-necrodermis] which means the vessel that lets a star god touch reality is
repurposed shipbuilding stock. There is a consistent pattern here worth naming: the C'tan
are enormously powerful and almost entirely dependent on their servants' engineering for
anything they actually do.

Within that constraint they have latitude. A necrodermis can be reshaped at will by its
C'tan inhabitant, which is why the bodies of the various star gods differ so
enormously — and it allows the spontaneous formation of cutting and slashing weapons as
required.[^lex-necrodermis] Some Necrons, Trazyn among them, have shown a high degree of
control over necrodermis themselves.[^lex-necrodermis]

Breaching it is survivable. If the necrodermis is broken open the C'tan's essence is
released along with an explosion that can damage anything nearby — but this is described as
a minor setback: a new necrodermis can be reforged, and the C'tan crosses the incorporeal
starlight bridge back into the material realm.[^lex-necrodermis]

That is the crucial military fact about them, and it is easy to miss. A C'tan cannot be
killed by destroying its body. Defeating one in the field accomplishes an inconvenience.
Doing anything permanent to a C'tan required a method their own servants had to invent, and
that is [[I.T4.Ch15]]'s subject.

## Rewriting the Laws of Reality {#reality}

What a C'tan does is not magic in the Warp sense. It is closer to editing.

The claim made for them is that their presence undoes physical law directly. One recorded
effect, the Writhing Worldscape, describes the natural world revolting at a C'tan shard's
presence — the ground itself writhing and shaking as the physical laws holding reality
together are undone.[^lex-c-tan] The formulation is worth attending to: not laws being
*overpowered*, but laws being *unmade*.

Their individual capacities follow the same pattern of scale. Yggra'nya, the World Shaper,
is described as a C'tan whose very thoughts could break a planet apart and reshape it into a
form more pleasing to him.[^lex-c-tan] Not a weapon, not a ritual — a thought, with a planet
as its object.

It is worth being careful here, because this is where accounts of the C'tan become least
disciplined. What the sources give are effects and epithets rather than mechanism. Nothing
explains *how* a being of energy unmakes physical law, or what the limits are, or why a
power capable of reshaping planets by thinking was ever containable by the species that
woke it. The C'tan are described in superlatives and demonstrated in incidents, and the gap
between those two registers is never closed.

That gap matters for reading everything downstream. Set against [[I.T1.Ch3]], where the Warp
is described with careful analogies its own sources label as analogies, the C'tan material
is unusually assertive and unusually thin. The distinction is not between reliable and
unreliable sources — both are tier 2 — but between a subject the setting has thought
carefully about and one it mostly gestures at.

One structural consequence does hold up. Because the C'tan operate on matter and physical
law rather than through the Immaterium, none of the setting's defences against the Warp work
on them. A Gellar field, described in [[I.T1.Ch3]], is irrelevant. A blank's null field,
from [[I.T2.Ch6]], is irrelevant. The wards that keep the Webway sealed, in
[[I.T3.Ch10]], are irrelevant. Every protection humanity and the Aeldari possess is
addressed to a threat coming from one direction, and the C'tan come from the other.
