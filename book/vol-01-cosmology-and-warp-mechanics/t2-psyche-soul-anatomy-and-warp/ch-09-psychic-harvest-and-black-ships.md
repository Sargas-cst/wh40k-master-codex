# Psychic Harvest & Black Ships

## The Mandate of the Black Ships {#black-ships}

The Adeptus Astra Telepathica has two primary divisions. One trains psykers; the other
collects them. The **League of Black Ships** is the recruiting division: a substantial
fleet charged with the ceaseless collection and transportation of psykers from across the
galaxy to Terra.[^lex-league-of-black-ships]

The scale is deliberately obscured. There are many thousands of Black Ships, and the
fleet is the second-largest in the galaxy after the Imperial Navy — but only the
highest-ranking adepts of the Adeptus Astra Telepathica know its true size and the full
scope of its operations.[^lex-league-of-black-ships] That is an in-universe secret rather
than a gap in the sources, and this codex reports it as withheld rather than guessing.
The fleet is based at the Magadan Orbital Construct in the Sol
System,[^lex-league-of-black-ships] operates independently of the Navy, and reports
directly to the Master of the Adeptus Astra Telepathica.[^lex-league-of-black-ships]

Against that fleet, the arithmetic does not work. The Adeptus Astra Telepathica can hope
to gather only a minuscule fraction of the psykers born, at
most.[^lex-league-of-black-ships] Two developments have made it worse: since the opening
of the Great Rift the frequency of psyker births among humanity has
increased,[^lex-league-of-black-ships] while the Rift has simultaneously impeded or
blocked routes to innumerable worlds — compounding the build-up of rogue psykers on any
world the ships can no longer reach.[^lex-league-of-black-ships] Worlds cut off from
having their tithes taken are now forced to devise their own restraints on their untrained
psykers.[^lex-league-of-black-ships] The event itself is covered at [[III.T5.Ch23]].

Under Imperial law all psykers must undergo testing and
sanctioning.[^lex-sanctioned-psyker] Those who do not are hunted, detained, or simply
culled — often burned as witches — and those duties fall to the Ordo Hereticus, treated at
[[IV.T7.Ch23]].[^lex-sanctioned-psyker] Most never need hunting, because they are
collected as small children while their powers are still crude and easy to
recognise.[^lex-sanctioned-psyker] Powers usually emerge between the ages of ten and
twenty, though sometimes in infancy and sometimes in middle
age.[^lex-sanctioned-psyker] Those whose talent surfaces late, or who evaded the ships,
are rogues and fugitives, left to choose between a life on the run and surrendering
themselves.[^lex-sanctioned-psyker]

There is a bleak coda to the doctrine. Imperial citizens are indoctrinated to fear and
hate psykers whether or not they serve the Throne, and many sanctioned psykers struggle
with their own self-loathing, reviled by what they are.[^lex-sanctioned-psyker] The
Imperium teaches contempt for a class of person it cannot function without.

## Transport, Wards and the Tithe {#transport-and-tithe}

The ships themselves are built for containment rather than carriage. They are covered in
anti-psychic wards and constructed from a psychically resistant ebony
metal,[^lex-league-of-black-ships] with hexagrammic and pentagrammic wards used
extensively.[^lex-league-of-black-ships] Their cargo rides in cavernous, psi-shielded
holds.[^lex-league-of-black-ships] As noted in [[I.T2.Ch6]], the crews are themselves
selected from the psionically dense grades — people the cargo cannot reach.

Each ship is overseen by a member of the Chamber Astra of the Sisters of Silence, under
the **Mistress of the Black Fleet**.[^lex-league-of-black-ships] The Sisters are Omega
grade; their own Chapter is [[IV.T8.Ch28]]. So the vessels are warded against the Warp,
crewed by the psychically blunt, and commanded by the actively anti-psychic — three
independent layers of insulation between the Imperium and what it is carrying.

Collection runs on a fixed cycle. Each Imperial world is visited roughly once a century,
and on a ship's approach the planetary governor is instructed to prepare the customary
levy: a **tithe** of psykers.[^lex-league-of-black-ships] Gathering them is local work,
performed variously by Inquisitorial storm troopers, local enforcers, or Administratum and
Arbites personnel, and the collected are held in a **Sanctum Psykana** — a well-defended
ancient monastery-prison protected by great Gellar-field engines — until a ship
comes.[^lex-league-of-black-ships]

The system has begun to fail in ways worth recording precisely, because they show an
institution under strain rather than in operation. Overabundance and late or absent tithe
vessels have produced prisoner overflow, and some governors now use mundane prisons to
hold the surplus.[^lex-league-of-black-ships] More strikingly, worlds failing to meet
quota have been known to hire mercenaries — Leagues of Votann kindreds among them — to raid
Black Ships and steal their human cargo under the guise of xenos piracy, delivering
psykers stolen from one world's tithe so that another governor can avoid summary execution
for failing to meet his own.[^lex-league-of-black-ships] Imperial worlds commission piracy
against Imperial ships to launder a quota. The Votann are [[VI.T6.Ch19]].

Once a levy is aboard, the captain makes an initial evaluation before moving to the next
world in the circuit.[^lex-league-of-black-ships] That evaluation has teeth: those tainted
by Chaos, already possessed, or judged too dangerously powerful or corrupt are killed
outright, and this is the captain's own
responsibility.[^lex-league-of-black-ships] Inquisitors commonly travel aboard, since the
voyage is a good opportunity to assess a planet's potential for psychic
corruption.[^lex-league-of-black-ships] Should the Administratum declare a world
abandoned, the ships simply stop calling.[^lex-league-of-black-ships]

## Triage — Sanctioning and Sacrifice {#sanctioning}

What happens on Terra is the part of this Title that most deserves plain statement.
Millions of psykers are collected each day across the Imperium and brought
in.[^lex-sanctioned-psyker] The **Scholastia Psykana** — also spelled Scholastica
Psykana — receives them: a vast teaching institution with secret locations across the
Imperium, devoted to developing and controlling young psykers' powers and forging them
into instruments of the God-Emperor's will.[^lex-scholastia-psykana]

Testing decides everything. The process examines power level, control, strength of will,
willingness to learn and other factors, and its outcome is binary: harsh training and
possible survival into Imperial service, or death as a
sacrifice.[^lex-sanctioned-psyker] Initial evaluation divides the levy by innate power,
character and willingness; the youngest are preferred, being easiest to train and
indoctrinate, and it is those recruited as children who go on to fill important
positions.[^lex-scholastia-psykana]

The majority do not. Most of the millions collected are found wanting and shipped to Terra
as sacrifices[^lex-sanctioned-psyker] — for being too weak of will, for lacking useful
potential, or for other reasons.[^lex-sanctioned-psyker] Those deemed weak in character or
power, too dangerous, or too old are sacrificed to the Emperor, their souls leeched from
their bodies to sustain him and to power the
Astronomican.[^lex-scholastia-psykana] Which closes a circuit this codex opened in
[[IV.T9.Ch31]]: the beacon that makes Imperial travel possible is fuelled by the psykers
that travel collects.

Survival is not assured even for those kept. Acolytes murder each other and form gangs;
the **Black Sentinels** patrol the facilities in armour granting immunity to Warp
abilities, and will beat an acolyte to death for the smallest infraction; the teachers are
called prefectors.[^lex-scholastia-psykana] During a psyker's first year, the source
states, being killed by the training is about as likely as being disposed of by a
prefector or killed by the Sentinels.[^lex-scholastia-psykana]

For the few who complete it, the terminal rite is the **Soul Binding**. The candidate's
mind is brought before the Emperor and a portion of his essence transferred into it. The
process is traumatic; not all survive it despite years of preparation, and many who do
lose much of their sanity.[^lex-soul-binding] Its most visible cost is damage to the optic
nerves, leaving almost all Astropaths blind — though some instead lose smell, hearing,
taste or touch.[^lex-soul-binding] In exchange it grants greater psychic capability and
added protection against the perils described in
[[I.T2.Ch8]].[^lex-soul-binding] Legend holds the first to undergo it was Malcador the
Sigillite.[^lex-soul-binding] The Astropaths themselves are [[IV.T9.Ch29]].

The figures above — millions collected daily, the majority expended — are the ones the
setting gives, and they are poorly attested even by its own standards. Treat them as an
indication of scale and intent rather than as a count.
