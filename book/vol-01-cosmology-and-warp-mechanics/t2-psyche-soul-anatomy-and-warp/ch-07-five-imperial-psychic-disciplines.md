# Five Imperial Psychic Disciplines

## The Five Most Common {#the-five}

Psychic powers are many and varied, but they usually fall into one of several
disciplines — branches of talent rather than lists of individual effects. The source
names five as the most common:[^lex-psyker]

| Discipline | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Biomancy** | Manipulation of biological energy and processes |
| **Divination** | Predicting the future |
| **Pyromancy** | Manipulation of fire |
| **Telekinesis** | Converting psychic energy into physical force |
| **Telepathy** | Contacting and controlling the minds of others |

Two corrections to this Chapter's own title are needed before going further, and both come
from the source rather than from any view of ours.

First, these are **the five most common**, not the only five.[^lex-psyker] The same article
goes on to list a dozen further disciplines organised by culture — daemonology, geokinesis,
interromancy, obscuration, promethean pyromancy and others — plus generic powers found
across species that are not considered to share a discipline at all.[^lex-psyker] A reader
who takes "five disciplines" as a closed taxonomy will be wrong.

Second, they are **not Imperial**. Powers can be organised by faction, as many sourcebooks
do, or by discipline, which cuts across factions: both humans and the Aeldari practise
telepathy.[^lex-psionics] Calling these five *Imperial* disciplines describes who is
classifying them, not who uses them.

Within the Imperium the five are not evenly distributed. Divination is recorded as the
least common of them, and pyromancy as one of the most.[^lex-psyker] That asymmetry is
worth noticing: the most common discipline is the one that throws fire, and the rarest is
the one that sees.

    The Section proposed here was "Divination & Spatial Magic". **No retrieved source
    records a discipline called spatial magic**, and no article exists under that name.
    It is not among the five, nor among the culture-specific disciplines the Psyker
    article enumerates. It has been dropped rather than invented, and biomancy — which
    the proposed breakdown left without a home — takes its place alongside divination.

One general caution applies to the two Sections below. The discipline articles are
largely built from game-rules material across several editions, and several carry
`{{Cite Marker End}}` and `{{Trivia}}` markers. What follows is confined to what each
discipline *is* and what its practitioners are called. The catalogues of named individual
powers are rules content rather than setting description, and are not summarised here.

## Fire, Force and Mind {#fire-force-mind}

The three disciplines that act outward on the physical or mental world share a structure:
each converts Warp energy into a familiar kind of effect.

**Pyromancy** allows a psyker to create and manipulate fire. Practitioners are called
pyromancers, or pyrokines.[^lex-pyromancy] The source's assessment is unusually
double-edged — it calls pyromancy one of the most common and spectacular forms of psychic
ability, and in the same breath says its uses are fairly limited.[^lex-pyromancy] That is
a rare piece of candour in a setting inclined to overstate, and it explains the
distribution noted above: the commonest discipline is also the least versatile.

**Telekinesis** converts mental energy into physical force. Its practitioners are
telekines, psykinetics or kinetics — three names for one discipline, which is itself a
small illustration of how unstandardised this vocabulary is.[^lex-telekinesis] They
manipulate the material world directly: lashing at enemies with psychic force, raising
invisible barriers, and — in the article's own phrasing — rending apart the fabric of
reality.[^lex-telekinesis] That last is worth flagging as description rather than
mechanism. Nothing in the retrieved material explains what tearing reality by telekinesis
consists of, and [[I.T1.Ch4]] records why this codex declines to fill such gaps.

**Telepathy** is the contacting and controlling of other minds. Its practitioners are
telepaths, and the described range runs from communication to outright domination: a
telepath can strip away a victim's sanity, induce states of terror, or possess their
body.[^lex-telepathy] It has recorded sub-forms — empathy, the ability to feel others'
emotions and thoughts, and verminspeaking, telepathy directed at animals and beasts, which
the source notes is common among unsanctioned psykers who have evaded the Black
Ships.[^lex-psyker] That last detail is quietly useful: it implies the powers that escape
detection are the ones with no military application.

Telepathy also does the Imperium's heaviest lifting. The astropathic relay that carries
messages between worlds is telepathy industrialised, and it is the subject of
[[IV.T9.Ch29]]; the rite that makes it possible is covered in [[I.T2.Ch9]].

## Flesh and Foresight {#flesh-and-foresight}

The remaining two disciplines act on the body and on time, and they are the two the
Imperium finds hardest to live with.

**Biomancy** is the manipulation of biological energy and processes. Through it, a
biomancer can change or influence change in the physical form of themselves or their
enemies.[^lex-biomancy][^lex-psyker] The described applications run from healing to
accelerating a victim's pulse until the heart or brain ruptures.[^lex-biomancy] It is the
discipline that makes the body negotiable — and in a setting whose doctrine treats
mutation as heresy, a sanctioned psyker able to reshape flesh sits in an uncomfortable
position that Volume IV's material on the Inquisition takes up.

**Divination** is the reading of what is hidden — the past of the galaxy and the course of
events yet to come. Its practitioners are diviners, or fortune tellers, and they cast
their minds into the strands of the Immaterium in search of answers, sometimes influencing
the outcomes they observe.[^lex-divination]

The mechanism given for it is one of the more elegant pieces of internal logic in the
setting. Time in the Warp appears to run at "right angles" to time in realspace, or in
the wrong direction entirely, and it is this that allows some psykers to make predictions
about future events.[^lex-psyker] Prophecy is not a supernatural exception here; it is a
consequence of the geometry described in [[I.T1.Ch3]].

The limits are given just as carefully. Omens are rarely clear-cut, because there is
considerable "white noise" in the Warp — the Astronomican's constant signal, ships and
creatures moving through it, warp storms, unexplained psychic echoes, and the whirlpools
of psychic misery that shroud some systems.[^lex-psyker] The beacon described in
[[IV.T9.Ch31]] is, among other things, interference.

And there is a trap the sources name explicitly: a common failure among inexperienced
diviners is that a vision warning of a possibility prompts actions that bring about
exactly the events they were trying to avoid.[^lex-divination] Divination is the least
common of the five in the Imperium,[^lex-psyker] which given the above may be less a
scarcity than a survival statistic.
