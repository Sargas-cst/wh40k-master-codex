# Imperial Assignment Scale

## The Twenty-Four Point Scale {#the-scale}

The Imperium measures psychic power on a rating system called **the Assignment**, or
Imperial assignment: a twenty-four point scale whose purpose is stated without
euphemism — it simplifies the comparison of psykers so that Imperial authorities can
recognise possible threats.[^lex-the-assignment] Classification is performed by the
Scholastica Psykana.[^lex-the-assignment] It is a threat index, not a talent index.

The grades are named for Greek letters, and they run in reverse: the *lower* the letter
sits in the Greek alphabet, the *higher* the power. Omega is the negative extreme, Alpha
the positive one.

| Band | Grades | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Negative | Omega, Psi, Chi, Phi, Upsilon, Tau, Sigma | Psychically inert to actively anti-psychic |
| Inert | Rho, Pi | The ordinary human range |
| Minor | Omicron, Xi, Nu, Mu, Lambda, Kappa | Unconscious, usually unnoticed activity |
| True psyker | Iota, Theta, Eta, Zeta, Epsilon, Delta, Gamma, Beta, Alpha | Deliberate, controlled power |

Those are twenty-four grades exactly — seven negative, two inert, fifteen positive — which
is a small but worthwhile check: the article's description of a twenty-four point scale
and the table it prints agree.

The ordinary human is rated **Rho** or **Pi**. Such people cannot draw on the Immaterium
consciously or unconsciously and cannot be possessed without outside psychic influence;
because almost all of mankind falls here, the common citizen poses little psychic
security threat.[^lex-the-assignment] **Pi** is subtler than Rho: it carries some genuine
psionic capacity, but so slight that any manifestation is dismissed as good luck or a
pretension to a sixth sense.[^lex-the-assignment]

By Imperial edict, anyone above Pi is to be found and reported to the Inquisition. The
article then concedes the obvious: with the multitude of threats besieging the Imperium
it is only *truly practical* to investigate those suspected of **Iota** or
greater.[^lex-the-assignment] The lower minor grades are legally under Inquisitorial
jurisdiction and administratively beyond reach — a gap between doctrine and capacity that
recurs throughout this codex.

**Iota** is the threshold that matters. At Iota and above an individual can control their
abilities with effort, and such people are true psykers who on discovery are normally
placed in the care of the Inquisition and the League of Black
Ships.[^lex-the-assignment] Higher up, the numbers thin sharply: **Delta** is given as
occurring in roughly one human birth per billion.[^lex-the-assignment] A grade is not
fixed for life, either — psykers not uncommonly unlock greater power with practice and
maturity, and are reclassified accordingly.[^lex-the-assignment]

!!! undefined "Not Imperial notation"

    The grades are sometimes seen numbered — Omega as −7, Alpha as 16, and upward from
    there. That numbering was added by later readers to show the order at a glance. The
    Imperium does not use it, and no source treats it as approved
    terminology.[^lex-the-assignment] A Delta is a Delta, not a 13.

!!! unverified "Thinly attested"

    Almost the entire scale — every grade, every description above — traces to a single
    page of one background book.[^lex-the-assignment] For a system the Imperium
    supposedly applies to every human birth, that is a remarkably narrow foundation, and
    the detail thins further at the extremes. Take the scale as the shape of Imperial
    thinking rather than as a specification.

## Beyond the Scale — the Plus Grades {#plus-scale}

In the rarest cases the twenty-four points do not characterise a being at all, and such
individuals pass beyond the scale entirely. These are the **plus** or **reversed** grades,
so called because the naming convention inverts: the sequence restarts at Alpha Plus and
climbs back through the alphabet.[^lex-the-assignment] Alpha Plus is therefore the
*lowest* of the grades above the scale, which is a persistent source of confusion in
casual usage.

What the sources claim for them is close to unbounded. In theory there is nothing a
trained plus psyker cannot achieve through force of will, from snapping a Titan in half
to summoning a legion of Greater Daemons.[^lex-the-assignment] A rogue one is counted by
the Ordo Hereticus among the greatest threats it can face, and this is where the metaphor
noted in [[I.T2.Ch5]] appears: their souls are *poetically said* to burn like supernovae
within the Empyrean, a beacon that daemons flock to and compete over for the right to
possess an unguarded mind.[^lex-the-assignment]

The upper grades are best read as a series of stated ceilings, each one a limit on what a
body or a mind can bear:

- **Alpha Plus** — the first of the plus grades. Their state of mind *always* comes with
  a perceivable degree of dementation or insanity.[^lex-the-assignment]
- **Beta Plus** — assumed to be the highest a human brain can withstand while retaining
  a fading semblance of sanity.[^lex-the-assignment]
- **Gamma Plus** — the highest psionic level ever registered in a human. More widely
  achievable by some unspecified alien species and by daemons — but not even they are
  known to exceed it.[^lex-the-assignment]
- **Delta Plus** and **Epsilon Plus** — never measured. Theoretical
  classifications.[^lex-the-assignment]
- **Zeta Plus** — considered the highest that living tissue can
  sustain.[^lex-the-assignment]
- **Eta Plus** and beyond — levels requiring a mind to rely on non-corporeal structures
  in order to survive at all.[^lex-the-assignment]

Read as a sequence, that list is doing something more interesting than escalation. It
describes the point at which each successive container fails: first sanity, then the
brain, then the record of observation, then measurement itself, then living tissue, then
corporeality. The scale does not run out of numbers. It runs out of things capable of
holding a mind.

## The Negative Grades and the Untouchables {#negative-scale}

The same scale runs downward into psychic absence, and the Imperium finds uses for that
too. The precise terminology for a negative grade appends "-Minus", so a negative Omega
is properly an **Omega-Minus** psyker — though the article notes the form is rarely
used.[^lex-the-assignment]

The six grades from **Psi** down to **Sigma** describe the psionically dense: individuals
oblivious to Warp fluctuation and psychic probing, ranging from merely blunt to extremely
resistant, yet still *touchable* by a sufficiently extreme application of
power.[^lex-the-assignment] This is not a curiosity. These grades generally qualify
someone to crew a Black Ship[^lex-the-assignment] — the Imperium selects its psyker
transports' crews for their inability to be reached by their cargo, which is covered in
[[I.T2.Ch9]].

**Omega** is the extreme, and its occupants are the [[g:blank]]s, also called
Untouchables and pariahs. They are so inert in the Warp as to exert actively negative
psychic influence on those around them.[^lex-the-assignment] The effect manifests as a
small region of blankness surrounding the individual, which psychic powers and Warp
creatures cannot penetrate.[^lex-the-assignment]

The described consequences are worth separating, because they are of two kinds. For
ordinary humans, the effect is social: Imperial science holds that the dead area around
the subject interferes with natural electrical-mental function, which produces irrational
fear and loathing of the person.[^lex-the-assignment] Blanks are hated without their
neighbours being able to say why. For psykers, the effect is direct — they are physically
and mentally painful to be near.[^lex-the-assignment]

The Imperium therefore fields them as weapons. Omega is given as the grade of the Sisters
of Silence, of Culexus Assassins, of the Preceptors-Intendant of the Ordo Sinister, and
of Aeldari Solitaires.[^lex-the-assignment][^lex-blank] Those bodies are treated at
[[IV.T8.Ch28]] and [[IV.T1.Ch3]]. What belongs here is the shape of the thing: a
civilisation that classifies psychic absence on the same instrument as psychic power,
and then recruits from both ends of it.

One caution about the word *pariah*, which the setting uses for at least four unrelated
things: human blanks, the Necron Pariahs built from captured ones, noctilith — "pariah
stone" — and the Pariah Nexus. Only the first is the subject of this Chapter, and
conflating them is an easy mistake to make.
